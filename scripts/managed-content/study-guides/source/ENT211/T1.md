## Topic 1: Concept of Entrepreneurship

**What you must know:** the Aliko Dangote story; the two definitions of entrepreneurship and its simple formula; the nine scopes of entrepreneurship with their examples; the eight characteristics of an entrepreneur; the seven dimensions of entrepreneurship with their examples; the Tunde reflection case.

### Lesson 1.1: Learning goals and the Aliko Dangote story

**Must know**

- **Learning objectives (4):** by the end of this topic you should be able to:
    1. Define Entrepreneurship.
    2. Describe the Scope and Dimensions of Entrepreneurship.
    3. Understand the Characteristics of an Entrepreneur.
    4. Understand the Dimensions of Entrepreneurship.
- **Aliko Dangote's story:**
    - A young man, **21 years old**, from **Kano State**, started a small **trading** business with a **loan from his uncle**.
    - He bought and sold **sugar, rice and cement**, delivering goods **himself** to customers.
    - Over time he noticed that **Nigeria imported almost every product it consumed**. He saw a **bigger opportunity**: **produce locally what the country was importing**.
    - He took the **bold step** into **manufacturing**, starting with **cement**.
    - Today his companies produce **cement, sugar, salt, pasta, fertilizer and petroleum products**, **creating jobs and transforming industries** (the Dangote Refinery is one of his plants).
- **Lesson of the story:** entrepreneurship begins with **spotting opportunities, taking risks and creating value that meets national needs**.

**Explain more**

- Read the story as a path: small trader (sugar, rice, cement) → spots that Nigeria imports almost everything → moves into manufacturing (cement first) → many products and many jobs. Each step matches an idea you will meet later: spotting the import gap is **opportunity recognition**, moving into manufacturing is **risk taking**, and the jobs and industries he built are **value creation**.
- Exam traps on the numbers and names: age is **21** (not 18, 25 or 30); state is **Kano** (not Lagos, Kaduna or Kogi); the money came from an **uncle** (not a bank, a salary or a government grant).
- Do not mix the two product lists. What he **traded** at the start: sugar, rice, cement. What his companies **produce** today: cement, sugar, salt, pasta, fertilizer, petroleum products. Salt, pasta and fertilizer belong to the later list only. Purified bottled water is **not** a Dangote product; it is Tunde's business in Lesson 1.6.
- His first manufacturing product was **cement**, not sugar or fertilizer.
- The "bigger opportunity" was to **produce locally** what Nigeria imported. It was not to import more cheaply, export raw produce or open shops for imported goods.
- An option that ignores the learning objectives (for example, calculating a break-even point) is not one of the four goals of this topic.

### Lesson 1.2: What entrepreneurship means

**Must know**

- **Definition 1 (a process):** entrepreneurship is the process of **identifying opportunities, mobilising resources, creating innovative products or services and taking risks to generate value and achieve profit**.
- **Definition 2 (a mindset and process):** it is the **mindset** and process of **discovering problems, designing innovative solutions and delivering value** to **society, markets or organisations**.
- **In simple terms:** **Entrepreneurship = Opportunity + Innovation + Value Creation + Risk**

**Explain more**

- Definition 1 has four actions and one goal. Actions: identify opportunities, mobilise resources, create innovative products or services, take risks. Goal: **generate value and achieve profit**. If an option says "delegate all risks to the government" or "avoid competition", it is not part of the definition.
- Definition 2 adds the word **mindset**: entrepreneurship is also a way of thinking. Its three steps run in order: discover a problem → design an innovative solution → deliver value. The value goes to **society, markets or organisations**, not just to family, shareholders or government.
- The formula has exactly **four** parts: Opportunity, Innovation, Value Creation, Risk. Common distractors are words from elsewhere in this topic, such as Autonomy, Resource Mobilisation or Profit, or everyday words like Capital, Labour, Money. If asked to complete "Opportunity + Innovation + \_\_\_\_ + Risk", the answer is **Value Creation**.
- Think of a Yaba student who sees that classmates waste time queuing to print handouts, sets up a quick printing service, and earns from it. That is opportunity, innovation, value and risk in one small example.

### Lesson 1.3: Scope of entrepreneurship

**Must know**

Entrepreneurship covers **nine** scopes (areas where it happens):

| Scope | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technological** | Tech-driven solutions | **Paystack** and **Flutterwave** revolutionising digital payments |
| **Social Entrepreneurship** | Innovative solutions to solving social problems | **Leap Africa** tackling youth development challenges |
| **Agricultural** | Innovative ideas to agriculture for profit | **FarmCrowdy** using crowdfunding for farming |
| **Educational** | Innovation in teaching, learning and educational services | **uLesson** |
| **Creative Entrepreneurship** | Creativity, talent and cultural expressions to produce value | **Nollywood** film, music and fashion brands |
| **Corporate Entrepreneurship** | Innovative activities carried out inside a large organisation to create new products, services or processes | **MTN MoMo**, **Gmail at Google** |
| **International Entrepreneurship** | Activities across national borders: exporting, expansion and foreign partnership | **Dangote Cement in 10 African countries** |
| **Public Sector (Government)** | Innovating within public agencies to improve service delivery | **Digital tax collection systems**, **online passport application portals** |
| **Green/Environmental** | Developing solutions that protect the environment or promote sustainability | **Sahara Energy Renewable Project** |

**Explain more**

- Learn the first four as a group (Technological, Social, Agricultural, Educational) and the other five as a second group (Creative, Corporate, International, Public Sector, Green/Environmental). Nine in all.
- How to tell look-alikes apart:
    - **Corporate** vs **Public Sector**: both innovate *inside* an existing body. Corporate is inside a **large organisation** (MTN, Google, a big bank). Public Sector is inside **government agencies** to improve service delivery (tax, passports, an online licence-renewal portal).
    - **Social** vs **Green/Environmental**: Social solves **social problems** (Leap Africa and youth development). Green protects the **environment** or promotes **sustainability** (Sahara Energy's renewable project; a firm installing solar systems to replace polluting diesel generators).
    - **Technological** vs **Corporate**: Paystack and Flutterwave are **technological** (tech-driven payments). MTN MoMo is also about payments, but it is listed as **corporate** because it was built inside a large company.
    - **Creative** covers talent and culture: films, music, fashion. A Lagos musician building a brand around Yoruba culture is creative entrepreneurship.
    - **International** means crossing borders: exporting, expanding, foreign partnerships. An Aba shoemaker exporting to Ghana and partnering with a distributor in Senegal is international entrepreneurship.
- Number trap: Dangote Cement operates in **10** African countries (not 5, 15 or 20).
- FarmCrowdy appears twice in this topic: as the example of **Agricultural** scope (crowdfunding for farming) and as the example of the **Value Creation** dimension (Lesson 1.5).
- Do not confuse lists. Proactiveness and Autonomy are **dimensions**; Vision, Resourceful and Risk Taker are **characteristics**. None of them is a scope.

### Lesson 1.4: Characteristics of an entrepreneur

**Must know**

**Eight** characteristics of an entrepreneur:

1. Vision
2. Creativity
3. Passion
4. Ability to Network
5. Self Confidence
6. Risk Taker
7. Resourceful
8. Never Give-up (**Grit**)

**Explain more**

- These describe the **person**. In plain terms: **vision** is seeing where you want the business to go; **creativity** is coming up with new ideas; **passion** is a deep love for what you do; **ability to network** is building relationships that bring partners, mentors and customers; **self confidence** is belief in yourself; **risk taker** is being willing to step into the unknown; **resourceful** is making the most of what little you have; **never give-up** is pushing on after failure.
- **Grit** is the other name for **Never Give-up**. Not passion, not vision.
- Applying them to stories:
    - A caterer whose business failed twice but who learned and started again until the third attempt worked: **Never Give-up (Grit)**.
    - Someone who attends business events, keeps in touch with mentors and builds relationships that later bring partners and customers: **Ability to Network**.
    - Someone with very little money who starts a snack business with her mother's kitchen, second-hand utensils and free social media advertising: **Resourceful**.
- Count trap: there are **eight** characteristics (not seven, which is the number of dimensions).
- Look-alike trap: **Risk Taker** is a characteristic; **Risk taking** is also a dimension (Lesson 1.5). **Creativity** is a characteristic; the related dimension is **Innovativeness**. Autonomy, Value Creation, Proactiveness, Opportunity Recognition and Technological are **not** characteristics.

### Lesson 1.5: Dimensions of entrepreneurship

**Must know**

Entrepreneurship has several dimensions that describe the **behaviours, processes and characteristics that drive entrepreneurial activity**. There are **seven**:

| Dimension | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Innovativeness** | Creating something new or improving existing products, services or processes | **Flutterwave** created an innovative payment infrastructure |
| **Proactiveness** | Entrepreneurs anticipate future trends, act ahead of competitors and seize opportunity early | **Sim Shagaya** pioneered **Konga** (e-commerce) online shopping |
| **Risk taking** | Entrepreneurs take calculated risks in uncertain environments | **Dangote** invested in local industries despite import dominance and market uncertainty |
| **Value Creation** | Creating value: economic, social or environmental | **FarmCrowdy** connected farmers with digital funding and agricultural support |
| **Opportunity Recognition** | Entrepreneurship begins with identifying unmet needs, market gaps or solutions to problems | **Flutterwave**'s innovative payment structure |
| **Resource Mobilisation** | Entrepreneurs know how to gather and manage the resources needed to bring ideas to life | **Tony Elumelu** turned **UBA** into a global bank |
| **Autonomy** | Independence in decision-making and control over their goals and actions | **Startup founders** starting enterprises |

**Explain more**

- In the picture, "Entrepreneurial Dimensions" sits at the centre with the seven dimensions around it, showing that all seven work together.
- Key words to spot each one in a question:
    - "Creating something new / improving existing" → **Innovativeness**.
    - "Anticipate trends / ahead of competitors / seize early" → **Proactiveness**. A startup that predicts cashless payments at roadside markets and launches before any competitor is proactive.
    - "Calculated risks / uncertain environments" → **Risk taking**. Note *calculated*: not reckless, not avoiding all risk, not passing risk to customers.
    - "Economic, social or environmental value" → **Value Creation**. Value is not only economic profit.
    - "Unmet needs / market gap" → **Opportunity Recognition**. A UNILAG student who sees that students struggle to find affordable hostels near campus is recognising an opportunity.
    - "Gather and manage resources" → **Resource Mobilisation**.
    - "Independence / own decisions / control over goals" → **Autonomy**. Someone who resigns a salaried job to make her own decisions is showing autonomy.
- Example traps: **Flutterwave** illustrates both **Innovativeness** and **Opportunity Recognition**. **Sim Shagaya** pioneered **Konga** (not Paystack, uLesson or FarmCrowdy), for **Proactiveness**. **Tony Elumelu and UBA** is **Resource Mobilisation**, not Proactiveness. **Dangote** here is **Risk taking**. **Startup founders** is **Autonomy**.
- Do not confuse lists: Passion, Creativity, Vision and Grit are **characteristics**; Social Entrepreneurship is a **scope**. None is a dimension.

### Lesson 1.6: Reflection case: Tunde's water business

**Must know**

- **Problem Tunde noticed:** people in his neighbourhood often struggled to find **clean, affordable drinking water**. Many relied on **bottled water, which was expensive**, or **unsafe local sources**.
- **His business:** producing **purified bottled water**.
- **What he did:** **rented a small space**, **bought equipment**, **hired staff** and **marketed** his product to **local shops and households**.
- **Result:** within **a year** the business became **profitable**, and he started **expanding into nearby towns**.
- **Four reflection questions:** (i) identify the opportunity Tunde recognised; (ii) what resources did he mobilise; (iii) what risks did he take; (iv) how did he create value for the community.

**Explain more**

- Link each question to a dimension:
    - (i) **Opportunity Recognition:** the unmet need for clean, affordable drinking water.
    - (ii) **Resource Mobilisation:** the rented space, the equipment, the staff (and the marketing effort).
    - (iii) **Risk taking:** he spent money on rent, equipment and wages before knowing if people would buy.
    - (iv) **Value Creation:** safe, affordable water for the community, plus jobs for his staff.
- Traps: a **loan from his uncle** belongs to Dangote's story, not Tunde's. Tunde sold to **local shops and households**, not exporters, factories or government. Within a year he **expanded into nearby towns**; he did not export or sell the business.
- The water problem had two sides: bottled water was **expensive**, and local sources were **unsafe**.

### One-minute recap

- Dangote: 21, Kano, uncle's loan; traded sugar, rice, cement; saw Nigeria imported almost everything; started manufacturing with cement.
- Entrepreneurship begins with spotting opportunities, taking risks and creating value that meets national needs.
- Definition: identifying opportunities, mobilising resources, creating innovative products or services and taking risks to generate value and achieve profit.
- Also a mindset: discovering problems, designing innovative solutions, delivering value to society, markets or organisations.
- Formula: Opportunity + Innovation + Value Creation + Risk.
- 9 scopes: Technological, Social, Agricultural, Educational, Creative, Corporate, International, Public Sector, Green/Environmental.
- 8 characteristics: Vision, Creativity, Passion, Ability to Network, Self Confidence, Risk Taker, Resourceful, Never Give-up (Grit).
- 7 dimensions: Innovativeness, Proactiveness, Risk taking, Value Creation, Opportunity Recognition, Resource Mobilisation, Autonomy.
- Tunde: purified bottled water; space, equipment, staff; profitable within a year; expanding to nearby towns.
