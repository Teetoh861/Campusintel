## Topic 4: Enterprise Formation (Business Plan)

**What you must know:** the three learning outcomes; the business plan (Bank of America, 2025) and its 8 core components in order; the Swift Clean case and its 7 stages; sole proprietorship, limited liability partnership, general partnership and cooperative with their cited definitions; the comparison table of liability, key disadvantage, best for and key advantage.

### Lesson 4.1: Learning outcomes and the basics of a business plan

**Must know**

This topic (Weeks 6 and 7, Enterprise Formation: Business Plan) has **3 learning outcomes**. By the end you should be able to:

1. **Identify** the different forms of business ownership in Nigeria.
2. **Evaluate** the advantages and disadvantages of each ownership type.
3. **Develop** the outline of a simple business plan.

- **Business plan:** a strategic roadmap detailing a company's goals and the methods to achieve them **(Bank of America, 2025)**.
- A business plan is **not just for investors**; it is your **foundational roadmap** that forces you to:
    - think through every step,
    - identify weaknesses early,
    - align your team.
- You **don't need 50 pages**: start with a **clear, practical and brief mini plan**.

**Core components of a simple plan (8), in order:**

| No. | Component | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive Summary | Summary of the plan |
| 2 | Company Description | What does your company do? |
| 3 | Business Opportunity | What is the market potential? |
| 4 | Competitor Analysis | What makes you stand out? |
| 5 | SWOT Analysis | Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats |
| 6 | Revenue Model | How will you make money? |
| 7 | Financial Projection | Company's growth prediction |
| 8 | Team | How you structure and manage your company |

The 8 components are often shown as two rows of four boxes: top row Executive Summary, Company Description, Business Opportunity, Competitor Analysis; bottom row SWOT Analysis, Revenue Model, Financial Projection, Team.

**Explain more**

- Think of the business plan as a map for a journey from Akoka to Lekki: the goals are the destination, the methods are the route. The key words in the definition are **strategic roadmap**, **goals** and **methods**. Do not confuse it with a record of past profits, a partnership agreement or a registration certificate; those are different things.
- The citation is **Bank of America (2025)**. Other names you meet in this topic (Tyler, Beattie, Bloomenthal, Lip) belong to the ownership forms, not to the business plan.
- The plan is first for **you**, the founder. An exam may offer "it is needed only when you want investors" or "it must be at least 50 pages": both are false. Equally, a plan does **not guarantee** that investors will fund you; the three things it helps you do are think through every step, identify weaknesses early and align your team.
- If a friend says he cannot start until he writes a 50-page plan, the right advice is to start with a clear, practical and brief **mini plan**.
- Learn each component by the question it answers:
    - "Summary of the plan" = **Executive Summary** (the short overview at the front).
    - "What does your company do?" = **Company Description**.
    - "What is the market potential?" = **Business Opportunity**. If a food delivery startup estimates how many office workers in Ikeja would pay for its service, that is Business Opportunity.
    - "What makes you stand out?" = **Competitor Analysis** (how you compare with rivals).
    - **SWOT** = Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. Strengths and weaknesses describe your own business; opportunities and threats are what you face around you. Watch for fake expansions such as "Strategy", "Workforce" or "Trends".
    - "How will you make money?" = **Revenue Model**. A printing shop that charges per page and sells monthly packages to student associations is describing its Revenue Model.
    - "Company's growth prediction" = **Financial Projection**. A caterer predicting how her company will grow over the next three years is writing her Financial Projection.
    - "How you structure and manage your company" = **Team**.
- **Traps:** Revenue Model (how money comes in) vs Financial Projection (how the company is predicted to grow). Business Opportunity (market potential) vs Competitor Analysis (standing out). Items such as **Go-to-Market**, **USP**, **Milestones** and **Target Market** are stages in the Swift Clean case (Lesson 4.2), **not** among the 8 core components. "Patronage refund" and "Key Disadvantage" belong to the ownership forms, not the plan. The count is **8**, not 6, 7 or 10.

### Lesson 4.2: Case study: Swift Clean

**Must know**

**Swift Clean:** a **Lagos-based** idea, a **Mobile Laundry Service Startup**. Its plan runs in **7 stages**:

1. **Business Idea:** on-demand laundry **pickup and delivery via an app** for students in **Akoka and Yaba**.
2. **Problem & Solution:** students **waste time and money on unreliable laundry services**. Swift Clean offers **scheduled pickups**, **transparent pricing** and **48-hour turnaround**.
3. **Target Market:** **15,000+ undergraduate students** in **UNILAG and YabaTech hostels**.
4. **USP:** **student-friendly pricing**, **free pickup/delivery on campus**, and a **loyalty points system**.
5. **Go-to-Market:** **campus ambassador programme**, **targeted Instagram/Facebook ads**, **partnerships with student associations**.
6. **Revenue Model:** **per-kg pricing**, with discounted monthly **"bulk" plans for hostels and couples**.
7. **Milestones (First 90 Days):** **launch app**, **onboard 200 customers**, **secure 1 hostel partnership**.

The stages are shown on a horizontal dashed timeline, with Stages 1, 3, 5 and 7 below the line and Stages 2, 4 and 6 above it.

**Explain more**

- Swift Clean shows how a simple idea becomes a plan step by step: start with the idea, name the problem and your answer to it, say who your customers are and how many, say why they should choose you, how you will reach them, how you will make money, and what you will achieve first.
- **USP** (unique selling proposition) is what makes Swift Clean special to the customer: cheap for students, free campus pickup and delivery, loyalty points. **Go-to-Market** is how it reaches customers: ambassadors, social media ads, student associations. Exams love swapping these two. Free pickup/delivery is USP, not Go-to-Market; the campus ambassador programme is Go-to-Market, not USP and not part of Problem & Solution.
- **Problem & Solution** contains exactly three offers: scheduled pickups, transparent pricing, 48-hour turnaround.
- **Numbers to protect:** **48** hours (not 24 or 72), **15,000+** students (not 1,500 or 50,000), **200** customers (not 2,000 or 15,000), **1** hostel partnership (not 10), **90** days (not 30 days, 6 months or a year), **7** stages.
- **Places to protect:** the idea is for students in **Akoka and Yaba**; the target market is **UNILAG and YabaTech** hostels (not LASU or UNIBEN). It is Lagos-based and a laundry service, not food delivery or hostel booking.
- Revenue is **per kg**, not per item, per hour or a flat yearly fee; the discounted **monthly bulk plans** are for **hostels and couples**.
- Order to remember: Idea, Problem, Market, USP, Go-to-Market, Revenue, Milestones. So the stage after Target Market (3) is USP (4); Go-to-Market is Stage 5; the first is Business Idea; the last is Milestones.
- Note that Stage 6 of the case (Revenue Model) is also one of the 8 core components, but the case has no "Executive Summary" stage.

### Lesson 4.3: Sole proprietorship and general partnership

**Must know**

**Sole proprietorship**

- A **single owner** with **no legal difference between the owner and the business**: the owner has **total control**, **keeps all profits**, but also has **unlimited personal liability** for all debts and losses.
- It is **simple to register** and is often called a **'Business Name'**.
- "Someone who owns an **unincorporated business by himself or herself** is a sole proprietor" **(Tyler, 2025)**.

**General partnership**

- A business arrangement by which **two or more individuals** agree to **share responsibilities, assets, profits, and financial and legal liabilities** associated with a jointly-owned business **(Bloomenthal, 2025)**.
- **Less expensive to form than a corporation**; common for **small, trusted teams** (e.g., a **boutique firm run by spouses**).

**Explain more**

- The sole proprietor **is** the business in the eyes of the law. That is why the owner enjoys everything (control, all the profit) and also carries everything (all the debts). If Mama Nkechi runs her provision shop in Mushin alone under a business name and a supplier can claim against her personal property for a shop debt, she is a sole proprietor. The picture of a smiling market vendor beside a "RISK" gauge sums it up: simple and yours, but risky.
- "Business Name" is the everyday label for a sole proprietorship; do not confuse it with "Limited Company" or "Cooperative Society".
- A sole proprietorship is **not** a distinct legal entity and does **not** have perpetual existence; those are features of the LLP (Lesson 4.4).
- **Tyler (2025)** goes with the sole proprietor; the key word in his line is **unincorporated**.
- A general partnership is like two friends who open a phone accessories shop together and share the work, the money and the debts. **Bloomenthal (2025)** goes with the general partnership. The key word is **share**: responsibilities, assets, profits and liabilities, including legal and financial ones.
- Compared with a corporation it is **cheaper** to form (not more expensive), and there is no rule requiring investors or a minimum of ten partners.
- The example is a **boutique firm run by spouses**. Do not mix it up with the LLP example (law, accounting, consulting) or the cooperative example (farmers pooling resources).

### Lesson 4.4: Limited liability partnership and cooperative

**Must know**

**Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)**

- A business structure **blending features of partnerships and corporations**, offering partners **limited personal liability** for business debts and obligations, **protecting their personal assets from business failures or co-partners' misconduct**, while allowing **flexibility in management** **(Beattie, 2025)**.
- It is a **distinct legal entity** with **perpetual existence**, **separate from its members**, enabling it to **own property, sue, and be sued**.
- Ideal for **professional services** like **law, accounting, or consulting**.

**Cooperative (co-op)**

- A business **owned and democratically controlled by its members**, who are also its **customers, workers, or producers** **(Lip, 2024)**.
- Focus: meeting **shared economic, social, and cultural needs rather than just profit**.
- Profits are often **returned as patronage refunds** or **reinvested for member benefit**.
- **Key features (4):** voluntary membership; equal voting rights (**one member, one vote**); collective management; mutual benefit.
- Differs from traditional corporations, where **control is based on share ownership**.
- **Types (3):**
    1. **Consumer** co-op (grocery).
    2. **Worker** co-op (employee-owned).
    3. **Producer** co-op (farmers pooling resources).

**Explain more**

- The LLP sits between a partnership and a corporation: partners run it flexibly like a partnership, but the business stands on its own like a corporation. Because it is a **separate legal entity**, it can own property and be sued in its own name, and it continues even if members change (perpetual existence).
- **Limited** liability is the heart of it: if the firm fails, or a co-partner misbehaves, the other partners' personal assets are protected. An option saying LLP partners have **unlimited** liability is false. The protection is from business failures or co-partners' misconduct, not from taxes, competition or government regulation.
- Three chartered accountants who want protection from each other's misconduct and want the firm to own property in its own name should choose an **LLP**.
- **Beattie (2025)** goes with the LLP. Memory hook: the four ownership citations are **T**yler (sole), **B**eattie (LLP), **B**loomenthal (general partnership), **L**ip (cooperative, the only 2024 one).
- The cooperative's members are the very people it serves: shoppers, workers or producers. Control is **democratic**, one member one vote, no matter how much money each puts in. "Control based on share ownership" is the opposite of a co-op; it describes traditional corporations.
- **Patronage refund** means profit given back to members; it belongs to cooperatives only.
- Match the type to the members:
    - Shoppers own the grocery store = **consumer** co-op.
    - Employees own the bakery in Ibadan and each has an equal vote = **worker** co-op.
    - Rice farmers in Kebbi pool money to buy a shared milling machine and sell together, one vote each = **producer** co-op.
- A co-op is not about maximising profit for one owner, protecting professional partners, or attracting outside investors to scale; it exists to meet members' shared economic, social and cultural needs.

### Lesson 4.5: Comparing forms of business ownership

**Must know**

| Form of ownership | Liability | Key disadvantage | Best for | Key advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole Proprietorship | Unlimited (owner is the business) | Personal risk, hard to raise capital | Solo vendors, freelancers | Simple, low cost, full control |
| General Partnership | Unlimited (partners are jointly liable) | Potential for conflict, shared personal risk | Small teams with high trust (e.g., family business) | Shared skills/resources, easy to form |
| Limited Liability Co. (Ltd) | Limited (to shares owned) | More complex/expensive to register and run | Most scalable startups, any business seeking investment | Protects personal assets, credible for investors |
| Cooperative | Varies | Can be slow to decide | Agricultural groups, credit unions, collective retail | Member-owned, democratic control |

**Explain more**

- The table compares **four** forms: sole proprietorship, general partnership, limited liability company (Ltd) and cooperative. Note that the **Ltd** (limited liability company) appears here; it is not the same as the LLP from Lesson 4.4, so read options carefully.
- **Liability:** the two **unlimited** forms are **sole proprietorship** and **general partnership**. The difference is the reason: for the sole proprietor, "owner is the business"; for the partnership, "partners are jointly liable". **Ltd** is limited **to shares owned**. **Cooperative** liability **varies**.
- **Key disadvantages** line up with each form's nature:
    - One person carries everything, so sole proprietorship = **personal risk, hard to raise capital**.
    - People share decisions and debts, so general partnership = **potential for conflict, shared personal risk**.
    - Ltd has more rules, so = **more complex/expensive to register and run**.
    - Everyone votes, so cooperative = **can be slow to decide**.
- **Best for:** solo vendors and freelancers (sole); small high-trust teams such as a family business (general partnership); scalable startups and businesses seeking investment (Ltd); agricultural groups, credit unions, collective retail (cooperative).
- **Key advantages:** simple, low cost, full control (sole); shared skills/resources, easy to form (general partnership); protects personal assets, credible for investors (Ltd); member-owned, democratic control (cooperative).
- Scenario: Zainab is starting a fintech in Lagos, wants to scale by raising money from investors, and wants her personal assets protected. Choose the **Ltd**, because it is best for scalable startups seeking investment and protects personal assets.

### One-minute recap

- 3 outcomes: identify ownership forms in Nigeria, evaluate their advantages and disadvantages, develop a simple business plan outline.
- Business plan = strategic roadmap of goals and methods (Bank of America, 2025); not just for investors; start with a brief mini plan, not 50 pages.
- 8 components: Executive Summary, Company Description, Business Opportunity, Competitor Analysis, SWOT, Revenue Model, Financial Projection, Team.
- Swift Clean: 7 stages; Akoka and Yaba; 48-hour turnaround; 15,000+ UNILAG and YabaTech students; per-kg pricing; 200 customers and 1 hostel partnership in 90 days.
- Sole proprietorship (Tyler, 2025): one owner, "Business Name", unlimited liability.
- General partnership (Bloomenthal, 2025): two or more share everything; cheaper than a corporation; spouses' boutique firm.
- LLP (Beattie, 2025): limited liability, separate legal entity, perpetual existence; law, accounting, consulting.
- Cooperative (Lip, 2024): member-owned, one member one vote, patronage refunds; consumer, worker, producer types.
- Table: sole and general partnership unlimited; Ltd limited to shares; co-op varies and can be slow to decide.
