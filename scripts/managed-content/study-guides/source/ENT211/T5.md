## Topic 5: Business Registration and Collaboration

**What you must know:** the three learning outcomes; registration as a legal requirement within 28 days and its benefits; CAC, CAMA and the iCRP Portal; the 4 registration steps in order plus the post-registration TIN from FIRS; the 4 pitfalls with their consequences and fixes; partnership, strategic alliance (Wintel), joint venture and networking; the comparison table; the simple 4-step networking plan.

### Lesson 5.1: Business registration in Nigeria and why it matters

**Must know**

This topic (Weeks 8 and 9, Business Registration) has **3 learning outcomes**. By the end you should be able to:

1. Understand what **business registration** means.
2. Understand the **process** of business registration.
3. Know the **common pitfalls** to avoid.

- Registering your business is a **legal requirement within 28 days of starting**.
- Registration:
    - gives your business a **legal identity**,
    - **protects your name**,
    - **builds trust** with customers and banks,
    - is **essential for government contracts**,
    - is **needed to open a corporate bank account**.

The benefits are often shown as **4 labelled icons**:

| Icon label | Picture | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Name Protection | Padlock on stacked layers | Your business name is locked to you |
| Legal Identity | Gavel on documents | The business exists in law |
| Trust Building | Two businessmen shaking hands beside a document | Customers and banks see you as credible |
| Contracts | A signed "CONTRACT" document | You can take on government contracts |

**Explain more**

- Registration is **not optional** and not something you do "when the business grows". The law expects it within **28 days** of starting. Watch for wrong numbers in options: 14, 30 or 90 days are all traps. The answer is **28**.
- Match each benefit to a situation. If Tola registers "Tola's Kitchen" so nobody else can trade under that name, that is **name protection**. If a Surulere tailor's customers and bank start to see the business as more reliable after registration, that is **trust building**. If a business wants to bid for a government job, it needs registration because it is **essential for government contracts**. If it wants a **corporate** (business) bank account, it needs registration; a personal savings account is not what is meant.
- Registration does **not** excuse you from tax. In fact, tax registration comes straight after it (Lesson 5.2). Any option saying registration "exempts the business from tax registration" is false.

### Lesson 5.2: The registration process

**Must know**

- In Nigeria, business registration is managed by the **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)** under the **Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA)**.
- As of **2026**, the process is primarily completed **online** through the **iCRP Portal**.

**The 4 steps, in order:**

1. **Choose and Reserve a Name:** have **2 alternative names**. Check for and reserve an available name on the **CAC portal**.
2. **Complete Pre-Registration Form (CAC-BN01):** submit details **online**: **business activity** and **proprietor/partner details** (**NIN**, address, photo).
3. **Pay Filing Fees:** pay the prescribed fees **online**.
4. **Submit and Await Approval:** **CAC** processes the application. If approved, you download your **electronic Certificate of Registration** and **Certified Extract**.

- **Post-Registration:** you must then obtain a **Tax Identification Number (TIN)** from the **Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS)**.

**Explain more**

- Keep the two bodies apart. **CAC** registers the business (under **CAMA**). **FIRS** issues the **TIN**. A common trap swaps them, or brings in other bodies such as SMEDAN or CBN; neither of those manages registration in this topic.
- Keep the law apart from the portal: **CAMA** is the Act; the **iCRP Portal** is the online platform. An NIN enrolment portal or a "FIRS TIN portal" is not the registration platform.
- Memory aid for the order: **Name, Form, Fees, Submit** (then TIN). You cannot fill a form for a business that has no name yet, and you cannot submit before you pay.
    - Straight after the form (step 2) comes **paying the fees** (step 3), not submission.
    - The **first** step is always choosing and reserving a name.
- Count the alternatives: **2** alternative names, not 1, 3 or 5. They are your backups if your first choice is taken.
- The form's code is **CAC-BN01**. Do not accept look-alikes such as CAC-BN02 or CAMA-01. Notice what the form asks for: business activity plus the proprietor's or partners' **NIN**, address and photo. It does **not** ask for a business plan, bank statements, audited accounts or the TIN (the TIN comes later).
- Two documents arrive at approval: the **electronic Certificate of Registration** and the **Certified Extract**. A TIN or tax clearance certificate is not part of this download.
- The TIN is a **post-registration** step: it happens after approval, not during name reservation or form filling. So if Femi has just downloaded his Certificate of Registration, his next move is to get a **TIN from FIRS**.
- "Identify venues" belongs to the networking plan (Lesson 5.6), not to registration. Exams like to slip it into a list of registration steps.

### Lesson 5.3: Common registration pitfalls to avoid

**Must know**

There are **4 "Don'ts"**, each with a consequence and a matching **"Do"**:

| Don't | Consequence | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Skip the Name Search | Conflicting names cause **rejection** | Conduct a **thorough search** and **have backups** |
| Provide Inaccurate Information | Errors in documents cause **delays** | **Double-check** all names, addresses and NIN details |
| Forget Tax Registration | It leads to **penalties** | Proceed to **FIRS** for your TIN **immediately after CAC registration** |
| Miss Deadlines | You pay a **default fee** after the **28-day grace period** | **Register as soon as you decide** to start the business |

- **Consider Professional Help:** for **companies (Ltd)**, using a **CAC-accredited agent** can prevent costly mistakes.

**Explain more**

- Learn each pitfall as a chain: **mistake, result, cure**. Exams swap the results, so fix them firmly:
    - name search skipped = **rejection**;
    - wrong information = **delays**;
    - no tax registration = **penalties**;
    - missed deadline = **default fee**.
- Scenarios: Bisi's "Bisi Foods" is rejected because a business already uses a very similar name: she **skipped the name search**. Kunle's application is stuck for weeks because the address on his papers does not match his NIN record: he **provided inaccurate information**.
- The "Do" for the name search (thorough search plus backups) links back to step 1's **2 alternative names**.
- The tax "Do" says **immediately after CAC registration**, not before reserving a name, not after a year of trading and not only when chasing a contract.
- The deadline "Do" is to register **as soon as you decide** to start, not to wait until the grace period ends or until your first profit. The **28-day** figure is the same one from Lesson 5.1, now called the grace period.
- The CAC-accredited agent is recommended for **companies (Ltd)**; the point is to prevent costly mistakes. It is not recommended "for every business name", and it has nothing to do with alliances or networking groups.
- "Failing to join a strategic alliance" is not a registration pitfall; it is a distractor from the collaboration lessons.

### Lesson 5.4: Forms of business collaboration

**Must know**

- **Partnership:** a **formal** business arrangement where **two or more parties** combine resources and share **management responsibilities, profits and liabilities** for an **ongoing** business. The partners operate the business with a common view of **making a profit**.
- **Strategic Alliance:** a collaborative agreement between **two or more independent organisations** to pursue **mutually beneficial goals** while **maintaining their autonomy**.
    - Generally **more flexible and less integrated** than JVs.
    - Often involves **contractual agreements for specific activities** like **joint marketing, research or distribution**, **without creating a new company**.
    - Famous example in the tech industry: the **Wintel (Microsoft and Intel)** alliance.
- **Joint Venture (JV):** a **specific type of strategic alliance** where the partnering firms create a **new, jointly owned, separate legal entity** to pursue a **shared, often time-bound, objective**. This structure allows companies to:
    - **pool significant resources**,
    - **share risks and costs**,
    - **access new markets or technologies** they couldn't alone,
    - with a **clear legal and governance framework** in place.
- **Networking:** the **informal** process of building relationships and connections with other professionals or businesses for **potential future collaboration, information exchange and career development**. Unlike the other forms, networking does **not** involve **formal agreements, shared capital or a common business goal** at the outset.

The four forms are often shown with icons: a **handshake** (Partnership); two people shaking hands under a **dollar coin** (Strategic Alliance); people fitting together **jigsaw-puzzle pieces** (Joint Venture); two businessmen shaking hands, one holding a **briefcase** (Networking).

**Explain more**

- Spot the key words in each definition:
    - **Partnership:** "combine resources", "share management, profits and liabilities", "ongoing", "making a profit".
    - **Strategic alliance:** "independent", "maintaining their autonomy", "mutually beneficial goals", "without creating a new company".
    - **Joint venture:** "new, jointly owned, separate legal entity", "time-bound".
    - **Networking:** "informal", "relationships", "information exchange", "career development".
- A **JV is a type of strategic alliance**, not a type of partnership or networking. The difference is that a JV goes further and **creates a new company**; an ordinary alliance does not.
- **Wintel** = **Microsoft and Intel**, and it is a **strategic alliance**, not a JV. Watch for look-alike pairs such as Microsoft and IBM.
- The specific activities of an alliance are joint marketing, research and distribution. "Creating a new jointly owned company" is **not** one of them; that would make it a JV.
- The JV's advantages are pooling resources, sharing risks and costs, and reaching new markets or technologies, all **with** a clear legal and governance framework. An option claiming a JV "avoids any legal or governance framework" is false.
- Scenarios to practise:
    - Ngozi and Ibrahim pool their savings to run a pharmacy in Ikeja, sharing management, profits and liabilities on an ongoing basis = **partnership**.
    - A Lagos fintech and a telecom company sign a contract to jointly market a mobile wallet; both stay independent and no new company is formed = **strategic alliance**.
    - Two firms form a brand-new company they jointly own to run one project for a fixed number of years = **joint venture**.
    - A student attends tech meetups to meet professionals and swap ideas, with no agreement and no money put in = **networking**.
- Networking is not about shared capital in a common business. Building relationships, exchanging information and developing your career **are** features of networking.

### Lesson 5.5: Business collaboration compared

**Must know**

- Collaboration can take the form of **partnerships, alliances, joint ventures and networking**, differing primarily in their **formality, legal structure and objectives**.
- The **main distinction** lies in **whether a new legal entity is formed** and the **level of integration and commitment** involved.

| Feature | Strategic Alliance | Partnership | Joint Venture (JV) | Networking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | Formal agreement, but looser than a JV | Formal, typically long-term | Highly formal, contractual agreement | Informal relationship building |
| Legal Entity | No new entity created; partners remain independent | Single entity (two or more persons/companies) | Creates a new, separate legal entity, jointly owned | No new entity |
| Objective | Specific, mutually beneficial goals (e.g. R&D, marketing) | Ongoing general business for profit | Single, specific project or objective | Information exchange, relationship building, future opportunities |
| Risk/Control | Greater flexibility, less control/commitment than a JV | Shared management and liability | Pooled resources and shared risks/costs; shared control | Minimal financial risk |

**Explain more**

- Read the table row by row, because exams pick one cell and ask you to name the form:
    - **Formality** ladder, from most to least formal: JV (highly formal, contractual) and partnership (formal, typically long-term), then alliance (formal but looser than a JV), then networking (informal).
    - **Legal entity:** only the **JV creates a new, separate, jointly owned entity**. The partnership is a **single entity** made up of two or more persons or companies. The alliance creates **no new entity** and the partners **remain independent**. Networking creates **no new entity**.
    - **Objective:** partnership = **ongoing general business for profit**; JV = **single, specific project or objective**; alliance = **specific, mutually beneficial goals** such as R&D or marketing; networking = **information exchange, relationship building, future opportunities**.
    - **Risk/control:** networking carries **minimal financial risk**; partnership = **shared management and liability**; alliance = **greater flexibility, less control/commitment than a JV**; JV = **pooled resources, shared risks/costs, shared control**.
- Common traps: "formal, typically long-term" is the **partnership**, not the JV; "single, specific project" is the **JV**, not the partnership; "no new entity, partners remain independent" is the **alliance**, while networking's cell simply says "no new entity".
- Do not mix in unrelated features. The forms do not differ "primarily" by size, location, number of employees, prices or industry; the answer is **formality, legal structure and objectives**.

### Lesson 5.6: A simple 4-step networking plan

**Must know**

The 4 steps, in order:

1. **Goal:** *What do you need?* It could be a **technical co-founder for an app idea** or **feedback on a product from 10 potential customers**.
2. **Identify Venues:**
    - **Online:** optimise your **LinkedIn**. Join groups like **"Nigeria Tech Community"**.
    - **Offline:** attend events like **Lagos Startup Week**, **Techpoint Build**, or meetups at hubs like **Hub One by FCMB in Yaba**.
3. **Engage:** **don't just collect cards**. **Offer value**, **ask insightful questions**, and **follow up within 48 hours** with a **personalised message**.
4. **Maintain:** **nurture relationships**. **Share useful information**, **make introductions**, and **check in periodically**.

The plan is often drawn as four diamond-shaped tiles: **Goal** at the top (a target/bullseye with an arrow), **Identify Venues** on the right (a location pin), **Engage** at the bottom (a handshake with speech bubbles), **Maintain** on the left (a laptop).

**Explain more**

- Memory aid: **G-I-E-M**, "Get In, Engage, Maintain". First decide **what** you need, then **where** to find the people, then **how** to connect, then how to **keep** the connection.
- Numbers to know: **10** potential customers (Goal example) and **48 hours** (Engage follow-up). Do not confuse 48 hours with the **28 days** of business registration.
- Online versus offline: **LinkedIn** and the **"Nigeria Tech Community"** group are **online**. **Lagos Startup Week**, **Techpoint Build** and **Hub One by FCMB** meetups are **offline**. Hub One by FCMB is in **Yaba**, not Lekki, Victoria Island or Ikeja.
- Tell Engage from Maintain by timing. **Engage** is the first contact and the quick follow-up: at Lagos Startup Week, Dayo asks a speaker thoughtful questions, offers to help with her project and sends a personalised message the next day. **Maintain** is the long run: months after meeting a mentor, Seyi still shares useful articles, introduces her to other founders and checks in now and then.
- "Collecting as many business cards as possible" is exactly what the plan warns against. "Choose and reserve a name" belongs to CAC registration, not to networking.

### One-minute recap

- Registration is a legal requirement within **28 days** of starting; it gives legal identity, protects your name, builds trust with customers and banks, is essential for government contracts and is needed for a corporate bank account.
- **CAC** manages registration under **CAMA**; as of 2026 it is mainly online through the **iCRP Portal**.
- 4 steps: choose and reserve a name (2 alternatives), complete **CAC-BN01** (business activity, NIN, address, photo), pay filing fees online, submit and await approval (electronic Certificate of Registration and Certified Extract).
- Post-registration: get a **TIN** from **FIRS**.
- Pitfalls: skipped name search = rejection; inaccurate information = delays; no tax registration = penalties; missed deadline = default fee after the 28-day grace period. Companies (Ltd): consider a CAC-accredited agent.
- Partnership = formal, ongoing, shared management, profits and liabilities, for profit.
- Strategic alliance = independent firms, mutual goals, autonomy kept, no new company; Wintel (Microsoft and Intel).
- Joint venture = a type of strategic alliance that creates a new, jointly owned, separate legal entity for a shared, often time-bound objective.
- Networking = informal relationship building; no formal agreement, shared capital or common goal at the outset; minimal financial risk.
- Networking plan: Goal, Identify Venues, Engage (follow up within 48 hours), Maintain.
