## Topic 7: Contemporary Issues in Entrepreneurship

**What you must know:** the three learning outcomes; knowledge (definition, tacit and explicit, the 4 types of entrepreneurial knowledge); entrepreneurial skills (2 definitions, 9 types); the 10 roles of knowledge and skills; digital entrepreneurship and its 5 kinds of digital business; digital technologies and the 6 groups of digital tools; the 6 impacts of digital technology; the virtual office (features, tools, 5 importances); online networking (3 definitions, 5 importances); e-commerce (3 definitions, 6 types, 8 strategies, 10 opportunities).

### Lesson 7.1: Learning outcomes, knowledge and skills

**Must know**

This topic (Weeks 12 and 13, Contemporary Issues in Entrepreneurship) has **3 learning outcomes**. By the end you should be able to:

1. **Understand** the role of knowledge and skills in modern entrepreneurship.
2. **Assess** the impact of digital technology and **apply** relevant digital tools for entrepreneurship.
3. **Demonstrate** basic understanding of e-commerce in business operation, and **apply** basic e-commerce strategies to entrepreneurship.

- **Knowledge (in entrepreneurship):** the **information, understanding and expertise** that entrepreneurs acquire through **education, training and experience**, which enable them to **identify opportunities, make informed decisions and manage business activities effectively** (Drucker, 1985; Shane, 2003).

**Major types of knowledge (2):**

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tacit knowledge** | Knowledge embedded in **experience, skills and intuition** |
| **Explicit knowledge** | Knowledge that can be **codified, documented and easily shared** |

**Types of entrepreneurial knowledge (4), in order:**

1. **Market knowledge:** understanding customer needs and competitors.
2. **Financial knowledge:** budgeting, costing, pricing.
3. **Technical knowledge:** product or service development.
4. **Legal knowledge:** business laws and regulations.

**Entrepreneurial skills: 2 definitions**

- The **practical abilities and competencies** that enable entrepreneurs to **apply knowledge effectively** in planning, organising, managing resources and solving problems in order to **create and sustain business ventures**.
- The **capability to perform entrepreneurial tasks effectively**, such as **opportunity recognition, relationship building and strategic decision-making**.

**Types of skills (9), in order:**

1. **Managerial skills:** involve planning, organising, controlling.
2. Communication and negotiation.
3. Problem-solving and decision-making skills.
4. Digital and technological skills.
5. Technical skills.
6. Financial and budgeting skills.
7. Marketing skills.
8. Strategic skills.
9. Personal and creative skills.

**Explain more**

- **Knowledge versus skills.** Knowledge is information and understanding (what you know); skills are the practical abilities to apply that knowledge (what you can do). Do not swap where knowledge comes from (**education, training and experience**) for look-alike triples such as "capital, labour and time" or "collection, storage and processing".
- **Tacit or explicit?** A caterer who can tell her jollof rice is ready by its smell after years in the kitchen has **tacit** knowledge; a bakery's recipe manual that every new staff member reads is **explicit**.
- **Matching knowledge types:** studying the regulations that govern your trade = **legal**; learning to design a better product = **technical**. "Managerial knowledge" is **not** one of the four (managerial is a **skill**), and **legal** is a knowledge type but **not** a skill type.
- Match skill types to behaviour: persuading a supplier to cut the price and give you longer to pay = **communication and negotiation**; preparing a budget and tracking every naira against it = **financial and budgeting skills**.
- Memory picture: **EXPERTISE** is built from learning, experience, practice, ego, purpose, talent, ideas, skill and intelligence.

### Lesson 7.2: Role of knowledge and skills in entrepreneurship

**Must know**

There are **10 roles**, shown in two sets of five. In each one, **knowledge** does one thing and **skills** do another.

| No. | Role | What knowledge does | What skills do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opportunity recognition** | Helps entrepreneurs understand market trends and customers' needs | Enable them to analyse information and recognise viable business opportunities |
| 2 | **Innovation and creativity** | Supports research, idea generation and technological adoption | Allow entrepreneurs to transform ideas into innovative products and services |
| 3 | **Efficient resource management** | Financial and operational knowledge guide optimal resource allocation | Help entrepreneurs manage capital, labour and time effectively |
| 4 | **Effective business planning** | Financial knowledge improves budgeting, costing and pricing decisions | Ensure profitability, cash flow management and long-term survival |
| 5 | **Financial performance and sustainability** | Aids in preparing business plans, market analysis and feasibility studies | Managerial skills ensure proper planning, organising and control of business activities |
| 6 | **Adoption of digital technology** | Enables understanding of digital tools and platforms | Digital skills support e-commerce, online marketing and virtual operations |
| 7 | **Risk-management and decision making** | Improves assessment of business risks and uncertainties | Enhance problem-solving and informed decision-making |
| 8 | **Business growth and competitiveness** | Helps entrepreneurs understand competitive strategies and market positioning | Enable scaling, customer retention and market expansion |
| 9 | **Ethical and sustainable entrepreneurship** | Knowledge of laws and ethics promotes responsible business conduct | Support implementation of sustainable and socially responsible practices |
| 10 | **Continuous learning and adaptability** | Knowledge acquisition supports lifelong planning | Enable adaptability to changing business environments and technologies |

**Explain more**

- Learn each role by its key phrase and picture:
    - **Efficient resource management** = *capital, labour and time*. A laundry owner in Yaba sharing limited capital, staff hours and machine time so nothing is wasted.
    - **Ethical and sustainable entrepreneurship** = *laws and ethics*, **responsible business conduct**. A recycling start-up that refuses to dump waste illegally because its founders know the environmental laws.
    - **Continuous learning and adaptability** = adapting to *changing environments and technologies*. A printer who keeps taking short courses and changing how he works as customers move to new technology.
    - Note the placing: the budgeting, costing and pricing text sits under **effective business planning**; business plans, market analysis and feasibility studies sit under **financial performance and sustainability**, with **managerial skills**. Learn them exactly as placed.
- **Exam trap:** the "NOT a role" question will slip in an item from another list in this topic. These are **not** roles of knowledge and skills: improved market access and cost reduction (impacts of digital technology), cost efficiency (virtual office importance), resource mobilisation and market visibility and brand building (online networking importances), build trust and credibility (an e-commerce strategy).

### Lesson 7.3: Digital entrepreneurship, digital technologies and digital tools

**Must know**

**Digital entrepreneurship: 3 statements**

- The **creation, development and growth of business ventures** through the use of **digital technologies**.
- The **creation of new ventures** and the **transformation of existing businesses** through the development of **novel digital technologies and innovative business models**.
- Digital entrepreneurial activities rely on **digital artifacts, digital platforms and digital infrastructures** for **value creation and delivery**.

**Examples of digital businesses (5 kinds):**

| No. | Kind | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **E-commerce businesses** (online retail stores) | Jumia, Konga, Amazon, Slot |
| 2 | **Digital service businesses** | Online education platforms: Coursera, LinkedIn, Udemy. Software as a service: Zoom, Dropbox, Google, Slack |
| 3 | **Digital media services** (subscription-based) | Streaming services: Netflix, Disney. Podcasting platforms: Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube channels |
| 4 | **Digital health businesses** | Telemedicine platforms, wellness apps, online pharmacy services |
| 5 | **Digital finance businesses** | Online banking and payments, crypto currency exchanges; PayPal, Flutterwave, Paystack, Opay |

**Digital technologies: 2 definitions**

- **Tools and systems based on digital computing** that enable the **collection, storage, processing and exchange of information**.
- **Platforms and infrastructures** that enable **new forms of entrepreneurial activity, innovation and value creation**.

**Examples of digital technologies (7):**

1. Internet and cloud computing
2. Computers, smartphones and tablets
3. Software applications and mobile apps
4. E-commerce platforms
5. Social media and digital marketing tools
6. Artificial Intelligence (AI) and data analytics
7. Financial technologies (FinTech)

**Relevant digital technology tools for entrepreneurial growth (6 groups):**

| Group | Tools | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **E-commerce platforms** | Shopify, Jumia, Temu | Enable online selling and digital storefronts |
| **Digital marketing tools** | Social media platforms (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn); Google Ads and Search Engine Optimisation (SEO) tools; email marketing platform (Mailchimp) | Social media, search advertising and SEO, email marketing |
| **Financial and accounting tools** | Mobile payment systems (Paystack, Flutterwave, PayPal); accounting software (QuickBook, Wave) | Mobile payments and accounting software |
| **Communication and collaboration tools** | Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet; Slack, WhatsApp Business | Slack and WhatsApp Business support virtual offices and teamwork |
| **Data analytics and customer management tools** | Google Analytics; Customer Relationship Management (CRM) systems (HubSpot) | Support data-driven decision-making |
| **Productivity and project management tools** | Trello, Asana, Notion | Help manage tasks, time and resources effectively |

**Impact of digital technology on entrepreneurial growth (6), in order:**

1. **Improved market access:** reach local, national and global markets; reduce geographical barriers through online platforms; enhance customer reach and market expansion.
2. **Cost reduction and efficiency:** lowers operational and transaction costs; reduces need for physical offices and large workforce; improves productivity through automation.
3. **Innovation and new business models:** supports development of digital products and services; enables platform-based and sharing economy models; encourages creativity and continuous innovation.
4. **Enhance customer engagement:** enables real-time communication with customers; improves customer feedback and relationship management; supports personalised marketing strategies.
5. **Financial inclusion and access to capital:** digital finance tools enable online payments and transactions; improve access to loans, crowdfunding and investors; support cash flow management.
6. **Scalability and business growth:** enables rapid scaling without a proportionate increase in costs; supports remote work and global collaboration; enhances competitiveness and sustainability.

**Explain more**

- **Digital technology** is the tool; **digital entrepreneurship** is the business built with it, covering **new** ventures and the **transformation of existing** ones.
- **Traps:** Netflix is **not** e-commerce; Spotify is a **podcasting platform**; Zoom here is **software as a service**; digital media services are **subscription-based**; a video-consultation app for patients is **digital health**. Crowdfunding, affiliate marketing and tacit knowledge are **not** examples of digital technologies.
- **Same name, different lists:** Jumia is an e-commerce business and an e-commerce platform; Paystack, Flutterwave and PayPal are digital finance businesses and also **mobile payment systems**; LinkedIn is an online education platform and a social media platform. Answer by the group the question names. An events planner who wants to assign tasks and track deadlines would pick **Trello**.
- **Impacts in practice:** an Aba shoemaker selling to buyers in Ghana and the UK online without opening shops abroad = **improved market access**; an agritech founder raising start-up money from hundreds of small backers by crowdfunding = **financial inclusion and access to capital**.
- There are **6** impacts. "Professional business presence" is a virtual office importance, not an impact of digital technology. Both this list and the virtual office list contain "**Scalability and business growth**", but with different meanings: here it is scaling without a proportionate rise in costs; for the virtual office it is expanding or downsizing without relocation.

### Lesson 7.4: Virtual office and online networking

**Must know**

- **Virtual office:** a business solution that allows organisations and entrepreneurs to **operate remotely** by providing digital services such as a **business address, communication tools and administrative support** without the need for a **physical office space**.

**Key features of a virtual office (5), in order:**

1. Registered business address
2. Mail handling and forwarding
3. Virtual receptionist / call answering services
4. Online meeting tools (Zoom, Microsoft Teams)
5. Cloud-based collaboration tools (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365)

**Virtual office tools (4):**

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| Google Workspace | Email and document collaboration |
| Zoom / Microsoft Teams | Virtual meetings |
| Slack | Team communication |
| Regus / WeWork | Virtual office address and admin services |

**Importance of virtual office to entrepreneurship (5), in order:**

1. **Access to global talents:** entrepreneurs can recruit skilled professionals remotely, increasing access to diverse and specialised people.
2. **Cost efficiency:** reduces start-up and operational costs by eliminating expenses related to rent, utilities and physical infrastructure.
3. **Flexible and work life balance:** entrepreneurs can operate from any location, enabling flexible working arrangements and improved productivity.
4. **Professional business presence:** provides a credible business address, mail handling and professional communication services.
5. **Scalability and business growth:** allows businesses to expand or downsize operations easily without relocation or heavy capital investment.

**Online networking: 3 definitions**

- The use of **internet-based applications** that enable the **creation and exchange of user-generated content** and facilitate **interaction among individuals and organisations**.
- **Social interaction and relationship building** through **digitally mediated networks** that **transcend time and space constraints**.
- The use of **digital platforms and internet tools** to **create and sustain relationships** for **professional, social and entrepreneurial purposes**.

**Importance of online networking to entrepreneurship (5), in order:**

1. **Access to business opportunities:** identify customers, partners, investors and suppliers beyond local boundaries.
2. **Resource mobilisation:** access to funding opportunities, collaborations and strategic alliances.
3. **Knowledge sharing and learning:** industry insights, best practices and mentorship through online professional communities.
4. **Innovation and collaboration:** collaboration, co-creation and idea exchange among entrepreneurs and stakeholders.
5. **Market visibility and brand building:** social media and professional platforms help entrepreneurs promote their ventures and build brand awareness.

**Explain more**

- **Features versus tools.** The 5 features are what a virtual office provides (address, mail, receptionist, online meetings, cloud collaboration). The 4 tools are the products used. Zoom and Microsoft Teams are the **online meeting tools** feature; Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 are the **cloud-based collaboration** feature. In the tools list, Google Workspace = **email and document collaboration**, Slack = **team communication**, Regus and WeWork = **virtual office address and admin services**. Order tracking and automated notification is an e-commerce strategy, **not** a virtual office feature.
- A Lagos start-up hiring an analyst in Kano and a designer in Nairobi, all working remotely, shows **access to global talents**.
- **Online networking** is relationship building on the internet. The three definitions give you three exam hooks: **user-generated content**; **transcend time and space constraints**; **professional, social and entrepreneurial purposes**.
- A fashion entrepreneur who picks up insights and a mentor in an online community shows **knowledge sharing and learning**; a skincare brand promoting itself on Instagram and LinkedIn shows **market visibility and brand building**.
- **Do not mix the two lists of five.** Virtual office: global talents, cost efficiency, flexibility, professional presence, scalability. Online networking: business opportunities, resource mobilisation, knowledge sharing, innovation and collaboration, market visibility. So "resource mobilisation" is **not** a virtual office importance, and "cost efficiency" is **not** an online networking importance. "Professional business presence" (virtual office) and "market visibility and brand building" (online networking) sound alike; the first is about a credible address, the second about promotion and brand awareness.

### Lesson 7.5: Electronic commerce: types, strategies and opportunities

**Must know**

**E-commerce: 3 definitions**

- **Electronic commerce (e-commerce):** the **buying and selling of goods and services**, as well as the **exchange of money and data**, through **electronic networks, primarily the internet**.
- **Commercial transactions** involving the **exchange of value** conducted over **digital networks**.
- The **sale or purchase** of goods or services conducted over a **computer network** by methods **specifically designed for receiving or placing orders**.

Points listed with e-commerce in business operation: 24/7 business availability; reduced operational costs; faster transaction processing; improved record-keeping and data management; enhanced customer convenience and satisfaction.

**Types of e-commerce (6):**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Business-to-Consumer (B2C)** | Businesses sell products or services directly to individual consumers online | Jumia, Amazon, Konga |
| **Business-to-Business (B2B)** | Businesses sell products or services to other businesses through digital platforms | Alibaba, software subscription |
| **Consumer-to-Consumer (C2C)** | Individuals sell goods or services directly to other individuals via online marketplaces | eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Jiji.ng |
| **Consumer-to-Business (C2B)** | Individuals offer products or services to businesses | Freelancer offering services on Upwork; influencers promoting brands |
| **Business-to-Government (B2G)** | Businesses provide goods or services to government agencies through platforms | Online procurement portals, e-tendering systems |
| **Government-to-Consumer (G2C)** | Government services delivered electronically to citizens | Online tax payment platform, e-visa and passport application system |

**Applying basic e-commerce strategies to entrepreneurship (8), in order:**

1. **Develop an online presence:** create a website, mobile app or social media store to showcase products and services.
    - Use platforms like Shopify, Temu, Facebook or Instagram.
    - Ensure clear product descriptions, pricing and images.
2. **Choose the right e-commerce model:** adopt an appropriate model based on your offerings.
    - B2C for retail products and B2B for wholesale or services.
    - C2C for marketplaces and C2B for freelancing and digital services.
3. **Implement secure online payment system:** provide multiple and secure payment options.
    - Integrate Paystack, Flutterwave, PayPal or bank transfers.
    - Offer mobile money and card payment options.
4. **Focus on digital marketing strategies:** e-commerce success depends on **visibility and customer engagement**.
    - Use social media marketing, SEO and email marketing.
    - Run targeted online advertisements.
5. **Optimise customer experience:** user-friendly design improves **customer satisfaction and retention**.
    - Ensure easy navigation and fast-loading websites.
    - Provide live chat and prompt customer support.
6. **Manage logistics and order fulfilment:** efficient delivery systems **build customer trust**.
    - Partner with logistics providers for timely delivery.
    - Use order tracking and automated notification.
7. **Use data analytics for decision-making:** e-commerce platforms provide valuable business insights.
    - Track customer behaviour, sales trends and conversion rates.
    - Adjust pricing, promotions and inventory accordingly.
8. **Build trust and credibility:** trust is essential in online transactions.
    - Display customer reviews and ratings.
    - Use secure checkout systems and transparent return policies.

**Entrepreneurial opportunities in e-commerce (10):**

| First five | Second five |
|---|---|
| Online retail marketplaces and niche stores | AI-enabled e-commerce solutions |
| Dropshipping business | Sustainable and green e-commerce |
| Subscription-based e-commerce | Local-to-global export platforms |
| Social commerce | Digital marketplace for services |
| Affiliate marketing | Mobile commerce (M-commerce) |

**Explain more**

- The five points with e-commerce are the gains it brings to a business: the shop never closes (24/7), it costs less to run, payments go through faster, records are kept automatically, and customers enjoy more convenience and satisfaction.
- **Reading the type codes.** The first letter is the **seller or provider**, the last letter is the **buyer or receiver**. So B2C is business selling to you; C2B is you (a consumer) offering something to a business. Examples:
    - A 200-level student selling his used phone to another student on Jiji.ng = **C2C**.
    - A freelance graphic designer in Ilorin selling logo designs to companies on Upwork, or an influencer promoting a brand = **C2B**.
    - A construction company submitting a bid through a government e-tendering system = **B2G**.
    - A citizen paying her personal income tax online, or applying for an e-visa or passport = **G2C**.
- **Traps:** there are **6** types. Influencers are **C2B**, not B2C. Facebook Marketplace is **C2C**. B2G runs **from** business **to** government (procurement, e-tendering); G2C runs **from** government **to** citizens (tax, e-visa, passport). Dropshipping is an **opportunity**, not a type.
- **Strategies:** there are **8**, in two sets of four. Model choice: **B2B for wholesale or services**, **C2B for freelancing and digital services**. A bag seller who starts using SEO, email marketing and targeted Instagram adverts is applying **focus on digital marketing strategies**.
- Watch the look-alikes: "build customer trust" is the result of **logistics**, while "build trust and credibility" is the separate **eighth strategy**. "Access to global talents" is a virtual office importance, not a strategy.
- **Opportunities:** there are **10**. Picture a dartboard with ten arrows aimed at the bullseye, five from each side. **M-commerce** means **mobile commerce**. Social commerce, affiliate marketing, dropshipping and subscription-based e-commerce are all opportunities; mail handling, online meeting tools and registered addresses belong to the virtual office, not here.

### One-minute recap

- Knowledge = information, understanding and expertise from education, training and experience (Drucker, 1985; Shane, 2003). Tacit = experience, skills, intuition; explicit = codified, documented, shared.
- 4 entrepreneurial knowledge types: market, financial, technical, legal. Skills = practical abilities to apply knowledge; 9 types, starting with managerial (planning, organising, controlling).
- 10 roles of knowledge and skills, from opportunity recognition to continuous learning and adaptability.
- Digital entrepreneurship relies on digital artifacts, platforms and infrastructures; 5 kinds of digital business: e-commerce, digital service, digital media, digital health, digital finance.
- Digital technologies collect, store, process and exchange information; tools fall into 6 groups (e-commerce, marketing, financial and accounting, communication, data analytics and CRM, productivity).
- 6 impacts: market access, cost reduction, new business models, customer engagement, financial inclusion, scalability.
- Virtual office: 5 features, 4 tools, 5 importances (global talents, cost efficiency, flexibility, professional presence, scalability).
- Online networking: 3 definitions, 5 importances (opportunities, resource mobilisation, knowledge sharing, innovation and collaboration, market visibility).
- E-commerce: 6 types (B2C, B2B, C2C, C2B, B2G, G2C), 8 strategies, 10 opportunities.
