## Topic 2: Entrepreneurship Traits

**What you must know:** entrepreneurship as a mindset and a process; the four entrepreneurial traits (opportunity seeker, risk-taker, problem solver, change agent) with what each involves and its Nigerian example; whether entrepreneurs are born or made, and the Igba Boi system; the five self-assessment questions; innovation versus creativity and the three innovator cases.

### Lesson 2.1: Entrepreneurship as a mindset, and the four traits

**Must know**

- **Entrepreneurship** is not just about starting a business; it is a **mindset** and a **process** of **creating value** by **identifying opportunities, taking risks, and innovating**.
- **The 4 entrepreneurial traits** (in this order):

1. **Opportunity seeker**
2. **Risk-taker** (full name: **the calculated risk-taker**)
3. **Problem solver**
4. **Change agent**

- The pictures that go with each trait: a man running with a briefcase = **opportunity seeker**; a man pulling a gauge needle towards a red "RISK" zone = **risk-taker**; a man fitting jigsaw-puzzle pieces beside a lightbulb = **problem solver**; a head with arrows cycling from a sad face to a happy face = **change agent**.

**Explain more**

- The key idea is that entrepreneurship is bigger than "opening a shop". Someone who registers a business but never spots a need, never takes a sensible risk and never brings anything new is not really showing the entrepreneurial mindset. The three verbs in the definition (identifying opportunities, taking risks, innovating) are the heart of it, and they line up with the traits you study below.
- Read the pictures as memory hooks: the runner is chasing an opening before others get there; the gauge shows someone deliberately moving into risk; the puzzle and lightbulb show fitting a solution to a problem; the sad-to-happy face shows a situation being changed for the better.
- **Traps:** the list has exactly **four** traits. Look-alikes such as "fund manager", "profit maximiser", "market follower", "rent seeker", "cost controller", "policy maker" or "risk avoider" are **not** on it. Watch for a list that swaps "risk-taker" for "risk avoider" or "problem solver" for "profit maximiser": one wrong word makes the option wrong.

### Lesson 2.2: The opportunity seeker and the calculated risk-taker

**Must know**

| Trait | Definition | It involves | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Opportunity seeker** | The ability to **identify and capitalise on market gaps or unmet needs** | **Market awareness, proactiveness, visionary thinking** | **Sim Shagaya** (**Konga**) |
| **Calculated risk-taker** | **Willingness to take calculated risks after assessing potential downsides** | **Risk assessment, tolerance for failure, a high-risk, high-reward mindset** | **Aliko Dangote** (cement, Oil & Gas) |

- **Sim Shagaya** saw (1) the **low penetration of formal retail** and (2) the **growing internet use** in Nigeria. He identified the opportunity for a **trusted online marketplace**, leading him to found **Konga**, **one of Nigeria's earliest e-commerce giants**.
- Takeaway: opportunity-seeking **isn't about waiting for a "eureka" moment**. It is about **actively looking for frustrations and inefficiencies around you**. Sim Shagaya **didn't invent e-commerce**; he **applied it to the specific challenges and opportunities of the Nigerian market**.
- **Aliko Dangote** invested heavily in **cement production and Oil & Gas** in Nigeria **when others were sceptical about local manufacturing capacity**. This calculated risk built a **multi-billion-dollar industrial empire**.
- Takeaway: Dangote's risk was **calculated**. He understood **Nigeria's infrastructure deficit** and bet on the **long-term need for local production**. **Entrepreneurial risk is about managing uncertainty, not eliminating it.**

**Explain more**

- **Opportunity seeker in plain words:** a gap is something people need but cannot get, or cannot get well. *Market awareness* means knowing what is happening around you; *proactiveness* means acting before others do; *visionary thinking* means seeing where things are heading. Shagaya noticed that few Nigerians shopped in formal retail outlets while more and more were going online, and joined those two facts into one idea: a marketplace online that people could trust.
- Note the point about invention. Shagaya did not create e-commerce; he adapted an existing idea to Nigeria. An exam may offer "invented e-commerce" or "imported a foreign company without changes" as distractors; the right answer is that he **applied it to the specific challenges and opportunities of the Nigerian market**. Similarly, if a student notices rising smartphone use in her town and no one selling groceries online, and acts first, she is an **opportunity seeker**.
- **Calculated risk-taker in plain words:** "calculated" means the risk is taken **after assessing potential downsides**, not blindly and not with a guarantee. *Risk assessment* is studying what could go wrong; *tolerance for failure* is being able to accept that things may not work; a *high-risk, high-reward* mindset accepts bigger risk for a bigger possible gain. Dangote was not gambling: he read the infrastructure deficit and expected Nigeria to need local production for a long time.
- The final line is a favourite exam point: entrepreneurial risk is about **managing** uncertainty, **not eliminating** it. Options such as "eliminating uncertainty before acting", "gambling without assessing downsides" or "a risk with no chance of failure" are all wrong. A trader who studies the possible losses on a rice mill, judges that long-term demand justifies it, then invests heavily, is a **calculated risk-taker**.
- **Traps:** do not swap the "involves" lists. Market awareness, proactiveness and visionary thinking belong to the **opportunity seeker**; risk assessment, tolerance for failure and high-risk, high-reward belong to the **risk-taker**. Do not swap the cases either: Shagaya and the e-commerce/retail/internet facts go with **opportunity seeker**; Dangote, cement, Oil & Gas, scepticism about **local manufacturing capacity** and the **infrastructure deficit** go with **risk-taker**. Konga is "one of Nigeria's earliest e-commerce giants"; Dangote built a "multi-billion-dollar industrial empire".

### Lesson 2.3: The problem solver and the change agent

**Must know**

- **Problem solver and change agent:** a **solution-oriented mindset** focused on **fixing inefficiencies and driving change**, often **creating social impact**. **Entrepreneurs thrive on solving real problems.**
- **Dr. Akintonwa Oluwayemisi (DentistAtYourDoor):** solved the problem of **dental care access in traffic-heavy Lagos** by launching **the first mobile dental clinic**.
- **Chinedu Azodoh (MAX.ng):** solved the problem of **unsafe, informal motorcycle taxis ("okada")** by creating a **tech-enabled, safer, energy-saving and more reliable ride-hailing and delivery service**.
- Takeaway: **the most sustainable businesses solve a persistent problem.** Dr. Oluwayemisi **didn't just complain about the traffic in Lagos; she built a mobile clinic to bring the service to the people. That's being a change agent.**

**Explain more**

- The two traits are taught together because they work as a pair: the **problem solver** finds a fix, and the **change agent** uses that fix to change the situation for people. Social impact is the typical result: life in the community improves, not just the founder's income.
- **DentistAtYourDoor:** in Lagos, getting to a dentist can mean hours in traffic. Instead of asking patients to travel, the clinic travels to them. Remember "**first mobile dental clinic**", not "largest dental hospital".
- **MAX.ng:** okada rides were informal and unsafe. MAX.ng used technology to make motorcycle transport **safer, energy-saving and more reliable**, and offered both **ride-hailing and delivery**. Learn the four describing words: tech-enabled, safer, energy-saving, more reliable.
- The change-agent sentence is often tested word for word: complaining about a problem is not enough; building something that brings the solution to people is what makes you a change agent. So if a student builds a cheap booking-and-delivery system that ends long queues at a borehole and improves life in the area, that is **problem solver and change agent**.
- **Traps:** do not mix up the problems. Traffic and dental care = **DentistAtYourDoor** (Dr. Akintonwa Oluwayemisi). Okada = **MAX.ng** (Chinedu Azodoh). Piracy and Nollywood belong to iROKOtv (Lesson 2.6), payments to Flutterwave, and retail/internet to Konga. "Entrepreneurs thrive on **solving real problems**", not on "eliminating uncertainty" or "eureka moments".

### Lesson 2.4: Are entrepreneurs born or made?

**Must know**

| Argument | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| **Natural Traits (Born)** | Some people have **high energy, natural charisma, and high risk tolerance from birth**. For some, **inherent qualities like resilience, creativity, or extraversion appear early**. |
| **Nurtured Traits (Made)** | Skills developed through **education, mentorship, and experience**. **Accounting, management, marketing, and emotional intelligence are learned skills.** |

**The Igbo Apprenticeship System (Igba Boi)**

- **The world's largest business incubator is right here in Nigeria.**
- Its 3 steps, in order:

1. Young people are **picked** (often with **no capital**).
2. They are **nurtured** through **years of mentorship**.
3. They are **"settled" (funded)** to start their own.

- If entrepreneurship were only natural, the Igba Boi system **wouldn't have a near 100% success rate**. It **proves entrepreneurs can be made**; it is **the perfect example of Nurture**.
- "**These boys aren't born knowing the price of spare parts.**" They are taught **trade, negotiation, and supply chain management**.

**Explain more**

- The "born" side says some people simply arrive with the energy, charm and comfort with risk that business needs, and that qualities like resilience, creativity or being outgoing (extraversion) can show from childhood. The "made" side says the important business skills can be learned at school, from a mentor or on the job.
- **Igba Boi** is the strongest evidence for "made". A young person, usually with no money, joins an established trader, learns the business over several years, and at the end the master "settles" him, meaning he is given funds to open his own business. Picture a 14-year-old who serves a spare-parts dealer in Onitsha for seven years and is then funded to open his own shop: that is the Igba Boi system.
- The near 100% success rate is the argument: if success depended only on inborn traits, a system that takes ordinary young people and trains them would not work so reliably. The spare-parts line makes the same point: nobody is born knowing prices; it is taught.
- **Traps:**
    - Keep the step order: **picked, nurtured, settled**. Any order with "settled" first or in the middle is wrong.
    - "Settled" = **funded to start his own business**, not "employed permanently" or "enrolled in school".
    - The success rate is **near 100%**, not 25% or 50%.
    - Igba Boi proves **nurture** (made), not nature (born).
    - Born list: high energy, natural charisma, high risk tolerance; early-appearing qualities: resilience, creativity, extraversion. Made list: accounting, management, marketing, emotional intelligence. **Emotional intelligence is a learned (made) skill**; **natural charisma and extraversion are born** qualities.
    - What apprentices are taught: **trade, negotiation, supply chain management**. Do not confuse with the opportunity-seeker list (market awareness, proactiveness, visionary thinking).

### Lesson 2.5: Assess your entrepreneurial profile

**Must know**

The self-assessment has **5 labelled dimensions**, each with its question:

| Dimension | Question it asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Opportunity Seeking** | How often do you spot problems or gaps around you? |
| **Risk Tolerance** | How comfortable are you with calculated, uncertain outcomes? |
| **Problem-Solving** | How quickly do you move from identifying a problem to brainstorming solutions? |
| **Creativity** | How easily do you generate new ideas or unconventional approaches? |
| **Resilience** | How do you bounce back from setbacks? |

**Explain more**

- This is a mirror: each question asks how strongly you show one trait. The first three match the traits you have already met (opportunity seeker, risk-taker, problem solver); creativity links to Lesson 2.6; resilience is about recovering after failure.
- Match by key words: "spot problems or gaps" = **opportunity seeking**; "calculated, uncertain outcomes" = **risk tolerance**; "from identifying a problem to brainstorming solutions" = **problem-solving**; "new ideas or unconventional approaches" = **creativity**; "bounce back from setbacks" = **resilience**.
- **Traps:** there are **five** labelled dimensions. "Emotional intelligence" is **not** one of them (it is a learned skill in Lesson 2.4). Do not confuse "spot problems" (opportunity seeking) with "move from a problem to solutions" (problem-solving).

### Lesson 2.6: Innovators and creative thinkers

**Must know**

- **Innovation** introduces **new products, services, or business models**.
- **Creativity** is **thinking outside conventional frameworks to develop unique solutions**.
- **Creativity involves having a better idea; innovation involves making it a reality.**

| Person | Venture | What they did |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bolanle Austen-Peters** | **Terra Kulture** | Reimagined the **cultural space in Lagos**, creating a vibrant hub for **arts, dining, and theatre** |
| **Jason Njoku** | **iROKOtv** | Revolutionised **Nollywood content distribution** by **moving it online**, solving **piracy and access** issues |
| **Olugbenga Agboola & Iyinoluwa Aboyeji** | **Flutterwave** | Solved the **payment challenge in Africa** by providing a **payment infrastructure for global merchants and payment service providers** across the continent |

**Explain more**

- Creativity happens in the head; innovation happens in the market. If one person sketches a clever solar phone charger and another later manufactures and sells it, the sketch is **creativity** and the launch is **innovation**. The classic trap reverses the two ("innovation is the idea, creativity makes it real"): that is wrong.
- **Terra Kulture** took the idea of a cultural centre and made it a lively place where art, food and theatre meet in one spot in Lagos.
- **iROKOtv** changed how Nollywood films reach viewers: by putting them online it cut piracy and made films easier to access.
- **Flutterwave** built the payment "pipes" that let global merchants and payment service providers accept payments across Africa.
- **Traps:** all three (Terra Kulture, iROKOtv, Flutterwave) illustrate **innovators and creative thinkers**, not risk-takers or Igba Boi. Flutterwave has **two** founders, Olugbenga Agboola and Iyinoluwa Aboyeji, paired together. Do not pair Jason Njoku with Flutterwave or MAX.ng; his venture is **iROKOtv**. Of all the people in this topic, only Austen-Peters, Njoku, Agboola and Aboyeji are innovator examples; Shagaya, Dangote, Oluwayemisi and Azodoh illustrate the other traits.

### One-minute recap

- Entrepreneurship = a mindset and a process of creating value by identifying opportunities, taking risks and innovating.
- Four traits: opportunity seeker, risk-taker, problem solver, change agent.
- Opportunity seeker = spots market gaps or unmet needs (market awareness, proactiveness, visionary thinking): Sim Shagaya, Konga.
- Calculated risk-taker = takes risks after assessing downsides (risk assessment, tolerance for failure, high-risk high-reward): Dangote, cement and Oil & Gas; risk is managing uncertainty, not eliminating it.
- Problem solver and change agent = fixes inefficiencies, drives change, often social impact: DentistAtYourDoor (first mobile dental clinic), MAX.ng (safer okada); the most sustainable businesses solve a persistent problem.
- Born: high energy, charisma, risk tolerance; made: education, mentorship, experience (accounting, management, marketing, emotional intelligence).
- Igba Boi: picked (no capital), nurtured (years of mentorship), settled (funded); near 100% success; proves entrepreneurs can be made.
- Self-assessment: opportunity seeking, risk tolerance, problem-solving, creativity, resilience.
- Creativity = the better idea; innovation = making it real: Terra Kulture, iROKOtv, Flutterwave.
