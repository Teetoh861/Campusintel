## Topic 3: Innovation and Change

**What you must know:** what innovation is (McKinsey, 2022) and how it differs from invention; the four dimensions of innovation with their Nigerian examples; how knowledge fuels innovation (Knowledge Management, the pyramid, the DIKW model); Kurt Lewin's 3-stage change model; and how external shocks force a business to pivot. By the end you should be able to define innovation and explain its dimensions, analyse the relationship between knowledge and innovation, and apply the principles of change management to entrepreneurial ventures.

### Lesson 3.1: What innovation is

**Must know**

- **Innovation (McKinsey, 2022):** the systematic practice of developing and marketing breakthrough products and services for adoption by customers.
- **Innovation (second definition):** the successful implementation of new ideas that create value.
- Innovation is **not just invention**; it is about **making an idea work in the real world**.
- **The four dimensions of innovation**, in order:

| Dimension | Short meaning |
|---|---|
| 1. Product Innovation | **What** you sell |
| 2. Process Innovation | **How** you create it |
| 3. Market Innovation | **Who** you sell to and how you reach them |
| 4. Organisational Innovation | How you **structure and manage** your company |

**Explain more**

- Read the McKinsey definition word by word: *systematic* (planned, not a lucky accident), *developing and marketing* (you must both build it and sell it), *breakthrough products and services*, *for adoption by customers* (if customers do not take it up, it has not succeeded). The citation is **McKinsey, 2022**. An exam may swap in the World Bank, OECD or Kurt Lewin; none of these gave this definition.
- The second definition puts the weight on **successful implementation** and **value**. An idea sitting in your notebook, or even a working prototype nobody uses, is an invention, not an innovation. Innovation is invention plus making it work in the real world.
- Trap: "Invention creates value while innovation only generates ideas" is backwards. Innovation is the one tied to value and real-world use.
- The four dimensions are easy to remember as four questions a business owner asks: **What** do I sell? (product) **How** do I make or deliver it? (process) **Who** do I sell to, and how do I reach them? (market) **How** is my company run? (organisational).
- There are exactly **four** dimensions. "Legal", "political", "financial" or "refreeze" innovation are not among them; "refreeze" belongs to Lewin's change model in Lesson 3.4.
- The pictures that go with the four dimensions help you hold the ideas: a man inside a phone surrounded by shopping icons and money signs (product: the thing you sell), a head with arrows cycling from a sad face to a happy face (process: improving how something is done), a man pulling a gauge needle towards "RISK" (market: reaching into new, riskier targets), and a man fitting jigsaw pieces beside a lightbulb (organisational: rearranging how the company fits together).

### Lesson 3.2: The four dimensions with examples

**Must know**

| Dimension | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product Innovation | Improving a product or creating a totally new one | **Cowrywise and Piggyvest** took the traditional wooden **Kolo** (savings box) and moved it to an **app with interest rates** |
| Process Innovation | Changing how a product is created or delivered to **lower cost or increase speed** | **Chowdeck and Glovo**: the logistics process of using **bikes/bicycles** that allowed delivery in Lagos traffic within **30 minutes** (beating the traffic) |
| Market Innovation (**New Targets**) | Finding new markets or packaging for existing products | **The "Sachet Economy"**: most Nigerians cannot buy big tins of **Peak Milk**; innovating the packaging (sachets) opened a massive market for **low-income earners** |
| Organisational Innovation (**New Structures**) | Changing workplace culture or structure | **Andela/Tech Startups**: moving from the **"Oga must see you at your desk"** mentality to a **results-based culture** where you can **work from home** |

**Explain more**

- **Product innovation (Cowrywise and Piggyvest).** The Kolo is the wooden box many Nigerian children dropped coins into and broke open later. It earned nothing. Cowrywise and Piggyvest kept the same habit (putting money aside) but changed the product itself: it now lives in an app and pays interest. The thing you sell is new or improved, so this is product innovation.
- **Process innovation (Chowdeck and Glovo).** The food is not new; restaurants already cooked it. What is new is **how it gets to you**: bikes and bicycles weave through Lagos traffic and deliver within **30 minutes**. Process innovation aims to **lower cost or increase speed**. Watch the number: 30 minutes, not 15, 45 or 60. And note the innovation is the **logistics process**, not a new type of food.
- **Market innovation (the Sachet Economy).** Peak Milk is the same milk whether in a big tin or a sachet. Companies realised most Nigerians cannot afford the big tin, so they changed the **packaging** and reached a huge new market of **low-income earners**. Its label is **New Targets**: same product, new customers. The definition covers both "new markets" and "packaging for existing products".
- **Organisational innovation (Andela and tech startups).** Nothing about the product, delivery or customers changes here; what changes is **how the workplace is run**. Instead of a boss ("Oga") needing to see you physically at your desk, staff are judged on results and can work from home. Its label is **New Structures**.
- Quick test for any scenario question: ask what actually changed.
  - The thing sold changed (an esusu savings habit turned into an interest-paying app) = **product**.
  - The way it is made or delivered changed (vans swapped for motorcycle riders to be faster and cheaper) = **process**.
  - The customers or the packaging changed (a 1kg detergent bag also sold in small single-use packs) = **market**.
  - The workplace culture or structure changed (a bank letting staff work from home, judged by results) = **organisational**.
- Label trap: **New Targets** = market innovation; **New Structures** = organisational innovation. Do not swap them. "Organisational" and "Organizational" are the same word in two spellings.

### Lesson 3.3: Knowledge and innovation

**Must know**

- **Innovation doesn't happen in a vacuum. It is fuelled by knowledge.**
- **Knowledge Management (KM):** the systematic process of **creating, sharing, using, and managing** an organisation's knowledge and information.
- **The Pyramid** (from the base upwards):
  1. **Data:** raw facts (e.g. "Rice is 80k per bag").
  2. **Information:** context (e.g. "Rice price went up because of the border closure").
  3. **Knowledge:** application (e.g. "I should switch to selling local rice or cassava alternatives").
  4. **Innovation:** creating value from that knowledge.
- **Balogun market:** every trader in Balogun market has data (prices). Only the innovators use that data to **predict what will sell next Christmas**.
- **DIKW Knowledge Management Model** (pyramid diagram):
  - Main labels, top to bottom: **Wisdom, Knowledge, Information, Data, Facts, Measurements**.
  - Side labels, top to bottom: **Focus, Adjust, Compare, Sample, Observe**.
  - D-I-K-W = **Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom**; **Wisdom** sits at the top.

**Explain more**

- "Not in a vacuum" means innovation does not appear from nowhere, by luck or only from government policy. It grows out of what a person or business knows. That is why businesses manage knowledge deliberately (KM): they create it, share it, use it and manage it, in that order of the definition.
- Walk up the pyramid with the rice example:
  - **Data** is a bare fact with no explanation: rice costs 80k (₦80,000) a bag.
  - **Information** adds context, the "why": the price rose because of the **border closure**. (Trap: not fuel subsidy removal, the dollar rate or the Okada ban.)
  - **Knowledge** is putting that understanding to use, the "so what do I do": switch to local rice or cassava alternatives.
  - **Innovation** is the top step: actually creating value from that knowledge.
- Keyword pairs to memorise: Data = **raw facts**; Information = **context**; Knowledge = **application**; Innovation = **creating value**. An exam will give you one statement and ask which level it is, or will scramble the order. The correct order upwards is Data, Information, Knowledge, Innovation.
- The Balogun point: having data is common; every trader knows today's prices. What makes the innovator different is not collecting more price lists but **using** the data to look ahead (what will sell next Christmas).
- The DIKW diagram is a second version of the same idea. Its top level is **Wisdom**, not innovation; the four letters are Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom. Beneath Data it adds **Facts** and **Measurements** as the raw base. The side words describe what you do as you climb: **Observe**, **Sample**, **Compare**, **Adjust**, **Focus** (read from the bottom up). So in the simple pyramid the top is Innovation; in the DIKW model the top is Wisdom. Know which one a question is asking about.

### Lesson 3.4: Change and innovation: Kurt Lewin's model

**Must know**

- Naturally, humans are **averse to change**. They prefer **"The Devil they know."**
- **Kurt Lewin** proposed a **3-stage model** for planning change:

| Stage | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Unfreeze** | Realising the old way is dangerous or expensive | Kerosene stoves |
| 2. **Change** | The messy transition to the new way | Switching to gas cylinders |
| 3. **Refreeze** | Making the new way the habit | |

- **Lagos Okada ban:** when Lagos banned Okadas in certain areas, the **Change** phase was chaotic; people had to trek. Eventually people **Refroze** into using buses or trekking.
- **You cannot just announce innovation.** You have to **Unfreeze** people first: convince them why the old way is bad.
- Diagram: melting ice cubes (Unfreeze), a splash of water (Change), solid ice cubes (Refreeze).

**Explain more**

- "The Devil they know" is short for the saying "better the devil you know than the angel you don't": people would rather keep a familiar bad situation than risk an unfamiliar one. That is why new ideas meet resistance, and why change has to be planned.
- Picture the ice. **Unfreeze**: the block melts, meaning people let go of the old way once they see it is dangerous or expensive (the kerosene stove that can explode or keeps costing more). **Change**: the water splashes about, meaning the actual switch (to gas cylinders) is messy and uncomfortable. **Refreeze**: the water sets into new ice cubes, meaning the new way hardens into the normal habit.
- Order is always **Unfreeze, Change, Refreeze**. Traps: "Refreeze, Change, Unfreeze" or putting Refreeze in the middle. There is no "Pivot", "Planning" or "Adoption" stage in Lewin's model.
- Kerosene-to-gas: *realising* kerosene is dangerous or expensive is Unfreeze; the *actual messy switching* to gas cylinders is Change; gas becoming what everyone just uses is Refreeze.
- Okada ban: the chaotic period of trekking = **Change**; settling into buses or trekking as the new normal = **Refreeze**.
- Applying it to your own venture: before you introduce anything new to staff or customers, first show them why the old way is bad. For example, a business owner who holds meetings showing staff how much money and time old manual record books are costing, *before* bringing in new software, is **unfreezing**. Announcing the change and "handling resistance later" skips the first stage.
- Lewin is the name tied to the **3-stage change model**. He did not give the four dimensions, the McKinsey definition or the DIKW pyramid.

### Lesson 3.5: Adapting to external change (the pivot)

**Must know**

- **External shocks** (**Dollar rate**, **Fuel subsidy removal**) force innovation. Adapting to external change is called **the pivot**.
- **Case study: Fuel Subsidy Removal (2023/2024)**
  - **Reaction:** transport costs **tripled**.
  - **Innovation:** companies shifted to **remote work** (**Organisational Change**) or **staff buses**; some mechanics started **converting cars to CNG** (**Product/Process Change**).
- **Lesson:** change is **not always voluntary**. Sometimes **the economy forces you to innovate**.
- Diagram: a country road blocked by a barrier with a "ROAD CLOSED" sign.

**Explain more**

- Lewin's model is about change you plan. This lesson covers change you did not choose: a shock from outside the business, like a jump in the dollar rate or the removal of fuel subsidy. The "ROAD CLOSED" picture captures it: the usual road is blocked, so you must turn and find another route. That turn is the **pivot**.
- Fuel subsidy removal: memorise the date **2023/2024** and the reaction, transport costs **tripled** (not doubled or halved).
- Match each response to its type, because exams test the labels:
  - Remote work = **Organisational Change** (how the company is run changed; compare Andela in Lesson 3.2).
  - Staff buses = another company response to the transport cost.
  - Mechanics converting cars to **CNG** = **Product/Process Change**.
- Trap: "selling milk in sachets" was **not** a response to fuel subsidy removal; it is the market innovation example (the Sachet Economy).
- The lesson is the opposite of waiting: shocks do not reduce the need for innovation and you cannot wait for a stable economy; sometimes the economy forces you to innovate. A scenario such as a fashion brand that imported fabric switching to locally woven aso-oke after the naira falls against the dollar is a pivot, adapting to external change.

### One-minute recap

- Innovation = the systematic practice of developing and marketing breakthrough products and services for adoption by customers (McKinsey, 2022); also, the successful implementation of new ideas that create value. Not just invention: making an idea work in the real world.
- Four dimensions: Product (what you sell), Process (how you create it), Market (who you sell to and how you reach them), Organisational (how you structure and manage your company).
- Examples: Cowrywise/Piggyvest and the Kolo = product; Chowdeck/Glovo bikes, 30 minutes = process; Peak Milk sachets for low-income earners = market (New Targets); Andela results-based, work-from-home culture = organisational (New Structures).
- Innovation is fuelled by knowledge. KM = creating, sharing, using and managing an organisation's knowledge and information.
- Pyramid: Data (raw facts) → Information (context) → Knowledge (application) → Innovation (creating value). Rice 80k / border closure / switch to local rice or cassava.
- DIKW = Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom; Wisdom at the top, Facts and Measurements at the base.
- Humans prefer "The Devil they know". Kurt Lewin: Unfreeze, Change, Refreeze (kerosene to gas; Okada ban). Unfreeze people before announcing innovation.
- External shocks (dollar rate, fuel subsidy removal) force a pivot. Subsidy removal 2023/2024: transport costs tripled; remote work (organisational), staff buses, CNG conversion (product/process). Change is not always voluntary.
