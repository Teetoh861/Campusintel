## Topic 6: Micro and Small Enterprises

**What you must know:** the definitions of micro and small enterprise (World Bank 2020, SMEDAN 2017, OECD) with their employee and ₦ asset limits; the 7 characteristics of micro enterprises and the 12 of small enterprises; the categories and types of each, with their examples; the 12-point comparison of micro and small; and the four areas of importance (wealth creation, employment generation, financial independence, poverty reduction) with every point under each.

### Lesson 6.1: Micro enterprise: definition and characteristics

**Must know**

The aims of this topic: define micro and small enterprise, state their characteristics and identify their community; distinguish between micro and small enterprise; state the role and importance of micro and small businesses in **wealth creation**, **employment**, **financial independence** and **poverty reduction**.

- **Micro enterprise:** a very small business operation that employs a **minimal number of people** and operates with **low capital investment**. It is often **owner-managed** and may be **informal** in nature.
- **World Bank (2020):** micro enterprises are firms with **fewer than 10 employees**.
- **SMEDAN (2017)**, in Nigeria: a micro enterprise is a business with:
    1. **less than 10 employees**, and
    2. **total assets (excluding land and building) not exceeding ₦5 million**.
- Picture to remember: a **barber shop**, one barber cutting a seated customer's hair while another customer waits.

**7 characteristics of micro enterprise** (in order):

1. **Low Start-Up Capital:** requires minimal financial investment to start and operate.
2. **Limited Use of Technology:** relies on basic tools and manual processes rather than advanced technology.
3. **Owner-Dependent Operations:** business success is largely dependent on the owner's skills, effort and decisions.
4. **Restricted Market Coverage:** serves local or community-based markets rather than national or international markets.
5. **Informal Business Structure:** often operates without full registration or formal documentation.
6. **Limited Access to Credit:** faces challenges accessing bank loans due to **lack of collaterals and financial records**.
7. **High Business Mortality Rate:** more vulnerable to **economic shocks, inflation and policy changes**.

**Explain more**

- Think of the barber in your area: one shop, a chair or two, clippers and a mirror. He did not need much money to start (low start-up capital), he uses simple tools (limited technology), the shop rises or falls with him (owner-dependent), his customers come from the same street or estate (restricted market), he may never have registered with anyone (informal), the bank will not lend to him because he has no collateral and no proper books (limited credit), and one bad season of rising prices can close him down (high mortality).
- SMEDAN's definition has **two** conditions joined by "and": the staff count **and** the asset limit. When you calculate assets, **land and building are left out**. Vehicles, machines, cash and stock are not excluded.
- Traps on numbers: micro is **fewer than 10** employees (not fewer than 5, not fewer than 50). The SMEDAN asset ceiling is **₦5 million** (not ₦10 million, ₦49 million or ₦50 million).
- Traps on citations: the **World Bank (2020)** gives only the employee count for micro. **SMEDAN (2017)** gives both the employee count and the ₦5 million asset limit. Both definitions agree on "fewer than 10".
- Look-alike terms: "**Low** Start-Up Capital" belongs to micro; "**Moderate** Capital Base" belongs to small. "**Limited** Use of Technology" is micro; "**Use of** Technology" is small. "**Restricted** Market Coverage" is micro; "**Wider** Market Coverage" is small. "**High** Business Mortality Rate" is micro; "**Lower** Business Mortality Rate" is small. "**Informal** Business Structure" is micro; "**Formal** Business Registration" is small.
- If a question describes a story, match the cause to the characteristic: a shop closing after inflation or a new policy = **High Business Mortality Rate**; a loan refused for lack of collateral and records = **Limited Access to Credit**; work stopping because the owner is ill = **Owner-Dependent Operations**.

### Lesson 6.2: Examples and types of micro enterprises

**Must know**

**Examples of micro enterprises: 6 categories, 4 examples each**

1. **Retail and Trading:** provision stores and neighbourhood mini-shops; phone accessories and gadget sales outlets; POS (point of sale) agency businesses; small-scale online vendors on Instagram, WhatsApp and Facebook.
2. **Food and Beverages:** local food vendors (bukaterias, roadside food sellers); small bakeries producing bread and pastries; pure water (sachet water production units); small catering and home-based food services.
3. **Service-Based:** hairdressing salons and barbing shops; fashion designers and tailoring shops; laundry and dry-cleaning outlets; photography and videography.
4. **Manufacturing and Craft:** local soap and detergent producers; small-scale cosmetic and skincare producers; furniture and carpentry workshops; welding and metal fabrication shops.
5. **ICT and Creative:** freelance graphic design businesses; social media management services; content creation and digital marketing freelancers; small printing and business centre operators.
6. **Agriculture-Based:** poultry farms with small stock size; fish farming (catfish and tilapia ponds); vegetable and crop farming enterprises; agro-input retail sellers.

**Types of micro enterprises: 3 types**

1. **Production and Craft:** shoe making and leather works; furniture carpentry; soap and detergent production; bead making and jewellery; local bakery or snacks production; small-scale tailoring.
2. **Service-oriented:** hairdressing/barbing salon; laundry and dry cleaning services; mobile phone repairs; car wash businesses; catering and small food vending.
3. **Retail and Trading:** small grocery or provision stores; roadside fruit and vegetable sellers; cosmetic and beauty products retail; recharge card and SIM card vending; keke/okada small transport business.

**Explain more**

- There are two separate lists here. The **examples** list has **6 categories** (Retail and Trading, Food and Beverages, Service-Based, Manufacturing and Craft, ICT and Creative, Agriculture-Based). The **types** list has **3 types** (Production and Craft, Service-oriented, Retail and Trading). Know which list a question is drawing from by the wording of its options.
- **Hospitality and Tourism**, **Trade and Commerce**, **Manufacturing-Based** and **ICT and Digital** are categories of **small** enterprises, not micro. If one appears as an option for a micro category, it is the odd one out.
- Some placements surprise people, so learn them exactly:
    - **POS agency** and **online vendors on Instagram, WhatsApp and Facebook** are **Retail and Trading**, not ICT and Creative. They are selling or trading.
    - **Pure water (sachet water)** is **Food and Beverages**, not Manufacturing and Craft.
    - **Photography and videography** is **Service-Based**.
    - **Agro-input retail sellers** sit under **Agriculture-Based**, even though they retail.
    - **Small printing and business centre operators** are **ICT and Creative**.
    - In the types list, **keke/okada small transport** is under **Retail and Trading**, and **mobile phone repairs** is under **Service-oriented**.
    - **Cosmetic and beauty products retail** (selling products other people made) is **Retail and Trading**; producing cosmetics yourself is **Manufacturing and Craft** in the examples list.
- Furniture and carpentry, soap and detergent, and tailoring appear in more than one list; the category tells you what the business does (making things versus selling or serving).

### Lesson 6.3: Small enterprise: definition, characteristics and types

**Must know**

- **SMEDAN (2017)** (Small and Medium Enterprise Development Agency of Nigeria): a small enterprise is an enterprise with **10-49 employees** and **total assets between ₦5 million and ₦49 million, excluding land and buildings**.
- **World Bank (2020):** a small enterprise is a firm that employs **10-49 people**.
- **OECD** (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development): small enterprises are firms that employ **fewer than 50 people** and **operate with limited resources compared to large firms**.
- Picture to remember: "**Small Business**" linked to a vegetable shop, bakery, barbershop, nail salon, pottery and flower shop.

**12 characteristics of small enterprises** (in order):

1. Moderate Capital Base
2. Formal Business Registration
3. Structured Management Systems
4. Improved Record-Keeping
5. Better Access to Finance
6. Wider Market Coverage
7. Use of Technology
8. Higher Productivity Levels
9. Growth and Expansion Orientation
10. Employment of Skilled Labour
11. Lower Business Mortality Rate
12. Compliance with Labour and Tax Laws

**Types of small enterprises: 6 types**

1. **Manufacturing-Based:** small-scale food processing companies (e.g. packaged snacks, bread, fruit juice); furniture and carpentry workshops; soap, cosmetic and detergent production companies; garment and textile production companies.
2. **Service-oriented:** private schools and tutorial centres; small hospitals, clinics and diagnostic centres; accounting, auditing and consulting firms; logistics and courier service companies.
3. **Trade and Commerce:** wholesale distribution businesses; supermarkets and mini-marts; import and export trading companies; building materials distribution firms.
4. **ICT and Digital:** software development companies; digital marketing agencies; web design and IT support firms; fintech startups operating on a small scale.
5. **Hospitality and Tourism:** hotels and guest houses; restaurants and catering services; travel and tour agencies; event management companies.
6. **Agriculture-based:** commercial farms (poultry, fishery, crop farming); agro-processing firms (rice milling, cassava processing); agricultural input supply companies.

**Explain more**

- Three definitions, three different emphases. **SMEDAN (2017)** uses staff **and** assets (10-49 employees, ₦5 million to ₦49 million, land and buildings excluded). **World Bank (2020)** uses staff only (10-49). **OECD** says **fewer than 50** and adds "**limited resources compared to large firms**". If an option mentions limited resources, it is OECD; if it mentions naira, it is SMEDAN.
- SMEDAN's full name in this course is the **Small and Medium Enterprise Development Agency of Nigeria**. Distractors swap in "Micro", "Authority", "Directorate" or "Association".
- Work out classification questions with both SMEDAN conditions: 4 staff and ₦3 million assets is **micro**; 25 staff and ₦20 million assets is **small**. Notice that ₦5 million is the line where micro ends and small begins.
- The 12 small-enterprise characteristics are mostly the "grown-up" versions of the micro ones: it is registered, it has a management structure, it keeps better records, banks are more willing to lend to it, it sells further afield, it uses technology, it is more productive, it plans to grow, it hires skilled people, it survives longer, and it obeys labour and tax law.
- Story matching: a firm that registers staff for pension, deducts tax and files returns on time shows **Compliance with Labour and Tax Laws**; a school with a principal, administrator and heads of department each with clear duties shows **Structured Management Systems**; a firm that deliberately hires qualified accountants and certified technicians shows **Employment of Skilled Labour**; a supermarket opening a second branch with plans for more shows **Growth and Expansion Orientation**.
- **Owner-Dependent Operations**, **Limited Use of Technology**, **Informal Business Structure**, **Restricted Market Coverage**, **Limited Access to Credit** and **High Business Mortality Rate** are **not** small-enterprise characteristics; they belong to micro.
- **Food and Beverages** is a micro category, not one of the six small-enterprise types. Hospitals and clinics, private schools, consulting firms, wholesalers, supermarkets, import/export companies and hotels are **small** enterprise examples, not micro.

### Lesson 6.4: Distinguishing between micro and small enterprises

**Must know**

| Point | Micro enterprises | Small enterprises |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employees | Less than 10 | 10-49 |
| Capital investment | Moderate capital investment (as listed; elsewhere micro is described as low start-up capital) | Moderate capital investment |
| Assets (excluding land and buildings) | Less than ₦5 million | ₦5 million-₦49 million |
| Ownership | Sole proprietorship, often family-owned | Sole proprietorship or partnership |
| Management | Owner-managed with no formal structure | Semi-formal management with defined roles |
| Registration | Mostly informal, limited registration | Mostly formal and registered |
| Credit | Very limited access to bank credit | Better access to loans and government support |
| Technology | Minimal or manual processes | Moderate use of technology |
| Market | Local or neighbourhood markets | Local, regional and sometimes national markets |
| Growth | Limited scalability | Higher growth and expansion potential |
| Stability | High vulnerability to shocks | More stable and sustainable |
| Records | Poor or informal records | Basic structured accounting records |

**Explain more**

- Read the table across: each row is a pair. Exams often swap a pair, for example "Micro: more stable and sustainable; Small: high vulnerability to shocks". That is the wrong way round. Micro is **vulnerable**; small is **more stable and sustainable**.
- Ownership: micro is **sole proprietorship, often family-owned**; small is **sole proprietorship or partnership**. So partnership appears only on the small side.
- Management: "owner-managed with no formal structure" is micro; "**semi-formal** management with defined roles" is small. Note: semi-formal, not fully formal.
- "**Moderate use of technology**" describes **small** enterprises; micro uses "minimal or manual processes".
- Markets: small enterprises reach "local, regional and **sometimes** national" markets, not mainly international ones.
- Credit: small enterprises get better access to **loans and government support**, not "no need for finance".
- Records: "poor or informal records" is micro; small keeps basic structured accounting records.
- On capital, the comparison lists "moderate capital investment" on both sides. When a question asks about the micro characteristic on its own, the answer is **Low Start-Up Capital** (minimal investment to start and operate).

### Lesson 6.5: Importance of micro and small businesses

**Must know**

**Wealth Creation (4)**

1. Enable individuals to generate income through **ownership of productive assets**.
2. Support **capital accumulation and reinvestment** at the household and community levels.
3. Encourage **entrepreneurship and venture creation** across various sectors of the economy.
4. Promote **inclusive economic growth** by distributing wealth broadly.

**Employment Generation (3)**

1. **Major source of job creation**, especially in **developing economies**.
2. Absorb **unskilled, semi-skilled and skilled labour**.
3. Provide employment opportunities for **youth, women and vulnerable groups**. Reduce **pressure on the public sector and large firms** to create jobs.

**Financial Independence (4)**

1. Provide **self-employment and income autonomy**.
2. Reduce **dependence on salaried employment and government support**.
3. Enable individuals to **manage personal and household finances** independently.
4. Foster a culture of **savings, investment and financial responsibility**.

**Poverty Reduction (4)**

1. Create **livelihood opportunities in rural and urban areas**.
2. Increase **household income and purchasing power**.
3. Reduce **income inequality** through inclusive participation in economic activities.
4. Support **community development** and improve **living standards**.

**Explain more**

- Four headings, **15 points** in total (4 + 3 + 4 + 4). Employment Generation is the only heading with three points; its third point carries two ideas (jobs for youth, women and vulnerable groups, and less pressure on government and big firms).
- Use key words to place a point under its heading:
    - **assets, capital accumulation, reinvestment, venture creation, distributing wealth** = Wealth Creation.
    - **jobs, labour, youth and women, pressure on the public sector** = Employment Generation.
    - **self-employment, autonomy, not depending on salary or government, managing your own finances, savings culture** = Financial Independence.
    - **livelihoods, household income and purchasing power, income inequality, community development, living standards** = Poverty Reduction.
- Traps between look-alikes: "**inclusive economic growth** by distributing wealth broadly" is **Wealth Creation**, while "reduce income inequality through **inclusive participation**" is **Poverty Reduction**. "Generate **income** through ownership of productive assets" is Wealth Creation, while "increase **household income** and purchasing power" is Poverty Reduction. "Self-employment" is Financial Independence, not Employment Generation.
- Job creation is described as most important in **developing** economies, not developed ones.
- Everyday illustration: a graduate who starts a catering business so she does not rely on a salaried job or government support, and runs her own household finances, shows **Financial Independence**. Several cassava-processing businesses in a village raising family income, supporting community projects and improving living standards show **Poverty Reduction**.

### One-minute recap

- Micro enterprise: very small, minimal staff, low capital, often owner-managed and informal.
- World Bank (2020): micro = fewer than 10 employees; small = 10-49 people.
- SMEDAN (2017): micro = less than 10 employees and assets not above ₦5 million; small = 10-49 employees and assets ₦5 million-₦49 million; land and buildings excluded.
- OECD: small = fewer than 50 people, limited resources compared to large firms.
- Micro has 7 characteristics (low capital to high mortality); small has 12 (moderate capital base to compliance with labour and tax laws).
- Micro examples: 6 categories; micro types: 3 (Production and Craft, Service-oriented, Retail and Trading); small types: 6 (Manufacturing, Service, Trade and Commerce, ICT and Digital, Hospitality and Tourism, Agriculture).
- Comparison: micro is family-owned, informal, vulnerable, local; small is sole proprietorship or partnership, semi-formal, registered, more stable, wider markets.
- Importance: wealth creation (4), employment generation (3), financial independence (4), poverty reduction (4).
