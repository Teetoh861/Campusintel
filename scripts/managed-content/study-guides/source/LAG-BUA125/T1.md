## Topic 1: Introduction to Managerial Economics and Managerial Decisions

**What you must know:** the definition, nature and scope of managerial economics; its micro and macro sides; the three business categories it serves; its goals and the decisions it guides; the objectives of firms, non-profits and the public sector (with computations); and the steps in decision making.

### Lesson 1.1: Meaning and nature of managerial economics

**Must know**

- **Managerial economics (ME):** the use of economic theories, concepts and models to make business decisions, irrespective of the type or category of organisation.
- **Second definition:** the application of economic tools to improve and enhance managers' decision making in businesses.
- **Why it is a subfield of economics:** it focuses on the production, distribution and consumption of goods and services in a system.
- **Where it applies:** profit-making organisations, non-profit-making organisations and the public sector (government activities that meet the needs of society). Not profit-making firms only.
- **Why it is strategic:** its application covers **both** microeconomic and macroeconomic concepts.
- **Managerial positions** are not limited to profit-making firms. In the public sector and non-profits they carry other titles: HoD, Minister, Commissioner, DG, Coordinator, Vice Chancellor, Provost, Dean.
- **Functions and operations:** using economic theories, models and concepts to analyse, solve and operationalise problems that arise in business decision making.

**Nature of ME**

1. **Applied and practical:** it aims to solve real decision problems of organisations. This is how it differs from traditional economic theory, which is more abstract.
2. **Normative (prescriptive):** it says what managers **ought** to do to achieve their objectives, not just what happened.
3. **Mainly micro, but also macro:** it deals with decision problems of the individual organisation while taking account of macroeconomic conditions (interest rates, exchange rates, government spending).
4. **A link (bridge):** it links traditional economic theory with the **decision sciences** (mathematics, statistics, econometrics).
5. **Pragmatic and quantitative:** it uses figures, models and forecasts.

**Explain more**

- **Relationship with other disciplines:** economics supplies the theory (demand, cost, markets); **mathematics** supplies tools for optimisation; **statistics and econometrics** estimate demand and forecast; **accounting** supplies cost and revenue data; **operations research** supplies techniques such as linear programming; **management** supplies the organisational setting in which decisions are taken.
- **Role of the managerial economist:** forecasting demand, advising on pricing policy, analysing investment and expansion proposals, studying the external economic environment. Example: an economist at a Lagos cement company (think Dangote Cement) studies expected interest rates, exchange rates and government spending, then advises the board whether to build a new plant. That is analysing the external economic environment to guide decisions.
- **Traps:** ME is **not** monetary policy by a central bank, **not** financial accounting (recording transactions), **not** business law, and **not** motivating staff (that is management/organisational behaviour). Signing the statutory audit report is an auditor's job, not a managerial economist's.

### Lesson 1.2: Scope of managerial economics

**Must know**

| Microeconomic side | Macroeconomic side |
|---|---|
| Demand and supply | Population and distribution |
| Production | Foreign exchange |
| Equilibrium | Interest rates |
| Opportunity cost | Output |
| Profit maximisation | Unemployment |
| Market competition | Distribution of income |

- **Microeconomics:** behaviour of individual units: households (consumers), firms and individual industries (e.g. the electronics industry).
- **Macroeconomics:** economic aggregates: total output, unemployment, inflation, aggregate demand, the national or regional economy.
- **Central problem of economics:** scarcity, which forces choice.
- **4 factors of production:** land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship. Rewards: rent, wages, interest, profit.
- **Marginal rule:** if marginal benefit (MB) > marginal cost (MC), do **more** of the activity; if MB < MC, do less; stop where MB = MC.

**Explain more**

- Aggregate demand is a macro idea, so microeconomics is **not** concerned with it. An industry is still micro.
- Exam options swap the two columns. Interest rates and foreign exchange are macro; opportunity cost and market competition are micro. Distribution of income is macro; distribution in the sense of a firm's distribution of goods is not on the list.
- **Money is not a factor of production.** A bank loan is finance used to buy capital; capital itself means man-made goods used in production (machines, buildings). The management phrase "3Ms" (men, money, materials) is a resource grouping, not the economist's list of factors.
- Marginal thinking: if one more hour of overtime at a Shoprite branch brings ₦9,000 of extra sales margin but costs ₦6,000 in wages, MB > MC, so the hour is worth adding.

### Lesson 1.3: Goals of managerial economics and the decisions it guides

**Must know**

**Goals and objectives of ME (8):**

1. Optimum resource allocation.
2. Achieving operational efficiency in business administration.
3. Enhancement and improved decision making in business.
4. Effective decisions on the use and distribution of resources (the 3Ms or the four factors of production).
5. Decisions on **production techniques** and the balance between labour and technology, to achieve efficiency or profitability.
6. Decisions on **equipment replacement**.
7. Decisions on the **use of capital**: loans, overdrafts, equity, debentures, bonds, donations (depending on the type of organisation).
8. Decisions on **labour usage and size**: number, category, employment, downsizing.

- **Optimum resource allocation:** resources are put to the uses that give the greatest benefit at the lowest cost (not kept idle, not spent fast, not shared equally).

**Explain more**

Match the case to the decision type:

| Case | Decision type |
|---|---|
| Firm chooses bank loan, new equity or debentures for expansion | Use of capital |
| Non-profit school finances a hostel by donations or overdraft | Use of capital |
| Hospital replaces old X-ray machines now or in two years | Equipment replacement |
| Lagos bank cuts 150 staff and hires IT specialists | Labour usage and size |
| Aba garment factory decides number of sewing machines and tailors for lowest cost | Production technique (labour vs technology) |

- **Not** goals: employing as many workers as possible, removing every risk, replacing managers with government directives, preparing a statutory audit.

### Lesson 1.4: The three business categories

**Must know**

1. **Profit-making organisations:** sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability companies, multinational companies (MNCs), and industry groups: FMCG, financial sector, oil and gas, construction, real estate.
2. **Non-profit-making organisations:**
    - NGOs: Doctors Without Borders (MSF), Girls' Brigade, Red Cross Society.
    - Orphanages: SOS Children's Village, Little Saints, Living Fountain.
    - International and local clubs/societies: Rotary Club, Lions Club, Eko Tennis Club, Ikeja Golf Club.
    - Traditional societies and age grades.
    - Faith-based organisations: churches (RCCG, MFM, Methodist), Islamic societies (NASFAT), ATR (African Traditional Religion), fraternities.
3. **Public sector:** State Government and its agencies, Local Government, Federal Government MDAs (ministries, departments, agencies), public corporations, welfare boards.

**Explain more**

- Foreign exchange markets are **not** one of the three categories.
- A Red Cross branch in Ibadan splitting donations between first-aid training and flood relief is a **non-profit** applying ME. A State Commissioner for Health using population and cost data to site three health centres is the **public sector**.
- Traps: a Federal Ministry is public sector, not a non-profit; a church is non-profit, not profit-making; a real-estate limited company is profit-making.

### Lesson 1.5: Objectives of managerial decisions

**Must know**

**Profit-seeking firms**

- **Profit maximisation:** choose output and price to make the gap TR − TC as large as possible. Standard theory assumes owners aim to maximise profit. Profit = TR − TC. At the best output MR = MC.
- **Wealth (value) maximisation:** maximise the present value of expected future profits.
  Value of the firm = sum over t = 1 to n of [profit in year t / (1 + i)^t], where i = discount rate.
  It is better than simple profit maximisation because it accounts for the **timing and riskiness** of profits over many years (time value of money).
- **Sales (revenue) maximisation (William Baumol):** maximise total revenue subject to a minimum acceptable profit.
- **Satisficing (Herbert Simon):** aim for a satisfactory level of profit, not the maximum, because managers have limited information and cannot evaluate every alternative (bounded rationality).
- **Growth maximisation (Robin Marris):** maximise the growth rate of the firm (size, assets, market share).
- **Principal-agent problem:** managers (agents) pursue their own interests, such as large offices or high salaries, instead of maximising value for the owners (principals).

**Non-profit organisations:** provide as much quality service as possible while covering costs (e.g. a church-owned mission hospital).

**Public sector:** maximise **net social benefit (social welfare)**. Tool: **cost-benefit analysis**, comparing total benefits to society with total costs to society.

**Explain more**

Worked example 1 (profit vs sales maximisation). A Lagos snack maker, daily figures:

| Q | TR (₦) | TC (₦) | Profit (₦) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 100,000 | 70,000 | 30,000 |
| 20 | 180,000 | 110,000 | 70,000 |
| 30 | 240,000 | 150,000 | 90,000 |
| 40 | 280,000 | 200,000 | 80,000 |
| 50 | 300,000 | 270,000 | 30,000 |

- Profit maximiser: highest profit is ₦90,000, so produce **30 units**.
- Baumol sales maximiser with minimum profit ₦75,000: outputs that meet the constraint are 30 (₦90,000) and 40 (₦80,000). Of these, TR is highest at 40 (₦280,000), so produce **40 units**. Q = 50 has higher TR but profit of only ₦30,000 fails the constraint.
- Lesson: a sales maximiser produces more than a profit maximiser.

Worked example 2 (value). Profits ₦10m at end of year 1 and ₦12m at end of year 2, i = 10%.
- Year 1: 10 / 1.10 = ₦9.0909m.
- Year 2: 12 / 1.10^2 = 12 / 1.21 = ₦9.9174m.
- Value = 9.0909 + 9.9174 = **₦19.01m** (2 d.p.). Not ₦22m: money received later is worth less today.

Worked example 3 (choosing a project). X gives ₦15m at end of year 1; Y gives ₦16.2m at end of year 2; i = 10%.
- PV(X) = 15 / 1.10 = ₦13.64m. PV(Y) = 16.2 / 1.21 = ₦13.39m (2 d.p.).
- Choose **X**. Picking Y because ₦16.2m > ₦15m ignores timing.

Worked example 4 (price choice). Kaduna cosmetics firm: low price TR ₦4.5m, TC ₦3.1m, profit ₦1.4m; medium TR ₦6.0m, TC ₦4.4m, profit ₦1.6m; high TR ₦7.2m, TC ₦5.9m, profit ₦1.3m. Choose the **medium** price. Neither lowest cost nor highest revenue decides it; profit does.

- **Satisficing case:** an Ibadan bakery owner earning ₦200,000 a month says it is good enough and stops searching for cheaper suppliers. **Sales maximisation case:** a detergent MD says "push revenue as high as we can, provided profit does not fall below ₦50 million."
- Do not swap the names: **Simon = satisficing**, **Baumol = sales maximisation**.

### Lesson 1.6: Steps in managerial decision making

**Must know**

1. **Define the problem** (always first).
2. **Determine the objective** (e.g. maximise profit or social welfare).
3. **Identify (explore) the alternatives.**
4. **Evaluate the alternatives:** predict the likely costs, benefits and consequences of each.
5. **Choose the best alternative and implement it.**
6. **Perform sensitivity analysis and monitor the results.**

- **Sensitivity analysis:** testing how the best decision would change if key assumptions (expected price, cost, demand) turned out different.

**Explain more**

- Core order to memorise: define the problem → identify alternatives → evaluate alternatives → choose and implement.
- A Kano distributor's logistics manager has listed three ways to cut delivery delays. The next step is to **evaluate the costs and benefits of each**, not to implement the first one on the list or restate the mission.
- If a medium price for Indomie cartons is best only when flour costs stay below a certain level, sensitivity analysis shows the manager how far costs can rise before the choice changes.

### One-minute recap

- ME = economic theories, concepts and models used for business decisions in any organisation.
- It is applied, normative, mainly micro but also macro, and links economics with the decision sciences.
- Micro scope: demand and supply, production, equilibrium, opportunity cost, profit maximisation, market competition. Macro scope: population, foreign exchange, interest rates, output, unemployment, income distribution.
- Three categories: profit-making, non-profit-making, public sector.
- 8 goals of ME, including decisions on production technique, equipment replacement, use of capital, labour usage and size.
- Firm objectives: profit, wealth/value (PV), sales (Baumol), satisficing (Simon), growth (Marris). Non-profits: maximum service at cost. Public sector: net social benefit via cost-benefit analysis.
- Do more of an activity while MB > MC.
- Decision steps start with defining the problem and end with sensitivity analysis and monitoring.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A firm expects profits of ₦8m, ₦9m and ₦10m at the end of years 1, 2 and 3. Using a 12% discount rate (compounded annually), find the value of the firm to 2 decimal places.

*Solution:*
- Year 1: 8 / 1.12 = ₦7.1429m.
- Year 2: 9 / 1.12^2 = 9 / 1.2544 = ₦7.1747m.
- Year 3: 10 / 1.12^3 = 10 / 1.404928 = ₦7.1178m.
- Value = 7.1429 + 7.1747 + 7.1178 = **₦21.44m** (rounded to 2 d.p.), well below the undiscounted ₦27m.
