## Topic 3: IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements

**What you must know:** the objective, scope and complete set of financial statements; the eight general features; the statement of financial position and current/non-current classification; profit or loss and other comprehensive income (nature vs function, OCI, reclassification); the statement of changes in equity, cash flows and notes; preparing the statements from a trial balance.

### Lesson 3.1: Objective, scope and the complete set

**Must know**

- **Objective:** to prescribe the basis for presentation of general purpose financial statements to ensure **comparability** both with the entity's own previous periods and with other entities. IAS 1 sets the line items for every statement **except the statement of cash flows** (IAS 7).
- **Scope:** all general purpose financial statements (individual, separate or consolidated; banks included), but **not** the structure and content of **condensed interim financial statements** (IAS 34).
- **General purpose financial statements:** those intended to meet the needs of users who are not in a position to require an entity to prepare reports tailored to their particular information needs.
- **Financial statements:** a **structured representation of the financial position and financial performance** of an entity, giving information useful for economic decisions. They also show the results of **management's stewardship** of the resources entrusted to it.

**Complete set of financial statements:**

1. Statement of financial position as at the end of the period.
2. Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income for the period.
3. Statement of changes in equity for the period.
4. Statement of cash flows for the period.
5. Notes, comprising material accounting policy information and other explanatory information.
6. Comparative information for the preceding period.
7. A statement of financial position at the **beginning of the preceding period**, when a retrospective policy change, retrospective restatement or reclassification has a material effect on it.

- A **directors' report**, a financial review by management, environmental reports and value added statements sit **outside** the financial statements and outside IFRSs: they are **not** part of the set.
- **Titles:** other titles are permitted, e.g. "statement of comprehensive income".
- **One or two statements:** a single statement (profit or loss section first, then OCI), or a separate statement of profit or loss that **immediately precedes** the statement presenting comprehensive income, which **begins with profit or loss**.

**Explain more**

- "Significant accounting policies" was replaced by "**material accounting policy information**" from 2023; options may use either phrase.
- **Third statement of financial position:** Ikeja Paints Plc (year end 31 December 2025) changes a policy retrospectively with a material effect at 1 January 2024. It presents **three**: 31 December 2025, 31 December 2024 and 1 January 2024.
- IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* (issued April 2024) replaces IAS 1 for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. Learn IAS 1 as set out here.

### Lesson 3.2: The eight general features

**Must know**

1. **Fair presentation and compliance:** faithful representation of transactions in line with the **Conceptual Framework's definitions and recognition criteria**. A complying entity makes an **explicit and unreserved statement of compliance in the notes**, and cannot claim compliance unless it meets **all** requirements. Disclosure or notes **cannot rectify** inappropriate accounting policies.
2. **Going concern:** prepare on this basis **unless management intends to liquidate the entity or cease trading, or has no realistic alternative but to do so**. Management looks **at least, but not limited to, twelve months from the end of the reporting period**. If not a going concern, disclose **that fact, the basis used and the reason**.
3. **Accrual basis:** for everything except cash flow information.
4. **Materiality and aggregation:** **Material** information is information whose omission, misstatement or obscuring could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that primary users make on the basis of general purpose financial statements. It depends on **nature or magnitude, or both**. An immaterial item is **aggregated** with others and may still warrant separate presentation in the notes.
5. **Offsetting:** do not offset assets and liabilities, or income and expenses, **unless required or permitted by an IFRS**.
6. **Frequency:** a complete set **at least annually**. For a longer or shorter period, disclose **the reason** and **that amounts are not entirely comparable**.
7. **Comparative information:** minimum **two** statements of financial position, two statements of profit or loss and OCI, two statements of cash flows and two statements of changes in equity, with related notes.
8. **Consistency of presentation:** keep presentation unless (a) a **significant change in the nature of operations** (or a review) makes another presentation more appropriate, or (b) **an IFRS requires** a change.

**Explain more**

- **Accrual:** Surulere Prints Ltd delivers goods worth ₦4m in December 2025 and is paid in February 2026: revenue belongs in 2025.
- **Not offsetting:** assets net of valuation allowances. Receivables ₦45m less allowance ₦3m are shown at **₦42m**.
- **Required netting:** a disposal gain = proceeds − carrying amount − selling expenses. Ota Bottling Ltd sells a generator for ₦8m, carrying amount ₦5.5m, selling costs ₦0.3m: gain = 8 − 5.5 − 0.3 = **₦2.2m**. Gains and losses on a group of similar transactions (e.g. foreign exchange) are shown **net, but separately if material**.
- **Departure** from an IFRS is allowed only in **extremely rare** cases where compliance would be so misleading that it conflicts with the objective of financial statements; the departure and its effect are disclosed.
- **Reclassifying comparatives:** Ibadan Ceramics Ltd moves delivery costs from administrative to distribution costs. It **reclassifies the comparatives unless impracticable** (cannot be done after every reasonable effort) and discloses the **nature, amount and reason**. A new director's preference or a higher profit is never a valid reason.

### Lesson 3.3: Statement of financial position

**Must know**

**Minimum line items:**

| Assets | Liabilities and equity |
|---|---|
| Property, plant and equipment | Trade and other payables |
| Investment property | Provisions |
| Intangible assets | Financial liabilities |
| Financial assets | Current tax liabilities and assets |
| Equity-accounted investments | Deferred tax liabilities and assets |
| Biological assets | Liabilities of disposal groups held for sale |
| Inventories | Non-controlling interests, within equity |
| Trade and other receivables | Issued capital and reserves of owners of the parent |
| Cash and cash equivalents; assets held for sale | |

- Cost of sales (or any income or expense) is **not** a line item here. Add lines and subtotals when relevant.
- Use a current/non-current split **unless** an **order of liquidity** presentation is **reliable and more relevant** (many financial institutions).
- **Deferred tax** is **always non-current**.

**Current asset** if: (1) realised, sold or consumed in the **normal operating cycle**; (2) held primarily for **trading**; (3) realised within **twelve months** after the reporting period; or (4) **cash or cash equivalent**, unless restricted for at least twelve months. Otherwise non-current.

**Current liability** if: (1) settled in the **normal operating cycle**; (2) held primarily for **trading**; (3) due within **twelve months**; or (4) the entity has **no right at the end of the reporting period** to defer settlement for at least twelve months. Otherwise non-current.

- **Operating cycle:** time from acquiring assets for processing to realising them in cash. If not clearly identifiable, **assume twelve months**.

**Explain more**

- **Cycle beats twelve months:** Onitsha Spirits Ltd's whisky matured over three years is a **current asset**. Port Harcourt Oil Services Ltd's trade payables due in 14 months are **current**, being working capital of the normal cycle.
- **Refinancing after year end:** Abuja Cement Plc's loan is due four months after 31 December 2025; long-term refinancing is agreed on 20 February 2026. At the reporting date there was no right to defer, so it is **current**; the refinancing is a **non-adjusting event**. (A right at the reporting date to roll over under an existing facility would make it non-current.)
- **Covenant breach:** a breach by the year end making the loan repayable on demand gives a **current** liability, even if the lender waives it after the year end. If, **by the year end**, the lender grants a grace period ending **at least twelve months** later, the loan is **non-current**.
- **Intention is irrelevant:** with a right to defer for twelve months, a liability is non-current even if management plans to repay early.
- **Instalment loan:** ₦60m repayable in four equal annual instalments, first due within twelve months: current ₦15m, non-current 60 − 15 = **₦45m**.
- **Classify (₦m):** inventories 25, receivables 14, cash 9, cash restricted by court order for four years 6, plant 70. Current assets = 25 + 14 + 9 = **48**. Trade payables 30, overdraft 5, current tax 12, current portion of loan 10, loan due 2029 50, deferred tax 7. Current liabilities = 30 + 5 + 12 + 10 = **57**.
- Notes subclassify lines (e.g. inventories into materials, work in progress, finished goods) and give share capital details and the nature and purpose of each reserve.

### Lesson 3.4: Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income

**Must know**

- Besides the two sections, present: **profit or loss; total other comprehensive income; comprehensive income for the period**. Also show profit and comprehensive income attributable to **non-controlling interests and owners of the parent**.
- **Other comprehensive income:** income and expenses (including reclassification adjustments) not recognised in profit or loss as required or permitted by other IFRSs.
- **Total comprehensive income:** the change in equity in a period from transactions and other events, **other than transactions with owners in their capacity as owners**. TCI = Profit or loss + OCI.
- **Reclassification adjustments:** amounts reclassified to profit or loss in the current period that were recognised in OCI in the current or previous periods.
- **Minimum profit or loss lines include:** revenue (interest revenue by the effective interest method shown separately); **finance costs**; impairment losses (IFRS 9); share of profit of associates and joint ventures; **tax expense**; **a single amount for discontinued operations**. Changes in revaluation surplus are **not** a profit or loss line.

| OCI: will NOT be reclassified to profit or loss | OCI: will be reclassified when conditions are met |
|---|---|
| Changes in revaluation surplus (IAS 16, IAS 38) | Translation differences on a foreign operation |
| Remeasurements of defined benefit plans | Effective portion of cash flow hedge gains and losses |
| Equity investments designated at fair value through OCI | Debt-type financial assets at fair value through OCI |

- OCI items are shown **net of tax**, or gross with one tax amount split between the two groups.
- **Extraordinary items are prohibited** in the statements and the notes.

| Nature of expense method | Function (cost of sales) method |
|---|---|
| Revenue; Other income | Revenue; Cost of sales → Gross profit |
| Changes in inventories of finished goods and WIP | Other income |
| Raw materials and consumables used | Distribution costs |
| Employee benefits; Depreciation and amortisation | Administrative expenses |
| Other expenses → Profit before tax | Other expenses → Profit before tax |

- **Nature** is simple: no allocation to functions. **Function** can be more relevant but may need arbitrary allocations; it **must also disclose depreciation and amortisation and employee benefits expense**.
- **Separate disclosure** of material items arises from: write-downs of inventories to NRV or PPE to recoverable amount (and reversals); restructurings; disposals of PPE; disposals of investments; discontinued operations; litigation settlements; other reversals of provisions.

**Explain more**

- **Function (₦m):** revenue 180, cost of sales 105, other income 4, distribution 12, administrative 20, other expenses 3, finance costs 6. Gross profit = 180 − 105 = **75**; PBT = 75 + 4 − 12 − 20 − 3 − 6 = **38**.
- **Nature (₦m):** revenue 250, other income 6, **decrease** in inventories 8, raw materials 110, employees 50, depreciation 20, other 15. Expenses = 203; PBT = 256 − 203 = **53**. An **increase** in inventories reduces total expenses.
- An IAS 16 revaluation gain goes to **OCI**. Impairment of receivables, disposal gains, interest revenue and share of an associate's profit go to **profit or loss**.
- **TCI:** profit ₦40m; revaluation gain ₦10m with 30% deferred tax, net = 10 × 70% = ₦7m; translation loss ₦2m. TCI = 40 + 7 − 2 = **₦45m**. If ₦9m belongs to non-controlling interests, owners of the parent get **₦36m**. Reverse: TCI ₦72m including OCI ₦12m means profit = **₦60m**.
- **Reclassification:** translation gain this year ₦3m, then the subsidiary is sold and cumulative gains of ₦5m go to profit or loss. Net OCI = 3 − 5 = **(₦2m)**, so the gain is not counted twice.

### Lesson 3.5: Statement of changes in equity, cash flows and notes

**Must know**

- The **statement of changes in equity** links the **statement of financial position and the statement of comprehensive income**. It shows: TCI split between owners of the parent and non-controlling interests; the effects of retrospective application or restatement (IAS 8) for each component; and, for each component, a reconciliation of opening to closing amounts showing changes from **(i) profit or loss, (ii) OCI, (iii) transactions with owners in their capacity as owners**.
- **Transactions with owners:** share issues, buy-backs, **dividends**. Dividends and dividends per share go **in the statement of changes in equity or in the notes**, never as an expense.
- **Statement of cash flows:** shows the **ability of the entity to generate cash and cash equivalents and its needs to utilise those cash flows** (IAS 7).
- **Notes:** (a) basis of preparation and accounting policies; (b) **information required by IFRSs not presented elsewhere** in the financial statements; (c) other information relevant to understanding them. Usual order: (1) **statement of compliance with IFRSs**; (2) material accounting policies; (3) supporting information for line items; (4) other disclosures such as contingent liabilities.

**Explain more**

- **Formulas:** Closing retained earnings = Opening (restated) + Profit − Dividends − Transfers to reserves. Share premium = Shares issued × (Issue price − Nominal value). Closing equity = Opening equity + TCI + Share issues − Dividends.
- **Worked example**, Ilupeju Foods Ltd, year to 31 December 2025 (₦'000): prior period error overstated earlier profits by 600; issued 4,000,000 ₦1 shares at ₦1.75; profit 5,200; revaluation gain 800; dividends 1,500.

| | Share capital | Share premium | Revaluation surplus | Retained earnings | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan 2025 | 10,000 | 2,000 | 1,500 | 8,000 | 21,500 |
| Prior period error | | | | (600) | (600) |
| Restated | 10,000 | 2,000 | 1,500 | 7,400 | 20,900 |
| Share issue | 4,000 | 3,000 | | | 7,000 |
| Profit | | | | 5,200 | 5,200 |
| OCI | | | 800 | | 800 |
| Dividends | | | | (1,500) | (1,500) |
| 31 Dec 2025 | 14,000 | 5,000 | 2,300 | 11,100 | 32,400 |

  Share premium = 4,000 × (1.75 − 1.00) = 3,000. TCI = 5,200 + 800 = 6,000. Check: 20,900 + 7,000 + 6,000 − 1,500 = 32,400.
- **Traps:** OCI goes to its own reserve, not retained earnings. An error adjusts the **opening** balance. A transfer to general reserve lowers retained earnings without changing total equity: ₦120m + ₦35m − ₦12m dividends − ₦5m transfer = **₦138m**.

### Lesson 3.6: Preparing the statements from a trial balance

**Must know**

1. **Cost of sales** = Opening inventory + Purchases − Closing inventory (add carriage inwards; deduct returns outwards).
2. Charge **depreciation** for the year and add it to accumulated depreciation.
3. **Accruals** increase the expense and are current liabilities; **prepayments** reduce the expense and are current assets.
4. Group by function: **distribution** (carriage outwards, selling); **administrative** (salaries, rent, rates, insurance, office, irrecoverable debts, depreciation); **finance costs** (full year's interest). Rent received is **other income**.
5. Tax is deducted after profit before tax; unpaid tax is a current liability. Dividends go to the statement of changes in equity.
6. Check total assets = total equity and liabilities.

**Explain more**

**Worked example.** Ikorodu Traders Ltd, trial balance at 31 December 2025 (₦'000):

Add rent received 119; deduct depreciation (10% × furniture 300 = 30), irrecoverable debts 60, insurance 69, office 115, rates 21, general 225 (total 520): PBT = 4,278 + 119 − 520 = **₦3,877,000**. Tax comes after this line.

### One-minute recap

- IAS 1 aims at comparability; it excludes condensed interim statements (IAS 34) and cash flow line items (IAS 7).
- Complete set: SFP, SPLOCI, SOCE, cash flows, notes, comparatives, plus an opening SFP after a material retrospective change. No directors' report.
- Eight features: fair presentation, going concern (at least 12 months ahead), accrual, materiality and aggregation, no offsetting unless an IFRS allows, at least annual, comparatives (two of each statement), consistency.
- Current: operating cycle, trading, within 12 months, unrestricted cash; liabilities also without a right at the year end to defer for 12 months. Deferred tax is non-current.
- Post-year-end refinancing or waivers do not make a loan non-current.
- TCI = profit + OCI; revaluation surplus and pension remeasurements are never reclassified; translation and cash flow hedge items are.
- Nature or function; function users disclose depreciation and employee benefits. No extraordinary items.
- SOCE: profit, OCI and owner transactions for each equity component.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Owerri Mills Plc reports profit of ₦30m. OCI (net of tax): revaluation gain on land ₦5m, remeasurement loss on its defined benefit plan ₦1m, translation gain on a foreign operation ₦2m. Opening retained earnings ₦50m; dividends paid ₦8m. Find (a) TCI, (b) OCI that will not be reclassified, (c) closing retained earnings.

*Solution:*
(a) OCI = 5 − 1 + 2 = ₦6m; TCI = 30 + 6 = **₦36m**.
(b) 5 − 1 = **₦4m** (the translation gain will be reclassified on disposal).
(c) 50 + 30 − 8 = **₦72m**. OCI goes to its own reserves, and dividends are a transaction with owners, not an expense.
