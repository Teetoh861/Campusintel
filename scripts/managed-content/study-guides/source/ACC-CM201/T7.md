## Topic 7: IAS 20 Government Grants and Disclosure of Government Assistance

**What you must know:** the meaning of government, grant and assistance; the two recognition conditions; the capital and income approaches; grants related to assets (deduct from cost or deferred income) and to income; prorating a grant over related costs; repayment; non-monetary grants and low-interest loans; disclosures.

### Lesson 7.1: Government, grants and assistance

**Must know**

- **Government:** the government of nations, government agencies and similar bodies, whether **local, national or international**.
- **Government grants:** government assistance, **quantified in monetary terms**, in the form of **transfers of resources** to an entity **in return for past or future compliance with certain conditions** relating to the operating activities of the entity.
- **Government assistance:** **any action of government** aimed at giving economic benefits to an entity or range of entities qualifying under certain criteria.
- **All government grants are assistance, but not all assistance is a grant.** A grant has a monetary value and involves a transfer of resources; much assistance does not.
- **Two types of grant:**
    1. **Grants related to assets:** the primary condition is that the entity **purchases, constructs or otherwise acquires non-current assets**.
    2. **Grants related to income:** grants for **any purpose other than** supporting the acquisition of non-current assets (e.g. towards wages, training or running costs).
- **Forgivable loans:** loans that the lender undertakes to waive repayment of under prescribed conditions.

| | Grant | Assistance without monetary value |
|---|---|---|
| Monetary value | Yes | No (cannot reasonably be valued) |
| Accounted for | Yes, under IAS 20 | Not recognised; disclosed if significant |
| Examples | Cash towards a machine; wage subsidy | Tax holiday, free technical or marketing advice, government guarantees |

**Outside IAS 20:** tax benefits such as tax holidays and investment tax credits (IAS 12); government ownership of shares in the entity; grants on biological assets measured at fair value less costs to sell (IAS 41). Public infrastructure available to everyone (roads, communication networks) is not government assistance.

**Explain more**

- A state agency, a local government council and an international development authority are all "government". A privately owned commercial bank is **not**, even if it lends to the entity.
- The Federal Government gives Ikeja Foods Ltd a five-year **pioneer status tax holiday** with no cash paid: this is **assistance without monetary value**, not a grant.
- Lagos State pays ₦40 million to Ota Clinics Ltd on condition it buys a dialysis machine: a **grant related to assets** (a state grant is still a grant). An agency gives Kano Textiles Ltd ₦15 million towards staff wages, with no asset purchase required: a **grant related to income**.
- Trap: options that reverse the rule ("all assistance is a grant, but not all grants are assistance") are wrong.

### Lesson 7.2: Recognition, approaches and special grants

**Must know**

A grant is **not recognised** until there is **reasonable assurance** that:

1. the entity **will comply with the conditions** attaching to it; and
2. the grant **will be received**.

**Both** must be met; one alone does not trigger recognition. Receiving the cash is not by itself proof that the conditions will be met.

**Core principle:** grants are recognised in **profit or loss on a systematic basis** over the periods in which the entity recognises as expenses the related costs the grants are intended to compensate.

**Two broad approaches:**

| Capital approach | Income approach |
|---|---|
| Grant recognised **outside profit or loss**, credited directly to shareholders' interests (equity) | Grant recognised **in profit or loss** over one or more periods |
| Argument: it is a financing device, not earned | Argument: it is received from a source other than shareholders and is earned by complying with conditions |
| **Not permitted** by IAS 20 | **Required** by IAS 20 |

Special cases:

1. **Grant compensating expenses or losses already incurred**, or giving immediate support with no future related costs: recognised in profit or loss **in the period it becomes receivable**.
2. **Grant for non-depreciable land** with conditions (e.g. build a factory on it): recognised over the periods that bear the cost of meeting the conditions (e.g. the life of the factory).
3. **Forgivable loan:** treated as a grant when there is reasonable assurance the terms for forgiveness will be met.
4. **Below-market-interest government loan:** the benefit is a grant, measured as **Loan proceeds − Fair value of the loan** (initial fair value under IFRS 9).
5. **Non-monetary grant** (e.g. land or other assets): recorded at **fair value**, or alternatively at a **nominal amount**.

**Explain more**

- Enugu Agro Ltd is reasonably assured the ₦20 million cash will be paid but not that it can meet the job-creation condition: **recognise nothing yet**. Once both conditions are reasonably assured, recognise it (with a contingent liability if conditions remain).
- **Low-interest loan:** the Bank of Industry lends Oyo Poultry Ltd ₦10,000,000 at a concessionary rate; the fair value of the loan at a market rate is ₦8,200,000. Grant = 10,000,000 − 8,200,000 = **₦1,800,000**, treated as a grant related to whatever it finances. The loan is carried at ₦8,200,000.
- **Non-monetary grant:** Ogun State gives Sagamu Ceramics Plc land worth ₦25,000,000 on condition it builds a factory: Dr Land 25,000,000; Cr Deferred income 25,000,000, released over the factory's life.
- Trap: the core principle says a **systematic basis**, not "straight-line only" and not "in full when received".
- Trap: the **capital approach** means crediting equity. Deducting a grant from an asset is **not** the capital approach: the grant still reaches profit or loss through lower depreciation.

### Lesson 7.3: Grants related to assets

**Must know**

IAS 20 allows two presentations:

1. **Deduct the grant from the cost (carrying amount) of the asset.** Dr Bank, Cr Asset. Depreciation is based on the **reduced amount**. The grant reaches profit or loss as lower depreciation.
2. **Deferred income method.** Dr Bank, Cr Deferred income. The asset stays at **full cost** and is depreciated on full cost; each year a portion of the grant is moved: **Dr Deferred income, Cr Income statement** (profit or loss).

Formulas (straight line, nil residual value):

- Method 1: Annual depreciation = (Cost − Grant) ÷ Useful life
- Method 2: Annual depreciation = Cost ÷ Useful life; Annual grant income = Grant ÷ Useful life
- Net annual charge (both methods) = (Cost − Grant) ÷ Useful life
- Deferred income balance after n years = Grant − n × annual grant income

| | Deduct from cost | Deferred income |
|---|---|---|
| Asset shown at | Cost less grant | Full cost |
| Depreciation | On cost less grant | On full cost |
| Grant income line | None | Yes, each year |
| Separate balance in SFP | None | Deferred income (current and non-current parts) |
| Net effect on profit | Same | Same |

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1.** XYZ Nigeria Limited receives a grant of ₦10,000,000 towards a Micro Magnetic machine costing ₦100,000,000, depreciated straight line over 5 years, nil residual value.

*Method 1 (deduct from cost):*

| Machine account | ₦ | | ₦ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank (cost) | 100,000,000 | Bank (grant) | 10,000,000 |
| | | Balance c/d | 90,000,000 |
| | 100,000,000 | | 100,000,000 |

Depreciation = 90,000,000 ÷ 5 = **₦18,000,000** a year for 5 years.

*Method 2 (deferred income):* machine stays at ₦100,000,000; depreciation = 100,000,000 ÷ 5 = **₦20,000,000** a year. Grant income = 10,000,000 ÷ 5 = **₦2,000,000** a year.

| Year | Released to income ₦ | Deferred income c/d ₦ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2,000,000 | 8,000,000 |
| 2 | 2,000,000 | 6,000,000 |
| 3 | 2,000,000 | 4,000,000 |
| 4 | 2,000,000 | 2,000,000 |
| 5 | 2,000,000 | nil |

Net charge under Method 2 = 20,000,000 − 2,000,000 = ₦18,000,000, the same as Method 1.

**Worked example 2 (balances after two years).** Onitsha Plastics Ltd buys a machine for ₦50,000,000 with a ₦15,000,000 grant; straight line over 5 years.

- Method 1: depreciation = 35,000,000 ÷ 5 = ₦7,000,000; carrying amount at end of year 2 = 35,000,000 − 14,000,000 = **₦21,000,000**. Total depreciation over the whole life = ₦35,000,000.
- Method 2: depreciation = 50,000,000 ÷ 5 = ₦10,000,000; grant income ₦3,000,000; net charge ₦7,000,000. End of year 2: machine = 50,000,000 − 20,000,000 = **₦30,000,000**; deferred income = 15,000,000 − 6,000,000 = **₦9,000,000** (₦3,000,000 current, ₦6,000,000 non-current). Net 30,000,000 − 9,000,000 = ₦21,000,000, as Method 1.

- Trap: under Method 2 the asset's carrying amount ignores the grant entirely. Under Method 1 the balance carried down straight after recording the grant is cost minus grant.
- Trap: the release entry credits the **income statement**, not bank, share capital or the asset.
- In the statement of cash flows, the asset purchase and the grant receipt are shown as separate items under either method.

### Lesson 7.4: Grants related to income

**Must know**

- Grants related to income are **not recognised as income immediately on receipt**; they are **matched against the related expense** in the periods the costs are incurred.
- **Presentation (either):**
    1. **separately**, or under a general heading such as **"other income"**; or
    2. as a **deduction from the related expense**.
- Never credited directly to equity or retained earnings.

**Prorating a grant over related costs (4 steps):**

1. Ascertain the **total cost**.
2. Ascertain the **total value of the grant**.
3. **Prorate the grant using each year's estimated cost**: divide each year's cost by the total cost and multiply by the grant value.
4. Allocate the amount to the income statement.

Grant income for a year = (Cost incurred in the year ÷ Total estimated cost) × Total grant

**Explain more**

**Worked example 3.** Aba Leather Ltd receives a grant of ₦12,000,000 for a three-year programme with costs: Year 1 ₦2,000,000; Year 2 ₦3,000,000; Year 3 ₦5,000,000 (total ₦10,000,000).

| Year | Working | Grant income ₦ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 × 12,000,000 | 2,400,000 |
| 2 | 3,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 × 12,000,000 | 3,600,000 |
| 3 | 5,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 × 12,000,000 | 6,000,000 |
| Total | | 12,000,000 |

- The total recognised over the whole period always equals the **total grant**, even if the grant is larger than the total cost. Cumulative income to any date = cumulative cost ÷ total cost × grant: by the end of Year 2, (2,000,000 + 3,000,000) ÷ 10,000,000 × 12,000,000 = ₦6,000,000.
- The same proportional method is used for a construction programme over several years (for example a grant for building a hangar spread over five years of construction costs).
- **Presentation example:** a ₦4,000,000 wage subsidy for the year, against wages of ₦30,000,000, is shown either as other income of ₦4,000,000 (wages ₦30,000,000) or as net wages of ₦26,000,000.
- A grant received to cover losses already suffered (e.g. flood damage last year) goes to profit or loss **when it becomes receivable**.

### Lesson 7.5: Repayment, assistance and disclosures

**Must know**

A grant that becomes repayable is accounted for as a **change in accounting estimate** (prospectively).

1. **Grant related to income:** apply the repayment first against any **unamortised deferred credit**; any excess goes to **profit or loss immediately**.
2. **Grant related to assets:** **increase the carrying amount of the asset** or **reduce the deferred income balance** by the amount repayable. The **cumulative extra depreciation** that would have been charged without the grant is recognised **immediately in profit or loss**.

**Government assistance not recognised** (no monetary value) is disclosed where significant, for example free technical or marketing advice and government guarantees.

**Disclosures:**

1. the accounting policy for government grants, including the **method of presentation** adopted;
2. the **nature and extent** of grants recognised, and an indication of other forms of government assistance from which the entity has directly benefited;
3. **unfulfilled conditions and other contingencies** attaching to government assistance that has been recognised.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 4 (repayment, income grant).** Ilorin Rice Mills Ltd received ₦6,000,000; ₦2,000,000 is still in deferred income when it must repay the full ₦6,000,000. Dr Deferred income 2,000,000; Dr Profit or loss 4,000,000; Cr Bank 6,000,000.

**Worked example 5 (repayment, asset grant).** A machine cost ₦40,000,000 with a ₦10,000,000 grant, straight line over 5 years. At the end of year 2 the whole grant becomes repayable.

- *Deduction method:* carrying amount = 30,000,000 − (2 × 6,000,000) = ₦18,000,000. Add back the grant: Dr Machine 10,000,000; Cr Bank 10,000,000. Extra depreciation that would have been charged = 10,000,000 ÷ 5 × 2 = ₦4,000,000: Dr Profit or loss 4,000,000; Cr Accumulated depreciation 4,000,000. New carrying amount = 18,000,000 + 10,000,000 − 4,000,000 = **₦24,000,000**, which equals 40,000,000 − (2 × 8,000,000).
- *Deferred income method:* deferred income left = 10,000,000 − (2 × 2,000,000) = ₦6,000,000. Dr Deferred income 6,000,000; Dr Profit or loss 4,000,000; Cr Bank 10,000,000.

Both give the same ₦4,000,000 charge.

### One-minute recap

- Government = local, national or international governments and agencies; a private bank is not government.
- Grant = assistance with a monetary value, a transfer of resources for compliance with conditions; a tax holiday is assistance, not a grant.
- Recognise only when reasonably assured of **both** compliance with conditions and receipt.
- Recognise in profit or loss on a systematic basis, matched with the related costs (income approach); the capital approach (credit equity) is not allowed.
- Asset grants: deduct from cost (depreciate the net amount) or deferred income (depreciate full cost, release grant ÷ life each year); net effect is identical.
- Income grants: other income or deduction from the expense; prorate by each year's cost ÷ total cost × grant.
- Repayment = change in estimate; extra depreciation to profit or loss at once.
- Non-monetary grants at fair value or nominal amount; low-interest loan benefit = proceeds − fair value.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Ilaro Farms Ltd buys a tractor for ₦54,000,000 and receives a government grant of ₦12,000,000 towards it. The tractor is depreciated straight line over 3 years with nil residual value. Show the annual depreciation, the annual grant income and the net charge to profit or loss under each IAS 20 method.

*Solution:*

- Deduct from cost: depreciation = (54,000,000 − 12,000,000) ÷ 3 = ₦14,000,000; no grant income line; net charge **₦14,000,000**.
- Deferred income: depreciation = 54,000,000 ÷ 3 = ₦18,000,000; grant income = 12,000,000 ÷ 3 = ₦4,000,000; net charge = 18,000,000 − 4,000,000 = **₦14,000,000**.
- Same net effect; only the presentation differs.
