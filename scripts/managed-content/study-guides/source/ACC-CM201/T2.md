## Topic 2: IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers

**What you must know:** the objective, background, scope and key terms of IFRS 15; the five-step model (contract, performance obligations, transaction price, allocation, recognition); variable consideration, financing, non-cash consideration and consideration payable to a customer; over time vs point in time; contract costs, principal vs agent and warranties; every computation.

### Lesson 2.1: Objective, background, scope and key terms

**Must know**

- **Objective:** to establish the principles an entity applies to report useful information to users of financial statements about the **nature, amount, timing and uncertainty** of revenue and cash flows arising from a contract with a customer (4 features; "profitability" is **not** one). It promotes **consistency and comparability across industries and capital markets**.
- **Issued:** 28 **May 2014**. **Effective:** **1 January 2018** (annual periods beginning on or after that date).
- **It replaced 6 pronouncements:**

1. IAS 18 Revenue
2. IAS 11 Construction Contracts
3. IFRIC 13 Customer Loyalty Programmes
4. IFRIC 15 Agreements for the Construction of Real Estate
5. IFRIC 18 Transfers of Assets from Customers
6. SIC-31 Revenue: Barter Transactions Involving Advertising Services

- **Core principle:** recognise revenue to depict the transfer of promised goods or services to customers at the amount of consideration the entity expects to be entitled to in exchange. Revenue is recognised **when (or as) control** of the goods or services passes to the customer (not risk only, legal title or physical possession only).
- **Scope:** all contracts with customers **except** (1) leases (IFRS 16); (2) insurance contracts (IFRS 17); (3) financial instruments and other contractual rights or obligations within IFRS 9, IFRS 10, IFRS 11, IAS 27 and IAS 28; (4) non-monetary exchanges between entities in the same line of business to help sales to customers.

**Key terms (IFRS 15 wording)**

- **Contract:** an agreement between two or more parties that creates enforceable rights and obligations.
- **Customer:** a party that has contracted with an entity to obtain goods or services that are an **output of the entity's ordinary activities** in exchange for consideration.
- **Income:** increases in economic benefits during the accounting period in the form of inflows or enhancements of assets or decreases of liabilities that result in an increase in equity, **other than those relating to contributions from equity participants**.
- **Revenue:** income arising in the course of an entity's **ordinary activities**.
- **Performance obligation:** a promise in a contract with a customer to transfer to the customer either (a) a good or service (or a bundle of goods or services) that is **distinct**; or (b) a **series** of distinct goods or services that are substantially the same and that have the same pattern of transfer to the customer.

**The five steps (in order)**

1. Identify the contract(s) with a customer.
2. Identify the performance obligations in the contract.
3. Determine the transaction price.
4. Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations.
5. Recognise revenue when (or as) the entity satisfies a performance obligation.

**Explain more**

- The income definition above is IFRS 15's own. The 2018 Conceptual Framework (Topic 1) words it as increases in assets, or decreases in liabilities, that increase equity, other than contributions from holders of equity claims. Either way, owners' contributions are never income.
- **Income vs revenue:** revenue is the part of income from ordinary activities. For Adaeze Foods Ltd, a Lagos supermarket chain, **sales of groceries** are revenue. Cash paid in by owners, new shares issued and a bank loan are **not** revenue (owners' contributions are excluded from income; a loan is a liability).
- **Traps:** a customer buys the entity's **output**; the buyer of Eko Foods Plc's old delivery van is not a customer, and the gain is not revenue. May 2014 is the **issue** date, 1 January 2018 the **effective** date.

### Lesson 2.2: Steps 1 and 2: the contract and performance obligations

**Must know**

**Step 1: a contract is within IFRS 15 only when all 5 criteria are met:**

1. The parties have **approved** the contract and are committed to perform their obligations.
2. Each party's **rights** regarding the goods or services can be identified.
3. The **payment terms** can be identified.
4. The contract has **commercial substance** (it is expected to change the entity's future cash flows).
5. It is **probable** that the entity will **collect** the consideration to which it will be entitled.

- **Form:** a contract may be **written, oral (non-written) or implied** by the entity's customary business practices. It also covers goods or services the customer reasonably expects because of the entity's practices or statements, even if not written down.
- **If any criterion fails:** an **accounting contract does not exist**, even though a legal contract may exist. The entity must **continue to assess** whether the criteria are subsequently met. Any consideration received is recognised as a **liability** (not revenue) until the criteria are met, or until the amount is non-refundable and either the entity has no remaining obligations and has received substantially all the consideration, or the contract has been terminated.

**Step 2: performance obligations.** At contract inception, assess the promised goods or services and identify each distinct good or service (or bundle), or series, as a performance obligation.

**A good or service is distinct if both conditions are met:**

1. **Capable of being distinct:** the customer can benefit from it **on its own or together with other readily available resources**.
2. **Distinct within the context of the contract:** the promise is **separately identifiable** from other promises in the contract. It is not separately identifiable when it is **integrated with, highly dependent on or highly interrelated with** other goods or services in the contract, or significantly modifies them.

- **Series:** a promise of goods or services that are substantially the same with the same pattern of transfer (e.g. daily cleaning for a year) is **one** performance obligation made up of a series of distinct services.

**Warranties**

| | Assurance-type warranty | Service-type warranty |
|---|---|---|
| What it gives | Assurance the product works as agreed | An extra service beyond that assurance, or customer can buy it separately |
| Performance obligation? | No | Yes, a separate one |
| Accounting | Provision under IAS 37 | Allocate part of the transaction price; recognise over the warranty period |

**Explain more**

- **Collectability (criterion 5):** Okon Plastics Ltd has made goods worth ₦15,000,000 exclusively for a customer restructuring because of liquidity problems, which promises a bank letter of credit not yet finalised. Collection is not yet probable, so no accounting contract exists. Revenue is recognised **only when the letter of credit is finalised (payment becomes probable) and the goods are transferred**: not when the goods are made, not on the customer's promise, and not "never".
- **Payment terms (criterion 3):** On 1 April 2024, Ilupeju Medical Ltd agrees to sell 1,000 machines, but the price depends on a part from another vendor, not yet priced. It receives a non-refundable ₦4,500,000. Payment terms are not identified, so no accounting contract exists:

    Dr Cash ₦4,500,000; Cr Liability (deposit received) ₦4,500,000

    A further ₦1,000,000 before terms are agreed makes the liability ₦5,500,000; revenue stays nil. Non-refundable does not make it revenue, and it never goes to equity.
- **Implied promise:** PhoneHub Ltd, an Ikeja retailer, always fits a free screen protector with every phone. Customers expect it from customary practice, so it is a promise in the contract though the receipt is silent.
- **Software illustration:** ABC Limited supplies a standard software licence, installs it, provides updates and technical support for five years. The licence works without the updates; installation is routine and is also done by several approved third-party installers. Each item benefits the customer and is separately identifiable: **four** performance obligations (licence, installation, updates, support). **Others can perform the installation**, the best evidence it is distinct; one invoice or price does not merge items.
- **Service-type warranty example:** Eko Electronics Plc sells a television with a 2-year extended warranty for ₦330,000. Stand-alone selling prices (SSPs): television ₦300,000; extended warranty ₦60,000 (total ₦360,000). Television = 300,000/360,000 × 330,000 = ₦275,000 (at delivery). Warranty = 60,000/360,000 × 330,000 = ₦55,000, recognised over 2 years = ₦27,500 a year. The basic manufacturer's guarantee that the set works is assurance-type: provide under IAS 37.

### Lesson 2.3: Step 3: determining the transaction price

**Must know**

- **Transaction price:** the amount of consideration to which an entity expects to be entitled in exchange for transferring promised goods or services to a customer, **excluding amounts collected on behalf of third parties** (e.g. VAT and some other sales taxes).
- **Determinants:** the terms of the contract and the entity's **customary business practices**, taking account of returns, bonuses, discounts, rebates and other variable consideration. The entity's **cost** of producing the goods is **not** a determinant.
- **The four things to consider:**

1. **Variable consideration:** consideration that depends on the occurrence or non-occurrence of a future event (volume discounts, rebates, refunds, bonuses, penalties, returns). Estimate it by the **expected value** (sum of probability-weighted amounts; best with many possible outcomes) or the **most likely amount** (best with two outcomes). **Constraint:** include it only to the extent it is **highly probable** that a significant reversal of revenue will not occur. Reassess at each reporting date.
2. **Significant financing component:** adjust for the **time value of money** when the timing of payment gives the customer or the entity a significant financing benefit. Practical expedient: no adjustment if the gap between transfer and payment is **one year or less**.
3. **Non-cash consideration:** measure at **fair value**; if fair value cannot be reasonably estimated, use the SSP of the goods or services promised.
4. **Consideration payable to a customer** (e.g. slotting fees, coupons): **reduce the transaction price**, unless it is payment for a distinct good or service from the customer.

- **Formulas:** Transaction price excluding VAT = VAT-inclusive amount ÷ (1 + VAT rate). Expected value = Σ (amount × probability). Present value = Amount due ÷ (1 + r)^n.

**Explain more**

- **VAT:** an invoice of ₦1,075,000 including VAT at 7.5% gives 1,075,000 ÷ 1.075 = **₦1,000,000**; the ₦75,000 is owed to the tax authority. (Trap: 1,075,000 × 92.5% = ₦994,375 is wrong.)
- **Rebate:** Eko Foods Plc sells 5,000 cartons at ₦2,000 to a distributor, with a 4% rebate it expects to pay. Transaction price = 5,000 × 2,000 × 96% = **₦9,600,000** (the ₦400,000 is a refund liability).
- **Expected value:** Benin Builders Ltd has a ₦10,000,000 fee plus a bonus: nil (20%), ₦500,000 (50%), ₦1,000,000 (30%). Expected bonus = 0 + 250,000 + 300,000 = ₦550,000, so transaction price = ₦10,550,000 (if the constraint is passed). If the bonus were all-or-nothing, use the most likely amount instead.
- **Volume discount (worked fully).** ABC Limited sells to XYZ at ₦200 per unit for 2017, falling to ₦150 per unit **for all units** if XYZ buys more than 1,200 units in the year. At the start ABC does not expect the target to be met.
  1. 30.06.17: 600 × ₦200 = ₦120,000. Dr Trade receivables 120,000; Cr Revenue 120,000.
  2. 01.10.17: 500 more units; ABC now expects the target. 500 × ₦150 = ₦75,000. Dr Trade receivables 75,000; Cr Revenue 75,000.
  3. Catch-up on the first 600 units: 600 × (200 − 150) = ₦30,000 **reduction**. Dr Revenue 30,000; Cr Trade receivables 30,000.
  4. 01.12.17: 200 × ₦150 = ₦30,000. Dr Trade receivables 30,000; Cr Revenue 30,000.
  5. Total = 120,000 + 75,000 − 30,000 + 30,000 = **₦195,000** = 1,300 × ₦150 (check). Net revenue on 01.10.17 = ₦45,000.

  If the target is expected **from the start**, use the lower price from day one (e.g. 1,200 units at ₦800, not ₦1,000 = ₦960,000); if it is **never** expected (and not met), use the full price throughout (700 × ₦5,000 = ₦3,500,000). Estimating a volume discount is part of **Step 3**.
- **Significant financing:** On 1 January 2025, Ikeja Motors Ltd delivers equipment; the customer pays ₦5,000,000 on 31 December 2026; discount rate 10%. Revenue on delivery = 5,000,000 ÷ 1.10² = **₦4,132,231** (nearest naira). Interest income: 2025 = 10% × 4,132,231 = ₦413,223; 2026 = 10% × 4,545,454 = ₦454,546 (rounded so the total reaches ₦5,000,000). Interest is finance income, not sales revenue.
- **Non-cash:** a customer pays with 2,000 bags of cement, fair value ₦5,500 each: revenue = **₦11,000,000**.
- **Consideration payable:** Eko Foods Plc sells ₦20,000,000 of goods to a supermarket and pays it a ₦1,000,000 shelf-placement fee for no distinct service. Revenue = 20,000,000 − 1,000,000 = **₦19,000,000**.

### Lesson 2.4: Step 4: allocating the transaction price

**Must know**

- **Stand-alone selling price (SSP):** the price at which an entity would sell a promised good or service separately to a customer.
- Allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation in proportion to **relative stand-alone selling prices** at contract inception (not costs, order of delivery or equal shares).
- **Formula:** Allocation to an obligation = (SSP of that obligation ÷ Total of all SSPs) × Transaction price.
- **Discount:** Discount = Total SSPs − Transaction price. Allocate it to **all** performance obligations proportionately (on an SSP basis), unless observable evidence shows it relates to specific obligations only.
- **If an SSP is not observable, estimate it**, using:

1. **Adjusted market assessment approach:** what customers in the market would pay.
2. **Expected cost plus a margin approach.**
3. **Residual approach:** total transaction price less observable SSPs of the other items (only where the SSP is highly variable or uncertain).

**Explain more**

- **Two items:** a software licence and 12-month support are sold for ₦1,200,000. SSPs: licence ₦1,000,000; support ₦300,000; total ₦1,300,000.
  - Licence: 1,000,000/1,300,000 × 1,200,000 = **₦923,077**
  - Support: 300,000/1,300,000 × 1,200,000 = **₦276,923**
  - Total ₦1,200,000 (nearest naira). Use fractions, not rounded percentages (76.92% × 1,200,000 = ₦923,040, slightly off).
- **Discount allocation:** a computer (SSP ₦500,000) and printer (SSP ₦300,000) sold for ₦700,000. Discount = 800,000 − 700,000 = **₦100,000**. Computer 5/8 × 700,000 = **₦437,500**; printer 3/8 × 700,000 = **₦262,500**. Discount borne: computer ₦62,500, printer ₦37,500. For one item: share of discount = (its SSP ÷ total SSPs) × discount, e.g. earbuds (SSP ₦100,000) in a ₦500,000 bundle sold for ₦450,000 take 1/5 × 50,000 = ₦10,000.
- **Three items:** Lagos Smart Homes Ltd sells CCTV, installation and one year's maintenance for ₦2,700,000. SSPs: CCTV ₦2,000,000; installation ₦400,000; maintenance ₦600,000; total ₦3,000,000. Ratio = 2,700,000/3,000,000 = 0.9: CCTV ₦1,800,000; installation ₦360,000; maintenance ₦540,000. Each item's percentage of the price equals its SSP share (CCTV 2/3 = 66.67%).
- **Estimating SSP:** a support service costs ₦400,000 to provide and the entity's normal margin is 25% on cost: SSP = 400,000 × 1.25 = ₦500,000.
- **Traps:** do not give items their full SSP and the leftover to one item (residual approach, limited cases only), or the discount to the last-delivered or dearest item.

### Lesson 2.5: Step 5: recognising revenue (point in time or over time)

**Must know**

- Recognise revenue **when (or as)** a performance obligation is satisfied by transferring control of a promised good or service.
- **Control:** the ability to **direct the use of, and obtain substantially all of the remaining benefits from**, the asset.
- **Over time** if **any one** of 3 criteria is met; otherwise **at a point in time**:

1. The customer **simultaneously receives and consumes** the benefits as the entity performs (e.g. cleaning, security).
2. The entity's performance **creates or enhances an asset that the customer controls** as it is created or enhanced (e.g. building on the customer's land).
3. The entity's performance **does not create an asset with an alternative use** to the entity **and** the entity has an **enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date** (both parts needed).

- **5 indicators that control has passed at a point in time:**

1. The entity has a **present right to payment**.
2. The customer has **legal title**.
3. The entity has transferred **physical possession**.
4. The customer has the **significant risks and rewards** of ownership.
5. The customer has **accepted** the asset.

- **Typical pattern:** goods at a point in time; services over time.
- **Over time:** select a **measure of progress** towards complete satisfaction. **Output methods** (surveys of work done, milestones, units delivered, time elapsed) or **input methods** (costs incurred, labour hours).
- **Formula (cost-to-cost input method):** Revenue to date = (Costs incurred to date ÷ Total expected costs) × Transaction price. Revenue this year = Revenue to date − Revenue recognised in earlier years.

**Explain more**

- **Point in time:** a customer pays for a generator in Alaba Market and carries it home: revenue at the point of sale (control passes), not on first use or over its warranty or life.
- **Over time:** GuardSafe Ltd guards a Lekki estate for 12 months (criterion 1); BuildRight Ltd builds on its customer's land in Abuja (criterion 2); Ekiti Fabrication Ltd makes a machine to a unique design with an enforceable right to payment for work done (criterion 3).
- **Trap:** "simultaneously receives and consumes" is an **over-time** criterion, not a point-in-time indicator; "legal title" is a point-in-time indicator, not a contract criterion or distinct test.
- **Time-based (straight line):** On 1 April 2025, Ibadan Cleaners Ltd signs a 12-month contract for ₦1,200,000. Year to 31 December 2025: 1,200,000 × 9/12 = **₦900,000**; ₦300,000 next year. Similarly ₦900,000 of support spread evenly over 5 years gives ₦180,000 a year.
- **Cost-to-cost:** Delta Roads Ltd: price ₦80,000,000; total expected costs ₦60,000,000.
  - First year: costs to date ₦15,000,000: 15/60 = 25%; revenue = ₦20,000,000.
  - Second year: costs to date ₦42,000,000 (estimate unchanged): 42/60 = 70%; cumulative revenue ₦56,000,000; second-year revenue = 56,000,000 − 20,000,000 = **₦36,000,000**.
- **Steps 4 and 5 together:** On 1 October 2025, a telecom company sells a phone plus 12 months of data for ₦360,000. SSPs: phone ₦250,000, data ₦200,000 (total ₦450,000). Phone = 250/450 × 360,000 = ₦200,000 (point in time, 1 October). Data = ₦160,000, earned evenly: 160,000 × 3/12 = ₦40,000. Revenue to 31 December 2025 = **₦240,000**; the other ₦120,000 is a contract liability (deferred revenue).

### Lesson 2.6: Contract costs and principal vs agent

**Must know**

- **Incremental costs of obtaining a contract** (costs the entity would not have incurred without the contract, e.g. a sales commission): recognise as an **asset** if the entity expects to recover them. Practical expedient: **expense** them if the amortisation period would be **one year or less**.
- Costs incurred **whether or not** the contract is won (bid costs, legal fees, travel) are **expensed**, unless explicitly chargeable to the customer.
- **Costs to fulfil a contract** (not covered by IAS 2, IAS 16 or IAS 38): an asset only if all 3 apply: (1) they relate directly to a contract; (2) they generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations; (3) they are expected to be recovered. General and administrative costs and wasted materials are expensed.
- Contract cost assets are **amortised** on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services, and tested for **impairment**.
- **Principal:** controls the good or service **before** it is transferred to the customer; recognises revenue **gross** (full amount). **Agent:** arranges for another party to provide it; recognises revenue **net** (its fee or commission).
- **Indicators of a principal:** (1) primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise; (2) bears **inventory risk**; (3) has **discretion in setting the price**.

**Explain more**

- **Commission:** Abuja Data Ltd pays its salesperson ₦600,000 commission for winning a 3-year service contract. Asset ₦600,000; amortise 600,000 ÷ 3 = **₦200,000** a year. The ₦150,000 legal fees on the bid, paid whether it won or not, are expensed.
- **Agent vs principal:** Oja Online Ltd sells a ₦50,000 blender on its website for a vendor who ships it, sets the price and bears inventory risk; Oja keeps 10%. Oja is an agent: revenue = **₦5,000**. Had Oja bought and held the blenders and set the price, it would be principal: revenue ₦50,000, cost of sales ₦45,000.

### One-minute recap

- IFRS 15: issued 28 May 2014, effective 1 January 2018; replaced IAS 18, IAS 11, IFRIC 13, 15, 18 and SIC-31.
- Revenue = income from ordinary activities; recognised when (or as) **control** passes.
- Five steps: contract, performance obligations, transaction price, allocate, recognise.
- Contract: 5 criteria; fail one and money received is a liability while you keep reassessing.
- Distinct = capable of being distinct + separately identifiable; a series counts as one obligation.
- Transaction price excludes VAT; covers variable consideration (expected value or most likely amount, constrained), financing, non-cash, consideration payable.
- Allocate on relative SSPs; spread any discount over all obligations; estimate SSPs not observable.
- Over time (3 criteria) with a measure of progress; otherwise point in time (5 indicators).
- Commissions capitalised (expedient if 1 year or less); principal gross, agent net; service-type warranty is a separate obligation.

### Theory practice

**Question:** On 1 July 2025, Ikeja Solar Ltd sells a solar system for ₦4,500,000, covering equipment, installation and 12 months' maintenance. SSPs: equipment ₦3,600,000; installation ₦600,000; maintenance ₦800,000. Equipment is delivered and installed in July 2025; maintenance is provided evenly. Calculate revenue for the year ended 31 December 2025.

*Solution:*
1. Total SSPs = 3,600,000 + 600,000 + 800,000 = ₦5,000,000. Ratio = 4,500,000 ÷ 5,000,000 = 0.9 (discount ₦500,000 spread over all three).
2. Allocation: equipment ₦3,240,000; installation ₦540,000; maintenance ₦720,000 (total ₦4,500,000).
3. Equipment and installation are satisfied in July: ₦3,240,000 + ₦540,000 = ₦3,780,000.
4. Maintenance over time: 720,000 × 6/12 = ₦360,000.
5. Revenue for 2025 = 3,780,000 + 360,000 = **₦4,140,000**. Contract liability at 31 December 2025 = ₦360,000.
