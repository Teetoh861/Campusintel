## Topic 5: IAS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors

**What you must know:** aim and definitions; selecting policies (the hierarchy) and consistency; changes in policy (retrospective); changes in estimates (prospective); prior period errors (retrospective restatement); impracticability; disclosures; computations.

### Lesson 5.1: Aim, scope and key definitions

**Must know**

- **Aim (objective):** to prescribe the criteria for selecting and changing accounting policies, and the treatment and disclosure of changes in policies, changes in estimates and corrections of errors, to **enhance the relevance and reliability** of financial statements and their **comparability** with previous periods and with other entities.
- **3 areas:** (1) changes in accounting policies; (2) changes in accounting estimates; (3) errors. Events after the reporting period are **not** one of them (that is IAS 10).
- **Accounting policies:** the specific principles, bases, conventions, rules and practices applied by an entity in preparing and presenting financial statements.
- **Accounting estimates:** monetary amounts in financial statements that are subject to measurement uncertainty (e.g. useful lives, residual values, allowance for doubtful debts, inventory obsolescence).
- **Prior period errors:** omissions from, and misstatements in, the financial statements for one or more prior periods arising from a failure to use, or misuse of, reliable information that (a) was available when those statements were authorised for issue and (b) could reasonably be expected to have been obtained and taken into account.
- **Retrospective application:** applying a new accounting policy as if it had always been applied.
- **Retrospective restatement:** correcting amounts as if a prior period error had never occurred.
- **Prospective application:** applying the change to current and future periods only.
- **Impracticable:** the entity cannot apply a requirement after making every reasonable effort to do so.
- **Material:** information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that primary users make on the basis of those financial statements. Materiality is judged by the **size or nature** of the item, or both, in the surrounding circumstances.

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Change in accounting policy | Retrospective application |
| Change in accounting estimate | Prospective |
| Prior period error | Retrospective restatement |

**Explain more**

- Watch the two "retrospective" words: *application* goes with a **policy**, *restatement* goes with an **error**. Options swap them.
- A policy is the **rule** ("inventory at weighted average cost"); an estimate is the **amount** the rule needs under uncertainty ("this lorry lasts 8 years"). An option calling policies monetary amounts and estimates rules has them backwards.
- Older wording defines a *change in accounting estimate* as an adjustment of the carrying amount of an asset or liability, or of an asset's periodic consumption, from assessing their present status and expected future benefits and obligations. The treatment is the same.

### Lesson 5.2: Selecting and applying accounting policies

**Must know**

- **Where an IFRS applies:** the policy for that item must be determined by applying that Standard or Interpretation and any relevant implementation guidance. Policies need not be applied where the effect is immaterial.
- **Where no IFRS applies:** management uses its judgement to develop and apply a policy that gives information that is:
  1. **relevant** to users' economic decision-making needs; and
  2. **reliable**, in that the financial statements:
     1. represent faithfully the financial position, financial performance and cash flows;
     2. reflect the **economic substance** of transactions, not merely their legal form;
     3. are **neutral** (free from bias);
     4. are **prudent**;
     5. are **complete** in all material respects.
- **The hierarchy (order management refers to):**
  1. **First:** requirements in IFRSs dealing with **similar and related issues**;
  2. **Then:** the definitions, recognition criteria and measurement concepts for assets, liabilities, income and expenses in the **Conceptual Framework**;
  3. **May also consider:** recent pronouncements of other standard-setting bodies using a similar framework, other accounting literature and accepted industry practices, so far as they do not conflict with 1 and 2.
- **Consistency:** apply consistent policies for similar transactions, other events and conditions, **unless** an IFRS specifically requires or permits categorisation of items for which different policies may be appropriate.
- It is **inappropriate** to make, or leave uncorrected, immaterial departures from IFRSs to achieve a particular presentation of financial position, performance or cash flows.

**Explain more**

- Kano Agro Ltd meets a transaction no IFRS covers. It must not omit it, copy the tax treatment, or pick "any policy and disclose it"; it uses judgement, working down the hierarchy.
- Traps: the Conceptual Framework is **second**, after similar IFRSs; industry practice is optional support; reflecting **legal form rather than substance** is not reliable.
- IAS 16 lets land and buildings be revalued while vehicles stay at cost: permitted categorisation, not inconsistency.

### Lesson 5.3: Changes in accounting policy

**Must know**

- Frequent changes are undesirable because they make comparison with previous periods harder. An entity changes a policy **only if** the change:
  1. is **required by an IFRS**; or
  2. results in financial statements providing **reliable and more relevant** information.
- A change in policy is a change in the **recognition**, **measurement** or **presentation** basis:
  - **Recognition:** e.g. capitalising expenditure previously written off.
  - **Measurement:** e.g. cost model to fair value model for investment property; FIFO to weighted average.
  - **Presentation:** e.g. reclassifying costs from administrative expenses to cost of sales.
- **Not a change in policy (2):** (1) applying a policy to transactions that **differ in substance** from those previously occurring; (2) applying a new policy to transactions that **did not occur before** or were immaterial.
- **Initial revaluation** under IAS 16 or IAS 38 **is** a change in policy, but it is accounted for under IAS 16/IAS 38 (as a revaluation), **not** retrospectively under IAS 8.
- **How to apply:** follow the IFRS's specific **transitional provisions**. If there are none, or the change is voluntary, apply it **retrospectively**: adjust the **opening balance of each affected component of equity for the earliest prior period presented**, and the other comparative amounts, as if the new policy had always been applied.
- IAS 1 then requires a **third statement of financial position** at the **beginning of the earliest comparative period** (where the effect on it is material).
- **Impracticable for a prior period** when (3): (a) the effects are not determinable; (b) it needs assumptions about management's intent in that period; (c) it needs significant estimates that cannot be objectively separated from information that came later. If so, apply from the **earliest date that is practicable**.

**Disclosures**

| Disclosure | Required by IFRS | Voluntary |
|---|---|---|
| Title of the Standard or Interpretation | Yes | |
| Nature of the change | Yes | Yes |
| Description of transitional provisions | Yes | |
| Reason the new policy gives reliable and more relevant information | | Yes |
| Adjustment to each line item (and EPS) for current and prior periods, so far as practicable | Yes | Yes |
| Adjustment relating to periods before those presented, so far as practicable | Yes | Yes |
| If retrospective application is impracticable: why, and how and from when applied | Yes | Yes |

- **New IFRS issued but not yet effective:** IAS 8 **requires** disclosure of that fact and of known or reasonably estimable information on its likely impact.

**Explain more**

- Ibadan Foods Plc changing policy only to hit a profit bonus: **not permitted**. Shareholder, auditor or accountant approval is not a ground either.
- Enugu Steel Ltd buys its **first** investment property and picks the fair value model: not a change in policy. Nor is capitalising development costs because a project **becomes eligible for the first time**.
- Jos Textiles Plc, year ended 31 December 2025, one year of comparatives, retrospective change: statements of financial position at **31 December 2025, 31 December 2024 and 1 January 2024**.

**Worked example 1 (cost model to fair value model).** Eko Holdings Plc bought an investment property for ₦60,000,000 on 1 January 2024 and used the cost model, depreciating ₦1,500,000 a year. In 2026 it voluntarily changes to the fair value model, presenting 2025 comparatives. Fair value: ₦66,000,000 at 31 December 2024; ₦70,000,000 at 31 December 2025. Ignore tax.

- Cost-model carrying amount at 31 December 2024 = ₦60,000,000 − ₦1,500,000 = ₦58,500,000.
- Adjustment to retained earnings at 1 January 2025 = ₦66,000,000 − ₦58,500,000 = **increase of ₦7,500,000** (Dr Investment property; Cr Retained earnings b/f).
- Restated 2025 profit: remove depreciation ₦1,500,000; add fair value gain ₦70,000,000 − ₦66,000,000 = ₦4,000,000. Profit is **₦5,500,000 higher**.

**Worked example 2 (FIFO to weighted average).** Ilupeju Chemicals Ltd changes from FIFO to weighted average in 2026, with 2025 comparatives. Inventory at 31 December 2024: FIFO ₦12,000,000, weighted average ₦11,000,000. At 31 December 2025: FIFO ₦15,000,000, weighted average ₦13,200,000. 2025 profit under FIFO was ₦30,000,000. Ignore tax.

- Adjustment to retained earnings at 1 January 2025 = ₦11,000,000 − ₦12,000,000 = **decrease of ₦1,000,000**.
- 2025: lower opening inventory reduces cost of sales (+₦1,000,000); lower closing inventory increases cost of sales (−₦1,800,000). Net −₦800,000.
- Restated 2025 profit = ₦30,000,000 + ₦1,000,000 − ₦1,800,000 = **₦29,200,000**.

Rule to remember: closing inventory down = profit down; opening inventory down = profit up.

### Lesson 5.4: Changes in accounting estimates

**Must know**

- An estimate is needed when an item **cannot be measured with precision** and there is some uncertainty. Reasonable estimates do **not** undermine reliability.
- **Examples:** inventory obsolescence; fair value of financial assets or liabilities; useful lives of non-current assets; residual values; the depreciation method (pattern of consumption); allowance for doubtful debts; warranty obligations.
- A change in estimate results from **new information or new developments**, so it is **not** the correction of an error.
- A change in **measurement basis** (e.g. cost model to revaluation or fair value model) is a change in **policy**, not estimate.
- A change in **depreciation method** (straight line to reducing balance) is a change in **estimate**.
- If it is **difficult to distinguish** a policy change from an estimate change, treat it as a change in **estimate**.
- **Prospective recognition** in profit or loss of:
  1. the **period of change only**, if it affects that period only; or
  2. the **period of change and future periods**, if it affects both.
- Where it changes assets or liabilities, adjust their **carrying amount in the period of change**.
- **Formula:** Revised depreciation = (Carrying amount at date of change − Revised residual value) ÷ Remaining useful life.
- **Disclose:** the nature and amount of the change affecting the current period or expected to affect future periods (or that the future effect is impracticable to estimate). No IFRS title, "more relevant" reason or restated comparatives.

**Explain more**

- Owerri Bottling Ltd switches vans from straight line to reducing balance: **estimate**, prospective. An entity **can** change method.
- Moving where depreciation is **presented** in profit or loss is a **presentation** change: a **policy**, not estimate.
- Ilorin Cement Plc cuts a kiln's life from 15 to 10 years on new wear information: estimate, not error.
- Kaduna Motors Ltd revises an estimate affecting only this year: current period only.

**Worked example 3 (useful life).** Ikorodu Plastics Ltd bought a machine for ₦24,000,000, straight line, 10 years, nil residual value. At the start of Year 5 (after 4 years) the **remaining** life is revised to 3 years.

- Old charge = ₦24,000,000 ÷ 10 = ₦2,400,000. Carrying amount after 4 years = ₦24,000,000 − (4 × ₦2,400,000) = ₦14,400,000.
- Year 5 charge = ₦14,400,000 ÷ 3 = **₦4,800,000**; Year 5 profit is ₦2,400,000 lower than under the old estimate.
- Carrying amount at end of Year 5 = ₦14,400,000 − ₦4,800,000 = **₦9,600,000**.
- Trap: if the **total** life is revised to 8 years, remaining life = 8 − 4 = 4, charge = ₦14,400,000 ÷ 4 = ₦3,600,000. Convert total life to remaining life; never re-spread cost from the start.

**Worked example 4 (residual value).** Apapa Shipping Ltd's crane cost ₦40,000,000, residual value ₦4,000,000, 9 years straight line. At the start of Year 4 the residual value is revised to ₦10,000,000; total life unchanged.

- Old charge = (₦40,000,000 − ₦4,000,000) ÷ 9 = ₦4,000,000. Carrying amount after 3 years = ₦40,000,000 − ₦12,000,000 = ₦28,000,000.
- Year 4 charge = (₦28,000,000 − ₦10,000,000) ÷ 6 = **₦3,000,000**.

**Worked example 5 (method).** Oshodi Couriers Ltd's van cost ₦20,000,000, straight line over 5 years, nil residual. At the start of Year 2 it changes to reducing balance at 30%. Carrying amount = ₦20,000,000 − ₦4,000,000 = ₦16,000,000; Year 2 charge = 30% × ₦16,000,000 = **₦4,800,000**. No restatement of Year 1.

### Lesson 5.5: Prior period errors

**Must know**

- **Errors include (5):** mathematical mistakes; mistakes in applying accounting policies; oversights; misinterpretations of facts; fraud.
- **Material prior period errors** are corrected **retrospectively** in the first set of financial statements authorised for issue after their discovery, by:
  1. restating the **comparative amounts** for the prior period(s) in which the error occurred; or
  2. if the error occurred before the earliest prior period presented, restating the **opening balances** of assets, liabilities and equity for the earliest prior period presented.
- The correction is **excluded from profit or loss of the period of discovery**.
- If impracticable, restate from the earliest date practicable (tests as in Lesson 5.3).
- Errors found **before** the statements are authorised for issue are simply corrected in those statements.
- **Disclose:** (1) nature of the error; (2) for each prior period presented, so far as practicable, the correction to each line item (and basic and diluted EPS); (3) the correction at the beginning of the earliest prior period presented; (4) if impracticable, the circumstances and how and from when corrected. These need not be repeated in later periods.

**Explain more**

- Aba Leather Ltd's 2024 depreciation was understated by a clerk's keying mistake with the information available: **error** (contrast Ilorin Cement's new information: estimate).
- Timing trap: 2017 depreciation found overstated on 25 March 2018. Statements authorised 1 April 2018: **correct the 2017 statements before issue**. Authorised 1 March 2018: **restate 2017 in the 2018 comparatives**. Never just "reduce 2018 depreciation".

**Worked example 6.** Preparing its 2026 statements, Mushin Distributors Ltd finds three material 2025 errors. Originally reported: 2025 profit ₦18,000,000; retained earnings at 31 December 2025 ₦40,000,000. Ignore tax; treat each error separately.

- (a) Closing inventory at 31 December 2025 overstated by ₦2,000,000 (counting mistake), so cost of sales was understated: restated 2025 profit = ₦18,000,000 − ₦2,000,000 = **₦16,000,000**.
- (b) Repairs of ₦2,500,000 wrongly capitalised as PPE in 2025 (ignore depreciation). Restated retained earnings at 1 January 2026 = ₦40,000,000 − ₦2,500,000 = **₦37,500,000** (Dr Retained earnings b/f ₦2,500,000; Cr PPE ₦2,500,000).
- (c) Depreciation of ₦1,200,000 omitted from 2025. Restate 2025 profit to ₦18,000,000 − ₦1,200,000 = **₦16,800,000**; **no** charge in 2026 profit or loss.

### One-minute recap

- IAS 8 aims at relevance, reliability and comparability; it covers policies, estimates and errors.
- Policy = principles, bases, conventions, rules and practices; estimate = monetary amount subject to measurement uncertainty.
- No IFRS? Judgement: relevant and reliable; look first to similar IFRSs, then the Conceptual Framework.
- Change a policy only if required by IFRS or reliable and more relevant; never to raise profit.
- Policy change: transitional provisions, else retrospective; adjust opening equity of the earliest period presented; third statement of financial position. First-time revaluation follows IAS 16/IAS 38.
- Estimate change (life, residual value, depreciation method): prospective; charge = (carrying amount − residual) ÷ remaining life.
- Doubtful? Treat as estimate. Measurement-basis change = policy.
- Material prior period error: retrospective restatement of comparatives, not through current profit.
- Impracticable = cannot after every reasonable effort; then apply from the earliest practicable date.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Kogi Power Ltd's generator cost ₦36,000,000, residual value ₦4,000,000, straight line over 8 years. At the start of Year 4 the remaining life is revised to 4 years and the residual value to ₦2,000,000. Classify the change, and compute the Year 4 charge and its effect on profit.

*Solution:*

1. Revised life and residual value are **changes in accounting estimate**, applied **prospectively**: Years 1 to 3 are not restated.
2. Old charge = (₦36,000,000 − ₦4,000,000) ÷ 8 = ₦4,000,000.
3. Carrying amount at start of Year 4 = ₦36,000,000 − (3 × ₦4,000,000) = ₦24,000,000.
4. Year 4 charge = (₦24,000,000 − ₦2,000,000) ÷ 4 = **₦5,500,000**.
5. Year 4 profit is ₦5,500,000 − ₦4,000,000 = **₦1,500,000 lower** than under the old estimate. Disclose the nature and amount of the change.
