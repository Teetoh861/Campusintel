## Topic 8: Bills of Exchange and Cheques

**What you must know:** negotiable instruments; bills of exchange, promissory notes and IOUs; acceptance, maturity, days of grace and discounting; holders, endorsement and dishonour; cheques, their parties, types and crossings; when a bank must not pay; and cheque clearing in Nigeria.

### Lesson 8.1: Negotiable instruments, bills, notes and IOUs

**Must know**

- **Law:** the Bills of Exchange Act (Cap B8, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004), modelled on the English Bills of Exchange Act 1882.
- **Negotiable instrument:** a written document that promises or orders payment of a sum of money, is transferable by delivery or by endorsement and delivery, and gives the holder in due course a good title free from defects in the title of prior parties.
- **6 characteristics:** (1) writing, (2) signature, (3) unconditional promise or order, (4) fixed sum (sum certain in money), (5) payable on demand or at a fixed or determinable future time, (6) transferability.
- **Bill of exchange:** an unconditional order in writing, addressed by one person to another, signed by the person giving it, requiring the person to whom it is addressed to pay on demand or at a fixed or determinable future time a sum certain in money to or to the order of a specified person or to bearer.
- **Cheque:** a bill of exchange drawn on a banker payable on demand.
- **Promissory note:** an unconditional promise in writing made by one person to another, signed by the maker, engaging to pay on demand or at a fixed or determinable future time a sum certain in money to, or to the order of, a specified person or to bearer.
- **IOU ("I owe you"):** an informal document acknowledging a debt owed by one person to another. It is **not** a negotiable instrument: it cannot be assigned, transferred or sold.

**Parties**

| Instrument | Parties |
|---|---|
| Bill of exchange | **Drawer** (gives the order), **drawee** (ordered to pay; becomes **acceptor** once he accepts), **payee** (to be paid) |
| Cheque | Drawer (customer who writes it), drawee (always a **bank**), payee |
| Promissory note | **Maker** (signs and promises to pay), payee: only 2 parties |

**Bill versus promissory note**

| Bill of exchange | Promissory note |
|---|---|
| An **order** to pay | A **promise** to pay |
| 3 parties | 2 parties |
| A time bill needs acceptance | No acceptance |
| Drawer liable only if the drawee fails to pay | Maker is primarily liable |

**Explain more**

- Mr Bello, who banks with Zenith Bank, writes a ₦250,000 cheque to Mrs Adeyemi: Bello = drawer, **Zenith Bank = drawee**, Adeyemi = payee. The Central Bank of Nigeria is never the drawee of an ordinary customer's cheque.
- Trap: a bill or a note must be in writing, so "need not be in writing" is always false. A promissory note can be payable on demand.
- **Contents of an IOU (5):** parties' details; payment information (amount, due date, interest, method); signatures of both parties; termination date; involvement of a legal practitioner.

### Lesson 8.2: Bills in operation: acceptance, maturity, days of grace and discounting

**Must know**

- **Acceptance:** the signification by the drawee of his assent to the order of the drawer. It must be written on the bill and signed by the drawee. After accepting he is the **acceptor** and is primarily liable.
- **General acceptance:** assents without qualification. **Qualified acceptance:** varies the bill's effect, e.g. **partial** ("Accepted for ₦300,000 only" on a ₦500,000 bill), **conditional**, **local** (one place only), qualified as to **time**.
- **When payable:**
    1. **On demand (sight bill):** expressed "on demand", "at sight" or "on presentation", or no time stated. A cheque is always on demand.
    2. **Usance (time) bill:** "X days after date", "X days after sight", or after an event **certain** to happen (e.g. someone's death).
- **Presentment for acceptance is necessary** for a bill payable **after sight** (to fix maturity), a bill that says so, and a bill payable away from the drawee's residence or place of business.
- **Days of grace:** **3 days** are added to a bill not payable on demand, unless the bill says otherwise. Cheques and demand bills get none.
- **Counting:** exclude the day of the date (or of sight), include the day of payment.
- **Inland bill:** drawn and payable within Nigeria (or drawn in Nigeria on a resident). **Foreign bill:** any other.
- **Accommodation party:** signs as drawer, acceptor or endorser **without receiving value**, to lend his name to another.
- **Discounting:** a bank buys an **accepted usance bill** before maturity for less than its face value.
    - Discount = Face value x Rate x Days / 365
    - Proceeds = Face value - Discount

**Explain more**

- **Contingency trap:** "30 days after the death of Chief Ade" is valid (death is certain; only its date is unknown). "On the day she gets married" is **not** a bill: marriage may never happen. Any condition ("provided the receipt is signed") also destroys a bill or cheque.
- "Ninety days after sight pay Chika Nwosu or order ₦1,200,000" is a **usance bill** and must be presented for acceptance to fix its maturity. A bill drawn in Accra and payable in Lagos is a **foreign bill**.

**Worked example 1 (maturity):** bill dated 15 January, payable 60 days after date (not a leap year).
1. Exclude 15 January. Days left in January: 31 - 15 = 16.
2. February: 28 more = 44. March: 60 - 44 = 16, so 16 March.
3. Add 3 days of grace: due **19 March**.

Same method: dated 1 March, 30 days after date: 30 days to 31 March, plus 3 = **3 April**.

**Worked example 2 (discounting):** Mrs Okon's accepted bill of ₦800,000 matures in 73 days; her bank discounts at 10% a year (365-day year).
1. Discount = 800,000 x 0.10 x 73/365 = ₦16,000.
2. Proceeds = 800,000 - 16,000 = **₦784,000**.

Another: ₦1,460,000 at 15% for 60 days: discount = 1,460,000 x 0.15 x 60/365 = ₦36,000; proceeds = **₦1,424,000**. The bank collects the full face value at maturity; the difference is its income. It discounts only for credible customers, with at least two parties (e.g. acceptor and customer) of good standing. Cheques are not discounted: they are already payable on demand.

### Lesson 8.3: Holders, endorsement and dishonour

**Must know**

- **Holder:** the payee or endorsee who is in possession of the bill, or the bearer of it.
- **Holder in due course:** a holder who took the bill:
    1. complete and regular on the face of it;
    2. before it was overdue, and without notice of any previous dishonour;
    3. in good faith and for value;
    4. without notice of any defect in the title of the person who negotiated it.
- He need **not** be the original payee. **Advantage:** he takes a good title free from defects in the title of prior parties.
- **Endorsement:** the signature of the holder on the instrument (usually on the back) made to transfer it. **Delivery** is transfer of possession; endorsement plus delivery transfers an order instrument; a bearer instrument passes by delivery alone.
- **Valid endorsement:** written on the bill itself and signed by the endorser; it must cover the **whole** bill (no partial endorsement).
- **Types of endorsement:**
    1. **Blank:** signature only; makes the instrument payable to **bearer**.
    2. **Special:** names the endorsee ("Pay Chinedu Okeke or order").
    3. **Restrictive:** stops further negotiation ("Pay Musa Ibrahim only").
    4. **Conditional:** adds a condition, which the payer may disregard.
- **Dishonour** happens in 2 ways: by **non-acceptance** and by **non-payment**.
- **Notice of dishonour:** given to the drawer and endorsers to **preserve the holder's right of recourse** against them.
- **Noting:** a notary public's minute on the bill. **Protest:** the formal notarial certificate attesting dishonour.
- **Foreign bill** dishonoured: protest is **compulsory**, or the drawer and endorsers are **discharged**. **Inland bill:** noting or protest is **optional**.

**Explain more**

- **Fictitious or non-existing payee:** the bill may be treated as payable to bearer.
- **Bill prohibiting transfer** ("Pay Ade only"): valid between the parties but not negotiable.
- **Material alteration** (date, sum, time or place of payment) without the assent of all parties liable: the bill is **avoided**, except against a party who made, authorised or assented to it, and later endorsers.
- **Liability:** the acceptor is primarily liable; the drawer and endorsers are liable if it is dishonoured. Until a cheque is endorsed, the **drawer** alone is liable on it: the drawee bank never accepts a cheque, so it owes nothing to the holder (it answers to its customer under their contract).
- **Thief and holder in due course:** a thief steals an uncrossed bearer cheque and passes it to Ada for goods; she takes it in good faith, for value, before overdue, without notice, complete and regular. Ada is a **holder in due course with a good title**. If the cheque was crossed **"not negotiable"**, Ada gets **no better title than the thief had**.
- Trap: noting, protest, crossing and countermand are not ways of dishonour.

### Lesson 8.4: Cheques: features, types, crossings and advantages

**Must know**

- **Characteristics of a cheque (7):** (1) in writing; (2) unconditional order; (3) drawn on a specified banker; (4) signed by the drawer; (5) payable on demand; (6) sum certain in money (amount specified); (7) payable to a specified person or order, or to bearer. It needs **no acceptance**.
- **Face of a completed cheque:** bank and branch, date, payee, amount in words and in figures, drawer's signature, cheque number and MICR code line. There is no acceptance signature.

**Cheque versus time bill**

| Cheque | Bill of exchange |
|---|---|
| Drawee is always a banker | Drawee may be any person, including a banker |
| Always payable on demand | On demand or at a future time |
| No acceptance | Time bill is presented for acceptance |
| No days of grace | 3 days of grace on time bills |
| Can be crossed | Not crossed |

**Similarities:** both are negotiable instruments, unconditional written orders, with three parties.

**Types of cheque**

1. **Open:** not crossed; can be cashed over the counter of the drawee bank.
2. **Crossed:** two parallel transverse lines across the face; payable only through a bank account.
3. **Bearer:** payable to whoever presents it, without endorsement.
4. **Order:** payable to the named payee or as he directs by endorsement.
5. **Stale:** in circulation unreasonably long; Nigerian banks refuse a cheque more than **6 months** old.
6. **Post-dated:** bears a future date; not paid before that date.
7. **Undated:** not invalid in law, but bankers by custom refuse it until dated.
8. **Certified:** marked by the drawee bank to confirm the drawer has sufficient funds.

**Crossings**

1. **General:** two parallel lines, with or without "& Co"; pay only to a banker.
2. **Special:** a bank's name between the lines (e.g. "Guaranty Trust Bank Plc"); pay **only to that bank**.
3. **"Not negotiable":** a transferee gets no better title than his transferor.
4. **"Account payee only":** a direction to the **collecting** bank to credit the proceeds only to the payee's account.

- **Who may cross:** the **drawer or a holder**; a banker may also cross for collection. A crossing is a **material part** of the cheque and may not be unlawfully obliterated or altered.

**Explain more**

- **Benefits of crossing:** payment goes through a bank account; protects the owner against theft; helps trace who received payment. It does **not** let the payee cash faster over the counter; it stops that.
- **Advantages of cheques:** economise currency notes and coins; safety (no carrying cash); proof of payment; payment can be stopped against fraud; convenient for large and distant payments. A cheque does **not** guarantee the drawer has funds; it can bounce. Issuing a cheque that bounces for lack of funds is a criminal offence under the Dishonoured Cheques (Offences) Act (Cap D11, LFN 2004).
- **Precautions in drawing a cheque:** write the amount in words and figures, and make them agree; leave no spaces; sign every alteration in full; date it; fill in the payee (never leave it blank); cross cheques sent by post; keep the cheque book safe.
- Ngozi's cheque dated 2 January, presented 10 August: over 6 months, **refused as stale**. A cheque dated 30 November presented on 15 November: **post-dated**, refused until 30 November.

### Lesson 8.5: Paying, collecting and clearing cheques

**Must know**

**When the bank's authority to pay ends (or it must not pay):**

1. Insufficient funds (no balance or overdraft limit to cover it).
2. **Countermand:** in writing, clear, signed in line with the mandate, **before** the cheque is paid.
3. Notice of the customer's **death** (express or constructive).
4. Suspicion that the cheque was lost or stolen (investigate first).
5. Assignment of the credit balance to a third party.
6. Court injunction or **garnishee order** (a court order attaching the balance to satisfy a judgment debt).
7. Notice of a **bankruptcy petition** (or a company's winding-up).
8. **Forgery** of the drawer's signature.
9. Irregularly drawn cheque (e.g. words and figures differ).
10. Presentation outside banking hours or at the wrong branch.
11. A lawful order freezing the account.

- **Forgery:** the cheque is wholly inoperative; the bank has **no mandate** and cannot debit the customer.
- **Wrongful dishonour:** the bank is liable to the customer in **damages for breach of contract**.
- **Paying banker protection:** paying a crossed cheque according to the crossing, **in good faith and without negligence**.
- **Collecting banker protection:** receiving payment for a **customer** in good faith and without negligence. Collecting for a non-customer, or without identity checks, loses protection; the bank becomes liable to the true owner.
- **Clearing:** the procedure by which a bank collects the proceeds of a cheque drawn on another bank for its own customer. An **in-house (on-us)** cheque, drawn on and paid into the same bank, needs no interbank clearing.
- **MICR:** Magnetic Ink Character Recognition.

**Explain more**

- A new cheque book does **not** end authority to pay. Notice of death on Monday: a cheque presented Tuesday must **not** be paid, even if signed earlier. Proper return reasons: "insufficient funds", "refer to drawer", "payment stopped", "account closed"; a large amount or the payee banking elsewhere is no reason.
- **Before paying cash, a cashier checks:** regular and complete; drawn on that branch; not crossed; not post-dated; not stale; not stopped; properly endorsed.
- **Clearing history in Nigeria:**
    - 1961: first bankers' clearing house, Lagos, run by the CBN (manual exchange).
    - 1988: MICR introduced in phases; conventional cheque exchange ended at the Lagos Clearing House on 1 July 1994.
    - 2002: **Nigeria Automated Clearing System (NACS)**, run by the Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System (**NIBSS**).
    - 1 April 2004: new settlement framework (settlement and non-settlement banks).
- **NACS participants:** the **Zonal Clearing House** (centre: registers participants, processes data, fixes settlement positions); **direct participating banks** (own clearing centre, the PBCC, with MICR reader/sorters, collateral in treasury bills); **indirect participating banks** (take part **through a direct participating bank**).
- **Current position:** the clearing cycle fell from 5 days to 3; cheque truncation (2012) then brought nationwide **T+1** (next-day) clearing from 2013, through NIBSS.

### One-minute recap

- Bill = unconditional written **order**; note = **promise** (maker); cheque = bill drawn on a banker payable on demand; IOU = not negotiable.
- Drawer writes, drawee pays (the bank for a cheque), payee receives; an accepting drawee becomes the acceptor.
- Time bills: acceptance (general or qualified), **3 days of grace**; after-sight bills must be presented for acceptance.
- Discount = Face x Rate x Days/365; proceeds = face - discount.
- Holder in due course: regular, not overdue, good faith, value, no notice; gets a title free of defects.
- Endorsements: blank (bearer), special, restrictive, conditional. Dishonour by non-acceptance or non-payment; foreign bills must be protested.
- Crossings: general, special, "not negotiable", "account payee"; drawer or holder may cross.
- Stale after 6 months; post-dated not paid early; undated not invalid.
- Death, countermand, garnishee, bankruptcy and forgery end authority to pay; wrongful dishonour = damages.
- Clearing: Lagos 1961, MICR 1988, NACS (NIBSS) 2002, T+1 from 2013.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A bill of exchange for ₦3,650,000 dated 5 May is payable 90 days after date. The holder discounts it with her bank on 28 June at 10% per annum (365-day year). Find the due date and the proceeds.

*Solution:*
1. 90 days after 5 May (excluding 5 May): May 26 days, June 30 (56), July 31 (87), August 3 (90): **3 August**.
2. Add 3 days of grace: due **6 August**.
3. Days from 28 June to 6 August: 2 (June) + 31 (July) + 6 (August) = **39 days**.
4. Discount = 3,650,000 x 0.10 x 39/365 = **₦39,000**.
5. Proceeds = 3,650,000 - 39,000 = **₦3,611,000**.
