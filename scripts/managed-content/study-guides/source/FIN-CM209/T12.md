## Topic 12: Bank Lending

**What you must know:** lending, borrowing, interest and risk assets; principles and objectives of lending; the 5 Cs and canons; security, its qualities and forms; loan procedure and agreement terms; loan versus overdraft; bad debts and recovery; loan computations.

### Lesson 12.1: Meaning of lending and its principles

**Must know**

- **Bank lending:** the extension of a credit facility to an intending borrower on the understanding that the principal and the interest on it will be repaid at an agreed future date. Lending is the **most profitable** business of banks.
- **Borrowing:** the ability of a willing and creditworthy customer to obtain a credit facility from a bank or other loan provider, with a view to repaying the sum at an agreed future date (with or without interest).
- **Interest:** the return paid by the borrower to the provider of funds, usually stated as a **percentage of the principal** per month or per annum. Banks charge a **higher rate** to compensate for the **risk that the borrower may default**.
- **Risk asset:** loans and advances are called risk assets because the **recovery of all the credit extended is uncertain**.
- **Three principles of lending (Mather, 1972):**

1. **Safety:** of **paramount importance**. The borrower's character, integrity and reliability must be sound; the source of repayment must be probed and certain.
2. **Suitability:** the purpose must not conflict with the government's economic and monetary policies. No lending for gambling, betting or other speculative activities.
3. **Profitability:** banks are not charities; every advance must earn profit (interest).

- **Objectives banks seek when granting credit (4):**

| Objective | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Profitability | loans, advances and investments yield good returns to shareholders |
| Liquidity | assets can quickly become cash to meet depositors' withdrawals and CBN requirements |
| Safety | depositors' funds are safeguarded so they can withdraw on demand without delay |
| Suitability | lending fits sound policy and regulators' rules; not all loans committed to long-term projects |

**Explain more**

- Trap: **speculation** and **growth** are not lending objectives.
- A bank that lends out almost all its deposits fails on **liquidity**; a bank that keeps everything as cash and lends nothing sacrifices **profitability**.
- A customer with a good record and good security who wants money for football betting or a gambling business is refused on **suitability**, not safety.
- **BOFIA 2020** (which replaced BOFIA 1991) requires that a bank manager or officer may grant an advance, loan or credit facility only if it is **authorised in accordance with the rules and regulations of the bank** and in line with the CBN's regulations on collateralisation (section 17).

### Lesson 12.2: The 5 Cs and the canons of lending

**Must know**

**The 5 Cs of lending:**

1. **Character:** honesty, integrity, willingness to repay. The **first and best security**; "the real security for any advance is the character of the borrower". Checked by a **status enquiry**.
2. **Capacity:** ability to repay; track record, technical and managerial know-how.
3. **Capital:** the borrower's own contribution to the project.
4. **Collateral:** security to fall back on. The **last and least** consideration, a residual matter not crucial to the decision.
5. **Conditions:** the economic environment and the sector's prospects.

- **Status enquiry:** obtaining background information on a prospective borrower (from other banks and sources) to find out whether he is **prudent or prodigal** with borrowed funds.

**The canons of lending: 7 questions on receiving a loan request**

1. How much does the customer require? (**Amount**)
2. What does he want it for? (**Purpose**)
3. For how long? (**Duration**)
4. What is the source of repayment? (**Repayment**)
5. Is the business strong enough to keep going if plans suffer a setback? (**Strength of the business**)
6. What security can he offer? (**Security**)
7. What is your assessment of the customer? (**The borrower**)

**Checklist for assessing proposals (9):** character, capacity, capital, purpose, amount, repayment, stability, security, monitoring.

**Explain more**

- **Capital:** before a ₦50 million cassava-processing loan, the bank insists the promoters put in substantial money of their own. A borrower with little at stake may not be committed, leaving the risk mostly on the bank.
- **Conditions:** in a recession, a Lagos bank cuts new loans to hotels and event centres even for good customers.
- **Collateral** never replaces assessing ability to repay. A highly creditworthy borrower with a proven track record may get an advance **without collateral**.
- **Amount:** lend too little and the project may be **abandoned uncompleted**, so repayment never comes; lend too much and the bank takes excess risk.
- **Purpose:** must be legal and outside banks' no-go areas. An illegal activity is the purpose a Nigerian banker is least willing to finance. Borrowing **short** to buy fixed assets such as a factory building means "borrowing short and investing long", which is risky because fixed assets are not quickly saleable.
- **Duration:** deposits are mostly **short-term**, so commercial banks prefer short-term working-capital finance. Medium and long-term requests go to merchant banks and development institutions such as the **Bank of Industry (BOI)**.
- **Repayment:** a personal customer shows earnings and outgoings; a company submits a **cash budget, projected profit and loss account and projected balance sheet**.
- **Strength of the business:** balance sheets and profit and loss accounts for about **three years** show whether it can remain a going concern.

### Lesson 12.3: Security and its qualities

**Must know**

- **Security (collateral):** anything of value deposited or pledged as a guarantee of repayment of a loan, to be forfeited on default; an asset the bank can fall back on if the borrower fails to repay.
- **Security interest:** an enforceable legal claim on collateral pledged for a loan, giving the lender the right to repossess the property if the borrower fails to repay.
- **Margin:** the difference between the market value of the security and the loan granted against it.
- **Why banks take security:** a safety net if repayment fails; a customer who has deposited his property or title documents is more likely to **use the funds judiciously** for the agreed purpose.
- **Rule:** obtain and **perfect** the security **before disbursing** funds.

**Qualities of good security (9):**

1. **Non-encumbrance:** free of disputes and of prior charges to other lenders.
2. **Stability of price** and ease of valuation.
3. **Legal acceptability.**
4. **Adequacy:** covers the full loan plus a reasonable margin.
5. **Proper documentation** (mortgage deed, pledge agreement).
6. **Ownership and possession** (undoubted title).
7. **Income generation / low maintenance cost.**
8. **Marketability:** a ready, broad market.
9. **Liquidity:** quickly convertible to cash at a known price.

**Explain more**

- Mrs Bello's shop already charged to another bank lacks **non-encumbrance**.
- Bankers dislike **luxury goods** as security because their prices swing with fashion and taste; necessities are more stable.
- An illegal asset is hard to dispose of and exposes the bank to legal consequences.
- A block of flats paying rent beats a vacant plot: **income generation**.
- Mr Ojo's late father's house, not in his name and disputed by family, lacks **undoubted title**.
- **High maintenance cost** is never a quality of good security.

### Lesson 12.4: Forms of security

**Must know**

- **Personal security:** a guarantee by the borrower or a third party instead of a tangible asset (personal guarantee, life policy).
- **Non-personal (tangible) security:** movable and immovable property: land (mortgage), debentures, shares, goods (lien, pledge, hypothecation). **Safer**, because the property can be sold to recover the unpaid amount.
- **Freehold** land: absolute title, lasts indefinitely, higher value. **Leasehold:** title expires; value **falls** as the unexpired years reduce.
- **Mortgage:** a security created by contract giving the lender an interest in the borrower's property until a sum is repaid; written agreement registered at the land registry. Borrower = **mortgagor**; bank = **mortgagee**.

| Point | Legal mortgage | Equitable mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| Legal estate | vests in the mortgagee | stays with the mortgagor |
| Creation | mortgage deed, under seal | deposit of title deeds |
| Possession | immediate right for mortgagee | mortgagor keeps the property |

- **Guarantee:** a promise by one person (**guarantor / surety**) to answer for the present or future debt of another (**principal debtor**), made to the creditor (the **bank**). **Specific** guarantee: one debt (e.g. a term loan). **Continuing** guarantee: a series of transactions, e.g. an overdraft, present and future balances.
- **Advantages of a guarantee:** easy to take (just sign); useful where the borrower has no property. **Disadvantages:** value depends **wholly on the guarantor's financial standing**; suing him is costly and slow; technical defects may free him.

| Charge over goods | Ownership | Possession | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lien | borrower | lender | ordinary lien: retain only; **banker's lien: may sell after proper notice** |
| Pledge | borrower | bank (actual or constructive delivery) | bailment of goods |
| Hypothecation | borrower | borrower | equitable charge; riskiest, because the goods stay in the borrower's possession |

- **Fixed charge:** over specific assets (a particular machine or property). **Floating charge:** over a class of assets (stock, receivables) the company may deal in until **crystallisation** on default or liquidation. Created by a **debenture** and registered with the **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)**.

**Explain more**

- **Constructive delivery:** the bank's padlock on the warehouse door holding a trader's rice, key kept by the bank, is a **pledge** although the rice never moves.
- Hypothecation suits raw materials a business must keep using; the bank should inspect the goods regularly.
- In Nigeria, since the **Land Use Act 1978**, land in each state is vested in the state Governor and held under a **right of occupancy** (evidenced by a certificate of occupancy, usually for up to 99 years), so the leasehold point above (value falls as the unexpired term shortens) applies to Nigerian titles.

**Procedure for taking security**

- **Savings account:** letter of authority to transfer; surrender the **passbook**; a signed blank withdrawal form (no date or amount). A certificate of occupancy is **not** needed.
- **Current account:** letter of authority; signed blank cheque.
- **Fixed deposit:** letter of authority; surrender the receipt or certificate; renew and endorse it if it expires first.
- **Life policy:** check the insurer, beneficiary, surrender value and premiums; complete a **deed of assignment**; **give notice to the insurance company**, which acknowledges it.
- **Stocks and shares:** customer signs a **memorandum of deposit** (rights including disposal on default); transfer by deed at the stock exchange (the traditional procedure; quoted shares are now held electronically in the Central Securities Clearing System, CSCS, where the bank's interest is recorded).
- **Land:** deposit the title deed or **certificate of occupancy**; **search at the land registry (ministry of lands)** to verify authenticity and ownership; physical inspection; then a legal mortgage (Governor's consent under the Land Use Act).

### Lesson 12.5: Procedure, facilities, agreements, bad debts and recovery

**Must know**

- **Individual applicant:** current account; application; **statement of affairs**; securities; agreement.
- **Corporate applicant** adds: audited accounts and tax clearance for 3 years; Memorandum and Articles; **certificate of incorporation**; cash-flow projection.
- **Ways banks extend credit:** loans, overdrafts, discounting of bills of exchange (a **standing order** is a payment service, not credit).
- **Overdraft:** an arrangement allowing a current account holder to withdraw more than his balance up to an agreed limit. **Conditions:** current account holder; evidence of honesty; account activity for at least **six months**; express approval of the manager.

| Point | Loan | Overdraft |
|---|---|---|
| Account | separate loan account | runs on the current account |
| Interest | on the whole amount granted | only on the amount actually overdrawn, daily |
| Term | specific purpose, fixed repayment; ends at maturity | short-term, repayable **on demand**, renewable |

- **Loan account entries:** **debit** a new loan account, **credit** the current account. Repayment and interest: **debit** current account, **credit** loan account.
- **Terms of loan agreements (5):** prohibition of further charges; bank approval of major management changes; rendering of financial returns; restriction of benefits to shareholders (dividends after repayments); insurance cover on mortgaged property kept in good repair. A share of the borrower's profits is **not** a usual term.
- **Monitoring (3):** regular demand for records; **physical inspection** of the project; watching the account for hard-core borrowing and missed reductions.
- **Bad debt:** a debt whose full or partial recovery is considered not possible.

**Causes of bad debts (10):** lack of in-depth knowledge of the customer's operations; excessive dependence on the balance sheet; **connected lending** (loans to relations or related businesses without the basic principles); lending on **overvalued securities**; inadequate project monitoring; lack of appraisal skills; manipulation of account records; borrowers' misconception of loans as windfalls; **natural hazards** (drought, flood, war: worst for **agricultural loans**); changes in economic policies.

**Explain more**

- **Recovery:** amicable methods first (restructuring, rescheduling, negotiated settlement), which **preserve the relationship and avoid costly litigation**; then demand letters, writ of summons, garnishee, winding-up or bankruptcy; **realisation of security** (power of sale under a mortgage, a receiver under a debenture, enforcing a pledge or guarantee); reporting defaulters to licensed **credit bureaus** such as CRC Credit Bureau, which **affects their future access to credit**; and, for eligible assets, **AMCON**.
- **Amortisation:** retiring a loan gradually by periodic payments rather than all at maturity. For a specific asset such as a delivery truck, repayments should match the asset's **productive life** and come from its cash flows.

### Lesson 12.6: Loan computations

**Must know**

- Flat (simple) interest = P x R x T (T in years; months / 12; days / 365)
- Total repayable = P + interest; equal instalment = total repayable / number of instalments
- Annuity payment = loan / PVIFA, where PVIFA = [1 - (1 + i)^-n] / i; interest each period = rate x opening balance; principal repaid = payment - interest
- Overdraft interest = amount overdrawn x rate x days / 365, summed for each balance
- LTV = loan / value of security x 100; margin = value - loan; margin % = (value - loan) / value x 100

**Explain more**

**1. Mr Eze's vehicle loan:** ₦200,000 on 1 January 1996, 2 years, 4 equal half-yearly instalments, 10% flat; current account ₦40,000 credit.

- Interest = 200,000 x 0.10 x 2 = ₦40,000; per half-year ₦10,000.
- Principal per half-year = 200,000 / 4 = ₦50,000.
- Debit each half-year = 50,000 + 10,000 = **₦60,000**.
- Current account: 40,000 + 200,000 = 240,000; then 180,000; 120,000; 60,000; **nil** at 31/12/97.

**2. Flat interest and instalments**

- ₦500,000, 3 years, 18%: 500,000 x 0.18 x 3 = **₦270,000** (not ₦90,000, one year only).
- ₦300,000, 9 months, 16%: 300,000 x 0.16 x 9/12 = **₦36,000**.
- ₦1,200,000, 12 months, 15%, 12 monthly instalments: interest 180,000; total 1,380,000; / 12 = **₦115,000**.
- ₦2,000,000, 2 years, 14%, 8 quarterly instalments: interest 560,000; total 2,560,000; / 8 = **₦320,000**.

**3. Balances**

- Mr Uche: ₦800,000 in 4 half-yearly principal instalments of 200,000. After the third: 800,000 - 600,000 = **₦200,000**.
- Bisi: 150,000 + 600,000 = 750,000 credit. Year 1 debit = 600,000 / 3 + 600,000 x 0.10 = 200,000 + 60,000 = 260,000. Balance = **₦490,000 credit**.

**4. Amortisation:** ₦1,000,000, 10%, 3 equal year-end payments.

- PVIFA = [1 - 1.1^-3] / 0.10 = 2.486852; payment = 1,000,000 / 2.486852 = **₦402,114.80** (to the nearest kobo).

| Year | Opening | Interest 10% | Principal | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,000,000.00 | 100,000.00 | 302,114.80 | 697,885.20 |
| 2 | 697,885.20 | 69,788.52 | 332,326.28 | 365,558.92 |
| 3 | 365,558.92 | 36,555.89 | 365,558.91 | 0.01 (rounding) |

- First year's principal reduction = 402,114.80 - 100,000 = **₦302,114.80**.

**5. Overdraft interest**

- Kola: 400,000 x 0.20 x 73/365 = **₦16,000.00**.
- Femi Stores (limit ₦1m, used ₦600,000): 600,000 x 0.24 x 30/365 = **₦11,835.62** (on the amount used, never the limit).
- Changing balance: 250,000 x 0.18 x 20/365 = 2,465.75; 450,000 x 0.18 x 10/365 = 2,219.18; total = **₦4,684.93** (rounded to kobo).

**6. LTV and margin**

- ₦12m against ₦20m: 12 / 20 x 100 = **60%**.
- Maximum LTV 70% on ₦15m: maximum loan 10,500,000; margin = 15,000,000 - 10,500,000 = **₦4,500,000**.
- ₦1.8m against shares worth ₦2.5m: (2,500,000 - 1,800,000) / 2,500,000 x 100 = **28%** (LTV is 72%).

### One-minute recap

- Lending = extending credit to be repaid with interest; loans are risk assets.
- Mather's principles: safety (paramount), suitability, profitability; objectives add liquidity.
- 5 Cs: character (first and best security), capacity, capital, collateral (last and least), conditions.
- Canons: amount, purpose, duration, repayment, strength of business, security, the borrower.
- Legal mortgage vests the legal estate; equitable mortgage is by deposit of deeds. Pledge = bank holds; hypothecation = borrower holds.
- Loan: separate account, interest on full amount. Overdraft: current account, interest on amount used, repayable on demand.
- Flat interest = P x R x T; annuity payment = loan / PVIFA; LTV = loan / value.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Ada borrows ₦900,000 for 18 months at 12% per annum flat interest on the original principal, repaying principal and interest in 18 equal monthly instalments. Find the total interest and each instalment.

*Solution:* Interest = 900,000 x 0.12 x 18/12 = ₦162,000. Total repayable = 900,000 + 162,000 = ₦1,062,000. Monthly instalment = 1,062,000 / 18 = **₦59,000**.
