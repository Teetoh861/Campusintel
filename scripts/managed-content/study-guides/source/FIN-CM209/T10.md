## Topic 10: Bank-Customer Relationship

**What you must know:** who a banker and a customer are (definitions and cases); the relationships between them; the banker's rights (charges and COT, lien, set-off, appropriation and Clayton's Rule) with their computations; the banker's duties, secrecy and when authority to pay ends; the customer's duties and rights; garnishee and bankruptcy; types of account, KYC, opening, mandates, powers of attorney and closing; special services.

### Lesson 10.1: Who is a banker and who is a customer?

**Must know**

- **J.W. Gilbert:** a banker is "a dealer in capital, or more properly a dealer in money. He is an intermediate party between the borrower and the lender. He borrows from one party and lends to another."
- **Hart:** a banker receives money and collects cheques for customers and honours cheques drawn on him by them, up to their current account balances.
- **Sir John Paget:** no one can be a banker who does not (1) take deposit accounts, (2) take current accounts, (3) issue and pay cheques, (4) collect cheques, crossed and uncrossed, for customers.
- **Halsbury's Laws of England:** a banker is an individual, partnership or corporation whose sole or predominant business is banking, that is, **the receipt of money on current or deposit account and the payment of cheques drawn by, and collection of cheques paid in by, a customer**.
- **Criticism of Hart, Paget and Halsbury:** they describe **deposit banking** well but are **not wide enough** to cover the other businesses banks now do.
- **United Dominions Trust v Kirkwood (1966):** the marks of a banker: accepting money from and collecting cheques for customers, and **honouring cheques or orders drawn on the bank**, and keeping current (or similar) accounts.
- **Akwule and Others v The Queen (1963), Supreme Court of Nigeria:** "banker" means **the company carrying on banking business**, not an employee of the bank.
- **Nigerian law today:** a bank is a company licensed by the CBN to carry on banking business under the **Banks and Other Financial Institutions Act (BOFIA) 2020** (which replaced BOFIA 1991).
- **Customer:** there is **no statutory definition**; the courts supply the principles. A person, firm, society or company becomes a customer when he **offers** to open an account and the bank **accepts** (offer and acceptance, as in any contract).

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Great Western Railway v London and County Banking Co. (1901) | A man who **merely cashed cheques** for his employer for many years was **not** a customer |
| Commissioners of Taxation v English, Scottish and Australian Bank (1920) | **Duration is not of the essence**; a person is a customer as soon as an account is opened |

**Explain more**

- Akwule in practice: if Mr Eze's funded cheque is wrongly returned at a Yaba branch, he sues **the bank**, his banker and debtor, not the cashier, the branch manager or the CBN.
- Duration and frequency of dealings are not decisive. Adaeze, whose account was opened yesterday with one ₦50,000 deposit, is already a customer. Tunde the messenger who cashed his employer's cheques weekly for ten years is not (GWR case).
- Trap: a customer does not need a loan, a cheque book or six months' operation. The relationship needs **both parties' intention**, so it is **contractual**.

### Lesson 10.2: The relationships between banker and customer

**Must know**

1. **Debtor and creditor (the primary relationship):** customer in credit = **creditor**, bank = **debtor**. Customer overdrawn = **debtor**, bank = **creditor**.
2. **Principal and agent:** bank is **agent** when it collects cheques, makes payments (standing orders, premiums), buys or sells shares and securities, or does foreign exchange for the customer.
3. **Bailor and bailee:** items left for **safe custody**; customer = **bailor**, bank = **bailee**. The bank charges a fee, so it is a **bailee for reward** and its standard of care is **high**; lost items are paid for.
4. **Mortgagor and mortgagee:** on a loan secured by property, customer = **mortgagor**, bank = **mortgagee**.
5. **Trustee and beneficiary:** where money is given to the bank for a **specific purpose** (held in a fiduciary capacity).
6. **Contractual:** the whole relationship rests on contract (offer and acceptance at account opening, plus terms and mandate).

- **Foley v Hill (1848):** money paid in **becomes the bank's money**, to use as it pleases; the bank only owes an **equivalent sum**. It is **not** held on trust and need not be kept separately.
- **Joachimson v Swiss Bank Corporation (1921):** the bank need not repay a current account balance **until the customer demands it**. Repayment is on **written demand, at the branch where the account is kept, during banking hours**.
- **General relationship:** from ordinary business: deposits, withdrawals, cheques, statements. **Special relationship:** from extra services: safe custody, advice, guarantees, letters of credit, powers of attorney, standing orders, direct debits, trustee services. Special services may carry **higher** duties.

**Explain more**

- Reversal of the ordinary rule: in law a debtor must seek out his creditor. In banking the **customer (creditor) must seek out the bank (debtor)** and demand payment.
- Because the bank is a debtor, not a trustee, it may lend depositors' money to a manufacturer in Ikeja **without asking them**.
- Matching scenarios: Halima with ₦300,000 in credit is creditor; Sola overdrawn by ₦150,000 is debtor. Kemi asking the bank to buy ₦500,000 of FGN bonds and collect a Kano cheque makes the bank her **agent**. Chief Okonkwo leaving his C of O and jewellery for a fee is **bailor**. Musa mortgaging his Lekki house for a ₦10 million loan is **mortgagor**, the bank **mortgagee**. Mrs Ade's ₦5 million held only for her late brother's children's school fees makes the bank a **trustee**, the children **beneficiaries**.
- **Employer and employee** is never a banker-customer relationship.

### Lesson 10.3: Rights of the banker

**Must know**

1. To charge **reasonable commission** for services and **interest** on advances.
2. **Banker's lien.**
3. **Set-off** (combination of accounts).
4. **Appropriation** of payments.
5. That the **customer seeks out the bank** for repayment.
6. To **return irregularly drawn** cheques.
7. To **return cheques that would create an unauthorised overdraft**.
8. To **recall an overdraft**, which is repayable on demand.
9. To be **reimbursed** for expenses incurred on the customer's behalf.

- **Banker's lien:** a **general lien**: the right to **retain** a customer's securities that come into the bank's hands **in the ordinary course of business** until his debt is paid. It carries an **implied power of sale** and is **lost if the bank voluntarily parts with possession**. It does **not** cover items left for safe custody or for a specific purpose, and does not apply where the parties agreed no lien.
- **Set-off:** combining a **credit balance on one account** with a **debit balance on another** of the same customer, using the credit to reduce the debt. Conditions: (1) accounts in the **same name and same right**; (2) the debt **due and payable**; (3) **no agreement excluding** set-off; (4) notice usually given as good practice. Personal and **trust** accounts cannot be combined.
- **Appropriation order:** the **customer** may appropriate first; if he does not, the **bank** may; if neither does, **Clayton's Rule** applies to a running account.
- **Clayton's Rule (Devaynes v Noble, 1816):** the **first item on the debit side is discharged by the first item on the credit side** (first in, first out).
- **Commission on turnover (COT):** a charge on **current account** customers' **debit** entries for a period. **COT = periodic rate × total debit entries.** Savings accounts do **not** attract COT.

**Explain more**

- **COT (₦), bank-style.** Tola had ₦40,000 credit on 1 January; withdrew ₦50,000 (15 Jan), ₦30,000 (20 Feb), ₦10,000 (8 Mar). COT 15% per annum.
    1. Quarterly rate = 15% ÷ 4 = 3.75% = 0.0375.
    2. Total debits = 50,000 + 30,000 + 10,000 = ₦90,000. (The opening balance is not a debit entry.)
    3. COT = 0.0375 × 90,000 = **₦3,375**.
- Current position: the CBN phased COT down from 2013, abolished it from 2016 and replaced it with a capped **current account maintenance fee** charged **per mille** (per ₦1,000) of customer-induced debits (CBN Guide to Charges). Exam questions still compute COT as above.
- **Lien example.** Mr Bello owes ₦2 million. The bank holds share certificates received to collect his dividends (ordinary course of business) and a sealed jewellery box left for safe custody. Lien covers the **share certificates only**. No court order or consent is needed.
- **Set-off example.** Chidi: savings ₦450,000 Cr; current ₦620,000 Dr (due); trustee account for his father's estate ₦300,000 Cr. The trust account is in a different right, so leave it out. ₦620,000 − ₦450,000 = **₦170,000** still owed.
- **Clayton's Rule, partner's death.** Firm overdrawn ₦800,000 when Bayo dies; account not stopped; ₦500,000 paid in, ₦300,000 withdrawn.
    1. The ₦500,000 credit discharges the oldest debit first: ₦800,000 − ₦500,000 = **₦300,000** of the old debt left (Bayo's estate remains liable for this).
    2. The ₦300,000 withdrawal is a **new** debt, for which the estate is not liable.
- **Clayton's Rule, guarantee.** Mrs Ojo ends her guarantee at ₦1,200,000; Dapo pays in ₦700,000 and withdraws ₦900,000. Her liability = 1,200,000 − 700,000 = **₦500,000**. The ₦900,000 is new borrowing she never guaranteed. To protect itself, a bank **stops (rules off) the account** and opens a new one when a guarantee ends or a partner dies.

### Lesson 10.4: Duties of the banker, secrecy, and when authority to pay ends

**Must know**

Duties of the banker:
1. To **honour cheques** when (a) there are sufficient funds or an agreed overdraft, (b) the cheque is properly drawn and presented, (c) there is no legal bar.
2. To **receive** money and **collect** cheques for the customer with care.
3. To give **reasonable notice before closing** an account in credit.
4. To **furnish statements** of account periodically.
5. **Duty of care** (act in good faith, without negligence).
6. To **pay agreed interest** on deposits.
7. To **alert the customer** to irregularities on his account.
8. **Secrecy.**

- **Wrongful dishonour:** a bank that wrongly returns a funded cheque is **liable in damages for breach of contract, including injury to the customer's credit**.
- **Tournier v National Provincial and Union Bank of England (1924):** secrecy is a **legal duty arising from contract**, but **qualified** by **4 exceptions**:
    1. **Compulsion by law** (court order, subpoena, statute).
    2. **Duty to the public.**
    3. **Interest of the bank.**
    4. **Express or implied consent of the customer.**
- Secrecy **continues after the account is closed**. A spouse has no right to the other's account details without authority.

When the bank's authority to pay ends (12 cases):
1. Lack of funds. 2. Countermand (stop payment). 3. Notice of the customer's **death**. 4. Notice of defect in the presenter's title (lost or stolen cheque). 5. Notice of the customer's **mental incapacity**. 6. Assignment of the balance. 7. **Garnishee order** or court injunction. 8. **Bankruptcy** of the customer. 9. **Forged** drawer's signature. 10. Irregularly drawn cheque. 11. Demand outside banking hours or at the wrong branch. 12. A lawful order freezing the account.

Duties of the customer:
1. Draw cheques with **reasonable care** so as not to facilitate fraud.
2. **Seek out** the bank and demand repayment.
3. Issue cheques only against **available funds** or an agreed overdraft.
4. **Pay bank charges** as agreed.
5. **Inform the bank promptly** of forgery, a lost cheque book or other suspicious dealings.

Rights of the customer: to know his balance; to **withdraw without notice** by cheque if the current account is in funds; to appropriate his payments; to receive statements; to confidentiality; to reasonable notice of closure; to **close the account** if not indebted.

**Explain more**

- Secrecy scenarios: a Federal High Court order for a fraud suspect's statements = **compulsion by law** (so are suspicious transaction reports to the NFIU under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022). Suing a guarantor and showing the principal debtor's balance, or briefing the bank's solicitor on a debt = **interest of the bank**. Reporting a customer trading with an enemy in wartime, or a public officer looting public funds = **duty to the public**. Kunle writing to let his auditors have his statements, or authorising a status report = **express consent**. Funke closed her account in 2023; in 2026 the bank must still refuse a journalist.
- Statements let the customer **reconcile** his records and detect errors.
- **Countermand** must be **in writing, clear and unambiguous, signed per the mandate, and received before the cheque is paid**.
- A **forged** drawer's signature makes the cheque **wholly inoperative**; it carries no mandate, so the bank cannot debit the customer, however skilful the forgery.
- **Death** cancels all mandates: Mr Danjuma's cheques signed the week before must **not** be paid after notice of death. A customer travelling abroad on holiday does **not** end authority.
- **London Joint Stock Bank v Macmillan and Arthur (1918):** partners signed a cheque with blank spaces; a clerk raised the amount. The customer owes a duty to **draw cheques carefully so as not to facilitate fraud** and **bears the loss** of his carelessness.
- **Garnishee order:** a court order **attaching** funds in the customer's account to satisfy a **judgment debt** he owes a third party. Parties: judgment creditor (Mr Adamu), judgment debtor (Mrs Lawal), **garnishee = the bank** (Union Bank). On service of the **order nisi** the bank **freezes** the account and pays only when the order is made **absolute** or as the court directs.
- **Bankruptcy** (Bankruptcy Act): on notice of a bankruptcy order the customer's **authority to operate the account ceases**, the bank freezes it, and the **trustee in bankruptcy steps into his shoes**. For an unsecured debt the bank **proves in the bankruptcy** and shares with other creditors; a secured creditor may realise its security.

### Lesson 10.5: Accounts: types, opening, mandates and closing

**Must know**

The 3 main types of deposit account (a **loan account** is not one):

| Feature | Current | Savings | Fixed (time) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deposit type | **Demand** deposit | Savings deposit | **Time** deposit |
| Withdrawal | By **cheque**, on demand, no notice | Withdrawal slip, by holder in person | Only at **maturity**; penalty if early |
| Interest | Little or none | Earns interest | **Highest** |
| References | Required (traditionally) | Not required | Not required |
| COT / overdraft | COT; overdraft possible | No COT; no overdraft | Deposit receipt issued |

- **Savings rule (traditional):** more than **3 withdrawals in a month** forfeits that month's interest.
- **Domiciliary account:** a **foreign-currency** account (e.g. US dollars) for receiving remittances and holding foreign exchange, subject to CBN rules.
- **KYC (Know Your Customer):** verifying a customer's **identity, address and beneficial ownership** before and during the relationship. Documents: photograph, ID (NIN, passport, driver's licence, voter's card), proof of address, **BVN**; companies add CAC documents, TIN, board resolution. **Enhanced due diligence** for high-risk customers: **PEPs, non-residents**, complex structures.
- **Mandate:** the customer's formal authority stating **who may sign cheques and operate the account**. The bank must follow it strictly. Joint mandates: "EITHER TO SIGN", "BOTH TO SIGN", "ANY TWO OF THE THREE TO SIGN".
- **Power of attorney:** a formal written authority, **signed and sealed**, by which the **donor** empowers the **donee (attorney)** to act for him generally or in specified matters. **Specific** (one purpose) or **General** (anything the donor may lawfully do by an attorney).

On receiving a power of attorney the bank must:
1. Sight the original.
2. Keep a certified true copy in the customer's file.
3. Ensure it is stamped.
4. Operate strictly within the listed powers.
5. Follow the donor's instructions on revocation.

A power of attorney ends by:
1. Completion of its purpose. 2. Expiry of its time. 3. Death of donor or donee. 4. Mental incapacity of either. 5. Bankruptcy of either. 6. Express revocation by the donor. 7. Implication (the donor resumes operating the account).

Documents for opening:

| Customer | Key documents |
|---|---|
| Limited company | Board resolution and extract naming signatories, memorandum and articles, certificate of incorporation (plus CAC returns) |
| Partnership | Firm's name; application signed by all partners; business-name search; deed of partnership; mandate with **joint and several liability** clause |
| Club, union, association | Rules; resolution; **extract of minutes certified by Chairman/President and Secretary**; specimen signatures and photographs |
| Executor | **Grant of probate** (administrator: letters of administration) |
| Government or agency | Gazette establishing it; authority from Accountant-General (federal or state) or council Chairman and Secretary |
| Minor | Titled guardian's name **"IN RE"** minor's name and date of birth; guardian operates until **18** |

**Explain more**

- Tope and Yemi's mandate says "BOTH TO SIGN"; a ₦80,000 cheque signed only by Tope must be **refused**.
- On a joint account, the balance passes to the **survivor** (rule of survivorship) unless the terms say otherwise.
- **Delegation of mandate** rules: no delegation on **trust accounts** (all trustees must sign), so the Ibadan orphanage trustee cannot delegate to his secretary; minors are not accepted as agents; an agent who does not sign as agent is personally liable; joint holders must all authorise any delegation.
- Alhaji Sani's power letting his son only withdraw for school fees is **specific**. A salary credit never revokes a power of attorney.
- **Closing accounts.** A bank may close a **credit** account, after reasonable notice, for unreasonableness, frequent negligent operation, **habitual drawing on uncleared effects**, fraud links or image risk. General procedure: (1) closing form completed, (2) unused cheques returned, (3) closing charges determined, (4) closing entries posted, (5) closing balance determined, (6) balance paid, (7) ledger marked **"CLOSED"**, (8) entry in the opened and closed register. A new cheque book is never part of closing.

### Lesson 10.6: Special services that create special relationships

**Must know**

- **Safe custody:** items (title deeds, C of O, share certificates, wills, jewellery) taken in a sealed envelope or locked box **"contents unknown"** (or listed and signed for); the customer gets a **receipt that must be surrendered** to withdraw them. The bank does not open, value, lend or pay interest on them.
- **Status enquiry (banker's opinion):** a reply on a customer's creditworthiness, given **briefly, in general terms, in confidence and "without responsibility"**, without disclosing balances; the bank must still take care it is **not misleading**. It never makes the bank a guarantor.

| Reply | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Undoubted for your figures" | **Strongest**: considerable strength |
| "Considered good for your figures" | Satisfactory |
| "Should be good for your figures" | Some doubt |
| "We cannot speak for your figures" | Unfavourable |

- **Standing order:** customer instructs his bank to pay a **fixed** amount regularly to a named beneficiary. **Direct debit:** customer authorises a **third party (originator)** to collect **variable** amounts from his account.
- **Agency services:** collecting cheques and dividend warrants, paying premiums, rent and bills, buying and selling shares, acting as trustee or executor. Accepting fixed deposits is **deposit-taking**, not agency.

**Explain more**

- Bisi's phone company pulling a different amount monthly = **direct debit**; her bank paying a fixed ₦20,000 to her landlord = **standing order**.

### One-minute recap

- No statutory definition of customer: offer and acceptance; duration not essential (Taxation Commissioners 1920); mere cheque-cashing is not enough (GWR 1901).
- Akwule (1963): the banker is the bank, not its staff.
- Customer in credit = creditor; overdrawn = debtor. Foley v Hill: deposit is the bank's money. Joachimson: written demand, at the branch, in banking hours.
- Bailor-bailee (safe custody, bailee for reward), agent, mortgagor-mortgagee, trustee; employer-employee never.
- Lien is general, with implied power of sale, not over safe custody. Set-off needs same name and same right.
- Clayton's Rule: first debit cleared by first credit; stop the account on death or end of guarantee.
- Tournier: 4 exceptions: law, public duty, bank's interest, consent; secrecy survives closure.
- Death, countermand, garnishee, bankruptcy, forgery and mental incapacity end authority to pay; travel does not.
- COT = periodic rate × total debits; current accounts only.
- KYC with EDD for PEPs; executors need probate; minors "IN RE"; no delegation on trust accounts.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Ngozi guaranteed Ade's overdraft and gave notice ending her guarantee when the balance was ₦950,000 Dr. The bank did not stop the account. Ade then paid in ₦400,000 and ₦250,000 and withdrew ₦600,000. Using Clayton's Rule, find (a) Ngozi's remaining liability and (b) the total overdraft.

*Solution:*
1. Under Clayton's Rule the credits clear the oldest debit first: 950,000 − 400,000 − 250,000 = **₦300,000**. This is all Ngozi still owes under her guarantee.
2. The ₦600,000 withdrawal is new borrowing after the guarantee ended; she is not liable for it.
3. Total overdraft = 300,000 + 600,000 = **₦900,000 Dr**.
