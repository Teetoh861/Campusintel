## Topic 9: Methods of Payment through the Banking System

**What you must know:** domestic methods; standing orders, direct debits and their computations; international methods and Nostro/Vostro; electronic banking, cards and Nigerian payment systems; the e-Naira.

### Lesson 9.1: Domestic methods of payment

**Must know**

- **Domestic methods to list:**
  1. **Standing order** (Lesson 9.2)
  2. **Direct debit** (Lesson 9.2)
  3. **Mail and telegraphic transfers**
  4. **Bank drafts**
  5. **Bank cheques**
  6. **Intra-bank transfers**
  7. **Banker's payments**
  8. **Personal cheques**
  9. **Credit transfer (bank giro)**
- **Personal cheque:** the **simplest and most common** method of paying a debt within the economy. Its weakness: **fraud and dishonour**, so many creditors prefer drafts or standing orders.
- **Bank draft:** a cheque **drawn by a branch of a bank on another branch or on the head office of the same bank**, signed by the **bank's own authorised signatories**. The issuing branch sends the paying branch an **advice** with the draft's particulars so the paying branch can **confirm the draft is genuine**. Because it is a **bank's own instrument, it is not normally dishonoured**. Used for large payments, locally and internationally.
- **Bank cheque** (also called a certified cheque): issued by the bank on the customer's written request (paid for in cash or by his own cheque) and cleared in the usual way. It is used **within the same clearing area only**, while a **bank draft is used where other clearing areas are involved**, so a bank cheque is of narrower use.
- **Mail transfer (MT) and telegraphic transfer (TT):** the customer gives written instructions stating the **beneficiary's name, the beneficiary's bank, the account number and the amount**; the bank debits him and pays. A **TT is faster** (cable, telex or electronic message) than an MT (post).
- **Intra-bank transfer:** a customer with **several accounts at the same bank** moves funds between them, e.g. from savings to current so that a cheque already issued can be paid.
- **Banker's payment:** an instrument **banks use to pay each other**, (1) for services between banks such as **cash supplied** by one bank to another, and (2) for the **proceeds of a cheque sent for special clearing**. It passes through clearing: the issuing bank is debited, the receiving bank credited.
- **Credit transfer (bank giro):** the payer fills **credit (giro) slips** at his own bank, and the funds are credited directly to the payees' accounts at the same or other banks.

**Explain more**

- Trap: neither a bank cheque nor a draft is drawn by the customer on his own account or by the CBN; the tested difference is **same clearing area (bank cheque)** versus **other clearing areas (draft)**.
- Example: after heavy withdrawals, Bank X asks Bank Y for ₦50 million cash and settles with a banker's payment in favour of Bank Y; through clearing, Bank X is debited and Bank Y credited. In special clearing, the paying bank sends a banker's payment for the cheque's proceeds.
- Domestic settlement between banks is **direct** (cheques, drafts or banker's payments) or **through the clearing house** (cheques, or credit and debit vouchers).

### Lesson 9.2: Standing orders and direct debits

**Must know**

- **Standing order:** an instruction by a customer to his bank to make **regular payments of a fixed amount** at weekly, monthly or quarterly intervals, from his account (current or savings), to a **named third party** at the same or another bank. It is signed **in line with the account mandate**; the bank acts as **agent**.
- **Used for (6):** insurance premiums; regular subscriptions; house rents; hire purchase instalments; instalment loan repayments; school fees. (Not one-off payments.)
- **Information the bank needs before opening one (11):**
  1. the customer's application
  2. name of the customer giving the order
  3. name of the beneficiary
  4. address of the beneficiary
  5. destination of the remittance
  6. mode of payment (air, sea, land, rail and so on)
  7. amount to be remitted
  8. intervals of payment
  9. customer's signature
  10. account number of the applicant
  11. account number of the beneficiary
- **Customer's duties (4):** give accurate information; keep sufficient funds; pay the bank's charges; **notify the bank promptly when the order is to be discontinued**.
- **Banker's duties (4):** handle the order with **diligence and care**; remit strictly according to the mandate, provided funds are available; warn the customer not to cause delay or non-payment (the bank is not responsible for these); send **periodic statements** to the customer.
- **Banker's rights (2):** charge **commission**; **cancel** the order where the customer has caused non-payment on the due date **three consecutive times or more**.
- **Advantages to the bank (2):** commission income; widens the range of services offered.
- **Advantages to the customer (4):** pays recurring items; provides a record of payment; cheap transmission charges; **the onus of paying on the due dates rests with the banker**.
- **Bank's considerations:**
  1. **Insufficient funds:** the bank **is not obliged to pay and incurs no liability** for not paying.
  2. **Payee asks the bank to cancel:** **disregard it unless the customer confirms**.
  3. **Payee refuses the payments:** **continue to make each payment as it falls due and notify the customer of each refusal**.
  4. **Payee asks why a payment is late:** do not answer; **advise the payee to contact the customer** (secrecy).
- **The bank's obligation to pay stops when (4):** funds are insufficient; the balance is insufficient and there is **no credit (overdraft) arrangement**; the customer **countermands**; a **garnishee order** stops payments out of the account.
- **Direct debit:** the **beneficiary (creditor) initiates** the payment. The paying customer endorses (authorises) the creditor's document, and the creditor then sends signed debit claims to the bank **from time to time**. Used by **insurance companies and finance houses**; suits **variable amounts**; the customer can cancel the authority.

| Point | Standing order | Direct debit |
|---|---|---|
| Started by | the customer (payer) | the beneficiary (creditor), with the payer's signed authority |
| Amount | fixed | fixed or variable |
| Typical user | individual paying rent, fees | insurers, finance houses, clubs |

**Explain more**

- Paying ₦45,000 rent to a landlord on the first of every month "until further notice" is a standing order. An insurer sending signed debit claims under a policyholder's endorsed authority uses a direct debit; so should a gym whose fees change monthly, because a standing order is for a fixed amount.
- Traps: "the beneficiary writes to cancel" does **not** end the duty to pay (only the customer cancels); sending statements and taking care are the **bank's** duties, notifying discontinuance is the **customer's**; the beneficiary's income is never needed.

### Lesson 9.3: Standing order computations

**Must know**

- **Total debit** = standing order amount + commission (rate × amount) + postage.
- **Balance before** = opening balance (CR +, DR −) + credits − debits. **Balance after** = balance before − total debit.
- **Decision rule:** pay if the balance after is CR or a DR **within the approved overdraft limit**; otherwise the bank **does not pay**.
- **Annual commission** = rate × amount × number of payments in the year.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (Mr Emeka).** Standing order ₦5,000 due at month end. Opening balance ₦1,000 DR; cheque paid in ₦8,000; cheque book ₦100; cheque issued to Ben ₦1,950; cash lodged ₦920. Charges: ½% commission and ₦50 postage. Overdraft limit ₦1,000.

1. Debits: ₦1,000 (opening DR) + ₦100 + ₦1,950 = ₦3,050. Credits: ₦8,000 + ₦920 = ₦8,920.
2. Balance before: ₦8,920 − ₦3,050 = **₦5,870 CR**.
3. Commission 0.5% × ₦5,000 = ₦25; total debit ₦5,000 + ₦25 + ₦50 = ₦5,075.
4. Balance after: ₦5,870 − ₦5,075 = **₦795 CR**, so the bank **pays**.

**Worked example 2 (total debit).** ₦12,000 at ½% plus ₦100 postage: commission 0.5% × ₦12,000 = ₦60; total debit ₦12,000 + ₦60 + ₦100 = **₦12,160**.

**Worked example 3 (within the limit).** Balance ₦4,000 CR; order ₦4,500; ½% and ₦50; limit ₦1,000. Charges ₦22.50 + ₦50 = ₦72.50; total debit ₦4,572.50; balance after ₦4,000 − ₦4,572.50 = **₦572.50 DR**, inside the limit, so the bank **pays**.

**Worked example 4 (beyond the limit).** Balance ₦3,000 CR; order ₦5,000; ½% and ₦50; limit ₦1,500. Total debit ₦5,075; balance after ₦3,000 − ₦5,075 = **₦2,075 DR**, beyond ₦1,500, so the bank **does not pay**. (Forgetting charges gives ₦2,000 DR, the trap.)

**Worked example 5 (1% commission).** Opening ₦2,500 CR; in ₦15,000 and ₦1,800; out ₦6,200 and ₦200. Balance before ₦12,900 CR; total debit ₦10,000 + ₦100 + ₦50 = ₦10,150; after **₦2,750 CR**.

**Worked example 6 (annual commission).** ₦25,000 monthly at ½%: ₦125 per payment × 12 = **₦1,500** (₦125 is one month only).

### Lesson 9.4: International methods of payment and settlement

**Must know**

- **International methods (6):**
  1. **Payment in advance:** the importer pays before shipment (safest for the exporter).
  2. **Open account:** the exporter sends the **documents of title direct to the importer**, loses control of the goods, and relies on the importer's honesty to remit after selling. **Riskiest** for the exporter; discouraged.
  3. **Clean collection:** **only the bill of exchange** is sent to the bank for collection.
  4. **Documentary collection:** the bill is sent **with commercial documents** (bill of lading, insurance certificate or policy). Still not certain, because the importer may not pay.
  5. **Documentary letter of credit (LC):** the **surest** method; a **bank's undertaking to pay** the exporter **provided he complies with the terms** of the credit.
  6. **Bill negotiation:** the exporter needing cash now **endorses and delivers** the bill to his bank, which pays the **face value less a discount** for commission and interest and collects the full amount at maturity.
- **Parties to an LC:** **issuing bank** = the **importer's bank**, which opens the credit in the exporter's favour; **advising bank** = the **exporter's bank**, which tells the exporter the credit has been opened.
- **D/P (documents against payment):** documents of title released when the bill is **paid**. **D/A (documents against acceptance):** released when the bill is **accepted**.
- In all these methods, the actual money moves by **mail transfer, telegraphic transfer or bank draft**.
- **Nostro** ("our" in Latin): an account a **Nigerian bank keeps with a correspondent bank abroad**, in foreign currency, to receive export proceeds and make foreign payments.
- **Vostro** ("your" in Latin): an account a **foreign bank keeps with a Nigerian bank**, in naira, to settle its obligations in Nigeria.
- **Bill negotiation:** amount received = face value − (discount rate × face value).

**Explain more**

- Exporter's risk, least to most: payment in advance, letter of credit, documentary collection, clean collection, open account.
- LC process: sale agreed; issuing bank opens the credit; advising bank informs the exporter; goods shipped; documents released only on payment or acceptance, so the exporter keeps control.
- Point of view decides the name: a London bank's naira account with Zenith Bank in Lagos is a **Vostro** account to Zenith Bank (and a Nostro account to the London bank).
- **Worked example (bill negotiation, bank figures).** Face value ₦800,000; discount 2.5%. Discount = 0.025 × ₦800,000 = ₦20,000. Exporter receives ₦800,000 − ₦20,000 = **₦780,000**.

### Lesson 9.5: Electronic banking, cards and the Nigerian payment system

**Must know**

- **Electronic banking:** delivering banking services through electronic channels: **payment cards** (credit, cheque, charge, debit and cash cards), **electronic funds transfer, ATMs, POS terminals, telephone and internet (home) banking**. Over-the-counter cash withdrawal with a signed slip is **not** e-banking.
- **Problems with cash that pushed banks to alternatives (4):**
  1. the **falling value of the naira** means large sums must be carried
  2. single over-the-counter transactions can run into millions of naira
  3. carrying cash invites **robbery and loss of life**
  4. banks spend heavily on **staff and note-counting machines**
- **E-banking products in Nigeria (4):** **electronic smart card; electronic funds transfer; telephone banking; internet banking.**
- **Smart card:** looks like a credit card but has an **intelligent memory chip** that stores value and keeps an **up-to-date record of the transactions** made with it. Called a **"cashless wallet"**. Value can be loaded and withdrawn.
- **Electronic funds transfer (EFT):** **electronically recorded pre-authorised debits and credits** that **replace paper cheques**. Three areas: between financial institutions; wholesale (corporate), e.g. the **direct deposit (payroll) programme** paying salaries straight into staff accounts; retail (consumer): electronic bill payment, **POS using a debit card**, and cash-based money transfer (Western Union, MoneyGram).
- **ATM services (5):** cash withdrawal; balance enquiry; statement request; cash deposit; **transfer between accounts**. Operated with a card **plus a secret personal identification number (PIN)**. Not done at an ATM: approving loans, opening fixed deposits, granting unapproved overdrafts.
- **ATM advantages:** cash outside banking hours; shorter queues in banking halls; time saved for customers' own business.
- **Cards:**
  - **Debit card:** payment taken **directly from the holder's own account** at the time of use.
  - **Credit card:** a **revolving credit line**; pay a minimum and carry the rest with interest.
  - **Charge card:** the **full balance must be settled at the end of each billing period**.
- **SWIFT = Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication:** a non-profit, cooperative message network **based in Belgium**. It carries **secure payment instructions, not the funds**; money settles through correspondent (Nostro/Vostro) accounts. Its premium is **security**, not speed.
- **NIBSS (Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System):** runs **NIP (NIBSS Instant Payment)**, which credits beneficiaries **in real time**, and **NEFT (Nigeria Electronic Funds Transfer)**, which works in **batches** with a longer delay. NIBSS also runs automated cheque clearing.
- **RTGS (Real-Time Gross Settlement):** run by the **CBN**; **each payment is settled individually and immediately** across banks' accounts, mainly for **large-value** transfers.
- **Cashless policy:** the CBN policy to reduce cash in circulation. **First piloted in Lagos State from 1 January 2012.**

**Explain more**

- **Internet banking:** the bank's website for balances, history and transfers from home or office. **USSD** (dial a short code such as *XYZ#) is popular in Nigeria because it **works on basic phones without internet data**; mobile apps need a smartphone.
- Paying at a Shoprite till with a debit card and PIN uses a **POS terminal**. The **BVN (Bank Verification Number)** identifies a customer across banks; it is not the PIN, and a **sort code** identifies a bank branch.
- The cashless policy later went nationwide. **Current position (from 1 January 2026):** no cash-deposit limit; weekly cash withdrawals capped at **₦500,000 for individuals and ₦5 million for corporates**, with **3% and 5%** charged on the excess.
- Trap: "cash leaves an electronic record" and "cash has no handling cost" are false, so they are never problems of cash.

### Lesson 9.6: The e-Naira (central bank digital currency)

**Must know**

- **Central bank digital currency (CBDC):** an **electronic form of central bank money** that people use to make digital payments and which can also act as a **store of value**.
- **e-Naira:** Nigeria's CBDC, developed under **Project Giant**; the CBN announced its technical partner for the project on **30 August 2021**; officially **launched in October 2021** (25 October 2021), the first CBDC in Africa.
- It is **legal tender**, issued by the CBN, and **complements (does not replace) the cash naira** at **one e-Naira = one naira**.
- **Benefits:** digital-economy catalyst; better monetary policy (payment data); **financial inclusion**; safer payments; cheaper cross-border trade; banking stability; **lower naira printing cost**; less abuse of the naira, theft and disease from dirty notes.
- **CBDC versus cryptocurrency (4):**

| Point | CBDC (e-Naira) | Cryptocurrency (e.g. Bitcoin) |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | central bank | privately created by users |
| Legal tender | yes | no |
| Volatility | low (fixed 1:1 with the naira) | high |
| Legal backing | backed by law (state) | not backed by law |

**Explain more**

- Challenges: cyber fraud, a foreign technology provider, public enlightenment, teething problems.
- Trap: making the naira **more volatile** for speculation is never a benefit; CBDCs are designed to avoid volatility.
- Do not confuse dates: **January 2012** is the cashless pilot in Lagos; **October 2021** is the e-Naira launch.

### One-minute recap

- Personal cheque = simplest domestic method; draft = branch on branch/head office, rarely dishonoured; bank cheque = same clearing area only.
- Banker's payment = bank pays bank; intra-bank transfer = between a customer's own accounts.
- Standing order = customer starts, fixed amount; direct debit = beneficiary starts under the payer's authority, can be variable.
- No funds, no overdraft, countermand or garnishee order ends the duty to pay; a payee cannot cancel.
- Total debit = amount + commission + postage; pay only if the balance after stays CR or within the overdraft limit.
- Open account riskiest; letter of credit surest; issuing = importer's bank, advising = exporter's bank; D/P against payment, D/A against acceptance.
- Nostro = "our" account abroad; Vostro = "your" account with us. SWIFT sends messages, not money.
- ATM = card + PIN; debit card = own account now; charge card = settle in full. NIP real time, NEFT batch, RTGS large-value; cashless pilot Lagos, 1 January 2012.
- e-Naira launched October 2021, legal tender, 1:1 with the naira, complements cash.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Mrs Bello's account opened the month at ₦2,000 DR. She paid in a cheque of ₦9,500, issued a cheque of ₦4,800 and was charged ₦150 for a cheque book. Her standing order of ₦6,000 falls due at month end; the bank charges ½% commission and ₦50 postage, and her overdraft limit is ₦2,000. Will the bank pay?

*Solution:*
1. Balance before the order: −₦2,000 + ₦9,500 − ₦4,800 − ₦150 = ₦2,550 CR.
2. Commission: 0.5% × ₦6,000 = ₦30. Charges: ₦30 + ₦50 = ₦80.
3. Total debit: ₦6,000 + ₦80 = ₦6,080.
4. Balance after: ₦2,550 − ₦6,080 = ₦3,530 DR.
5. ₦3,530 DR is beyond the ₦2,000 limit, so the bank **will not pay** and incurs no liability for not paying.
