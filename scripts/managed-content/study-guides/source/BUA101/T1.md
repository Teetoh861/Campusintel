## Topic 1: Developing a Business Mindset

**What you must know:** what a business is and how it adds value; types of business and stakeholders; making the leap from buyer to seller; the business environments (internal, external, general, task) and Nigerian regulators; the functional areas; careers in business; the seven traits of professionalism.

### Lesson 1.1: What businesses do

**Must know**

- **Business:** any profit-seeking organisation that provides goods and services designed to satisfy customers' needs. Another definition: purposeful or deliberate activities concerned with the provision of goods and services which are distributed through an organised system (distribution).
- **Primary purpose of a business:** to generate and provide **customer value**. Profit is the reward for doing this well.
- **Adding value:** a business is a system that transforms **lower-value inputs** into **higher-value outputs**. At every stage it adds value to make the product appealing to the next customer in the chain.
- **Business model:** a concise description of how a business intends to **generate revenue**.
- **Revenue** = money brought in from sales. **Profit** = revenue minus costs.

**3 major types of organisation:**

1. **Not-for-profit (nonprofit):** provides goods and services **without a profit motive**, e.g. the World Health Organization (WHO), the Red Cross, a mosque or church charity.
2. **Goods-producing:** creates value by making "things", mostly **tangible** (digital products such as software are the notable exception).
3. **Service:** creates value by **performing activities** that deliver a benefit, e.g. banks, hairdressers, consultants, MTN's call and data services.

| Type | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Makes a product and sells it | Dangote Cement |
| Wholesale | Sells goods in **large quantities to retailers** | A distributor supplying shops |
| Retail | Sells to **final consumers** | Shoprite, a corner shop |
| Service-based | Profits by providing services, not goods | Bank, salon, software developer |

**Stakeholders and what they receive**

| Stakeholder | Gives the business | Receives |
|---|---|---|
| Shareholders (owners) | Capital | **Dividends** |
| Employees | Labour | **Wages and salaries** |
| Customers | Payment | Goods and services (value) |
| Government | **Infrastructure**, law and order | **Taxes** |
| Community | Location, goodwill | **Community services**, jobs |
| Suppliers | Inputs | Payment |

**Explain more**

- A **software development company** that builds systems for clients sells its expertise, so it is a **service business**. A car dealership, bakery or furniture store deals in physical goods.
- McDonald's, Apple and Tesla are profit-seeking; WHO is not-for-profit. Nonprofits still need money and good management; they simply do not distribute profit to owners.
- Understanding what businesses do means recognising their **creation of products and services**, not tax avoidance or isolation from competition.
- Trap: **dividends** go to shareholders; **wages** go to employees. "Provision of infrastructure" is what government gives, not what shareholders receive.

### Lesson 1.2: Making the leap from buyer to seller

**Must know**

- As a consumer you already know a lot: the impact of poor customer service, product value, and the whole experience of searching for, buying and owning products.
- **3 steps to make the leap from consumer to business professional:**

1. **Seeing business from the inside out:** think about the cost of making, promoting and distributing a product, not just buying it; see business as an integrated system of **inputs, processes and outputs**.
2. **Appreciating the role of business in society.**
3. **Using this course to jump-start your career.**

- **4 ways business benefits society:** (1) offering valuable goods and services; (2) providing employment; (3) paying taxes; (4) contributing to national growth, stability and security.
- **4 negative effects:** (1) generating pollution and creating waste; (2) creating health and safety risks; (3) disrupting communities; (4) causing financial instability.

**Explain more**

- Making the leap begins with **grasping customer preferences**: what customers want and value. Ignoring market shifts or chasing profit margins alone is the opposite.
- A **business mindset** means **comprehending operational success factors**: the many decisions and challenges a company must handle before it can deliver products that satisfy customers. It is not targeting only quick wins or shunning all uncertainty.
- The course builds a business vocabulary, introduces jobs (accounting, economics, HR, management, finance, marketing) and shows the legal and ethical questions managers face.

### Lesson 1.3: The environments of business

**Must know**

- No business operates in a vacuum. **Business environment:** all the forces, inside and outside a firm, that affect and are affected by it.
- **2 types:** **internal** and **external**.

| Internal environment | External environment |
|---|---|
| Inside the firm; **controllable** | Outside the firm; **uncontrollable** |
| Staff, structure, resources, management, **organisational (workplace) culture**, values, operational procedures | Economy, technology, politics, law, society, competitors, customers |

- The external environment splits into:
  - **General (macro) forces:** affect all firms: **political-legal, economic, socio-cultural, technological** (PESTLE adds legal and environmental/ecological).
  - **Task (micro, operating) forces:** close to the firm: customers, competitors, suppliers, investors.

**The 5 environments every business operates in:**

1. **Social:** trends and forces in society at large.
2. **Technological:** forces from the practical application of science to innovations, products and processes.
3. **Economic:** conditions and forces that affect the cost and availability of goods, services and labour, and so shape the behaviour of buyers and sellers.
4. **Legal and regulatory:** laws and regulations at local, state, national and international levels.
5. **Market:** target customers, the buying influences that shape their behaviour, and competitors marketing similar products.

- **Features of the business environment:** **dynamic** (always changing), **uncertain**, **relative** (differs by place and firm), complex, multi-faceted. **Certainty is not a feature.**
- **Importance:** identifying opportunities and threats, improving performance, coping with rapid changes, better planning. ("All of these" is the answer.)
- **Environmental scanning:** careful monitoring of the internal and external environments to detect early signs of opportunities and threats that may influence current and future plans.

**Explain more**

- **Political-legal environment** = the relationship between **business and government**. Customs, values, attitudes and demographics are the **socio-cultural** environment; intensity of competition is the **market/competitive** environment.
- Technological conditions are a **general** force; customers, competitors and investors are task forces.
- Emerging technology and political change are **external**; staff dynamics, corporate values and operating protocols are **internal**.
- Traps: SWOT analysis uses scanning results (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats) but is not the definition of scanning. PESTLE covers only the external macro environment.

### Lesson 1.4: Regulators and the Nigerian legal environment

**Must know**

| Body | Full name | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** | Corporate Affairs Commission | Established by the **Companies and Allied Matters Act** (first 1990; now **CAMA 2020**) and **implements its provisions**: registers companies, business names and incorporated trustees; regulates their formation, management and winding up |
| **SON** | Standards Organisation of Nigeria | Sets and enforces **product standards** |
| **SEC** | Securities and Exchange Commission | **Apex regulator of the Nigerian capital market** |
| **NEPC** | Nigerian Export Promotion Council | Promotes **non-oil exports** |
| **NAFDAC** | National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control | Regulates food, drugs, cosmetics, chemicals and packaged water |

**Functions of SON:**

1. Organises **tests** and does what is necessary to ensure compliance with approved standards.
2. Investigates the **quality of facilities, materials and products** used in production.
3. **Registers and regulates standard marks and specifications.**
4. **Certifies products** in commerce and industry (also factories and laboratories).
5. **Advises government** on problems relating to standards specification.

**Functions of NEPC:** provides **grants and incentives** to exporters, provides **information on products** and markets, provides **lists of exportable products**, and trains exporters.

**Functions of SEC:** registers securities and capital market operators, protects investors, **disseminates information to investors on securities**, and keeps the market fair and transparent.

**Challenges of complying with government regulations:**

1. **Corruption.**
2. **Costs of compliance** (fees, paperwork, staff time, upgrades).
3. **Complexity of regulations.**
4. Political **instability** and frequent policy changes.

**Explain more**

- Trap: **political stability** is not a compliance challenge; it helps business.
- The stock exchange (Nigerian Exchange, NGX, formerly the Nigerian Stock Exchange, NSE) is a market **regulated by SEC**; it is not the apex regulator.
- "Providing opportunities for businesses to raise funds" is a capital market role, **not** SON's. "Disseminating information on securities" is SEC's, **not** NEPC's.
- Testing the **quality of materials and facilities** points to **SON**, not NAFDAC; NAFDAC is about food, drugs and similar regulated products.

### Lesson 1.5: The major functional areas

**Must know**

**The 6 major functional areas:**

1. **Research and development (R&D):** conceiving and designing new products.
2. **Manufacturing, production and operations:** where the company makes what it makes, or does what it does (service firms).
3. **Marketing, sales, distribution and customer support:** marketing identifies opportunities and customer wants, works with R&D to develop products, creates branding and advertising, and sets prices; sales builds relationships and persuades customers to buy; distribution moves products, before and after the sale; customer support helps buyers after purchase.
4. **Finance and accounting:** ensures the company has the funds it needs, monitors and controls spending, and drafts reports for management, investors and government regulators.
5. **Human resources (HR):** recruiting, hiring, developing and supporting employees.
6. **Business services:** law, banking, real estate and similar, done in-house, by outside firms or both.

**Other functions you will meet in options:**

- **Employment relations and HRM:** puts the **right people in the right posts under the right conditions**; handles **remuneration** (pay and benefits).
- **Insurance and risk management:** identifies risks such as **safety issues that could lead to lawsuits**, and **implements plans and strategies to minimise business losses** (avoid, reduce, retain or transfer risk, e.g. by buying insurance).
- **Information technology (IT):** systems that deliver the right information to the right people at the right time; supports every other area.

- **Reasons for separating operations into functional areas:** **efficiency, expertise, effectiveness** (through specialisation). **Increased costs is not a reason.**

**Explain more**

- **HR activities:** **acquisition** (getting staff, made up of **recruitment** then **selection**), development (training), compensation, maintenance. **Recruitment** = advertising to attract a **pool of candidates**; **selection** = choosing from that pool.
- Marketing **identifies customer wants and develops products** to satisfy them, and covers **advertising, selling and distributing**; "market outreach" (promoting products) is marketing. Process optimisation is operations; fiscal planning is finance; product innovation is R&D.
- **Mining** is an industry, not a functional area.
- Production, R&D and HR together aid business processes toward profit and essential services. Dividends, wages and regular orders are not functions.
- Not every company has a formal R&D function; many service firms and small businesses do not, though most still need to improve their offerings.

### Lesson 1.6: Careers in business and professionalism

**Must know**

**7 important business professions:**

1. **Operations manager:** all people and processes that create the goods and perform the services a company sells.
2. **Human resources specialist:** recruiting, training and development, compensation and benefits, employee and labour relations, health and safety.
3. **Information technology manager:** designs, implements and maintains information systems.
4. **Marketing specialist:** branding, e-commerce, advertising, public relations, social media.
5. **Sales professional:** suits people who thrive on competition, enjoy solving problems and like working with many people.
6. **Accountant:** management accountants **collect, analyse and report** on financial matters.
7. **Financial manager:** **controllers** oversee financial reports and often manage accounting; **treasurers and finance officers** set long-term financial goals and budgets, **invest the firm's funds and raise capital**.

- **Professionalism:** the quality of performing at a high level and conducting oneself with **purpose and pride**.
- **7 traits:** (1) striving to excel; (2) being dependable and accountable; (3) being a team player; (4) communicating effectively; (5) demonstrating etiquette; (6) making ethical decisions; (7) maintaining a confident, positive outlook.

**Explain more**

- Accountant vs financial manager: the accountant **records and reports**; the financial manager **plans and acts** on the numbers. A "financial resource director" manages a company's financial resources.
- **Logistics and distribution** is a business career; structural engineering, clinical healthcare and crop cultivation are not.
- **Dependable:** meet commitments, on schedule and within budget. **Team player:** pull your own weight while watching the team's success. **Etiquette:** expected norms of behaviour in a situation. **Ethical decisions:** avoid ethical lapses; weigh options carefully in ethical dilemmas.
- **7 guidelines for communicating effectively:** listen actively; provide practical information; give facts rather than vague impressions; do not present opinions as facts; be concise and efficient; clarify expectations and responsibilities; offer compelling, persuasive arguments and recommendations.
- Trap: professionalism is **not** holding a degree, wearing formal clothes or obeying without question.

### One-minute recap

- Business = profit-seeking provider of goods and services; purpose = customer value; it adds value by turning inputs into higher-value outputs.
- Business model = how a business intends to generate revenue.
- Types: not-for-profit, goods-producing, service; also manufacturing, wholesale (to retailers), retail (to consumers).
- Shareholders get dividends, employees wages, government taxes.
- Leap from buyer to seller: inside-out view, role in society, jump-start your career.
- Environments: internal (controllable) vs external (uncontrollable); general vs task; five environments: social, technological, economic, legal-regulatory, market.
- CAC (CAMA), SON (standards), SEC (apex capital market regulator), NEPC (exports).
- Six functional areas: R&D, production/operations, marketing, finance and accounting, HR, business services; plus insurance and risk management.
- Seven professions; professionalism has seven traits.
