## Topic 3: Functions, Goals and Components of SCM

**What you must know:** the main functions SCM performs; the SCOR model's six processes; the goals of SCM, including supply chain surplus and the efficiency versus responsiveness trade-off; the components of a supply chain, viewed as its members and as its six drivers; why integration and collaboration matter, and what flexibility, visibility, velocity and agility mean.

### Lesson 3.1: Functions of supply chain management

**Must know**

- **Functions of SCM** = the main jobs SCM performs to move value from suppliers to customers:
  1. **Purchasing and procurement:** sourcing materials and services from suppliers.
  2. **Operations and production management:** turning inputs into products efficiently.
  3. **Logistics and distribution:** transporting, storing and delivering goods.
  4. **Inventory (resource) management:** holding the right amount of stock in the right place.
  5. **Demand planning and forecasting:** predicting demand and planning supply to match it.
  6. **Information management:** sharing accurate data (orders, stock levels, forecasts) along the chain.
  7. **Supplier relationship management:** selecting, developing and working closely with suppliers.
  8. **Customer service:** meeting customer requirements and handling orders and complaints.
  9. **Returns management (reverse logistics):** handling returned, faulty or end-of-life products.
  10. **Supply chain network design (facility location):** deciding the number, location and capacity of plants, warehouses and distribution centres.
- **The SCOR model** (Supply Chain Operations Reference model, developed by the Supply Chain Council in 1996, now kept by ASCM) groups supply chain work into **6 processes:**
  1. **Plan:** balance demand and supply.
  2. **Source:** obtain materials and services.
  3. **Make:** turn materials into finished products.
  4. **Deliver:** manage orders, transport and distribution.
  5. **Return:** handle returns of products in either direction.
  6. **Enable:** manage the rules, data, performance, risks and resources that support the other five.

**Explain more**

- The functions match the elements in Topic 2. Purchasing, inventory, production and transportation are elements; the functions describe the work done in and across them.
- A quick way to remember SCOR's first five: a firm must **plan** what to make, **source** what it needs, **make** the product, **deliver** it, and accept **returns**. "Enable" sits behind all five.
- Information management is the function that ties the others together. Without shared information, each firm in the chain makes decisions in the dark (recall the bullwhip effect in Topic 2).

### Lesson 3.2: Goals of supply chain management

**Must know**

- **The goals (objectives) of SCM:**
  1. **Customer satisfaction:** deliver what customers want, when and where they want it.
  2. **Cost reduction:** lower purchasing, production, transport and inventory costs.
  3. **Efficiency:** use resources well and cut waste.
  4. **Responsiveness:** react quickly to changes in demand.
  5. **Quality:** deliver products that meet the required standard.
  6. **Shorter lead times:** reduce the time from order to delivery.
  7. **Inventory optimisation:** hold neither too much nor too little stock.
  8. **Flexibility and resilience:** adapt to and recover from disruptions.
  9. **Sustainability:** reduce harm to the environment and society.
  10. **Competitive advantage:** perform better than rival supply chains.
- **Supply chain surplus** = value of the product to the customer minus total supply chain cost. The overall goal of SCM is to **maximise the supply chain surplus**.
- **Efficiency versus responsiveness:**

| Point | Efficient supply chain | Responsive supply chain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main aim | Lowest cost | Speed and flexibility |
| Best for | Functional products with stable, predictable demand | Innovative products with unpredictable demand |
| Inventory | Low | Buffer stock kept to meet surges |
| Example | Salt, sugar, bottled water | Fashion items, new phones |

- **Fisher (1997)** argued that **functional** products need **efficient** supply chains and **innovative** products need **responsive** supply chains.

**Explain more**

- Worked example of supply chain surplus: a customer is willing to pay ₦6,000 for a carton of juice. The total cost of producing and delivering it along the whole chain (fruit, processing, packaging, transport, retail) is ₦4,200. Supply chain surplus = ₦6,000 − ₦4,200 = **₦1,800**. This surplus is shared between the customer (if the price is below ₦6,000) and the firms in the chain. Better SCM raises the surplus by cutting cost or adding value.
- Goals can conflict. Holding more stock improves customer satisfaction (fewer stockouts) but raises cost. Faster delivery by air improves responsiveness but costs more. Good SCM finds the balance that fits the product.
- Trap: "the main goal of SCM is to minimise transport cost" is too narrow. The overall goal is to **maximise total value (supply chain surplus)** for the chain as a whole.

### Lesson 3.3: Components of a supply chain

**Must know**

- **Components as members (the parties in the chain):**
  1. Suppliers (raw materials, components)
  2. Manufacturers or producers
  3. Distributors and wholesalers
  4. Retailers
  5. Customers (final consumers)
  6. Service providers that support the chain: transport and logistics companies, warehouses, banks, insurers, IT providers
- **Components as drivers (the 6 drivers of supply chain performance, Chopra and Meindl):**

| Driver | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Facilities | Plants, warehouses and stores: where, how many, how big |
| Inventory | Raw materials, WIP and finished goods held in the chain |
| Transportation | Moving goods between stages: modes and routes |
| Information | Data on demand, stock, orders and costs shared along the chain |
| Sourcing | Who performs each activity: in-house or outsourced, which suppliers |
| Pricing | How much to charge, which affects customer demand and the use of capacity |

- The first three (facilities, inventory, transportation) are **logistical** drivers; the last three (information, sourcing, pricing) are **cross-functional** drivers.

**Explain more**

- The member view answers "**who** is in the chain?". The driver view answers "**what** do managers adjust to improve the chain?". Both describe components; read the question carefully to see which it wants.
- Example of drivers at work: a cement company can open more depots (facilities), keep more stock in each depot (inventory), switch from road to rail (transportation), share sales data with distributors (information), outsource haulage to a logistics firm (sourcing), and offer lower prices in the rainy season to smooth demand (pricing).
- The SCOR processes from Lesson 3.1 (plan, source, make, deliver, return, enable) are also sometimes called the **components** of SCM. If a question asks for "the components of SCM" and gives Plan, Source, Make, Deliver and Return as options, it is using the SCOR view.

### Lesson 3.4: Integration, collaboration and resilience

**Must know**

- **Supply chain integration:** linking the firms and functions in the chain so they plan and act as **one system** rather than separately.
- **Collaboration:** firms in the chain working together through **information sharing**, **resource sharing**, joint planning and trust.
- **Key qualities of a strong supply chain:**
  - **Flexibility:** ability to change volume, product mix or delivery to meet new conditions.
  - **Visibility:** ability to see what is happening across the chain (stock levels, shipments, demand).
  - **Velocity:** speed at which the chain reacts and recovers.
  - **Agility:** ability to respond quickly and effectively to sudden changes in demand or supply.
- **Supply chain resilience:** the ability of a supply chain to **prepare for, withstand and recover from disruptions** (for example port congestion, fuel scarcity, a supplier's factory shutting down).
- **Benefits of integration and collaboration:** lower costs, less inventory, fewer stockouts, faster response, a weaker bullwhip effect, and stronger resilience.

**Explain more**

- Sharing resources with suppliers is a strong way to build resilience, and collaboration also makes a chain more flexible. For a Nigerian food and beverage firm, flexibility, agility and collaboration together help it keep performing when roads, ports or fuel supply are disrupted.
- Visibility in practice: a beverage company whose distributors share their daily stock levels can see a depot running low in Kano before customers notice, and send stock in time.
- Collaboration is not the same as integration. Collaboration is the behaviour (working together, sharing); integration is the result (the chain acts as one system).

### One-minute recap

- Functions: purchasing, operations, logistics, inventory, demand planning, information, supplier relationships, customer service, returns, network design (facility location).
- SCOR (Supply Chain Council, 1996): Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, Return, Enable.
- Goals: customer satisfaction, lower cost, efficiency, responsiveness, quality, shorter lead times, optimal inventory, resilience, sustainability, competitive advantage.
- Overall goal: maximise supply chain surplus = customer value minus total supply chain cost.
- Fisher (1997): functional products need efficient chains; innovative products need responsive chains.
- Components as members: suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, retailers, customers, service providers.
- Six drivers (Chopra and Meindl): facilities, inventory, transportation, information, sourcing, pricing.
- Integration, collaboration, flexibility, visibility, velocity, agility and resilience make a strong chain.

### Theory practice

**Question:** State five goals of supply chain management and explain why "maximising supply chain surplus" is the overall goal.

*Answer:* Five goals of SCM are customer satisfaction, cost reduction, efficiency, responsiveness and quality (others include shorter lead times, optimal inventory, resilience, sustainability and competitive advantage). Supply chain surplus is the value of the product to the customer minus the total cost of the whole supply chain. Maximising it is the overall goal because it captures all the other goals at once: cutting costs raises the surplus, and so does adding value the customer will pay for through quality, speed and good service. It also shifts attention from one firm's cost to the performance of the whole chain, which is what SCM is about.
