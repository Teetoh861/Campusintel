## Topic 1: Overview of Logistics and Supply Chain Management

**What you must know:** what a supply chain is and what supply chain management (SCM) means; why SCM is also called value chain management and how value is added at each stage; the three flows in a supply chain; what logistics is and how it differs from SCM; who suppliers are and how to select the right supplier; how to trace a real Nigerian supply chain from supplier to consumer.

### Lesson 1.1: Meaning of supply chain and supply chain management

**Must know**

- **Supply chain:** the network of organisations, people, activities, information and resources involved in moving a product or service from the **supplier** of raw materials to the **final consumer**. It includes every party involved, directly or indirectly, in meeting a customer's request.
- **Supply chain management (SCM):** the **movement of value from the suppliers until it reaches the final consumers**. Put fully, it is the management of the movement of **goods, services, information and ideas** from the **supplier**, to the **organisation**, to the **distributors**, until they reach the **final consumers**, with **value added at each stage**.
- **Formal definition (Mentzer et al., 2001):** SCM is the systemic, strategic coordination of business functions **within a company and across the businesses in the supply chain**, to improve the long-term performance of each company and of the supply chain as a whole.
- **Basic stages of a supply chain:**
  1. Suppliers (raw materials, components)
  2. Manufacturer or firm (converts inputs into products)
  3. Distributors and wholesalers
  4. Retailers
  5. Final consumers
- **Three flows in every supply chain:**

| Flow | Direction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product (goods/services) flow | Mainly downstream: supplier to consumer | Cartons of milk moving from factory to supermarket |
| Information flow | Both ways | Orders, forecasts, delivery updates |
| Funds (money) flow | Mainly upstream: consumer to supplier | Payment for goods, credit terms |

- **Upstream** = the supplier side of the firm. **Downstream** = the customer side of the firm.
- The **final customer is the only source of new money (revenue)** for the whole supply chain. Every other payment is money passing between members of the chain.

**Explain more**

- Notice that SCM is about **value**, not just boxes. A bag of raw cocoa beans is worth little to a student in Akoka; by the time it has been processed, packed, branded, transported and placed on a shelf as a chocolate drink, each stage has added something the customer will pay for.
- SCM goes **beyond one firm**. A company can run its own factory perfectly and still fail if its supplier delivers late or its distributor runs out of stock. SCM coordinates all the firms in the chain so they work as one system.
- Goods are not the only thing that moves. **Services** (a bank processing payments), **information** (a retailer sharing sales figures with the manufacturer) and **ideas** (product designs, customer feedback) also travel along the chain.
- Trap: an exam may say "SCM is the management of the physical movement of goods only." That describes **logistics**, not SCM. SCM is wider.
- Trap: the funds flow runs **upstream** (from customer back towards the supplier), the opposite direction to the product flow. Information flows **both ways**.

### Lesson 1.2: SCM as value chain management

**Must know**

- SCM is also called **value chain management** because the chain exists to **add value at every stage** until the product reaches the final consumer.
- **Value chain (Michael Porter, 1985):** the set of activities a firm performs to design, produce, market, deliver and support its product. Each activity should add value, and the difference between the value created and the cost of creating it is the firm's **margin**.
- Porter's **5 primary activities:**
  1. Inbound logistics (receiving, storing and handling inputs)
  2. Operations (turning inputs into products)
  3. Outbound logistics (storing and distributing finished products)
  4. Marketing and sales
  5. Service (after-sales support)
- Porter's **4 support activities:**
  1. Firm infrastructure (general management, finance, planning)
  2. Human resource management
  3. Technology development
  4. Procurement (purchasing)
- **Value added** = what a stage adds to the worth of the product in the eyes of the customer (for example processing, packaging, transport to a convenient place, timely delivery).

**Explain more**

- The value chain looks **inside one firm**; the supply chain links **many firms' value chains** together. A manufacturer's outbound logistics becomes the distributor's inbound logistics.
- Worked example of value added in ₦: a farmer sells a 50 kg bag of maize for ₦45,000. A mill cleans and grinds it and sells the flour for ₦62,000 (value added by the mill = ₦62,000 − ₦45,000 = **₦17,000**). A distributor carries it to Ibadan and sells it for ₦66,500 (value added = **₦4,500**). A shop sells it to consumers in smaller packs for a total of ₦72,000 (value added = **₦5,500**). Total value added along the chain = ₦72,000 − ₦45,000 = **₦27,000**, which equals ₦17,000 + ₦4,500 + ₦5,500. (These figures ignore the stages' own costs; they show how the price grows as each stage adds something the customer pays for.)
- Trap: procurement is a **support** activity in Porter's model, while inbound and outbound logistics are **primary** activities. Students often swap these.

### Lesson 1.3: Logistics and how it differs from SCM

**Must know**

- **Logistics:** the **management of the physical movement of goods**. More fully, it is the part of SCM that plans, implements and controls the efficient, effective **forward and reverse** flow and **storage** of goods, services and related information between the **point of origin** and the **point of consumption**, to meet customers' requirements.
- **Main logistics activities:** transportation, warehousing, inventory management, materials handling, packaging, order processing.
- **Types by direction:**
  1. **Inbound logistics:** bringing materials from suppliers into the firm.
  2. **Outbound logistics:** moving finished goods from the firm to customers.
  3. **Reverse logistics:** moving goods back from customers (returns, recycling, repairs).
- **The 7 Rs of logistics:** getting the **right product**, in the **right quantity**, in the **right condition**, to the **right place**, at the **right time**, to the **right customer**, at the **right cost**.
- **Logistics versus SCM:**

| Point | Logistics | Supply chain management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Narrower: physical movement and storage of goods | Wider: all activities and firms from supplier to consumer |
| Focus | Moving and storing goods efficiently | Coordinating and adding value across the whole chain |
| Players | Mainly one firm and its transport and warehouse partners | Suppliers, firm, distributors, retailers, customers |
| Relationship | A part of SCM | Includes logistics, purchasing, planning, production and more |

**Explain more**

- A simple way to remember it: **all logistics is part of SCM, but not all SCM is logistics.** Choosing a supplier, negotiating prices or forecasting demand are SCM activities, but they are not logistics.
- Think of a soft-drink company. Logistics is the trucks, depots and stock rooms that get crates to shops. SCM also covers buying sugar and bottles, planning how much to produce, and agreeing terms with distributors.
- Reverse logistics matters in Nigeria for things like returnable glass bottles, empty gas cylinders, and plastic waste collected for recycling.

### Lesson 1.4: Suppliers and selecting the right supplier

**Must know**

- **Suppliers:** organisations and intermediaries that **provide raw materials or finished goods** (and sometimes services) to a firm.
- **Selecting the right supplier**, the 4 key points:
  1. **Capability:** can the supplier deliver the right quality and quantity, on time, every time? (capacity, technology, skills)
  2. **Antecedents:** the supplier's **track record and history**: past performance, reputation and reliability with other buyers.
  3. **Understanding of the organisation's goals:** does the supplier understand and support what the firm is trying to achieve (quality standards, delivery schedules, values)?
  4. **Multiple options:** do not depend on only one supplier; have **alternatives** so that a failure by one supplier does not stop production.
- **Other common supplier selection criteria:** quality, price (total cost), delivery reliability and lead time, financial stability, location, after-sales service, ethics and environmental practices.
- **Dickson (1966)** studied 23 supplier selection criteria; **quality**, **delivery** and **performance history** were rated among the most important.
- **Single sourcing** = buying an item from one supplier. **Multiple sourcing** = buying it from two or more suppliers.

**Explain more**

- "Antecedents" simply means what came before. A supplier that has supplied a bottling company reliably for ten years has strong antecedents; a new supplier with no history is a bigger risk.
- Why multiple options? If a firm buys all its packaging from one supplier and that supplier's factory shuts down, the firm's own production stops. Having two or three approved suppliers spreads this **risk**. The trade-off is that single sourcing can bring a closer relationship and better prices because of volume.
- Lowest price is **not** the same as best supplier. A cheap supplier that delivers late or sends faulty materials can cost the firm more in lost production and unhappy customers. This is why firms look at **total cost** and capability, not just the quoted price.
- **Collaborating** with suppliers, especially **sharing resources and information**, helps supply chains recover from disruptions. Choosing a supplier is the start of a relationship, not a one-off purchase.

### Lesson 1.5: A Nigerian supply chain from supplier to consumer

**Must know**

- **The Dangote Petroleum Refinery supply chain:**

| Stage | Who or what | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suppliers | Crude oil suppliers (A, B, C) | Provide the raw material (crude oil) |
| Firm | Dangote Refinery (Lekki, Lagos) | Refines crude into products (adds value) |
| Products | PMS, AGO, DPK, LPG | The finished goods |
| Consumers | Industrial consumers and the public | Use the products |

- **Product abbreviations:**
  - **PMS** = Premium Motor Spirit (petrol)
  - **AGO** = Automotive Gas Oil (diesel)
  - **DPK** = Dual Purpose Kerosene (kerosene)
  - **LPG** = Liquefied Petroleum Gas (cooking gas)
- **Industrial consumers** buy products to use in their own production or operations (for example a factory buying diesel to run generators). **Final (end) consumers** buy for personal use.

**Explain more**

- Read the chain from left to right: **suppliers** provide crude oil, the **firm** refines it, the **products** come out, and they go to **consumers**. Value is added at the refining stage, because crude oil cannot be put straight into a car or a gas cooker.
- The refinery uses **several crude suppliers**, which is the "multiple options" point from Lesson 1.4 in action: if one source is delayed, others can keep the refinery running.
- Between the refinery and consumers sit **distributors**: depot owners, tanker transporters and filling stations. Each one adds value (storage, transport, convenience) and takes a margin.
- The same pattern works for any product: cocoa farmers → processor → distributors → supermarkets → you. Practise drawing this diagram for any product you buy.

### One-minute recap

- Supply chain = every party, activity and resource from supplier to final consumer.
- SCM = movement of value (goods, services, information, ideas) from supplier to organisation to distributors to final consumers, with value added at each stage.
- Three flows: product (downstream), information (both ways), funds (upstream).
- SCM = value chain management; Porter (1985): 5 primary and 4 support activities; margin = value created minus cost.
- Logistics = management of the physical movement (and storage) of goods; inbound, outbound, reverse; 7 Rs.
- Logistics is a part of SCM; SCM is wider.
- Suppliers provide raw materials or finished goods. Select on capability, antecedents (track record), understanding of the organisation's goals, and multiple options.
- Dangote Refinery: crude suppliers → refinery → PMS, AGO, DPK, LPG → industrial consumers.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Define supply chain management and, using the Dangote Refinery as an example, explain how value is added as products move from suppliers to consumers.

*Answer:* Supply chain management is the movement of value from suppliers until it reaches the final consumers: the management of goods, services, information and ideas as they move from the supplier, to the organisation, to distributors and finally to consumers, with value added at each stage. In the Dangote Refinery chain, crude oil suppliers provide the raw material. The refinery adds value by refining the crude into usable products: PMS (petrol), AGO (diesel), DPK (kerosene) and LPG (cooking gas). Distributors such as depots, tanker transporters and filling stations add further value by storing the products and bringing them close to where they are needed. Industrial consumers and the public then buy and use them. Each stage turns the product into something more useful to the customer, which is why SCM is also called value chain management.
