## Topic 4: Foundation of Financial Mathematics

**What you must know:** time value of money; simple and compound interest; future and present value of a lump sum (including intra-year and continuous compounding); effective vs nominal rates; solving for time and rate; annuities (ordinary, due, deferred), perpetuities, sinking funds and loan amortisation; arithmetic and geometric progressions; investment, consumption and exchange.

### Lesson 4.1: Time value of money, simple and compound interest

**Must know**

- **Time value of money (TVM):** money has different values at different times. A naira today is worth **more** than a naira tomorrow (present funds outvalue future funds). TVM is a fundamental concept in **finance and investment decisions**, because the **timing** of cash flows affects which choice is best. Its key components are **future value** and **present value**.
- **Interest:** the payment made for the use of money.
- **Why a naira today is worth more:**
  1. People prefer to consume now rather than later.
  2. Money held today can be invested to earn interest.
  3. Inflation reduces what money will buy later.
  4. A future receipt is uncertain (risk).
- **Simple interest:** interest earned on the **original amount invested (principal)** only. The principal and the yearly interest stay the same from period to period.
  - SI = P × R × T (P = principal, R = rate per year as a decimal, T = time in years)
  - A = P + PRT = P(1 + RT) (A = total amount at the end)
  - P = A ÷ (1 + RT)
- **Compound interest:** interest earned on the **principal plus previously earned interest**. Interest can be compounded daily, monthly, quarterly, semi-annually or annually.
- **Compounding:** the arithmetic process of finding the **final value** of a payment or series of payments when interest is known.
- **Banker's (ordinary) interest:** simple interest worked on a 360-day year; exact interest uses 365 days.

| | Simple interest | Compound interest |
|---|---|---|
| Interest earned on | Principal only | Principal + past interest |
| Yearly interest | Constant | Rises each year |
| Amounts over time | Arithmetic progression | Geometric progression |
| Result | Lower | Higher (same rate and time) |

**Explain more**

*Example (simple interest):* Betty invests ₦100,000 for 3 years at 10% simple interest. SI = 100,000 × 0.1 × 3 = **₦30,000**. For 4 years it is ₦40,000 and for 5 years ₦50,000: the interest rises by the same ₦10,000 each year.

*Example (amount):* ₦10,000,000 saved for 3 years at 10% simple interest: A = 10,000,000 + 10,000,000 × 0.1 × 3 = **₦13,000,000**.

*Example (finding the principal):* How much must be invested now at 12.5% simple interest to have ₦150,000 in 4 years? P = 150,000 ÷ (1 + 0.125 × 4) = 150,000 ÷ 1.5 = **₦100,000**.

*Example (compound interest, same ₦100,000 at 10% for 3 years):*

| Year | Opening (₦) | Interest at 10% (₦) | Closing (₦) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 110,000 |
| 2 | 110,000 | 11,000 | 121,000 |
| 3 | 121,000 | 12,100 | 133,100 |

Total interest = **₦33,100**, compared with ₦30,000 under simple interest.

- **Trap:** "Interest accrues solely on the initial amount" describes simple, not compound, interest. The simple interest formula is I = P × R × T, not P × R ÷ T.
- **Trap:** when an option gives the interest only and another gives principal plus interest, read whether the question asks "how much interest" or "how much will he have".

### Lesson 4.2: Future value, present value and effective rates of a lump sum

**Must know**

- **Lump sum:** a single amount invested for a specified period at a specified rate.
- **Future (terminal) value:** what a sum today grows to. It takes into account the **impact of time on value**.
  - FV = PV × (1 + r)^n
  - Compounding m times a year: FV = PV × (1 + r/m)^(n × m). Semi-annual m = 2; every four months m = 3; quarterly m = 4; twice a quarter m = 8; monthly m = 12; twice a month m = 24; weekly m = 52; daily m = 365.
  - Continuous compounding: FV = PV × e^(r × n), e = 2.71828
- **Present value:** today's worth of a future cash flow. **Discounting** is the process of finding it; the **discount rate** is used to find current value.
  - PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n = FV × (1 + r)^-n
  - With m periods: PV = FV ÷ (1 + r/m)^(n × m); continuous: PV = FV × e^(-r × n)
- A **higher** interest rate gives a **lower** present value; a longer time also lowers PV.
- **Nominal rate:** the quoted annual rate. **Effective annual rate (EAR):** the true yearly rate once the **frequency of compounding** is allowed for.
  - EAR = (1 + r/m)^m - 1; continuous: EAR = e^r - 1
- **Solving for time:** n = log(FV/PV) ÷ log(1 + r) (log base 10 or ln, both work)
- **Solving for the rate:** r = (FV/PV)^(1/n) - 1; with m periods, r = m × [(FV/PV)^(1/(n × m)) - 1]

**Explain more**

*Example (FV):* ₦10,000,000 at 10% compounded annually for 3 years: FV = 10,000,000 × 1.1^3 = 10,000,000 × 1.331 = **₦13,310,000**.

*Example (fractional years):* ₦800,000 for 4½ years at 12%: FV = 800,000 × 1.12^4.5 = **₦1,332,205**.

*Example (quarterly):* ₦20,000,000 for 5 years at 24% compounded quarterly: rate per quarter = 0.24 ÷ 4 = 0.06, periods = 5 × 4 = 20. FV = 20,000,000 × 1.06^20 = 20,000,000 × 3.2071 = **₦64,142,709** (to the nearest naira).

*Example (twice a quarter):* ₦100,000 at 24% payable eight times a year for 3 years: 0.24 ÷ 8 = 0.03, 3 × 8 = 24 periods. FV = 100,000 × 1.03^24 = **₦203,279.41**.

*Example (continuous):* ₦15,000,000 at 16% for 2 years: FV = 15,000,000 × e^(0.32) = 15,000,000 × 1.3771 = **₦20,656,916** (₦20,656,500 if you round e^0.32 to 1.3771 first). Effective rate = e^0.16 - 1 = 0.1735 = **17.35%**.

*Example (PV):* ₦14,400,000 due in 2 years at 20%: PV = 14,400,000 ÷ 1.2^2 = 14,400,000 ÷ 1.44 = **₦10,000,000**. ₦7,500,000 due in 5 years at 20% compounded continuously: PV = 7,500,000 × e^(-1) = **₦2,759,096**.

*Example (PV, semi-annual):* ₦10m due in 5 years at 10% payable semi-annually: 5% for 10 periods. PV = 10m ÷ 1.05^10 = 10m ÷ 1.6289 = **₦6.14m**. Using 10% annually (₦6.21m) is the trap.

*Example (EAR):* 24% compounded quarterly: EAR = 1.06^4 - 1 = **26.25%**. The more often interest is compounded, the higher the EAR: annual < quarterly < monthly < continuous.

*Example (time):* How long for ₦3.5m to reach ₦45m at 25%? 1.25^n = 45 ÷ 3.5 = 12.8571. n = log 12.8571 ÷ log 1.25 = 1.1091 ÷ 0.0969 = **11.45 years, 11 years to the nearest year**. Round as the question says: "to the nearest year" means normal rounding.

*Example (doubling, tripling, quadrupling):* the PV cancels. To quadruple at 12%: 1.12^n = 4, n = log 4 ÷ log 1.12 = 0.6021 ÷ 0.0492 = **12.23 years**.

*Example (time with quarterly compounding):* ₦2m to ₦3m at 12% compounded quarterly: 1.03^k = 1.5, k = log 1.5 ÷ log 1.03 = 13.72 quarters, so n = 13.72 ÷ 4 = **3.43 years**. Trap: 13.72 is quarters, not years.

*Example (rate):* ₦100,000 grows to ₦133,100 in 3 years: (1 + r)^3 = 1.331, 1 + r = 1.331^(1/3) = 1.1, **r = 10%**. With quarterly compounding, ₦10,000 to ₦20,000 in 5 years: (1 + r/4)^20 = 2, r/4 = 2^(0.05) - 1 = 0.03526, **r = 14.10%** (rounded). Trap: 3.53% is only the quarterly rate.

### Lesson 4.3: Annuities and perpetuities

**Must know**

- **Annuity:** a series of **equal**, regular, periodic payments or receipts for a specified number of periods. It need not be annual: weekly, monthly or any regular interval counts.
- **Types of annuity (3):**
  1. **Ordinary annuity:** payments at the **end** of each period.
  2. **Annuity due:** payments at the **beginning** of each period.
  3. **Perpetual annuity (perpetuity):** equal payments expected **indefinitely**.
- The **two major types** are ordinary annuity and annuity due.
- **FV of ordinary annuity** (sum of all payments plus compound interest on each): FV = A × [(1 + r)^n - 1] ÷ r
- **PV of ordinary annuity** (amount to invest today to fund the series): PV = A × [1 - (1 + r)^-n] ÷ r
- **FV of annuity due:** FV = A × [(1 + r)^(n+1) - (1 + r)] ÷ r = ordinary FV × (1 + r)
- **PV of annuity due:** PV = A × [(1 + r) - (1 + r)^-(n-1)] ÷ r = ordinary PV × (1 + r)
- **PV of perpetuity:** PV = A ÷ r
- **Deferred annuity** (first payment later than one period away): PV = A × [1 - (1 + r)^-n] ÷ r × (1 + r)^-k, where k = periods of delay before the ordinary annuity starts.
- **Net present value:** NPV = PV of inflows - initial cost. Accept if NPV is positive.

**Explain more**

*Example (FV ordinary):* ₦20,000,000 deposited at the end of each of 3 years at 20%. Year by year: end of year 1 ₦20m; year 2: 20m + 4m interest + 20m = ₦44m; year 3: 44m + 8.8m + 20m = ₦72.8m. By formula: 20,000,000 × (1.2^3 - 1) ÷ 0.2 = 20,000,000 × 3.64 = **₦72,800,000**. Only two years of interest are earned, because the last payment earns none.

*Example (FV due):* the same deposits at the beginning of each year: 20,000,000 × (1.2^4 - 1.2) ÷ 0.2 = **₦87,360,000**, which is 72,800,000 × 1.2.

*Example (PV ordinary):* Joshua must pay ₦120,000 at the end of each of 3 years; money earns 12%. PV = 120,000 × [1 - 1.12^-3] ÷ 0.12 = 120,000 × 2.4018 = **₦288,219.75**. Proof: 288,219.75 earns ₦34,586.37 in year 1, leaving 202,806.12 after paying 120,000; then 24,336.73 interest leaves 107,142.85; then 12,857.14 interest leaves 0.

*Example (PV due):* the same ₦120,000 at the beginning of each year: PV = 120,000 × [1.12 - 1.12^-2] ÷ 0.12 = **₦322,806.12** (= 288,219.75 × 1.12).

*Example (perpetuity):* Chukwu expects ₦250,000 a year forever at 20%: PV = 250,000 ÷ 0.2 = **₦1,250,000**. A bond paying ₦1,200,000 a year in perpetuity at 10% is worth ₦12,000,000.

*Example (deferred):* ₦200,000 a year for 5 years, first payment at the end of year 4, at 10%. Value at end of year 3 = 200,000 × 3.7908 = ₦758,157.35. Discount 3 years: 758,157.35 ÷ 1.331 = **₦569,614.84**. Trap: stopping at ₦758,157.35.

*Example (solving n, ordinary):* ₦50,000 at the end of each year at 10% to reach ₦500,000: (1.1^n - 1) ÷ 0.1 = 10, so 1.1^n = 2, n = log 2 ÷ log 1.1 = **7.27 years**. For an annuity due, divide by (1 + r) first: 1.1^n = 1 + 500,000 × 0.1 ÷ (50,000 × 1.1) = 1.9091, n = **6.78 years**.

*Example (NPV):* a project costs ₦100,000 and returns ₦30,000 a year for 4 years at 8%. PV of inflows = 30,000 × 3.3121 = ₦99,363.81; NPV = 99,363.81 - 100,000 = **-₦636.19**, so reject. Trap: an option showing only the PV of inflows.

*Mixed streams:* value each stream at its own rate and add. A monthly salary uses the monthly rate and number of months (3 years = 36 months); a yearly bonus uses the yearly rate and 3 years.

- **Trap:** an annuity due is always worth more than the ordinary annuity by the factor (1 + r), for both PV and FV.

### Lesson 4.4: Sinking funds and loan amortisation

**Must know**

- **Sinking fund:** equal periodic deposits made to accumulate a target sum at a future date (an FV-of-annuity problem). Deposit A = FV × r ÷ [(1 + r)^n - 1]
- **Loan amortisation:** repaying a loan by equal instalments that cover interest and part of the principal (a PV-of-annuity problem). Instalment A = Loan × r ÷ [1 - (1 + r)^-n]
- In each instalment, **interest** = opening balance × r; **principal repaid** = instalment - interest; closing balance = opening balance - principal repaid.
- **Outstanding balance** after k instalments = PV of the instalments still to be paid.

**Explain more**

*Example (sinking fund):* a school wants ₦1,000,000 in 4 years, saving at the end of each year at 10%. A = 1,000,000 × 0.1 ÷ (1.1^4 - 1) = 100,000 ÷ 0.4641 = **₦215,470.80**. Fund after 2 years = 215,470.80 × 1.1 + 215,470.80 = 215,470.80 × 2.1 = **₦452,488.69**.

*Example (amortisation):* ₦500,000 borrowed at 12% for 3 years. A = 500,000 × 0.12 ÷ (1 - 1.12^-3) = **₦208,174.49**.

| Year | Opening (₦) | Interest 12% (₦) | Principal repaid (₦) | Closing (₦) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 500,000.00 | 60,000.00 | 148,174.49 | 351,825.51 |
| 2 | 351,825.51 | 42,219.06 | 165,955.43 | 185,870.08 |
| 3 | 185,870.08 | 22,304.41 | 185,870.08 | 0.00 |

Shortcut: balance after year 2 = one instalment left = 208,174.49 ÷ 1.12 = ₦185,870.08. Interest falls and principal repaid rises each year.

- **Trap:** the instalment is not the balance; and a sinking-fund deposit is not the fund balance.

### Lesson 4.5: Arithmetic and geometric progressions

**Must know**

- **Arithmetic progression (AP):** each term differs from the one before by a constant **common difference d**. First term a.
  - nth term: Tn = a + (n - 1)d
  - Sum of n terms: Sn = n/2 × [2a + (n - 1)d] = n/2 × (a + l), l = last term
  - Number of terms: n = (l - a) ÷ d + 1
- **Geometric progression (GP):** each term is the one before multiplied by a constant **common ratio r**.
  - nth term: Tn = a × r^(n-1)
  - Sum of n terms: Sn = a × (r^n - 1) ÷ (r - 1) when r > 1, or a × (1 - r^n) ÷ (1 - r) when r < 1
  - Sum to infinity (only when -1 < r < 1): S∞ = a ÷ (1 - r)
- Simple-interest amounts form an AP (d = PR); compound amounts form a GP (ratio 1 + r); annuity formulas are GP sums.

**Explain more**

*Example (AP):* 5, 8, 11, ...: a = 5, d = 3. T20 = 5 + 19 × 3 = **62**; S20 = 20/2 × (5 + 62) = **670**.

*Example (AP in naira):* a salary of ₦600,000 rising by ₦50,000 a year. Year 10 salary = 600,000 + 9 × 50,000 = **₦1,050,000**; total over 10 years = 10/2 × (600,000 + 1,050,000) = **₦8,250,000**.

*Example (GP):* 2, 6, 18, ...: a = 2, r = 3. T6 = 2 × 3^5 = **486**; S6 = 2 × (3^6 - 1) ÷ (3 - 1) = **728**.

*Example (sum to infinity):* ₦100,000 + ₦80,000 + ₦64,000 + ...: r = 0.8, S∞ = 100,000 ÷ (1 - 0.8) = **₦500,000**.

*Why annuity formulas work:* the end-year deposits of ₦20m at 20% grow to 20m, 24m and 28.8m: a GP with a = 20m, r = 1.2, n = 3. S3 = 20m × (1.2^3 - 1) ÷ 0.2 = ₦72.8m, the FV of the annuity. A perpetuity is a GP sum to infinity with a = A ÷ (1 + r) and ratio 1 ÷ (1 + r), which simplifies to A ÷ r.

- **Trap:** the nth term uses n - 1, not n. Sum to infinity exists only when -1 < r < 1.

### Lesson 4.6: Investment, consumption and exchange

**Must know**

- Each person has income now (Y0) and next period (Y1) and chooses consumption now (C0) and later (C1).
- **Lending:** giving up ₦1 of consumption today gets ₦(1 + r) next period.
- **Borrowing:** ₦1 of next period's income can be spent today as ₦1 ÷ (1 + r).
- The interest rate is the **price of exchanging present for future consumption**.
- **Present value of wealth:** W0 = Y0 + Y1 ÷ (1 + r). Maximum future consumption = W0 × (1 + r).
- **Budget line:** C1 = Y1 + (Y0 - C0) × (1 + r); slope = -(1 + r).
- **Productive investment:** invest while the project's return exceeds the market rate (positive NPV). This raises wealth, and every person then borrows or lends to reach the consumption pattern they like.
- **Separation:** because people can borrow and lend at r, the investment decision (maximise NPV) is separate from consumption preferences.

**Explain more**

*Example:* Ada earns ₦100,000 now and ₦110,000 next year; the rate is 10%. W0 = 100,000 + 110,000 ÷ 1.1 = **₦200,000**; maximum next-year consumption = 200,000 × 1.1 = ₦220,000.

- To spend ₦150,000 now, she borrows ₦50,000 and repays 50,000 × 1.1 = ₦55,000, leaving C1 = 110,000 - 55,000 = **₦55,000**.
- To spend only ₦60,000 now, she lends ₦40,000 and gets back ₦44,000, so C1 = 110,000 + 44,000 = **₦154,000**.

*Investment:* a project costs ₦40,000 now and pays ₦50,000 next year (a 25% return, above 10%). NPV = 50,000 ÷ 1.1 - 40,000 = **₦5,454.55**, so her wealth rises to ₦205,454.55 whatever her tastes. Exchange through banks or treasury bills lets savers (lenders) and spenders (borrowers) both gain.

### One-minute recap

- A naira today is worth more than a naira tomorrow; compounding moves value forward, discounting moves it back.
- SI = PRT and A = P(1 + RT); compound FV = PV(1 + r)^n, or PV(1 + r/m)^(nm), or PV × e^(rn).
- PV = FV(1 + r)^-n; higher rate means lower PV.
- EAR = (1 + r/m)^m - 1; continuous EAR = e^r - 1.
- n = log(FV/PV) ÷ log(1 + r); r = (FV/PV)^(1/n) - 1.
- Ordinary annuity: FV = A[(1 + r)^n - 1]/r, PV = A[1 - (1 + r)^-n]/r; annuity due = ordinary × (1 + r); perpetuity = A/r.
- Sinking fund deposit = FV × r/[(1 + r)^n - 1]; loan instalment = Loan × r/[1 - (1 + r)^-n].
- AP: a + (n - 1)d and n/2[2a + (n - 1)d]; GP: ar^(n-1), a(r^n - 1)/(r - 1), a/(1 - r).
- Borrowing and lending at r let people trade present for future consumption; invest when NPV is positive.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Unilag Ventures deposits ₦150,000 at the end of each year for 4 years at 15% per annum. How much will be in the fund at the end of year 4, and how much if each deposit were made at the beginning of the year?

*Solution:* Ordinary annuity: FV = 150,000 × (1.15^4 - 1) ÷ 0.15 = 150,000 × (1.74901 - 1) ÷ 0.15 = 150,000 × 4.99337 = **₦749,006.25**. Annuity due: 749,006.25 × 1.15 = **₦861,357.19**.
