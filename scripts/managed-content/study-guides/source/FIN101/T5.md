## Topic 5: Financial Statement Analysis

**What you must know:** the three financial statements and what goes in each; users of financial statements and ratios; methods and limitations of analysis; the four groups of ratios with their other names; every formula for liquidity, activity, profitability, gearing and stock market ratios; ideal benchmarks; how to compute every ratio from one set of statements.

### Lesson 5.1: Financial statements, users and methods of analysis

**Must know**

- **Financial statement analysis:** the process of examining a company's financial reports to assess its performance, stability and future prospects, so that stakeholders can make informed decisions.
- **3 main financial statements:**
  1. **Statement of financial position (balance sheet):** assets, liabilities and equity at a **specific point in time** (one date).
  2. **Income statement (profit and loss account):** revenue, expenses and net income (profit) **over a period**; it shows **performance**. Liabilities do **not** appear in it.
  3. **Statement of cash flows:** actual cash **inflows and outflows** over a period, in **3 sections:** operating, investing and financing activities.
- A **trial balance** is a bookkeeping check (a list of ledger balances), **not** a financial statement.
- **Non-current (fixed) assets:** held for long-term use, not for sale, e.g. **land**, buildings, plant, machinery.
- **Current assets:** cash or items that become cash within a year: inventory (stock), accounts receivable (debtors), bank, cash.
- **Current liabilities:** obligations to be settled **within 1 year**: trade creditors (accounts payable), bank overdraft, accrued expenses, tax payable.
- **Shareholders' equity:** ordinary share capital (common stock), share premium (additional paid-in capital), reserves, retained earnings. Accounts payable is a **liability**, not equity.
- **Working capital = current assets - current liabilities.** Its components are current assets and current liabilities; fixed assets, long-term debt and share capital are not part of it. A rise in working capital usually means **higher liquidity**.
- **Net profit = total revenue - total expenses.** Gross profit = sales - cost of goods sold. Operating profit = gross profit - operating expenses.
- **Liquidity:** the ease of converting assets to cash. **Leverage:** using **debt** to finance investments.
- **Users of financial ratio analysis (10):** 1. company management, 2. shareholders, 3. employees, 4. creditors, 5. government, 6. rational investors, 7. competitors, 8. customers, 9. financial analysts, 10. financial institutions.
- **Internal user:** management. **External users:** investors, shareholders, creditors, government and the rest.
- **5 objectives of analysis:** assess profitability; evaluate liquidity; measure solvency; understand operational efficiency; support decision-making.
- **5 methods of analysis:**
  1. **Horizontal (trend) analysis:** compares figures across periods. Percentage change = (current year value - base year value) ÷ base year value × 100.
  2. **Vertical (common-size) analysis:** each item as a percentage of a base figure (revenue or total assets).
  3. **Ratio analysis.**
  4. **Cash flow analysis:** evaluating and comparing cash inflows and outflows from operating, investing and financing activities.
  5. **Comparative analysis:** against industry benchmarks or competitors.
- **5 steps:** understand the business; review the statements; perform trend and ratio analysis; interpret results; make recommendations.

**Explain more**

- *Horizontal example:* sales rose from ₦800,000 to ₦900,000. Change = (900,000 - 800,000) ÷ 800,000 × 100 = **12.5%**.
- *Vertical example:* cost of sales ₦540,000 on sales ₦900,000 is 540,000 ÷ 900,000 × 100 = **60%** of sales.
- **Debtors** (customers who owe the firm) are **not** listed as users of ratio analysis; **creditors** are. Do not confuse them. Customers are users, because they care whether a supplier will survive.
- A **financial analyst** is the user who studies statements in order to **advise clients**. Researchers study them for academic work.
- **ROI** stands for **Return on Investment**: gain from an investment relative to its cost, × 100.
- Nigerian listed companies publish audited statements and file them with the SEC and the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), which is where investors find the figures for ratios.

### Lesson 5.2: Ratio analysis and liquidity ratios

**Must know**

- **Financial ratio analysis:** the relationship between financial data in the financial statements that aids the evaluation of the financial condition and performance of a company. It is used to gauge a firm's operational success and to track performance trends.
- **Advantages (5):** 1. very easy to understand; 2. very simple to calculate; 3. allows comparison between two similar companies; 4. allows comparison with the industry average; 5. useful for identifying problem areas of the company.
- **Disadvantages (4):** 1. taken by itself, a ratio gives little useful information; 2. ratios are easily misinterpreted; 3. ratios do not identify the causes of a company's problems; 4. problems of definition.
- **Limitations of financial statement analysis (5):** historical data; differing accounting policies reduce comparability; non-financial factors (management quality, market conditions) are left out; window dressing; inflation.
- **4 groups of ratios:**

| Group | Other names | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Short-term solvency | **Liquidity**, working capital ratios | Ability to meet current obligations from current assets |
| 2. Efficiency and profitability | Performance ratios | Performance in relative terms |
| 3. Long-term solvency | **Stability** ratios, gearing | Ability to meet long-term obligations |
| 4. Stock market | Investor interest, actual growth and potential, valuation, market value ratios | Help investors make investment decisions |

- **Liquidity formulas:**
  - Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities (written as x : 1). **Ideal 2:1.**
  - Quick (acid-test) ratio = (current assets - inventory) ÷ current liabilities. **Ideal 1:1.** Inventory is excluded because it is the least liquid current asset.
  - Cash ratio = cash and cash equivalents ÷ current liabilities.

**Explain more**

- The **current ratio** shows how far the claims of short-term creditors are covered by assets that will become cash within a year. Above 1 suggests good liquidity; far too high suggests idle assets. A **low quick ratio** warns of difficulty paying short-term debts. A very high cash ratio may mean underused cash.
- *Example 1:* stock ₦5,000; trade creditors ₦2,000; debtors ₦3,000; bank overdraft ₦1,600; bank ₦1,500; accrued expenses ₦400; cash ₦500.
  - Current assets = 5,000 + 3,000 + 1,500 + 500 = ₦10,000. Current liabilities = 2,000 + 1,600 + 400 = ₦4,000.
  - Current ratio = 10,000 ÷ 4,000 = **2.5:1**. Quick ratio = (10,000 - 5,000) ÷ 4,000 = **1.25:1**.
- *Example 2:* inventories ₦3,100; receivables ₦8,000; payables ₦4,000; cash ₦6,260; taxation ₦2,250.
  - Current assets = ₦17,360. Current liabilities = 4,000 + 2,250 = ₦6,250 (tax payable is due within the year, so include it).
  - Current ratio = 17,360 ÷ 6,250 = **2.78:1**. Quick = 14,260 ÷ 6,250 = **2.28:1** (both to 2 d.p.).
- *Example 3:* stock ₦3,000, cash ₦7,000, debtors ₦10,000, trade creditors ₦5,900. Current ratio = 20,000 ÷ 5,900 = **3.4:1**; acid test = 17,000 ÷ 5,900 = **2.9:1** (1 d.p.).
- **Traps:** a bank overdraft is a current liability, but money **in** the bank is a current asset. Options often list the ideal (2:1 or 1:1) beside the firm's actual figure; compute, do not guess the ideal.

### Lesson 5.3: Activity (efficiency) ratios

**Must know**

- **Activity ratios** measure how efficiently a firm uses its resources (stock, debtors, assets).
- Inventory (stock) turnover = cost of sales ÷ average stock (in **times**).
  - Average stock = (opening stock + closing stock) ÷ 2.
  - Inventory days = 365 ÷ inventory turnover.
- Average collection period (receivables days) = trade debtors ÷ credit sales × 365 days.
- Receivables turnover = credit sales ÷ average receivables (times).
- Average payment period (payables days) = trade creditors ÷ credit purchases × 365 days.
- Credit purchases (if not given) = cost of sales + closing stock - opening stock.
- Asset turnover = sales ÷ total assets (times).

**Explain more**

- Stock turnover, the payment period and the collection period are also grouped with the **short-term (working capital) ratios**, because they show how fast stock and debtors turn into cash. Accept either grouping.
- Inventory turnover measures the **speed of inventory sales**. A **high receivables turnover** means **swift collection** from customers; a low one means slow payment. A long payment period means the firm takes longer to pay suppliers.
- *Example:* opening stock ₦13,000, closing stock ₦9,000, cost of sales ₦77,000. Average stock = 0.5 × (13,000 + 9,000) = ₦11,000. Turnover = 77,000 ÷ 11,000 = **7 times**; inventory days = 365 ÷ 7 = **52.1 days** (1 d.p.).
- *Example:* credit sales ₦40,000,000; average debtors ₦10,000,000. Receivables turnover = **4 times**; collection period = 10,000,000 ÷ 40,000,000 × 365 = **91.25 days**.
- **Trap:** stock turnover uses **cost of sales**, not sales; the collection period uses **credit sales**; the payment period uses **credit purchases**.

### Lesson 5.4: Profitability ratios

**Must know**

- **Profitability ratios** show a firm's ability to generate profit relative to revenue, assets or equity. All are × 100 (%).
- Gross profit margin = gross profit ÷ sales × 100.
- Operating profit margin = operating profit (EBIT) ÷ sales × 100.
- Net profit margin = net profit ÷ sales × 100 (profit after **all** costs, including interest and tax). The core formula also allows EBIT as the numerator; follow what the question says.
- Return on total assets (ROTA) = EBIT ÷ total assets × 100.
- Return on assets (ROA), net-income version = net income ÷ total assets × 100.
- Return on capital employed (ROCE) = EBIT ÷ capital employed × 100.
  - Capital employed = total assets - current liabilities = equity + preference capital + long-term debt.
- Return on shareholders' equity (ROSE or ROE) = EAIT (earnings after interest and tax) ÷ shareholders' equity × 100.

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| EBIT | Earnings (profit) before interest and tax |
| EBT / PBT | Earnings (profit) before tax |
| EAIT / PAT | Earnings after interest and tax / profit after tax |

**Explain more**

- **Moving between profit lines:** EBIT = PBT + interest. PAT = PBT × (1 - tax rate).
- *Example (₦'000):* credit sales 40,000; PBT 6,000; interest on loan 8,000; total assets 54,000; tax rate 35%; shareholders' funds 160,000.
  - PAT = 6,000 × 0.65 = 3,900. ROSE = 3,900 ÷ 160,000 × 100 = 2.44% = **2%** to the nearest whole number. (Using PBT wrongly gives 3.75%, about 4%.)
  - EBIT = 6,000 + 8,000 = 14,000. ROTA = 14,000 ÷ 54,000 × 100 = 25.93% = **26%**.
- *Example:* sales ₦40,000, EBIT ₦9,000, total assets ₦54,900. ROTA = 9,000 ÷ 54,900 × 100 = **16.4%**. With capital employed ₦100,900, ROCE = 9,000 ÷ 100,900 × 100 = **8.9%**, about 9%.
- *Example:* sales ₦108,700, cost of sales ₦91,000. Gross profit = ₦17,700; margin = 17,700 ÷ 108,700 × 100 = **16.3%**.
- A higher gross margin means efficient production and cost control; a higher ROA means better asset use; ROE measures **profit from shareholders' funds**, so it is a profitability ratio (unlike the debt, current and quick ratios).

### Lesson 5.5: Gearing (long-term solvency) and stock market ratios

**Must know**

- **Gearing ratio = fixed interest capital ÷ equity capital** (x : 1).
  - Fixed interest capital = debt capital + preference share capital.
  - Equity capital = ordinary share capital + share premium + revenue reserves + retained earnings.
- Debt-to-equity ratio = total debt (or total liabilities) ÷ shareholders' equity.
- Debt ratio = total debt ÷ total assets × 100, where total debt = current liabilities + long-term debt.
- Fixed interest cover (interest coverage, times interest earned) = EBIT ÷ annual fixed interest charged on debt (times).
- Proprietary ratio = shareholders' equity ÷ total tangible assets × 100.
- **Stock market ratios:**
  - Dividend per share (DPS) = gross (ordinary) dividend ÷ number of ordinary shares issued and fully paid.
  - Earnings per share (EPS) = earnings after interest and tax (less preference dividend) ÷ number of ordinary shares issued and fully paid.
  - Dividend yield = DPS ÷ market price per share × 100.
  - Earnings yield = EPS ÷ market price per share × 100.
  - Price/earnings (P/E) ratio = market price per share ÷ EPS (times).
  - **Dividend cover = earnings available to ordinary shareholders ÷ ordinary dividend = EPS ÷ DPS** (times).
  - Dividend payout ratio = DPS ÷ EPS × 100 (the reciprocal of dividend cover).
  - Price-to-book (P/B) = market price per share ÷ book value per share (book value per share = ordinary equity ÷ number of shares).

**Explain more**

- A **high debt-to-equity** (or gearing) ratio means **significant leverage**: heavy reliance on debt, so higher financial risk. A lower debt ratio means a stronger position; a higher interest cover means interest is paid more easily.
- *Gearing example:* debt capital ₦130,000; preference capital ₦90,000; equity ₦658,700. Gearing = 220,000 ÷ 658,700 = **0.33:1**.
- *Debt ratio example:* current liabilities ₦13,650; long-term debt ₦5,400; total assets ₦87,000. Debt ratio = 19,050 ÷ 87,000 × 100 = **21.9%**. Trap: add **both** kinds of debt; using current liabilities alone gives 15.7%.
- *DPS example:* gross dividend ₦108,700 on 900,000 shares. DPS = **₦0.12 (12 kobo)**, to the nearest kobo.
- *Dividend yield example:* DPS ₦1.70, market price ₦11.45. Yield = 1.70 ÷ 11.45 × 100 = 14.8%, about **15%**.
- **EPS and P/E are inversely related:** for a given price, higher EPS means lower P/E. Earnings yield is P/E turned upside down. A **high P/E** signals **potential overvaluation**, or strong growth expectations; a low P/E may mean undervaluation.
- **Dividend cover** shows how many times the ordinary dividend could be paid out of the year's earnings. Cover of 2 times means half of earnings is paid out and half retained; cover below 1 means part of the dividend comes from past reserves.
- Earnings yield and dividend yield use the **market price**, so they are **market value ratios**; gross profit margin and times interest earned are not.

### Lesson 5.6: One full worked set

**Must know**

Adebayo Ltd, figures in ₦'000. Ordinary shares: 200,000 of ₦1 each. Market price per share ₦3.60. All sales are on credit. Opening inventory ₦40,000.

| Cash | 40 |

| **Total assets** | **600** |
| Ordinary share capital | 200 |
| Share premium | 50 |
| Retained earnings | 100 |
| **Ordinary shareholders' equity** | **350** |
| 10% preference share capital | 50 |
| 10% debenture (long-term debt) | 100 |
| Trade payables | 70 |
| Tax payable | 30 |
| **Current liabilities** | **100** |
| **Total equity and liabilities** | **600** |

| Income statement for the year | ₦'000 |
|---|---|
| Sales | 900 |
| Cost of sales | (540) |

**Explain more**

Supporting figures: capital employed = 600 - 100 = 500 (also 350 + 50 + 100). Total debt = 100 + 100 = 200. Average inventory = (40 + 60) ÷ 2 = 50. Credit purchases = 540 + 60 - 40 = 560. Only closing receivables (50) are given, so they stand in for average receivables.

- **Reading the results:** the current ratio (1.5:1) and quick ratio (0.9:1) are below the 2:1 and 1:1 ideals, so liquidity is a little tight. Interest cover of 12 times and gearing of 0.43:1 show modest borrowing. ROCE of 24% is healthy.
- **Traps in this set:** EPS uses earnings **after** the preference dividend (72, not 77). Dividend cover uses the **ordinary** dividend only. ROCE uses capital employed (500), ROTA uses total assets (600). If a question says "total debt ÷ equity" use 200; if it says "long-term debt ÷ equity" use 100 ÷ 350 = 0.29:1. Some questions compute ROE from earnings for ordinary shareholders: 72 ÷ 350 × 100 = 20.6%. Always follow the formula the question states.

### One-minute recap

- Statement of financial position = one date; income statement = performance over a period; cash flow statement = 3 sections (operating, investing, financing).
- Current liabilities fall due within 1 year; land is a fixed asset; working capital = CA - CL.
- 10 users of ratios; management is internal; debtors are not users; analysts advise clients.
- 4 groups: liquidity (short-term solvency), efficiency and profitability, stability (long-term solvency), stock market.
- Current ratio ideal 2:1; quick ratio ideal 1:1 and excludes inventory.
- Stock turnover = cost of sales ÷ average stock; collection period = debtors ÷ credit sales × 365; payment period = creditors ÷ credit purchases × 365.
- ROTA = EBIT ÷ total assets; ROCE = EBIT ÷ capital employed; ROSE = EAIT ÷ equity.
- Gearing = (debt + preference capital) ÷ equity; debt ratio = (CL + long-term debt) ÷ total assets; interest cover = EBIT ÷ interest.
- EPS, DPS, dividend yield, earnings yield, P/E (inverse of EPS), dividend cover = EPS ÷ DPS.
- Ratios are easy and comparable, but alone tell little, are easily misread and do not show causes.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Bode Plc earned profit after tax of ₦4,800,000 and paid a preference dividend of ₦800,000 and an ordinary dividend of ₦1,600,000. It has 10,000,000 ordinary shares priced at ₦4.00 each. Calculate EPS, DPS, dividend cover, P/E ratio and dividend yield.

*Solution:* Earnings for ordinary shareholders = 4,800,000 - 800,000 = ₦4,000,000. EPS = 4,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 = **₦0.40**. DPS = 1,600,000 ÷ 10,000,000 = **₦0.16**. Dividend cover = 4,000,000 ÷ 1,600,000 = **2.5 times**. P/E = 4.00 ÷ 0.40 = **10 times**. Dividend yield = 0.16 ÷ 4.00 × 100 = **4%**.
