## Topic 1: Overview of Finance

**What you must know:** the meaning and scope of finance; how finance differs from economics and accounting; the branches of finance; the four finance decisions and their other names; the financial manager's role; financial management as a tool for planning and control; the limited liability company and the risks of finance.

### Lesson 1.1: Meaning and scope of finance

**Must know**

- **Finance:** the management of money and the process of acquiring the needed funds to achieve a set objective. Put more shortly, finance is the **management of money and money claims**: finance is money, and availability of finance is availability of money.
- Finance deals with the **inflows (sources)** and **outflows (uses)** of money (funds) within the firm. It serves as a **link** that makes business interrelationships possible.
- **Activities of finance (6):** saving, borrowing, lending, investing, budgeting, forecasting.
- Money is important to **individuals**, **businesses** and **government**, to finance their activities and achieve their targets.
- **Finance functions of the firm:** raising capital funds, using them to generate returns, and paying returns to the suppliers of funds (interest to lenders, dividends to shareholders).

**Importance (characteristics) of finance in a business firm (4):**

1. Finance **enhances business growth**.
2. Finance serves as a **standard (stand) upon which business performance can be measured**.
3. Finance **links all other activities together**: marketing, purchasing, production, selling and distribution.
4. Finance is a **new discipline which has grown rapidly**.

| | Finance | Economics | Accounting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main concern | Managing money: raising, investing, returning funds | How society allocates scarce resources (micro and macro) | Recording, classifying and reporting transactions |
| Basis | Cash flows, time value of money, risk and return | Theory of demand, supply, prices, national income | Accrual basis (income when earned, expense when incurred) |
| Output | Decisions (invest, finance, pay dividends) | Theories and policies | Financial statements |

**Explain more**

- Finance is primarily concerned with **managing monetary resources and investments**. If an option says workforce productivity, promoting goods, organisational hierarchy, product pricing or employee welfare, it belongs to human resources, marketing or management, not finance.
- Studying finance helps you **optimise resource allocation decisions**: putting scarce funds where they earn the best return for the risk taken. It also **enables informed strategic choices**. Options claiming finance "eliminates planning", "neglects risk" or "centres solely on profit" are traps: finance needs planning, treats risk as central, and looks beyond profit to value.
- The field of finance **includes investments and financial market dynamics**. Watch for words like "exclusively", "solely" and "purely" (ledger keeping, promotion, manufacturing); they mark wrong options.
- **Finance and economics:** finance borrows from economics. Microeconomics gives the firm marginal analysis (go ahead only if extra benefit exceeds extra cost); macroeconomics gives the setting: interest rates, inflation and CBN monetary policy all shape a Nigerian firm's cost of funds.
- **Finance and accounting:** the accountant records that a sale of ₦2,000,000 on credit is revenue today; the finance person asks when the ₦2,000,000 cash will actually arrive, because a firm can show profit and still run out of cash. Accounting provides the data; finance uses it to decide.

### Lesson 1.2: Branches of finance and financial management

**Must know**

**Three categories of finance:**

1. **Personal finance:** how an individual or household handles income, savings, spending, borrowing and investments (an individual's financial assets).
2. **Business (corporate) finance:** how a firm raises and uses funds; overseeing a firm's financial operations: raising capital, choosing investments, paying dividends.
3. **Public finance:** how government raises revenue (taxes, borrowing) and spends it; administering public sector budgets and tax policy.

A fourth branch is often added:

4. **International finance:** financial dealings across borders: exchange rates, foreign trade and investment, global trade deals.

- **Financial management:** the management of the finances of individuals, business or government in order to achieve set objectives.
- The focus of this course is **business (corporate) finance**, so financial management is **the study of how firms make their investment and financing decisions**.

**Explain more**

- Match the description to the branch. "Managing individual savings accounts" = personal. "Directing corporate cash flows" or "overseeing a firm's financial operations" = corporate. "Administering public sector budgets" or "administering tax policies" = public. "Facilitating global trade deals" = international.
- Nigerian examples: a student saving pocket money in a bank account and buying treasury bills is personal finance; Dangote Cement raising funds on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) is corporate finance; the Federal Government's annual budget and federal tax collection are public finance; a Nigerian importer paying a Chinese supplier in yuan is international finance.
- **Corporate finance for business funding** is a key area of finance; product design, staff development and consumer behaviour are not.

### Lesson 1.3: The finance function: the four decisions

**Must know**

- **Finance functions:** practices and activities focused on managing a business's financial resources to generate profits. They cover **investment, dividend, financing and liquidity** decisions, among others. They keep the firm financially stable and support growth and profitability.

1. **Investment (capital budgeting) decision:** the allocation of the firm's capital funds to investment proposals that yield future benefits meeting the expectations of all investors, i.e. how much to invest in physical assets or financial assets (securities). Also called the **capital budgeting decision**. Planning capital expenditure is an investment decision.
2. **Financing decision:** determining the **best financing mix (capital structure)**: sourcing funds at the **cheapest cost**, with the **most favourable tax**, at the **best mix**, to **maximise the market price per share (MPS)**. Also called the **capital mix decision**.
3. **Dividend decision:** determining the proportion of earnings to **pay out as dividend** or **retain** in the firm, bearing in mind the need to maximise shareholders' (owners') wealth. Also called the **profit allocation decision**.
4. **Liquidity decision:** the **working capital decision**: managing current assets and current liabilities (cash, inventory, receivables, payables) so the firm can pay its way day to day.

**Other finance functions:** managing cash flows, risk management, financial planning, financial analysis, financial control.

**Key terms:**

- **Capital budgeting:** making **long-term investment decisions** (new machinery, a new factory) that bring returns over many years.
- **Capital budgeting techniques:** net present value (NPV), **internal rate of return (IRR)**, payback period, accounting rate of return (ARR), profitability index.
- **Internal rate of return (IRR):** the discount rate at which a project's NPV equals zero. Accept the project if IRR is greater than the cost of capital.
- **Cost of capital:** the **cost of obtaining funds**: the return a firm must pay suppliers of funds (interest to lenders, the return shareholders expect).
- **Dividend:** a **portion of a company's profit distributed to shareholders**.
- **Retained earnings** = profit after tax − dividends paid.

| Decision | Other name | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Investment | Capital budgeting | Where should funds go? |
| Financing | Capital mix (capital structure) | Where should funds come from? |
| Dividend | Profit allocation | How much profit to pay out or keep? |
| Liquidity | Working capital | Can we meet short-term obligations? |

**Explain more**

- **Trap on names:** capital mix = financing; profit allocation = dividend; capital budgeting = investment. "Capital project decision" and "capital rating decision" are not the standard names. **Capital rationing** is only a special case within the investment decision (when funds are too few for all good projects), so it is not another name for any of the decisions.
- **Not finance functions:** **financial forensics** (investigating fraud, a specialist audit and legal activity) and an **acquisition decision** (a specific strategic choice). The three major managerial finance decisions of a limited liability company are investment, financing and dividend.
- Ratios such as debt-to-equity and current ratio, and the cash conversion cycle, are **not** capital budgeting techniques. Managing daily expenses is working capital, not capital budgeting.

**Worked example (dividend decision):** Ade Foods plc earns profit after tax of ₦50,000,000 and decides to pay out 40% as dividend.

- Dividend paid = 40% × ₦50,000,000 = ₦20,000,000.
- Retained = ₦50,000,000 − ₦20,000,000 = ₦30,000,000 (60%), available to finance new investment.

**Worked example (cost of capital and IRR):** A project costs ₦1,000,000 today and returns a single ₦1,210,000 in two years. The firm's cost of capital is 8%.

- NPV at 8% = 1,210,000 ÷ (1.08)^2 − 1,000,000 = 1,210,000 ÷ 1.1664 − 1,000,000 = ₦37,380 (to the nearest naira). Positive, so accept.
- IRR: find r where 1,000,000 × (1 + r)^2 = 1,210,000. (1 + r)^2 = 1.21, so 1 + r = 1.1 and IRR = 10%.
- IRR (10%) > cost of capital (8%), so accept. Both methods agree.

### Lesson 1.4: The financial manager; planning and control

**Must know**

- **Financial manager:** the finance employee who **manages the funds of an organisation** and undertakes financial management roles. The primary role is to **manage the firm's finances and maximise its value** (shareholders' wealth).

**Roles of the financial manager:**

1. Estimating the funds needed (financial planning and forecasting).
2. Raising funds from the best sources at the lowest cost (financing decision).
3. **Evaluating investment opportunities** and allocating funds (investment decision).
4. Recommending dividend and retention policy (dividend decision).
5. Managing working capital and cash flows (liquidity decision).
6. Managing financial risk.
7. Financial analysis, reporting and control.
8. Dealing with banks, investors and the capital market.

- **Financial planning:** deciding in advance the firm's financial goals and how to reach them. Components: **defining monetary (financial) objectives**, estimating funds needed, choosing sources, budgeting, forecasting.
- **Financial control:** checking that actual results match the plan and correcting deviations.

**Financial management as a tool for planning and control:** budgets turn plans into figures; actual results are measured and compared with budget; variances are investigated; corrective action is taken. This cycle is **plan → act → measure → compare → correct**.

**Principles for planning and control:**

1. **Clear objectives:** plans start from defined financial goals.
2. **Realistic forecasting:** based on reliable information.
3. **Flexibility:** plans can be adjusted when conditions change.
4. **Participation:** those who carry out the plan help set it.
5. **Responsibility:** each budget item is assigned to a named manager.
6. **Timely measurement and feedback:** results are reported quickly enough to act on.
7. **Management by exception:** attention goes to significant variances.
8. **Cost-benefit:** controls should cost less than the benefit they bring.

**Explain more**

- A **financial analyst** studies and reports on financial data; a **financial planner** usually advises individuals; a **financial controller** mainly oversees accounting and reporting. The one who manages the organisation's funds and makes the finance decisions is the **financial manager**.
- Recruiting staff, crafting product prototypes, analysing market demographics, ensuring customer satisfaction and preparing legal documents are not the financial manager's job.
- **Worked example (control):** A firm budgets ₦8,000,000 for transport in a year but spends ₦9,200,000. Variance = ₦9,200,000 − ₦8,000,000 = ₦1,200,000 adverse, i.e. 1,200,000 ÷ 8,000,000 × 100 = 15% over budget. Under management by exception, the manager investigates this large overspend (perhaps a fuel price rise) and corrects it.

### Lesson 1.5: The limited liability company and the nature and risks of finance

**Must know**

- **Limited liability company:** a business registered (in Nigeria with the Corporate Affairs Commission, CAC) as a separate legal person, owned by shareholders whose liability is **limited to the amount unpaid on their shares**.
- **Features:** separate legal entity; limited liability; perpetual succession (continues when owners die or sell); transferable shares; **ownership separate from control** (shareholders own, directors and managers run it); can raise large funds by issuing shares and debentures.
- **Private (Ltd)** cannot invite the public to buy its shares; **public (plc)** can, and may list on the NGX under SEC regulation.

**Nature of finance:** finance is about money over **time** and under **risk**. A naira today is worth more than a naira later, and future returns are uncertain, so every finance decision weighs **risk against return**.

**Risks of finance:**

1. **Business risk:** variability of operating profit from the nature of the business.
2. **Financial risk:** extra risk from using debt; interest must be paid even in bad years.
3. **Liquidity risk:** inability to meet short-term obligations.
4. **Interest-rate and inflation risk:** rising rates raise the cost of funds; inflation erodes the value of returns.
5. **Credit (default) risk:** customers or borrowers fail to pay.
6. **Exchange-rate risk:** naira movements change the value of foreign payments and receipts.

**Objectives of business firms (studied fully in Topic 2):** (A) profit maximisation (increased revenue with lower cost); (B) wealth maximisation (shareholders' wealth); (C) satisfaction (satisfied customers stay loyal, recommend and repeat purchase); (D) social responsibility; (E) increase in market share (to be market leader). Others: growth, improved productivity, diversification, technical leadership, a contented workforce, highest quality service to customers.

**Explain more**

- Limited liability is why people will buy shares in a company they do not run: the most a shareholder can lose is what he paid (or still owes) for his shares. If Kunle buys 10,000 shares at ₦5 fully paid (₦50,000) and the company fails owing ₦100,000,000, Kunle loses at most ₦50,000; creditors cannot take his house.
- Separation of ownership and control means managers may not always act for shareholders; the financial manager's task of maximising value is meant to align the firm's decisions with the owners' wealth.
- **Worked example (financial risk):** Two firms each earn operating profit of ₦10,000,000. Firm A has no debt; Firm B pays interest of ₦6,000,000. If operating profit falls by 40% to ₦6,000,000, Firm A still has ₦6,000,000 before tax, while Firm B has ₦6,000,000 − ₦6,000,000 = ₦0. Debt magnifies the effect of a fall; that is financial risk.

### One-minute recap

- Finance = management of money and money claims; inflows (sources) and outflows (uses) of funds.
- Activities: saving, borrowing, lending, investing, budgeting, forecasting.
- Importance: enhances growth, standard for measuring performance, links all activities, a new and fast-growing discipline.
- Branches: personal, business (corporate), public, plus international.
- Financial management = how firms make investment and financing decisions.
- Four decisions: investment (capital budgeting), financing (capital mix), dividend (profit allocation), liquidity (working capital).
- Cost of capital = cost of obtaining funds; IRR = rate where NPV = 0, a capital budgeting technique; dividend = profit paid to shareholders.
- Financial manager manages the organisation's funds to maximise value; financial forensics and acquisition decisions are not core finance functions.
- Planning and control: plan, act, measure, compare, correct.
- Limited liability company: separate legal entity, liability limited to unpaid shares, ownership separate from control.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Zenith Agro plc earns profit after tax of ₦80,000,000 and pays a dividend of 25%. (a) How much is retained? (b) Which finance decision is this, and what is its other name?

*Answer:* (a) Dividend = 25% × ₦80,000,000 = ₦20,000,000. Retained = ₦80,000,000 − ₦20,000,000 = ₦60,000,000. (b) It is the **dividend decision**, also called the **profit allocation decision**: choosing how much profit to pay out and how much to keep, with the aim of maximising shareholders' wealth.
