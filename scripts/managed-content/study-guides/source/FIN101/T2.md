## Topic 2: Objective of a Firm

**What you must know:** profit maximisation and its criticisms; shareholders' wealth maximisation, NPV and why it is superior; the other objectives (satisfaction, social responsibility, survival, market share, growth and the rest); stakeholders; the agency problem and agency costs; the objectives of financial decisions.

### Lesson 2.1: Profit maximisation objective

**Must know**

- **Profit maximisation:** the firm aims to make as much profit as possible by **maximising revenue** from the sale of its output while **minimising the cost** of producing that output (increased revenue with lower cost).
- It is regarded as the **primary (traditional) objective** of a firm. It was developed in the **early 19th century**, when firms were mainly **sole proprietorships** and **self-financing** (owner and manager were the same person).
- **Merit:** it is **very easy to measure**. It requires the firm to be efficient in its use of resources and focuses its effort on making money.
- Profit maximisation is the objective that has been **attacked by all and sundry**, for these **5 reasons**:

1. It does **not recognise the time value of money** (₦1 received today is treated the same as ₦1 received in five years).
2. It does **not consider risk or uncertainty** of future earnings.
3. **Profit has no clear definition:** it does not state whether it means profit before tax or after tax, short-term or long-term profit.
4. It does **not consider the quality aspect** of the firm's future activities.
5. It does **not give all participants or interest groups** (customers, creditors, shareholders, government, employees, competitors) a **satisfactory return** for their inputs or efforts.

**Explain more**

- Because it ignores timing, profit maximisation **focuses on immediate (short-term) financial gains**. A manager chasing this year's profit may cut anything whose benefit comes later.
- **What conflicts with profit maximisation?** Spending that raises cost today but pays off only later, such as **funding staff skill development (training)**, research or community projects. By contrast, streamlining production, trimming operating expenses and broadening market reach all **support** profit maximisation, because they cut cost or raise sales.
- Exam cue words: "**primary** objective of a business firm" or "**main objective of a business**" with profit among the options → **maximise profits for its owners**. Contrast with Lesson 2.2: "main goal of **financial management**" → **maximise shareholder wealth**.
- Trap: profit maximisation is easy to **measure**; its weakness is not measurement but timing, risk, vagueness, quality and fairness to other groups.

### Lesson 2.2: Shareholders' wealth maximisation objective

**Must know**

- **Shareholders' wealth maximisation (SWM):** widely accepted as the **best means of maximising the economic welfare of shareholders** of a firm. It is the **main goal of financial management**: to **maximise shareholder wealth over time**.
- It simply means **maximising the Net Present Value (NPV)** of a course of action.
- **NPV:** the difference between the **present value of the benefits** of an action and the **initial investment** required to achieve those benefits.
  - NPV = PV of future cash inflows − initial investment
  - PV of a cash flow C received in year n = C / (1 + r)^n, where r is the required rate of return.
  - Accept actions with NPV > 0; they add to shareholders' wealth.
- **Shareholder wealth is represented by the market price per share** of the firm's ordinary (common) shares. So the financial objective of a firm is to **maximise the market value of its shares**.
- Share value is set by the price at which the shares trade in the market place, such as the **Nigerian Exchange (NGX)** in Lagos.
- The market price reflects the firm's managerial decisions: **investment, financing and dividend decisions**.
- Maximising shareholder wealth means **enhancing share value and payouts** (rising share price plus dividends). It aligns with **building enduring (long-term) value**.

**Superiority of SWM over profit maximisation (8 points):**

1. It **recognises the time value of money**.
2. It **considers the effect of risk** or uncertainty of future earnings.
3. It **balances short-term and long-term profit** in a way profit maximisation cannot.
4. It **considers the quality aspect** of the firm's future activities.
5. It gives **all participants or interest groups** a satisfactory return for their inputs or efforts.
6. It is a **yardstick for measuring the overall performance** of a firm; share performance on the stock market has a direct relationship with shareholder wealth.
7. It is the **best factor in wooing further investors** into a firm.
8. It provides **appropriate and operationally feasible criteria** for choosing between alternative courses of action.

| Point | Profit maximisation | Shareholders' wealth maximisation |
|---|---|---|
| Time value of money | Ignored | Recognised (discounting, NPV) |
| Risk | Ignored | Considered |
| Time horizon | Short term, immediate gains | Long term, enduring value |
| Definition | Vague (before/after tax? short/long?) | Clear: market price per share |
| Shareholders' view | Not reflected | Reflected in the share price |

**Explain more**

- **Worked example (why timing matters).** Discount rate 10%. Each project costs ₦1,000,000 now.
  - Project A pays ₦600,000 at the end of year 1 and ₦600,000 at the end of year 2. Total profit = ₦1,200,000 − ₦1,000,000 = ₦200,000.
  - Project B pays ₦200,000 in year 1 and ₦1,000,000 in year 2. Total profit = ₦200,000 as well.
  - Profit maximisation sees no difference. Now find the NPV.
  - A: 600,000 / 1.1 = ₦545,454.55; 600,000 / 1.1^2 = ₦495,867.77. PV = ₦1,041,322.31. NPV = 1,041,322.31 − 1,000,000 = **₦41,322.31**.
  - B: 200,000 / 1.1 = ₦181,818.18; 1,000,000 / 1.1^2 = ₦826,446.28. PV = ₦1,008,264.46. NPV = **₦8,264.46**.
  - A adds more wealth because its money arrives sooner. (Figures rounded to the nearest kobo.)
- **Worked example (what wealth is).** You hold 2,000 shares in an NGX-listed bank. The price rises from ₦25 to ₦30 over the year and the bank pays a dividend of ₦2 per share. Gain in wealth = 2,000 × (30 − 25 + 2) = **₦14,000**. Wealth comes from **share price and dividends**, not from the firm's reported profit as such.
- **Traps:**
  - Shareholder wealth is the **market price** per share, **not** book value (assets less liabilities at historical cost), the number of employees or salaries paid.
  - "Which is **not** a reason SWM is superior?" Look for the option that **reverses** a true point: "wealth maximisation does **not** consider the timing of returns while profit maximisation does" is false, so it is the answer. Likewise "recognition of the prospects of a firm **in the short run**" is not a reason; SWM is about the **long run**.
  - Genuine reasons include: explicit consideration of risk, consideration of the time value of money, recognition of shareholders' perception of returns from investment (through the share price), and long-term prospects.
  - "Most appropriate objective of a business organisation" → **maximise shareholder wealth**. Long-term earnings growth ignores risk; efficient use of resources and minimising input costs are means, not the goal.

### Lesson 2.3: Other objectives of a firm

**Must know**

The **6 objectives** of a business firm:

1. **Profit maximisation** (Lesson 2.1).
2. **Shareholders' wealth maximisation** (Lesson 2.2).
3. **Satisfaction objective:** giving **all participants (stakeholders) a satisfactory return** for their inputs or efforts: customers, shareholders, government, employees, competitors and creditors. Satisfying these interest groups makes a modern firm grow or remain afloat. Satisfied customers stay loyal, recommend the firm and bring repeat business.
4. **Social responsibility objective:** firms not only make money but also **improve the environment** they operate in through **community development**: rural electrification, pipe-borne water, good roads, hospitals, scholarships and jobs for indigenes. This objective **enhances a good business environment**.
5. **Survival objective:** **staying in business continuously** rather than making profit this year and packing up next year. In an economic downturn it is the **most important** objective; keen competition and high inflation in Nigeria make it crucial.
6. **Increase in market share objective:** **capturing more of the market demand** for the firm's products or services in the industry, so as to maintain a **leadership position** (be a market leader).

**Other objectives of business firms (6):**

1. To seek **growth** of the firm.
2. To improve **productivity**.
3. To seek **diversification**.
4. To be a **technical leader** in their field.
5. To maintain a **contented workforce**.
6. To give the **highest quality services** to customers.

**Explain more**

- **Satisficing:** aiming for results that are "good enough" for every group rather than the maximum for one group. Managers balance owners, workers, lenders, suppliers and government. This is the same idea as the satisfaction objective.
- **Sales (revenue) maximisation:** managers may push sales volume, since their status and pay often rise with the size of the firm, even when profit does not rise as fast.
- **Growth:** getting bigger, for example by **entering untapped markets**, launching products or adding capacity. **Expanding market presence** is a **long-term** objective; covering daily operating costs and hiring short-term workers are short-run matters.
- **Liquidity:** **ensuring cash for short-term needs** (wages, supplier bills) as they fall due.
- **Efficiency:** **lowering costs and boosting output**.
- **Innovation:** leads to **greater market penetration** and also **higher short-term expenses** (research and development), so "both" is the answer when both appear.
- **Ethical objective:** **adhering to equitable (fair) practices**, obeying the law and being transparent.
- **Secondary objectives:** beside profit or wealth, a firm may pursue **corporate social responsibility** initiatives. A **social objective** is one like **enhancing community welfare**; boosting margins is a profit objective, not a social one.
- **Economic vs social objectives:** economic analysis of a firm looks at commercial performance: **sales growth, market share, profits and return on investment**. **Conservation of natural resources** is a social or environmental concern, **not** an economic analysis.
- Matching cues: "good business environment" → social responsibility; "staying afloat in a downturn" → survival; "satisfactory returns to all for their inputs" → satisfaction; "capturing more market demand" → increase in market share.

### Lesson 2.4: Stakeholders, the agency problem and objectives of financial decisions

**Must know**

- **Stakeholders (interest groups):** everyone with an interest in the firm: **shareholders, employees, customers, creditors (lenders, suppliers), government, competitors, management** and the **community**.
- **Agency relationship:** shareholders (**principals**) appoint managers (**agents**) to run the firm for them. This separation of ownership from control comes with the limited liability company.
- **Agency problem:** the conflict that arises when managers pursue **their own interests** (high pay, perks, job security, a bigger firm) instead of maximising shareholders' wealth.
- **Agency costs:** costs borne by shareholders to reduce or live with the agency problem:
  1. **Monitoring costs:** external audit, board oversight, reports.
  2. **Bonding and incentive costs:** costs of tying managers to owners' interests, such as contractual guarantees, bonuses, share options and performance-linked pay.
  3. **Residual loss:** value still lost because managers' and owners' interests are never perfectly aligned.
- **Objectives of financial decisions** (all aim to maximise the **market price per share**):
  1. **Investment decision:** allocate capital to proposals that yield future benefits (positive NPV) meeting investors' expectations.
  2. **Financing decision:** find the best financing mix (capital structure): funds at the **cheapest cost**, most favourable tax, best mix.
  3. **Dividend decision:** the proportion of earnings to **pay out** or **retain**, bearing in mind shareholders' wealth.
  4. **Liquidity (working capital) decision:** keep enough liquid resources for day-to-day needs.

**Explain more**

- **Signs of the agency problem:** lavish offices and cars, empire building (growth for its own sake), avoiding good but risky projects to protect jobs, short-termism to hit bonus targets.
- **Ways to reduce it:** performance-linked pay and share options; independent non-executive directors; shareholders' votes at the **annual general meeting (AGM)**; the threat of takeover; the market for managers; and corporate governance rules. In Nigeria, listed firms follow the **Nigerian Code of Corporate Governance 2018** (issued by the Financial Reporting Council of Nigeria) and **SEC** rules for public companies.
- A second agency conflict exists between **shareholders and creditors**: owners may push managers into very risky projects funded by lenders' money. Lenders protect themselves with loan covenants.
- Stakeholder interests can clash: higher wages please employees but cut dividends. SWM, done properly over the long run, still requires treating customers, staff and the community fairly, which is why it is said to give all groups a satisfactory return.

### One-minute recap

- Profit maximisation: maximise revenue, minimise cost; the primary, traditional objective from the early 19th century; easy to measure.
- It is attacked by all and sundry: ignores time value of money, ignores risk, profit is undefined, ignores quality, unfair to other interest groups.
- SWM = maximise NPV = maximise the market price per share; the main goal of financial management.
- NPV = PV of benefits − initial investment; PV = C / (1 + r)^n.
- SWM is superior on 8 counts: time value, risk, short/long balance, quality, all groups, yardstick, wooing investors, feasible criteria.
- Other objectives: satisfaction, social responsibility (good business environment), survival (most important in a downturn), market share (market leader).
- Also: growth, productivity, diversification, technical leadership, contented workforce, highest quality service; liquidity, efficiency, innovation, ethics.
- Agency problem: managers (agents) vs shareholders (principals); agency costs = monitoring, bonding, residual loss.
- Investment, financing, dividend and liquidity decisions all aim to maximise market price per share.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A firm can invest ₦700,000 today in a project that returns ₦300,000 at the end of each of the next 3 years. The required return is 10%. (a) What is the accounting profit? (b) What is the NPV? (c) Does the project increase shareholders' wealth?

*Solution:*
- (a) Profit = 3 × 300,000 − 700,000 = **₦200,000**.
- (b) PV year 1 = 300,000 / 1.1 = ₦272,727.27; year 2 = 300,000 / 1.1^2 = ₦247,933.88; year 3 = 300,000 / 1.1^3 = ₦225,394.44. Total PV = ₦746,055.60. NPV = 746,055.60 − 700,000 = **₦46,055.60** (rounded to the nearest kobo).
- (c) Yes. NPV is positive, so the project adds about ₦46,056 of value for shareholders. Note that the wealth gain is smaller than the ₦200,000 profit, because NPV allows for the time value of money and profit does not.
