## Topic 3: Differentiation and Marginal Analysis

**What you must know:** the meaning and notations of the derivative and the first principle; the rules of differentiation (power, product, quotient, chain, exponential, logarithmic, higher derivatives); implicit and logarithmic differentiation; rates of change, percentage rates and approximation; marginal cost, revenue, profit and propensities; elasticity of demand and of cost.

### Lesson 3.1: Meaning, notation and the first principle

**Must know**

- **Derivative (differential coefficient):** the instantaneous rate of change of y with respect to x. f'(a) is the **slope of the tangent** to y = f(x) at x = a. Finding it is called **differentiation**.
- **Average rate of change** from x = a to x = b: [f(b) − f(a)]/(b − a) (slope of the chord). **Instantaneous rate** at x = a: f'(a).
- **Change in f:** f(x + dx) − f(x), found by substituting both values.
- **First principle:** f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x + h) − f(x)]/h.
- **Tangent at x = a:** y − f(a) = f'(a)(x − a).

| Notation | Name | First derivative | Second derivative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio of differentials | Leibniz | dy/dx | d^2y/dx^2 |
| Prime | Lagrange | f'(x), y' | f''(x) |
| Operator D | Euler | Df(x), D\_x y | D^2 f(x) |
| Dot over variable | Newton | y-dot (rate with respect to time) | y-double-dot |

An integral sign, such as integral f(x) dx, is **not** a derivative notation.

**Explain more**

- **First principle for f(x) = x^2:** f(x + h) − f(x) = x^2 + 2xh + h^2 − x^2 = 2xh + h^2. Divide by h: 2x + h. Let h → 0: f'(x) = 2x.
- **First principle for f(x) = sqrt(x):** [sqrt(x + h) − sqrt(x)]/h. Multiply top and bottom by sqrt(x + h) + sqrt(x): the top becomes (x + h) − x = h, leaving 1/[sqrt(x + h) + sqrt(x)]. Let h → 0: f'(x) = 1/(2 sqrt(x)). In the same way, sqrt(ax) = sqrt(a)·x^(1/2) gives sqrt(a)/(2 sqrt(x)) = sqrt(ax)/(2x).
- **Change in f:** f(x) = x^2 + 3x, x from 5 to 5.5: f(5.5) = 30.25 + 16.5 = 46.75; f(5) = 40; change = 6.75. For f(x) = x^2 + 8x, x from 6 to 5.5: f(5.5) − f(6) = 74.25 − 84 = −9.75 (negative because x fell).
- **Average versus instantaneous:** f(x) = x^2 + 3x from 1 to 3: [f(3) − f(1)]/(3 − 1) = (18 − 4)/2 = 7. At x = 1, f'(x) = 2x + 3 = 5.
- **Tangent:** y = x^2 − 4x + 7 at x = 3: y = 4; slope = 2x − 4 = 2; y − 4 = 2(x − 3), so y = 2x − 2.

### Lesson 3.2: Rules of differentiation

**Must know**

1. **Constant:** d/dx (k) = 0.
2. **Power:** d/dx (x^n) = n·x^(n−1), for any real n (negative and fractional too).
3. **Constant multiple:** d/dx [k·f(x)] = k·f'(x).
4. **Sum/difference:** differentiate term by term.
5. **Product:** d/dx (uv) = u(dv/dx) + v(du/dx).
6. **Quotient:** d/dx (u/v) = [v(du/dx) − u(dv/dx)]/v^2.
7. **Chain (function of a function):** dy/dx = (dy/du)(du/dx); so d/dx [f(x)]^n = n[f(x)]^(n−1)·f'(x).

| Function | Derivative |
|---|---|
| e^x | e^x |
| e^(mx) | m·e^(mx) |
| e^(g(x)) | g'(x)·e^(g(x)) |
| a^x | a^x·ln a |
| ln x | 1/x |
| ln g(x) | g'(x)/g(x) |
| ln(kx), k constant | 1/x |

- **Higher derivatives:** f''(x) is the derivative of f'(x); f'''(x) the derivative of f''(x).
- **Differentiating y with respect to z** (both functions of x): dy/dz = (dy/dx)/(dz/dx).

**Explain more**

- **Rewrite before differentiating.** 1/x^3 = x^(−3), giving −3x^(−4) = −3/x^4. 1/x^27 gives −27x^(−28). 1/sqrt(x) = x^(−1/2) gives −(1/2)x^(−3/2). 3x^(2/3) gives 2x^(−1/3). x^15·x^(3/2)/x^20 = x^(−7/2), giving −(7/2)x^(−9/2). 3/sqrt(9x) = 1/sqrt(x), so 3/sqrt(9x) + 1/(2x^2) + sqrt(2/5) differentiates to −1/(2 sqrt(x^3)) − 1/x^3 (the constant sqrt(2/5) gives 0).
- **Constants stay or vanish:** y = ax^4 + bx^2 + c gives 4ax^3 + 2bx. y = x^3 − 20x^2 + 3x + 4 gives 3x^2 − 40x + 3.
- **Expand when easy:** (x^2 + 1/x)^2 = x^4 + 2x + x^(−2), giving 4x^3 + 2 − 2/x^3. (7x + 3)(4 − 3x) = −21x^2 + 19x + 12, giving 19 − 42x. (1 − x)(1 − 2x)(1 − 3x)(1 − 4x) expands to 24x^4 − 50x^3 + 35x^2 − 10x + 1, giving 96x^3 − 150x^2 + 70x − 10.
- **Product rule:** f(x) = (x^2 + 1)(3x − 2) at x = 2. u = x^2 + 1, u' = 2x; v = 3x − 2, v' = 3. f' = 2x(3x − 2) + 3(x^2 + 1) = 2(2)(4) + 3(5) = 31. Also x^2(3x + 1) gives 2x(3x + 1) + 3x^2 = 9x^2 + 2x.
- **Product with a fractional power:** x^3(6x − 1)^(2/3) gives 3x^2(6x − 1)^(2/3) + x^3·(2/3)(6x − 1)^(−1/3)·6. Factor out x^2(6x − 1)^(−1/3): x^2[3(6x − 1) + 4x]/(6x − 1)^(1/3) = x^2(22x − 3)/(6x − 1)^(1/3).
- **Quotient rule:** f(x) = (2x + 3)/(x − 1). f' = [2(x − 1) − 1(2x + 3)]/(x − 1)^2 = −5/(x − 1)^2; at x = 3, −5/4 = −1.25. Likewise 5x/(3 − 4x) gives [5(3 − 4x) + 4(5x)]/(3 − 4x)^2 = 15/(3 − 4x)^2; (x^2 + 2x − 21)/(x − 3) gives (x^2 − 6x + 15)/(x − 3)^2; x^2/(3x + 2)^(1/2) gives x(9x + 8)/[2(3x + 2)^(3/2)].
- **Chain rule:** (3x^2 + 4)^100 gives 100(3x^2 + 4)^99·6x = 600x(3x^2 + 4)^99 (never forget the inside derivative 6x). (2x^2 − 3)^4 gives 4(2x^2 − 3)^3·4x; at x = 1: 4(−1)(4) = −16. (2x^2 − 5x − 8)^2 gives 2(2x^2 − 5x − 8)(4x − 5). A term −45(5x^2 + 4)^(−1/2) gives −45·(−1/2)(5x^2 + 4)^(−3/2)·10x = +225x/(5x^2 + 4)^(3/2).
- **Chain rule with u:** y = u + sqrt(u), u = x^3 + 17, at x = 2. u = 25. dy/du = 1 + 1/(2 sqrt(u)) = 1 + 1/10 = 1.1; du/dx = 3x^2 = 12. dy/dx = 1.1 × 12 = 13.2 = 66/5. Similarly y = u/(u + 1), u = 3x^2 − 1, x = 1: u = 2, dy/du = 1/(u + 1)^2 = 1/9, du/dx = 6x = 6, dy/dx = 6/9 = 2/3.
- **Exponentials and logs:** 4x^4 + (2x + 1)^3 − (1/4)e^(4x) gives 16x^3 + 6(2x + 1)^2 − e^(4x). e^(x^2 + 2x) gives (2x + 2)e^(x^2 + 2x); e^(ax^2 + bx + c) + ln x gives (2ax + b)e^(ax^2 + bx + c) + 1/x. 3e^(2x) − 4 ln x + 7 gives 6e^(2x) − 4/x. (ln x)^2 gives 2 ln x·(1/x) = 2 ln x/x; (ln x)^5 gives (5/x)(ln x)^4. ln(3x^2 + 1) gives 6x/(3x^2 + 1) = 6/4 = 1.50 at x = 1. 2^x gives 2^x ln 2 = 8 × 0.6931 = 5.55 at x = 3 (2 d.p.).
- **Trap:** d/dx [ln(5x)] = 5/(5x) = 1/x, **not** 5/x.
- **Investment growth:** V(t) = 20,000e^(0.08t). V'(t) = 1,600e^(0.08t); at t = 5: 1,600e^(0.4) = 1,600 × 1.491825 = ₦2,386.92 per year (2 d.p.).
- **Higher derivatives:** f(x) = 5x^7 + 3x^3 − 2x^2 + 10: f' = 35x^6 + 9x^2 − 4x; f'' = 210x^5 + 18x − 4; f''' = 1050x^4 + 18. f(x) = 2x^4 − 3x^2 + 5x: f'' = 24x^2 − 6, so f''(1) = 18.
- **With respect to another function:** x^5 with respect to x^2: 5x^4/(2x) = (5/2)x^3. ln x with respect to x^2: (1/x)/(2x) = 1/(2x^2).
- **Log laws first:** ln[e^x((x − 1)/(x + 1))^(3/2)] = x + (3/2)ln(x − 1) − (3/2)ln(x + 1). Derivative: 1 + (3/2)[1/(x − 1) − 1/(x + 1)] = 1 + 3/(x^2 − 1) = (x^2 + 2)/(x^2 − 1). Also ln(sqrt(x + 1) − sqrt(x − 1)) differentiates to −1/(2 sqrt(x^2 − 1)) for x > 1.

### Lesson 3.3: Implicit and logarithmic differentiation

**Must know**

- **Implicit differentiation:** when y is not made the subject, differentiate every term with respect to x, writing d/dx (y^n) = n·y^(n−1)·(dy/dx) and using the product rule on terms like xy; then collect dy/dx.
- **Partial-derivative shortcut:** for f(x, y) = 0, dy/dx = −fx/fy, where fx treats y as constant and fy treats x as constant.
- **Logarithmic differentiation** (for u^v, a variable raised to a variable power): take ln of both sides, ln y = v·ln u; differentiate: (1/y)(dy/dx) = v'·ln u + v·u'/u; multiply by y.

**Explain more**

- **x^2 + y − 2x = 0:** 2x + dy/dx − 2 = 0, so dy/dx = 2 − 2x. By partials: fx = 2x − 2, fy = 1, dy/dx = −(2x − 2)/1 = 2 − 2x.
- **x^2 − y^2 + 3x = 5y:** 2x − 2y(dy/dx) + 3 = 5(dy/dx), so (2x + 3) = (5 + 2y)(dy/dx), giving dy/dx = (2x + 3)/(2y + 5).
- **2x^2 + 3x + y^2 = 0:** 4x + 3 + 2y(dy/dx) = 0, so dy/dx = −(4x + 3)/(2y). **5x^3 − 3x^2 + 3y^2 − 6 = 7:** 15x^2 − 6x + 6y(dy/dx) = 0, so dy/dx = x(2 − 5x)/(2y).
- **Cross terms:** x^2 + 2hxy + y^2 = 0: fx = 2x + 2hy, fy = 2hx + 2y, so dy/dx = −(x + hy)/(hx + y). ax^2 + 2mxy + by^2 = k gives −(ax + my)/(mx + by). x^3 + 3bxy + y^3 = b^4 gives −(x^2 + by)/(bx + y^2).
- **ln(xy) = x^2 + y^2:** ln x + ln y = x^2 + y^2, so 1/x + (1/y)(dy/dx) = 2x + 2y(dy/dx). Collect: (dy/dx)(1/y − 2y) = 2x − 1/x, giving dy/dx = y(2x^2 − 1)/[x(1 − 2y^2)].
- **e^(xy) − 4xy = 2:** e^(xy)(y + x·dy/dx) − 4(y + x·dy/dx) = 0, so (e^(xy) − 4)(y + x·dy/dx) = 0, giving dy/dx = −y/x.
- **(2x)^(5x + 2):** ln y = (5x + 2)ln(2x). (1/y)y' = 5 ln(2x) + (5x + 2)(2/(2x)) = 5 ln(2x) + (5x + 2)/x. So y' = (2x)^(5x + 2)[(5x + 2)/x + 5 ln(2x)].
- **(1 + x)^(2x):** ln y = 2x ln(1 + x); y'/y = 2 ln(1 + x) + 2x/(1 + x); y' = 2(1 + x)^(2x)[x/(1 + x) + ln(1 + x)].
- **x^(ln x):** ln y = (ln x)^2; y'/y = 2 ln x/x; y' = 2x^(ln x − 1)·ln x.
- **(12x^2 + 6)^(5x + 2):** y'/y = 5 ln(12x^2 + 6) + (5x + 2)(24x)/(12x^2 + 6), and 24x/(12x^2 + 6) = 4x/(2x^2 + 1).
- **x^(x^x):** ln y = x^x ln x. Since d/dx (x^x) = x^x(1 + ln x), y'/y = x^x(1 + ln x)ln x + x^x/x, so y' = x^(x^x)[x^x ln x(1 + ln x) + x^(x − 1)].
- **Trap:** d/dx (2x)^(5x + 2) is not 2(5x + 2)(2x)^(5x + 1); the power rule only works when the exponent is constant.

### Lesson 3.4: Rates of change, percentage rates and approximation

**Must know**

- **Rate of change** of Q with respect to t: Q'(t).
- **Percentage rate of change:** 100·f'(x)/f(x).
- **Approximation:** Δf ≈ f'(x)·Δx. **Percentage change** ≈ 100·f'(x)Δx/f(x).
- **Actual change** over an interval: f(new) − f(old). "During the 16th month" means from x = 15 to x = 16; "during the 6th year" means t = 5 to t = 6.
- **Input needed** for a target output change: ΔL ≈ ΔQ/Q'(L).
- **Time variable:** t counts years from the base year; "in 1991" with base 1987 means t = 4. Ten minutes = 1/6 hour; 6 months = 0.5 year; a quarter = 0.25 year.

**Explain more**

- **Population:** P(x) = x^2 + 20x + 8,000. P'(x) = 2x + 20; at x = 15: 50 people per month. Actual change in the 16th month: P(16) − P(15) = 8,576 − 8,525 = 51 people.
- **Newspaper:** C(t) = 100t^2 + 400t + 5,000. C'(t) = 200t + 400; at t = 5: 1,400 per year. Actual change in the 6th year: C(6) − C(5) = 11,000 − 9,500 = 1,500. Next 6 months from now: C'(0) × 0.5 = 400 × 0.5 = 200.
- **Worker output:** f(x) = −x^3 + 6x^2 + 15x radios, from 8:00 a.m. At 9:00 (x = 1): f'(x) = −3x^2 + 12x + 15 = 24 per hour. Actual between 9 and 10: f(2) − f(1) = 46 − 20 = 26. For Q(t) = −t^3 + 6t^2 + 24t at 11:00 (t = 3): Q' = −27 + 36 + 24 = 33 units per hour. Change in that rate from 11:00 to 11:10: Q''(t) = −6t + 12 = −6; × 1/6 hour = −1, a decrease of about 1 unit per hour.
- **Percentage rate:** GNP N(t) = t^2 + 5t + 106 (₦ billion, t after 1980). In 1988, t = 8: N' = 2(8) + 5 = 21; N = 210; 100 × 21/210 = 10% per year. For f(x) = x(x + 3)^2 at x = 3: f' = (x + 3)^2 + 2x(x + 3) = 72, f = 108, rate = 66.67%.
- **Earnings:** A(t) = 0.1t^2 + 10t + 20 (₦ thousand, formed 1987). In 1991, t = 4: A' = 0.2(4) + 10 = 10.8, i.e. ₦10,800 per year; A = 61.6; percentage = 100 × 10.8/61.6 = 17.53% (2 d.p.). For f(t) = sqrt(10t^2 + t + 236) in January 1992 (t = 4): f = sqrt(400) = 20; f' = (20t + 1)/(2f) = 81/40 = 2.025, i.e. ₦2,025 per year; percentage = 100 × 2.025/20 = 10.125%.
- **Long run:** P(t) = t^2 + 200t + 10,000 = (t + 100)^2. Percentage rate = 100 × 2(t + 100)/(t + 100)^2 = 200/(t + 100)%, which approaches 0 as t grows.
- **Linear growth:** GNP ₦125 billion (1986), ₦155 billion (1988): it rises ₦15 billion per year. In 1991 GNP = 125 + 5(15) = 200; percentage rate = 100 × 15/200 = 7.5%.
- **Estimating a change:** f(x) = x^2 − 3x + 5, x from 5 to 5.3: f'(5) = 7; Δf ≈ 7 × 0.3 = 2.1. Cost C(q) = 3q^2 + 5q + 10, q from 40 to 40.5: C'(40) = 245; ΔC ≈ 245 × 0.5 = ₦122.50. C(q) = 0.1q^3 − 0.5q^2 + 500q + 200, q from 4 to 4.1: C'(4) = 4.8 − 4 + 500 = 500.8; ΔC ≈ ₦50.08. Carbon monoxide Q(t) = 0.05t^2 + 0.1t + 3.4 over the coming 6 months: Q'(0) × 0.5 = 0.05 ppm.
- **Percentage change:** GNP N(t) = t^2 + 5t + 200 (base 1990), first quarter of 1998: t = 8, Δt = 0.25. N'(8) = 21, N(8) = 304. ΔN ≈ 5.25; 100 × 5.25/304 = 1.73%. Property tax T(x) = 60x^(3/2) + 40x + 1,200 (base 1988), first half of 1992: x = 4, Δx = 0.5; T' = 90x^(1/2) + 40 = 220; ΔT ≈ 110; T(4) = 1,840; 100 × 110/1,840 = 5.98%, about 6%.
- **Capital:** Q(k) = 600k^(1/2) with k in ₦1,000 units, k = 900 (₦900,000), extra ₦800 means Δk = 0.8. Q'(900) = 300/30 = 10; ΔQ ≈ 8 units.
- **1% rise in input:** Q = 4,000K^(1/2). ΔK = 0.01K; ΔQ ≈ 2,000K^(−1/2) × 0.01K = 20K^(1/2); percentage = 100 × 20K^(1/2)/(4,000K^(1/2)) = 0.5%. Rule: if Q = A·K^n, a 1% rise in K raises Q by about n%. So 400K^(1/2) also gives 0.5%.
- **Labour needed:** Q(L) = 900L^(1/3), L = 1,000. Q' = 300L^(−2/3) = 300/100 = 3; ΔL ≈ 15/3 = 5 worker-hours. Q(L) = 300L^(2/3), L = 512: Q' = 200L^(−1/3) = 200/8 = 25; ΔL ≈ 12.5/25 = 0.5 worker-hour.
- **Chain rule in context:** carbon monoxide C(p) = sqrt(0.5p^2 + 17), p(t) = 3.1 + 0.1t^2. At t = 3, p = 4. dC/dp = 0.5p/sqrt(0.5p^2 + 17) = 2/5 = 0.4; dp/dt = 0.2t = 0.6; dC/dt = 0.4 × 0.6 = 0.24 ppm per year.

### Lesson 3.5: Marginal analysis

**Must know**

- **Marginal cost (MC):** MC = C'(x) = dTC/dx, the rate of change of total cost; approximately the cost of the **(x + 1)th** unit.
- **Actual cost of the nth unit:** C(n) − C(n − 1).
- **Marginal revenue (MR):** MR = R'(x); TR = p·x. **Average revenue:** AR = R/x = p.
- **Marginal profit:** P'(x) = MR − MC, where P(x) = R(x) − C(x).
- **Average cost:** AC = C(x)/x. **Total cost from AC:** C = AC·x, then MC = dC/dx.
- **Marginal average cost:** MAC = d(AC)/dx. **Rate of change of MC:** C''(x).
- **MC and AC:** MC < AC means AC falling; MC > AC means AC rising; MC = AC at minimum AC.
- **Minimum AC:** solve d(AC)/dx = 0 and check d^2(AC)/dx^2 > 0.
- **MR = 0** gives the output of maximum total revenue; **MR = MC** gives the profit-maximising output.
- **Marginal propensity to consume:** MPC = dC/dY. **Savings** S = Y − C; **MPS** = dS/dY = 1 − MPC.
- **C''(x) > 0:** MC rising; **C''(x) < 0:** MC falling.

**Explain more**

- **Interpreting MC:** C(x) = 10,000 + 30x + 0.02x^2. MC = 30 + 0.04x; at 200: ₦38 per unit. This means raising output from 200 to 201 adds **about** ₦38. The actual 201st unit costs C(201) − C(200) = 16,838.02 − 16,800 = ₦38.02.
- **More MC values:** 25,000 + 40x + 0.05x^2 at 300: 40 + 0.1(300) = ₦70. 0.01x^3 − 0.6x^2 + 50x + 8,000 at 30: 0.03(900) − 1.2(30) + 50 = ₦41. 1,000 + 25x + 2x^2 at 15: 25 + 60 = 85. 2x^2 + 3x + 4 at 5: 23. Oil C = 50 + 1.5x + 0.02x^2: MC = 1.5 + 0.04x, ₦1.70 at x = 5, ₦3.10 at x = 40. Pencils 30 + 1.5x + 0.0008x^2 at 1,000: ₦3.10. Pens C(p) = 0.0015p^3 − 0.9p^2 + 200p + 60,000: MC = 0.0045p^2 − 1.8p + 200 = ₦65, ₦20, ₦65 at 100, 200, 300.
- **Estimate versus actual:** C = (1/8)x^2 + 3x + 98. The 9th unit is estimated by C'(8) = (1/4)(8) + 3 = ₦5.00; actual C(9) − C(8) = 135.125 − 130 = ₦5.125. C = 1,000 + 25x + 2x^2: actual 15th unit = C(15) − C(14) = 1,825 − 1,742 = 83. C(q) = q^3 − 30q^2 + 400q + 500: 20th unit = C(20) − C(19) = 4,500 − 4,129 = ₦371. C(q) = q^3 − 6q^2 + 20q + 400: MC at 10 = 300 − 120 + 20 = ₦200; 11th unit = 1,225 − 1,000 = ₦225.
- **MR from demand:** always form TR = p × quantity first. P = 60 − Q: TR = 60Q − Q^2, MR = 60 − 2Q = −40 at Q = 50; the actual change from 50 to 51 is TR(51) − TR(50) = 459 − 500 = −41. P = 100 − 4Q: MR = 100 − 8Q = 4 at Q = 12, so a 0.3 rise changes TR by about 4 × 0.3 = 1.2. p = 800 − 4x: MR = 800 − 8x = ₦320 at 60. p = 1,500 − 2x − x^2: TR = 1,500x − 2x^2 − x^3, MR = 1,500 − 4x − 3x^2 = 220 at 20. p = 30 + 2x − 5x^2: MR = 30 + 4x − 15x^2 = −93 at 3. R(x) = 12x + 2x^2 + 6: MR = 12 + 4x = 212 at 50; actual 51st item = R(51) − R(50) = 214. p = (1/3)(75 − x): R = 25x − x^2/3, R'(8) = 25 − 16/3 = ₦19.67 estimates the 9th unit; actual R(9) − R(8) = 198 − 178.67 = ₦19.33.
- **Make price the subject first:** Q = 400 − 2P gives P = 200 − 0.5Q; TR = 200Q − 0.5Q^2; MR = 200 − Q = ₦80 at Q = 120. x = 100 − 4p gives p = 25 − x/4; MR = 25 − x/2 = 0 at x = 50, so p = ₦12.50.
- **Linear demand from two points:** p = ax + b, selling 1,250 at ₦5 and 1,500 at ₦4. a = (4 − 5)/(1,500 − 1,250) = −1/250; 5 = −5 + b, b = 10. p = 10 − x/250; TR = 10x − x^2/250; MR = 10 − x/125. MR = 0 at x = 1,250, price ₦5. For 1,000 at ₦4 and 1,500 at ₦2: a = −1/250, b = 8, MR = 8 − x/125.
- **MC from AC:** AC = 100/Q + 2 gives TC = 100 + 2Q, MC = 2. Radios AC = x^2 − 10x + 30 (₦ hundred): C = x^3 − 10x^2 + 30x, MC = 3x^2 − 20x + 30 = 18 at x = 6, i.e. ₦1,800. AC = 0.006x^2 − 0.02x − 30 + 5,000/x: C = 0.006x^3 − 0.02x^2 − 30x + 5,000, MC = 0.018x^2 − 0.04x − 30 = 45 − 2 − 30 = 13 at 50. AC = x + 5 + 36/x: C = x^2 + 5x + 36, MC = 2x + 5 = 25 at 10.
- **AC and MC at a point:** C = 2x^2 + x − 5 at x = 4: AC = 31/4 = 7.75; MC = 4x + 1 = 17. C = 3,200 + 25x + 0.5x^2 at 50: MC = 25 + x = ₦75; AC = 64 + 25 + 25 = ₦114. MC < AC, so AC is **falling**.
- **When is AC rising?** AC = x + 5 + 36/x: d(AC)/dx = 1 − 36/x^2 > 0 when x > 6.
- **MAC and C'':** C = 0.002x^3 − 0.04x^2 + 5x + 1,500: AC = 0.002x^2 − 0.04x + 5 + 1,500/x; MAC = 0.004x − 0.04 − 1,500/x^2. MC = 0.006x^2 − 0.08x + 5; rate of change of MC = 0.012x − 0.08. For C = 0.02x^3 − 3x^2 + 200x + 5,000: C'' = 0.12x − 6 = −1.2 at 40, so MC is falling by about ₦1.20 per extra unit.
- **MC = AC:** C = (3/4)x^2 − 7x + 27. MC = 1.5x − 7; AC = 0.75x − 7 + 27/x. Equate: 0.75x = 27/x, x^2 = 36, x = 6.
- **Minimum AC:** C = 1,800 + 10x + 2x^2. AC = 1,800/x + 10 + 2x. d(AC)/dx = −1,800/x^2 + 2 = 0, so x = 30. d^2(AC)/dx^2 = 3,600/x^3 > 0, a minimum. AC = 60 + 10 + 60 = ₦130 per unit.
- **Marginal profit:** R = 120x − 0.5x^2, C = 2,000 + 40x + 0.3x^2. MR = 120 − x = 90; MC = 40 + 0.6x = 58 at x = 30. Marginal profit = ₦32.
- **MR = MC:** pencils sold at ₦5 each, so MR = 5. 1.5 + 0.0016x = 5 gives x = 2,187.5. C = 50x + 30,000 and p = 100 − 0.01x: MC = 50, MR = 100 − 0.02x; 100 − 0.02x = 50 gives x = 2,500.
- **MPC and MPS:** C = 40 + 0.7Y + 2 sqrt(Y): MPC = 0.7 + 1/sqrt(Y) = 0.7 + 0.1 = 0.80 at Y = 100. C = 150 + 0.9Y − 0.0002Y^2: MPC = 0.9 − 0.0004Y = 0.7 at Y = 500, so MPS = 1 − 0.7 = 0.30.

### Lesson 3.6: Elasticity

**Must know**

- **Price elasticity of demand (point):** eta = −(p/q)(dq/dp), a positive number. Some questions use the signed form E = (p/q)(dq/dp), which is negative for normal demand.
- **Meaning:** a 1% rise in price cuts quantity demanded by about eta%. % change in quantity ≈ eta × % change in price.

| Value of eta | Demand | Raising price makes TR |
|---|---|---|
| eta > 1 | Elastic | fall |
| eta < 1 | Inelastic | rise |
| eta = 1 | Unit elastic | stay about the same (TR is at its maximum) |

- With the signed form E, unit elasticity means E = −1.
- **Inverse demand** (P in terms of Q): dQ/dP = 1/(dP/dQ).
- **Constant elasticity:** Q = A/P^n gives eta = n at every price.
- **Elasticity of total cost with respect to output:** (dTC/dq)/(TC/q) = MC/AC.

**Explain more**

- **q = 240 − 2p at p = 100:** q = 40; dq/dp = −2; eta = (100/40)(2) = 5. Elastic: a 1% price rise cuts demand by about 5%. Unit elasticity: 2p/(240 − 2p) = 1 gives 4p = 240, p = 60.
- **q = 300 − p^2:** eta = (p/(300 − p^2))(2p) = 2p^2/(300 − p^2). eta = 1 when 3p^2 = 300, p = 10. Inelastic for 0 ≤ p < 10, elastic for p > 10. TR = 300p − p^3, TR' = 300 − 3p^2 = 0 at p = 10: total revenue is **greatest** at unit elasticity.
- **Signed form:** q = p/(p − 5), p > 5. dq/dp = [(p − 5) − p]/(p − 5)^2 = −5/(p − 5)^2. E = (p/q)(dq/dp) = (p − 5)·(−5)/(p − 5)^2 = −5/(p − 5) = 5/(5 − p). At p = 7: −2.5. Unit elastic: 5/(5 − p) = −1 gives p = 10.
- **Inverse demand:** P = 100 − 2Q at Q = 20: P = 60; dP/dQ = −2, so dQ/dP = −1/2. eta = (60/20)(1/2) = 1.5, elastic.
- **Constant elasticity:** Q = 2,000/P^2 = 2,000P^(−2); dQ/dP = −4,000P^(−3); eta = (P/(2,000P^(−2)))(4,000P^(−3)) = 2, elastic at every price.
- **Estimating a fall in demand:** Q = 500 − 5P at P = ₦60: Q = 200; eta = (60/200)(5) = 1.5. A 2% price rise cuts Q by about 1.5 × 2 = 3%.
- **Trap:** elasticity of cost divides MC **by** AC; it is not MC × AC, and it uses TC, not TR.

### One-minute recap

- Notations: dy/dx Leibniz, f'(x) Lagrange, Df(x) Euler, y-dot Newton; f'(a) is the slope of the tangent.
- First principle: f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x + h) − f(x)]/h.
- Power, product u·v' + v·u', quotient (v·u' − u·v')/v^2, chain (dy/du)(du/dx); d/dx e^(mx) = m·e^(mx), d/dx ln g = g'/g, d/dx a^x = a^x ln a.
- Implicit: dy/dx = −fx/fy; u^v needs logarithmic differentiation.
- Percentage rate = 100·f'/f; Δf ≈ f'(x)Δx; actual nth unit = f(n) − f(n − 1).
- MC = C', MR = R' (form TR = p·x first), marginal profit = MR − MC, MC from AC via C = AC·x.
- MC = AC at minimum AC; MR = 0 maximises TR; MR = MC maximises profit; MPS = 1 − MPC.
- eta = −(p/q)(dq/dp): above 1 elastic, below 1 inelastic, equal to 1 maximises TR; elasticity of cost = MC/AC.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A bakery in Yaba has total cost C(x) = 0.5x^2 + 20x + 800 naira for x loaves. Find (a) MC at x = 40, (b) the actual cost of the 41st loaf, (c) AC at x = 40, and (d) the output that minimises AC.

*Solution:*
- (a) MC = C'(x) = x + 20; at 40, MC = ₦60.
- (b) C(41) − C(40) = (840.5 + 820 + 800) − (800 + 800 + 800) = 2,460.5 − 2,400 = ₦60.50.
- (c) AC = C/x = 0.5x + 20 + 800/x = 20 + 20 + 20 = ₦60.
- (d) d(AC)/dx = 0.5 − 800/x^2 = 0 gives x^2 = 1,600, x = 40; d^2(AC)/dx^2 = 1,600/x^3 > 0, so this is a minimum. Check: at x = 40, MC = AC = ₦60, as the rule says.
