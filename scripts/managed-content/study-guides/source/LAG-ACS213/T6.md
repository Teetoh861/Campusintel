## Topic 6: Mathematics of Finance

**What you must know:** percentage change, profit and loss, discounts and depreciation; simple interest, exact and ordinary time, bank discount and stock yield; compound, effective and continuous interest, and solving for time or rate with logarithms; present value, net present value and bonds; annuities, sinking funds and perpetuities; present value of an annuity and amortisation of loans.

### Lesson 6.1: Percentages, profit and loss, depreciation

**Must know**

- **Multiplier rule:** a rise of r% multiplies by (1 + r/100); a fall of r% multiplies by (1 − r/100). Successive changes multiply, they never add.
- **Percentage change** = (new − old)/old × 100.
- **Profit %** = profit/cost price × 100. **Cost price** after a loss of r%: CP = SP/(1 − r/100); after a gain: CP = SP/(1 + r/100).
- **Straight-line (linear) depreciation:** annual charge = (cost − scrap value)/useful life; value after t years V(t) = cost − (annual charge) × t. The same naira amount is written off every year.
- **Reducing-balance depreciation:** V = C(1 − r)^n, r = annual rate as a decimal. The same **percentage of the previous year's value** is written off each year, so the naira charge falls every year and the value never reaches zero. The yearly values form a geometric progression with common ratio (1 − r).
- **Time to fall to a fraction k:** n = ln k / ln(1 − r). Rate from two values: r = 1 − (V/C)^(1/n).

**Explain more**

- **Successive changes.** Gold falls 34% then rises 12%: 0.66 × 1.12 = 0.7392, so the overall change is 0.7392 − 1 = −0.2608, a **decrease of about 26%**. A 25% rise then 20% fall: 1.25 × 0.8 = 1, **no overall change**. A 40% fall then 50% rise: 0.6 × 1.5 = 0.9, a **decrease of 10%**. Discounts of 20% then 10%: 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72, a **single discount of 28%** (not 30%). After a 20% fall, the price must rise 20/80 × 100 = **25%** to recover.
- **Single changes.** ₦2,500 to ₦3,375: 875/2,500 × 100 = 35%. Sale price after 20% off ₦580: 580 × 0.8 = ₦464. 15% tax on ₦1,360: 1,360 × 1.15 = ₦1,564. A 7% fall on ₦9,500: 9,500 × 0.93 = ₦8,835.
- **Profit and loss.** 50 crates at ₦4,800 cost ₦240,000; 45 sold at ₦6,000 bring ₦270,000; profit ₦30,000 = 30,000/240,000 × 100 = **12.5%**. Sold at ₦171,000 at a 5% loss: CP = 171,000/0.95 = **₦180,000**. Tunde buys at ₦6,000; two profits of x% make Musa pay 6,000 + 2,640 = ₦8,640, so 6,000(1 + x/100)^2 = 8,640, (1 + x/100)^2 = 1.44, 1 + x/100 = 1.2, **x = 20**.
- **Straight line.** Machine ₦2,400,000, scrap ₦400,000, 8 years: (2,400,000 − 400,000)/8 = **₦250,000** a year. Generator ₦1,800,000 to ₦300,000 over 5 years: charge ₦300,000; book value after 3 years = 1,800,000 − 3 × 300,000 = **₦900,000**. Books worth ₦1,500 depreciated linearly to zero over 10 years lose ₦150 a year: **V(t) = 1,500 − 150t**.
- **Reducing balance.** Flat ₦1,500,000 at 25%: 1,500,000 × 0.75^5 = 1,500,000 × 0.2373047 = **₦355,957**. Machine ₦50,000 at 10% for 7 years: 50,000 × 0.9^7 = **₦23,915**. - **Time to halve.** At 15%: n = ln 0.5/ln 0.85 = −0.693147/−0.162519 = **4.27 years**. At 7%: n = ln 0.5/ln 0.93 = 9.55; after 9 years the value is still above half (0.93^9 = 0.520), after 10 years it is below (0.484), so to the nearest whole year the answer is **10**.

### Lesson 6.2: Simple interest, bank discount and stock yield

**Must know**

- **Simple interest:** interest on the original principal only, never on interest. I = Pin; F = P + I = P(1 + in).
- P = principal, i = annual rate (decimal), n = time **in years**, F = future value (amount).
- Rearranged: P = I/(in); i = I/(Pn); n = I/(Pi). Months: n = months/12.
- **Days, 2 methods:**
  1. **Exact** method: n = days/365.
  2. **Ordinary** method (**Banker's Rule**): n = days/360 (twelve 30-day months). Ordinary interest is always the larger.
- **Yield on stock** = annual dividend per share / price per share × 100%.
- **Bank discount:** interest worked on the amount repaid (maturity value F), deducted in advance. The borrower receives the **proceeds** P = F(1 − dn); to receive P now, repay F = P/(1 − dn). d = bank discount rate.

**Explain more**

- **Principal:** ₦18,900 in 2 years 6 months at 14%: P = 18,900/(0.14 × 2.5) = **₦54,000**.

### Lesson 6.3: Compound, effective and continuous interest

**Must know**

- **Compound interest:** interest is added to the principal at the end of each period, so later interest is earned on principal **plus** interest already added ("interest on interest").
- **Period (conversion period):** the interval at the end of which interest is added to the principal. At the same nominal rate, **the shorter the period, the greater the interest**.
- F = P(1 + i)^n, i = rate per period, n = number of periods. With nominal annual rate r compounded m times a year for t years: **A = P(1 + r/m)^(mt)**. Here m (often written n) is **the number of times interest is compounded per year**: annually 1, semi-annually 2, quarterly 4, monthly 12, weekly 52, daily 365.
- **Time:** n = ln(F/P)/ln(1 + i) periods. Doubling, annual compounding: **t = ln 2/ln(1 + r)**.
- **Rate:** i = (F/P)^(1/n) − 1 per period; nominal = m × i.
- **Effective rate:** re = (1 + i)^m − 1, the equivalent once-a-year rate; used to compare offers. Nominal from effective: r = m[(1 + re)^(1/m) − 1].
- **Continuous compounding:** **A = Pe^(rt)** (the limit as m tends to infinity). Effective rate = e^r − 1. Rate: r = ln(A/P)/t. Time: t = ln(A/P)/r.

### Lesson 6.4: Present value, net present value and bonds

**Must know**

- **Discounting:** finding the present value of a sum due in the future (the reverse of compounding).
- **Present value:** P = F(1 + i)^(−n) = F/(1 + i)^n. (1 + i)^(−n) is the **compound discount factor**. Continuous: P = Fe^(−rt).
- **Net present value (NPV)** = sum of present values of all future returns − initial cost.
  1. NPV > 0: returns are worth more than the cost in today's money; worth undertaking at that rate.
  2. NPV < 0: reject. NPV = 0: the project earns exactly the discount rate.
- **Internal rate of return (IRR):** the rate at which NPV = 0.
- **Bond:** pays an annual return (coupon = coupon rate × face value) each year, plus the face value at maturity. Present value = PV of coupons + PV of face value, at the **current** interest rate. If the market rate is **above** the coupon rate, PV is **less than** face value; below it, PV is greater.

**Explain more**

### Lesson 6.5: Annuities, sinking funds and perpetuities

**Must know**

- **Annuity:** a series of equal payments at regular intervals. **2 types:**
  1. **Ordinary annuity:** each payment at the **end** of the period.
  2. **Annuity due:** each payment at the **beginning** of the period (also called an increasing annuity in savings questions).
- **Future value of an ordinary annuity:** F = R[(1 + i)^n − 1]/i. R = payment per period, i = rate per period, n = number of payments. It is the sum of a geometric progression R + R(1 + i) + ... + R(1 + i)^(n−1).
- **Future value of an annuity due:** F = R[(1 + i)^n − 1]/i × (1 + i) = R[(1 + i)^(n+1) − (1 + i)]/i. So **FV(due) = (1 + i) × FV(ordinary)**.
- **Sinking fund:** equal deposits to build a set sum A by a future date. **R = Ai/[(1 + i)^n − 1]**.
- **Perpetuity:** payments forever from interest alone, fund never used up. Fund = payment per period/rate per period. If payments are yearly but compounding is not, use the effective annual rate: fund = annual payment/[(1 + i)^m − 1].
- Total deposited = nR; interest earned = F − nR.

### Lesson 6.6: Present value of an annuity and amortisation

**Must know**

- **Present value of an ordinary annuity:** P = R[1 − (1 + i)^(−n)]/i: the lump sum now that provides n equal payments, the last one leaving zero.
- **Amortisation of a loan:** repaying a loan, with its interest, by a series of **equal instalments at regular intervals**. Instalment: **R = Pi/[1 − (1 + i)^(−n)]**.
- **Total interest** paid on a loan (or earned on an invested lump sum) = nR − P.
- **Outstanding balance after one payment** = P(1 + i) − R. **Principal part of the first payment** = R − Pi.
- Do not confuse: sinking fund R = Ai/[(1 + i)^n − 1] (builds a **future** sum); amortisation R = Pi/[1 − (1 + i)^(−n)] (repays a **present** loan).

### One-minute recap

- Successive percentage changes multiply: 0.66 × 1.12 = 0.7392 means a 26% fall.
- Straight line: (cost − scrap)/life each year; reducing balance: V = C(1 − r)^n.
- Simple: I = Pin, F = P(1 + in); exact uses 365 days, ordinary (Banker's Rule) 360. Bank discount: P = F(1 − dn).
- Compound: A = P(1 + r/m)^(mt); m = times compounded per year; shorter period, more interest.
- Continuous: A = Pe^(rt); effective rate (1 + i)^m − 1 or e^r − 1.
- Time with logs: n = ln(F/P)/ln(1 + i); round up for "first exceed".
- PV = F(1 + i)^(−n); NPV = PV of returns − cost; positive NPV means accept; IRR makes NPV zero.
- Ordinary annuity F = R[(1 + i)^n − 1]/i; annuity due multiplies this by (1 + i).
- Sinking fund R = Ai/[(1 + i)^n − 1]; amortisation R = Pi/[1 − (1 + i)^(−n)]; interest = nR − P.
- Perpetuity fund = payment/effective rate per payment period.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A bank lends ₦100,000 as a mortgage repaid by equal monthly payments over 5 years at 6% compounded monthly. Find the monthly payment and the total interest.

*Solution:* i = 0.06/12 = 0.005; n = 5 × 12 = 60. (1.005)^(−60) = 0.741372, so 1 − 0.741372 = 0.258628. R = Pi/[1 − (1 + i)^(−n)] = 100,000 × 0.005/0.258628 = 500/0.258628 = **₦1,933.28**. Total paid = 60 × 1,933.28 = ₦115,996.81 (unrounded R); interest = 115,996.81 − 100,000 = **₦15,997** (nearest naira).
