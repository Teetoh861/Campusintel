## Topic 5: Demand, Supply, Cost, Revenue and Break-even

**What you must know:** demand and supply functions and market equilibrium (linear, quadratic, fractional and two-product); excess demand and excess supply; taxes and subsidies; cost functions (FC, VC, AFC, AVC, ATC, MC); revenue, profit and market type; price elasticity of demand; break-even, contribution and margin of safety.

### Lesson 5.1: Demand, supply and market equilibrium

**Must know**

- **Demand function:** the amount of a commodity buyers are willing to purchase at various prices. Written x = f(p) or, as an **inverse demand function**, p = g(x). Demand falls as price rises.
- **Supply function:** the amount sellers have available to offer at various prices. Supply rises as price rises.
- **Market equilibrium:** the point where the demand and supply curves intersect: **Qd = Qs** (or D(p) = S(p)). No surplus, no shortage. (In set language, equilibrium is the **intersection** of the demand set and the supply set.)

Steps for any equilibrium question:

1. Set demand equal to supply.
2. Solve for the price (or quantity). Clear fractions first; a quadratic gives two roots.
3. **Reject** negative prices or quantities.
4. Substitute back into either function for the other value.

- **Linear function from two points:** slope d = (Q2 − Q1)/(P2 − P1); then Q = c + dP passes through one point.
- **Multi-variable demand:** Q = a − bP + cY + dT + ePr (Y = income, T = taste, Pr = price of a related good). Positive coefficient on Pr: **substitutes**; negative: **complements**.

**Explain more**

*Example 1 (linear).* Qd = 60 − 0.8P, Qs = 10 + 0.2P.
60 − 0.8P = 10 + 0.2P → 50 = P. Q = 10 + 0.2(50) = **20**.

*Example 2 (inverse form, units in thousands).* A made-in-Nigeria car: demand P = −14q + 740, supply P = 10q − 220 (P in ₦ thousand, q in thousands).
−14q + 740 = 10q − 220 → −24q = −960 → q = 40. P = 10(40) − 220 = 180.
So **40,000 cars at ₦180,000**. Trap: the option "q = 180,000, P = ₦40,000" swaps them.

*Example 3 (quadratic).* S(p) = p^2 + 3p − 70, D(p) = 410 − p.
p^2 + 3p − 70 = 410 − p → p^2 + 4p − 480 = 0 → (p + 24)(p − 20) = 0. Reject −24, so **p = ₦20**; units = 410 − 20 = **390**.
Inverse forms work the same way: 90 − 0.1q^2 = 50 + q + 0.2q^2 → 0.3q^2 + q − 40 = 0 → q = [−1 + √(1 + 48)]/0.6 = 10 (thousand chips), p = 90 − 0.1(100) = **₦80**.

*Example 4 (fraction).* S = 3p − 30, D = 600/p.
3p − 30 = 600/p → 3p^2 − 30p − 600 = 0 → p^2 − 10p − 200 = 0 → (p − 20)(p + 10) = 0 → **p = ₦20**, D = 600/20 = **30**.
For D = 10p/(p − 3), S = p^2: 10p = p^2(p − 3) → p(p^2 − 3p − 10) = 0 → p = 0, 5 or −2. Only p = 5 (> 3) works: **p = ₦5, quantity 25**. With a cubic such as (5p − 2)/(p − 3) = p^2 + 2, test whole-number prices: p = 4 gives 18/1 = 18 = 16 + 2, so **p = ₦4, quantity 18**.

*Example 5 (linear and nonlinear together).* Demand 2p^2 + q^2 = 11, supply p + 2q = 7. Substitute p = 7 − 2q: 2(7 − 2q)^2 + q^2 = 11 → 9q^2 − 56q + 87 = 0 → q = 3 or 29/9, giving p = 1 or 5/9. Both are positive, so **both** are equilibria. Likewise (q + 20)(p + 10) = 400 with q = 2p − 7 gives (2p + 13)(p + 10) = 400 → 2p^2 + 33p − 270 = 0 → p = 6 (reject −22.5), **q = 5**.

*Example 6 (two competing products).* qd1 = 100 − 2p1 + 3p2, qs1 = 2p1 − 4; qd2 = 150 + 4p1 − p2, qs2 = 3p2 − 6.
Market 1: 4p1 − 3p2 = 104. Market 2: 4p1 − 4p2 = −156. Subtract: p2 = 260; then 4p1 = 104 + 780 → p1 = 221. q1 = 2(221) − 4 = **438**, q2 = 3(260) − 6 = **774**.

*Example 7 (find the function or a constant).* If Qd = 200 − 5P, Qs = a + 3P and equilibrium is at P = 20: Qd = 100, so a + 60 = 100 → **a = 40**. From two points: 100 packs at ₦40, 180 at ₦60: d = 80/20 = 4; 100 = c + 4(40) → c = −60. **Qs = 4P − 60.** Demand works the same: 1,000 units at ₦4 and 1,500 at ₦2 give slope −250, so x = 2000 − 250p.

*Example 8 (multi-variable).* Q = 200 − 4P + 0.01Y + 2T, Y = 30,000, T = 10, Q = 260: 260 = 200 − 4P + 300 + 20 → 4P = 260 → **P = ₦65**. If Q = 800 − 10P + 0.04Y − 2Pr and income rises by ₦5,000, Q rises by 0.04 × 5,000 = **200 units**.

### Lesson 5.2: Excess demand, excess supply, taxes and subsidies

**Must know**

- **Excess demand = Qd − Qs** (a shortage). Occurs when price is **below** equilibrium; price is bid back up.
- **Excess supply = Qs − Qd** (a surplus). Occurs when price is **above** equilibrium; sellers bid the price down.
- **Per-unit tax t on producers:** producers receive (P − t), so replace P by (P − t) in the supply function. In inverse form P = S(Q), the new supply is P = S(Q) + t.
- **Per-unit subsidy s to producers:** producers receive (P + s): replace P by (P + s); inverse supply shifts down to P = S(Q) − s.
- **Tax revenue = t × new Q.** **Subsidy cost = s × new Q.** Price producers keep = P − t.
- Effect of a tax (normal curves): consumer price **rises by less than the tax**, quantity traded **falls**. A subsidy lowers price and raises quantity.

**Explain more**

*Example 1.* Qd = 60 − 0.8P, Qs = 10 + 0.2P (equilibrium P = 50).
At P = 40: Qd = 28, Qs = 18 → excess demand = 28 − 18 = **10**. At P = 70: Qd = 4, Qs = 24 → excess supply = 24 − 4 = **20**.

*Example 2 (inverse form).* P = −14q + 740, P = 10q − 220 (thousands). At ₦250,000: demand q = (740 − 250)/14 = 35; supply q = (250 + 220)/10 = 47. Excess demand = 35,000 − 47,000 = **−12,000** (negative, because ₦250,000 is above equilibrium: really a surplus). At ₦350,000: supply q = 57, demand q = 390/14 = 27.86. Excess supply = 57,000 − 27,857 ≈ **29,000** units (nearest thousand).

*Example 3 (tax).* Qd = 100 − 2P, Qs = 2P − 20; before tax: 100 − 2P = 2P − 20 → P = ₦30, Q = 40. A ₦4 tax: new Qs = 2(P − 4) − 20 = 2P − 28.
100 − 2P = 2P − 28 → 4P = 128 → **P = ₦32**. Consumers pay ₦2 more (less than ₦4); producers keep 32 − 4 = ₦28; Q = 100 − 64 = 36; tax revenue = 4 × 36 = **₦144**.

*Example 4 (subsidy).* Fertiliser: Qd = 150 − 3P, Qs = 3P + 30 (P in ₦ thousand); before, P = 20. Subsidy ₦6 thousand: Qs = 3(P + 6) + 30 = 3P + 48. 150 − 3P = 3P + 48 → **P = ₦17 thousand**.
Inverse form: P = 90 − 2Q, supply P = 30 + Q becomes P = 24 + Q. 90 − 2Q = 24 + Q → Q = 22; subsidy cost = 6 × 22 = **₦132**.

### Lesson 5.3: Cost functions

**Must know**

- **Cost function:** C(x) = F + V(x), total cost of producing x units.
- **Fixed cost (FC):** costs that do not change with output: rent, insurance, taxes. FC = TC(0), the constant term.
- **Variable cost (VC):** depends on output: materials, labour, packaging. VC = TC − FC.
- **Linear cost:** C(x) = vx + F (v = variable cost per unit).

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Average fixed cost | AFC = FC/Q |
| Average variable cost | AVC = VC/Q |
| Average total cost | ATC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC |
| Marginal cost | MC = d(TC)/dQ |
| Total cost from MC | TC = ∫ MC dQ + C, with C = FC |

- AVC is **not** TC/Q. AFC **falls continuously** as output rises (same fixed cost spread over more units).
- **Differentiation rules:** d/dx (constant) = 0; d/dx (ax) = a; d/dx (x^n) = n·x^(n−1); differentiate a sum term by term.
- **Cost of the nth unit (exact)** = C(n) − C(n − 1). MC at (n − 1) approximates it.

**Explain more**

*Example 1.* TC(Q) = 3600 − 177Q − 15Q^2 + 0.5Q^3.
FC = TC(0) = **3600**. VC = −177Q − 15Q^2 + 0.5Q^3. AVC = VC/Q = **−177 − 15Q + 0.5Q^2**. AFC = 3600/Q.
MC: differentiate each term: 0 − 177 − 15(2Q) + 0.5(3Q^2) = **−177 − 30Q + 1.5Q^2**. Integrating MC and using TC(0) = 3600 rebuilds TC.

*Example 2 (from totals).* TC = ₦1,000,000, VC = ₦200,000, output 40,000: FC = 800,000; AFC = 800,000/40,000 = **₦20**. TC ₦450,000 with FC ₦150,000 at 2,500 units: AVC = 300,000/2,500 = **₦120**. AFC ₦25 and AVC ₦75 at 400 units: TC = (25 + 75) × 400 = **₦40,000**.

*Example 3 (build the linear cost).* 500 items cost ₦40,000 including FC ₦2,000: v = 38,000/500 = **₦76**; 600 items cost 2,000 + 76(600) = **₦47,600**. If 300 units cost ₦51,000 and 500 cost ₦75,000: v = 24,000/200 = 120; FC = 51,000 − 120(300) = **₦15,000**. A Yaba bakery with FC ₦350,000, v = ₦180 and TC ₦1,070,000 made 720,000/180 = **4,000 loaves**.

*Example 4 (substitute).* C(x) = 10,000 + 30x + 0.02x^2 at x = 200: 10,000 + 6,000 + 800 = **₦16,800**; average = 16,800/200 = **₦84 per unit**. C(q) = q^3 − 30q^2 + 500q + 200: C(10) = 1000 − 3000 + 5000 + 200 = **₦3,200**; C(9) = 2,999, so the 10th unit costs 3,200 − 2,999 = **₦201** (MC(9) = 203 is only the estimate). From C(x) = 2,000 + 15x + 0.1x^2, the rise from 50 to 60 units is 3,260 − 3,000 = **₦260**.

*Example 5 (cost over time).* C(q) = q^2 + q + 900 and q(t) = 25t. After 3 hours q = 75, C = 5,625 + 75 + 900 = **₦6,600**. For C = 11,000: q^2 + q − 10,100 = 0 → q = 100 → t = 100/25 = **4 hours**.

### Lesson 5.4: Revenue, profit and market type

**Must know**

- **Revenue:** R = (price per unit) × (units sold) = x·p. Always write p in terms of x first (inverse demand), so R is a function of quantity.
- **Average revenue:** AR = R/x = p. **Marginal revenue:** MR = dR/dx.
- **Profit:** **P(x) = R(x) − C(x)** (never C − R).
- **Market type:** AR = MR → **perfectly competitive**; AR > MR at every Q → **monopolistic**.
- **Revenue maximised** where MR = 0. **Profit maximised** where dP/dx = 0 with second derivative negative; for P = ax^2 + bx + c (a < 0) the peak is at x = −b/(2a).
- Several products: R(x, y) = (price A)x + (price B)y. Aspirin at ₦1.25 and ₦1.50: R = 1.25x + 1.50y; for 100 and 150 bottles, 125 + 225 = **₦350**.

**Explain more**

*Example 1.* Demand Q = 40 − P. Inverse: P = 40 − Q. TR = (40 − Q)Q = 40Q − Q^2; AR = 40 − Q; MR = **40 − 2Q**. AR > MR, so the firm is **monopolistic**. MR = 0 gives Q = 20 for maximum revenue.

*Example 2 (make p the subject).* x = 100 − 4p → p = 25 − x/4 → R = **25x − x^2/4**. x = −5p + 100 → p = 20 − x/5 → R = 20x − x^2/5; at x = 15, R = 300 − 45 = **₦255**. p = 400 − 0.5x at x = 300: p = 250, R = **₦75,000**. If unit price is p = 35x + 15 kobo, R = x(35x + 15) = **35x^2 + 15x**.

*Example 3 (profit function).* C(x) = 500 + 140x, p = 200 − x. R = 200x − x^2. P = 200x − x^2 − 500 − 140x = **−x^2 + 60x − 500**.
Coffee makers: p = −0.27x + 51, C = 2.23x^2 + 3.5x + 85. R = −0.27x^2 + 51x; P = **−2.5x^2 + 47.5x − 85**. Profitable where P > 0: roots of x^2 − 19x + 34 = 0 are 2 and 17, so **2 < x < 17**.

*Example 4 (price-change model).* Radios cost ₦2, sell at ₦5, 4,000 bought monthly; each ₦1 rise loses 400 sales.
Units at price x: 4000 − 400(x − 5) = 6000 − 400x = **400(15 − x)**. Profit per unit = x − 2. P(x) = **400(15 − x)(x − 2)** = −400x^2 + 6,800x − 12,000.
Maximum: dP/dx = −800x + 6,800 = 0 → **x = ₦8.50**; P = 400(6.5)(6.5) = **₦16,900**.

*Example 5 (profit at a level).* p = 120 − 2x, C = 300 + 20x, x = 20: R = 80 × 20 = 1,600; C = 700; profit **₦900**. R = 500x − x^2, C = 20,000 + 100x at x = 150: 52,500 − 35,000 = **₦17,500**.

### Lesson 5.5: Price elasticity of demand

**Must know**

- **Elasticity of demand:** E = −(P/Q)(dQ/dP): the percentage change in quantity demanded caused by a 1% change in price. Percentage form: E = (% change in Q)/(% change in P), in absolute value.
- If demand is given as P = f(Q), use dQ/dP = 1 ÷ (dP/dQ).

| Coefficient (absolute) | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | perfectly inelastic |
| between 0 and 1 | relatively inelastic (inelastic) |
| 1 | unitary (unit) elastic |
| greater than 1 | relatively elastic (elastic) |
| infinity | perfectly elastic |

- **Inelastic:** a price rise **increases** total revenue. **Elastic:** a price rise **decreases** total revenue. Revenue peaks at unit elasticity.

**Explain more**

*Example 1.* Q = 200 − 4P at P = 20: Q = 120, dQ/dP = −4. E = (20/120)(4) = **0.67**: inelastic.

*Example 2 (inverse demand).* P = 60 − 2Q at Q = 10: P = 40, dP/dQ = −2 so dQ/dP = −1/2. E = (40/10)(1/2) = **2**: elastic.

*Example 3 (at equilibrium).* Qd = 90 − 3P, Qs = 2P − 10: P = 20, Q = 30. E = (20/30)(3) = **2.00**: elastic.

*Example 4.* Garri price rises 10%, quantity falls 4%: E = 4/10 = **0.4**: inelastic, so the seller's revenue rises.

### Lesson 5.6: Break-even analysis

**Must know**

- **Break-even point:** the output at which **total revenue equals total cost**, so profit is zero. Below it, loss; above it, profit. It is a planning tool for new products or ventures.
- At break-even: TR = TC; total contribution = FC; profit = 0. (TR = TVC is **not** a break-even condition.)
- With F = fixed cost, p = price per unit, v = variable cost per unit:

1. **Break-even quantity:** q = F/(p − v)
2. **Break-even sales (revenue):** R = p × q, or F ÷ contribution margin ratio
3. **Contribution per unit:** p − v; **total contribution** = (p − v) × units
4. **Contribution margin ratio (margin), M:** (p − v)/p; total cost y = (1 − M)x + F for sales x; break-even sales x = F/M
5. **Units for a target profit:** (F + target)/(p − v); **sales for a target profit:** (F + target)/M
6. **Price needed to break even at q units:** p = (F + vq)/q. Example: F ₦50,000, v ₦35, q = 2,000 → p = 120,000/2,000 = ₦60.
7. **Margin of safety:** actual (or budgeted) sales − break-even sales: how far sales can fall before a loss. As a percentage: ÷ budgeted sales.

- **Nonlinear break-even:** set R(x) = C(x) (or P(x) = 0) and solve the quadratic; profit lies **between** the two roots, loss outside them.

**Explain more**

*Example 1.* Cassettes: F = ₦60,000, v = ₦4, p = ₦7. q = 60,000/3 = **20,000 units**; sales = 7 × 20,000 = **₦140,000**. At 25,000 units: 7(25,000) − [4(25,000) + 60,000] = **₦15,000** profit. Keyboards (F ₦10,000, v ₦5, p ₦10): q = **2,000**; at 1,000 units, 5,000 − 10,000 = a **loss of ₦5,000**.

*Example 2 (target profit).* p = ₦110, v = ₦60, F = ₦7,500: break-even 7,500/50 = **150 units**; for ₦1,250 profit, 8,750/50 = **175 units**. If C(x) = 210x + 7000 and price rises from ₦350 to ₦385: 7,000/175 = **40 items** (was 50).

*Example 3 (variable cost as % of revenue).* p = ₦20, VC = 30% of revenue = ₦6 a unit, F = ₦18,000: P(x) = 20x − 6x − 18,000 = **14x − 18,000**. At p = ₦60 with VC 25%: contribution 45, break-even 18,000/45 = **400 units**.

*Example 4 (margin form).* Gross margin 33% of retail price, other variable costs ₦0.13 per naira of sales, F = ₦4,000. M = 0.33 − 0.13 = 0.20. Break-even sales = 4,000/0.20 = **₦20,000**. At ₦50,000 sales: cost = 0.80(50,000) + 4,000 = 44,000; profit **₦6,000**. Sales ₦40,000, F ₦12,000, M 25%: total cost = 0.75(40,000) + 12,000 = ₦42,000, a **loss of ₦2,000**.

*Example 5 (contribution and safety).* p = ₦800, v = ₦520: ratio 280/800 = **35%**. F = ₦72,000, p = ₦40, v = ₦22, sales 5,000 units: break-even 72,000/18 = 4,000; margin of safety **1,000 units**. Budgeted ₦500,000, break-even ₦350,000: 150,000/500,000 = **30%**. Ratio 40%, F ₦120,000, target ₦30,000: 150,000/0.40 = **₦375,000**.

*Example 6 (quadratic).* F = ₦18,000, v = ₦550, p(x) = 4000 − 150x. R = 4000x − 150x^2; C = 18,000 + 550x.
4000x − 150x^2 = 18,000 + 550x → 150x^2 − 3450x + 18,000 = 0 → x^2 − 23x + 120 = 0 → (x − 8)(x − 15) = 0 → **x = 8 and x = 15**.
TV sets: 6000x = 50x^2 + 3000x + 43,750 → x^2 − 60x + 875 = 0 → **x = 25 and 35**. With R = 5000x − 100x^2 and C = 35,000 + 500x the roots are 10 and 35, so a loss occurs for **0 ≤ x < 10 or x > 35**.

### One-minute recap

- Equilibrium: Qd = Qs; reject negative roots; substitute back.
- Excess demand = Qd − Qs (price below equilibrium); excess supply = Qs − Qd (price above).
- Tax t: replace P by (P − t) in supply; revenue = t × new Q; consumers bear less than the full tax.
- FC = TC(0); VC = TC − FC; AFC = FC/Q; AVC = VC/Q; ATC = AFC + AVC; MC = dTC/dQ.
- R = xp with p in terms of x; P = R − C; AR > MR means monopoly.
- E = −(P/Q)(dQ/dP); between 0 and 1 inelastic, above 1 elastic.
- Break-even q = F/(p − v); sales = F/M; margin of safety = actual − break-even.
- Quadratic break-even: profit between the two roots.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A Lagos print shop makes branded T-shirts with fixed costs of ₦240,000, variable cost ₦900 per shirt and price ₦1,500. Find the break-even quantity, the break-even revenue and the profit on 500 shirts.

*Solution:* Contribution per shirt = 1,500 − 900 = ₦600. Break-even q = 240,000/600 = **400 shirts**. Break-even revenue = 1,500 × 400 = **₦600,000**. Profit on 500 = 600 × 500 − 240,000 = **₦60,000**.
