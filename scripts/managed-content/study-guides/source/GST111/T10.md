## Topic 10: Grammar I: Word Classes and Phrases

**What you must know:** grammar and its two branches (morphology, syntax); the hierarchy morpheme to sentence; free and bound morphemes; 8 traditional parts of speech against 9 modern word classes and the three criteria; forms, sub-classes and functions of nouns; sub-classes and cases of pronouns; determiners; adjectives (sub-classes, position, degree); verbs (5 forms, tenses, transitive/intransitive, primary and modal auxiliaries); adverbs; prepositions, the four groups of conjunctions, interjections; the phrase and its 8 types.

### Lesson 10.1: Grammar, its units and word classes

**Must know**

- **Grammar:** the system of rules that determines how words are formed and combined to make sentences; in short, **the study of sentence structure**.
- **2 branches:** (1) **morphology**, how words are formed; (2) **syntax**, how constituents are arranged within a sentence.
- **2 steps in studying grammar:** (1) identify the **units or constituents** (words, phrases, clauses, sentences); (2) analyse the **patterns** they fall into and the meaning those patterns communicate.
- **Grammatical hierarchy, smallest to largest:** 1. morpheme, 2. word, 3. phrase, 4. clause, 5. sentence. The **morpheme** is the smallest unit, the **sentence** the largest. **Words are the building blocks of a sentence.**
- **Morpheme:** the smallest meaningful unit. **Free morphemes** stand alone as words (boy, go, in); **bound morphemes** cannot; they are affixes (un-, -s, -ed, -ly). Free morphemes sub-divide into **lexical** (content words: nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) and **functional** (grammar words: prepositions, conjunctions, determiners, pronouns).
- **Open class words:** classes to which new words can always be added (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs). **Closed class (grammatical) words** rarely gain members (prepositions, conjunctions, determiners, pronouns).
- **Parts of speech:** the **traditional** label, based on **notional (meaning)** criteria; **8** classes: nouns, pronouns, adjectives, verbs, prepositions, conjunctions, adverbs, interjections.
- **Word classes:** the **contemporary** label; **9** classes: nouns, pronouns, adjectives, **determiners**, verbs, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, interjections.
- **3 criteria for a word's class:** (1) **notional (semantic):** meaning; (2) **morphological:** the affixes it takes; (3) **syntactic:** its position in the sentence and whether a regular word of the same class can replace it.

**Explain more**

- Notional definitions: nouns signify **entities**; verbs **actions**; adjectives **states**; adverbs **manner**; prepositions **location**.
- Why these are vague: *extermination* signifies an action but is a **noun**; *quick* in *quick fix* signifies manner but is an **adjective**; *Ibadan* denotes a location but is a **noun**.
- Structural clues: a **determiner** signals a noun; a word that takes the **3rd person -s**, **past tense** and **-ing** endings is likely a **verb**.
- Trap: a paragraph is a unit of writing, not of grammar; lexis and syntax are areas of study, not units.
- Trap: "the study of the arrangement of constituents in a sentence" is **syntax**. Grammar is too broad (it includes morphology); graphology is the writing system.

### Lesson 10.2: Nouns

**Must know**

- **Noun:** names a **person** (David), **animal** (lion), **place** (Nigeria), **thing** (book), **idea** (love), **incident** (flood) or **event** (Christmas).
- **Structural definition:** takes the **plural** (tables), has a **possessive** (Bola's bag), can follow a **determiner** (a/this/my ball), occurs before and after a verb (Dayo is a boy). A noun **has no tense**.

**4 grammatical forms:**

1. **Singular:** one; the dictionary headword (sister, apple).
2. **Plural:** more than one; regular nouns add **-s/-es** (sisters, apples).
3. **Singular possessive:** apostrophe + s (wife's, clown's).
4. **Plural possessive:** apostrophe after the plural -s (girls', clowns'); irregular plurals keep the -s (men's, children's).

**Pronunciation of plural -s/-es:**

| Sound | After | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| /s/ | voiceless consonants | gates, taps, desks |
| /z/ | voiced consonants and vowels | roads, friends, bananas |
| /ɪz/ | sibilants /s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/ | nieces, wishes, judges, churches |

**6 kinds of irregular noun:**

1. **Vowel change:** foot/feet, mouse/mice, man/men, tooth/teeth, woman/women, louse/lice, goose/geese.
2. **-en/-ren:** brother/brethren, child/children, ox/oxen.
3. **-f/-fe to -ves:** knife/knives, half/halves, leaf/leaves, wife/wives, loaf/loaves, wolf/wolves, hoof/hooves, scarf/scarves (dwarf: dwarfs/dwarves).
4. **No change:** sheep, aircraft, furniture, deer, salmon, grouse.
5. **Plural only:** cattle, spectacles, shorts, glasses, clothes, pyjamas, tongs, scissors, trousers, binoculars.
6. **With or without -s:** fish/fishes, elk/elks, herring/herrings, antelope/antelopes.

**3 sub-class distinctions:**

1. **Proper** (particular person, place, thing or idea; **usually capitalised**: Chief Daniel, National Museum, Peugeot, Darwinism) versus **common** (general name: man, house, car, temptation). **Collective nouns** are common nouns naming a group: crew, staff, army, jury, chorus, panel, council, choir.
2. **Count** (singular and plural: dog/dogs) versus **non-count** (no plural: furniture, warmth, grass, humour). A division of **common** nouns.
3. **Concrete** (occupies space or is perceived by the senses: drug, sweet, music) versus **abstract** (idea, quality, characteristic: Christianity, Islam, Marxism, youth). Also a division of **common** nouns.

**8 functions of a noun:**

1. **Subject:** *Mr Dokunboh* donated some books.
2. **Direct object** (answers *what?*): Mr Dokunboh thanked *the lady*.
3. **Indirect object** (answers *to/for whom?*): he donated some books to *the school*.
4. **Subject complement:** Daniel is *an architect*.
5. **Object complement:** They made the boy *the class captain*.
6. **Prepositional complement:** I left him at *the bus-stop*.
7. **Qualifier of another noun:** a concert on *Independence* Day.
8. **Appositive:** The senator, *Mr Malaki*, is very smart.

**Explain more**

- Possessive in speech follows the /ɪz/, /z/, /s/ rules. A base ending in s keeps the written possessive -s if pronounced (Chris's) and drops it if not (Jesus' name, for conscience' sake).
- A collective noun is **singular** for the group as a whole (The choir *meets*) and **plural** for its members (The choir *have been* split).
- Count/non-count has **nothing to do with your ability to count**; some nouns are both (*two cup cakes* / *some cake*).
- Trap: common nouns are classified **count/non-count** and **concrete/abstract** (every noun also has singular/plural number). **Possessive/non-possessive** is not a classification; possessive is a form a noun takes.
- Trap: among *John, Kaduna, Ocean, mathematics*, the odd one is **mathematics**, a common noun naming a general field, not capitalised; the others carry the capital letter that marks a proper noun.
- **Gerund (verbal noun):** the -ing form used as a noun: *Driving* is my hobby.

### Lesson 10.3: Pronouns and determiners

**Must know**

- **Pronoun:** replaces a noun or noun phrase, usually to avoid repetition. *Mr James is my uncle. He lives in Ibadan.* The noun referred to is the **antecedent** (Mr James).
- A pronoun agrees with its antecedent in **4** things: **number, gender, person, case**.

**8 sub-classes of pronouns:**

1. **Personal:** 1st person I, me, we, us; 2nd person you; 3rd person he, she, him, her, it, they, them. Third person singular shows **gender**: he/him masculine, she/her feminine, it neuter.
2. **Possessive:** ownership; some stand alone (mine, yours, hers, his, theirs, ours), others go before nouns.
3. **Interrogative:** ask questions: who, whom, whose, what, which.
4. **Relative:** introduce a **relative (adjectival) clause**: *the dress which I wore; the students who were late; the writer whom I would like to meet.*
5. **Demonstrative:** this/that (singular), these/those (plural).
6. **Reciprocal:** two-way relationship; English has **two**: *each other, one another*.
7. **Reflexive and intensive:** -self/-selves forms (myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself; ourselves, yourselves, themselves). **Reflexive** reflects back to an earlier noun or pronoun (*He taught himself to drive*); **intensive** adds emphasis (*David himself cleaned the room*).
8. **Indefinite:** no specific person or thing. Singular: anyone, anybody, either, neither, someone, something, little, much; plural: both, several, few, many, any, some (*Several were taken. Few were expected*).

**3 cases:**

| Case | Use | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| Subjective | subject | I, we, he, she, they, you, who, it |
| Objective | object | me, us, him, her, them, you, whom, it |
| Possessive | ownership | my/mine, our/ours, his, her/hers, their/theirs, your/yours, whose, its |

- **Determiner:** a word used **before a noun** to give information about it. Like adjectives, determiners qualify nouns, but they **lack descriptive content** and name no qualities, so modern grammar separates them.

**5 classes of determiners:**

1. **Articles:** *the* (definite); *a, an* (indefinite).
2. **Possessives:** his, her, your, our, my, its, their.
3. **Demonstratives:** this, that, these, those.
4. **Quantifiers:** some, many, a few, few, little, a little, several, much.
5. **Numerals:** one, two, three (cardinal); second, third, fortieth (ordinal).

**Explain more**

- Trap: there is no "refractive" pronoun; it is a distortion of **reflexive**.
- *Who* is subjective, *whom* objective, *whose* possessive.
- *These, those, many, few, several* go only before **plural count** nouns; *much, little* with **non-count** nouns; *the, some* with both.
- **Two determiners cannot precede** one head noun (*that your book*, *some few things* are wrong). A **singular count noun always needs a determiner** (*I gave my brother a pencil*, not *pencil*).
- Trap: the phrase with an **indefinite article** is *a big fat hen*; *the Golden Globe awards* has the definite article, *that house* a demonstrative, *my friend* a possessive.

### Lesson 10.4: Adjectives and adverbs

**Must know**

- **Adjective:** describes a noun or pronoun by **limiting its meaning**; answers **what kind?** (a *beautiful* day), **which one?** (a *prestigious* award), **how many?** (*several* people).

**9 sub-classes of adjectives:**

1. **Qualitative:** a quality (honest, rich).
2. **Classifying:** the group it belongs to (democratic, psychological).
3. **Quantifying:** amount, usually uncountable (some food, all their money).
4. **Colour:** brown, red.
5. **Number:** exact number or order, countable only (ten people, first birthday).
6. **Emphatic:** emphasises feeling or degree (complete idiot, total nightmare).
7. **Interrogative:** which way? whose house? what kind?
8. **Demonstrative:** that ball, this child.
9. **Possessive:** my children, your mother.

- **Position:** before the noun = **attributive** (*the beautiful woman*); linked to the noun by a verb = **predicative** (*the food is delicious*). Attributive only: a *main* road, a *commanding* lead. Predicative only: the woman is *afraid*; the girl feels *faint*.
- **3 degrees:** **positive** (big), **comparative** (two things: bigger), **superlative** (three or more: biggest).
- **Gradable** adjectives (big, tall, lazy) can be expressed in degrees and modified by adverbs (fairly big, very big). **Non-gradable** adjectives name absolute or extreme qualities and have no comparative: perfect, real, right, terrible, fatal, superior, ghastly, unique, utter, inferior, fake, genuine.
- **Adverb:** modifies a **verb, an adjective or another adverb** by making its meaning more specific: *Very beautiful women have always enticed foolish men quite successfully.*
- **Forms:** many add **-ly** to adjectives (smartly, quickly); others are independent words (very, quite, here, yesterday, often, so, nearby).

**Types of adverb:**

| Type | Answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time | when? | He arrived *yesterday*. |
| Manner | how? | She walks *briskly*. |
| Place | where? | Tade travelled *to Ibadan*. |
| Degree | to what extent? | My mother cooks *very well*. |
| Frequency | how often? | The man jogs *regularly*. |
| How many times | how many times? | Muslims pray *five times*. |

**Explain more**

- Comparative adds **-er** (-r after -e); superlative **-est** (-st after -e). Longer adjectives take **more/most** (more difficult, most difficult); some allow either (politer/more polite, likelier/more likely).
- **Irregular adjectives:** good/better/best; bad/worse/worst; much/more/most; many/more/most; far/farther/farthest.
- Trap: *tall, taller, tallest* illustrates a **gradable** adjective (expressed in degrees); "comparative" names only the middle form.
- Trap: attributive/predicative = **position**; positive/comparative/superlative = **form (degree)**.
- **Order of adjectives:** quality, length, weight, size, shape, age, colour, origin, then the noun: *a beautiful big white house; an elegant short pink dress*.
- **Adverb degrees:** cheerfully/more cheerfully/most cheerfully; badly/worse/worst; well/better/best; soon/sooner/soonest; quickly/more quickly/most quickly. Adverbs also show order (first, last).
- Trap: *very well* is an adverb of **degree** (*very* tells the extent of *well*); *to Ibadan* works as an adverb of **place**.
- **Position:** an adverb modifying a **verb** can move (*Quickly he ate / He quickly ate / He ate quickly*); one modifying an **adjective or adverb** must come **immediately before** it (*very kind, so quickly*). Position changes meaning, since modifiers relate to the **closest modifiable word**: *Smartly, he walked past* (the walking was smart) versus *He smartly walked past* (he was smart).

### Lesson 10.5: Verbs

**Must know**

- **Verb:** expresses an **action** or a **state of being** and is **required to make a sentence**. Action: dance, clap, jump. State of being: **be** (be, am, is, are, was, were, been, being), become, seem, appear, look, remain, feel, sound, smell.
- **Forms:** *be* has **8**. Other verbs (action and state-of-being) usually have **5**:

1. **Base (infinitive):** dictionary form (look, take).
2. **Third person singular present:** -s/-es (looks, takes).
3. **Past tense** (looked, took).
4. **Perfect (past participle)** (looked, taken).
5. **Progressive (-ing)** (looking, taking).

- **Regular** verbs add **-ed** for past and perfect (look, kick, wait); **irregular** verbs form them otherwise (take, make, go).
- The -s/-es ending is pronounced like the noun plural: /ɪz/ passes, catches, pushes; /z/ robs, goes; /s/ cuts, looks, drinks.
- **Tense** relates the action to a **time**. There are **about nine**; five are treated here:

1. **Simple present:** something done **regularly** or going on all the time (*I jog every morning; The cock crows at dawn*). It signals **regularity**.
2. **Present continuous:** happening **at the time of speaking or writing**; **be + -ing** (*I am dancing*).
3. **Present perfect:** past action **linked to the present**; **has/have + past participle** (*I have lived in Lagos for ten years*: started then, still there).
4. **Simple past:** happened and **completed** in the past (*I went to school yesterday*).
5. **Simple future:** not yet happened; **will/shall + base** (*I shall leave for Kano tomorrow*).

- **Transitive verb:** action passes from subject to an **object**; it always occurs with an object (*The man shut the door*). **Intransitive verb:** subject but **no object** (*The baby slept; The baby cried*).
- **Auxiliary (helping) verbs** come in **2 types**: **primary** (**have, be, do**), which can also be main verbs; **modal** (can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must, ought to), which **cannot stand alone as main verbs**.

**Explain more**

- Primary auxiliaries in both roles: *The dress is long* (main) / *The man is playing* (auxiliary); *I have a car* / *I have bought a car*; *I do a lot of knitting* / *I don't drink*.
- Modals express possibility, probability, **ability** (*can*), **permission** (*may*), prediction, **obligation** (*must*), offers, promises.
- Trap: *will, shall, could, should, can, may* are **modal auxiliaries**. Auxiliaries are classified **primary and modal**, never "primary and secondary" or "modal and main"; regular/irregular classifies verbs by past-tense formation.
- Trap: simple present = **regularity**, not "now"; "now" is the present continuous.
- Trap: *The woman ate the food* has an object (transitive); *Your house is too far* has linking *is*; *The baby cried* follows the standard intransitive pattern.

### Lesson 10.6: Prepositions, conjunctions, interjections and phrases

**Must know**

- **Preposition:** shows the **relationship** (time, place) between a noun or pronoun and another word: about, above, at, behind, by, for, from, into, of, to, under, with. **Compound (phrasal) prepositions:** because of, along with, next to, on account of, according to, owing to, instead of, in addition to, in spite of.
- Prepositions **begin phrases that end with either a noun or a pronoun**, the **object of the preposition (prepositional complement)**: *behind the campus; under the table*.
- **Conjunction:** links words or groups of words. **4 groups:**

1. **Coordinating:** join items of **equal grammatical status**: **and, yet, but, or, nor, for, so** (*The man and his wife; firm yet fair*). They build **compound sentences**.
2. **Correlative:** **pairs** joining items of equal status: **both...and; either...or; just as...so; neither...nor; not only...but (also); whether...or**.
3. **Subordinating:** join two clauses, making one **dependent**: because, when, if, as, although, though, since, before, after, as soon as, whenever, until, so that. They introduce **adverb clauses** and build **complex sentences**.
4. **Conjunctive adverbs:** clarify the relationship between clauses of **equal status**, more strongly than coordinators: also, however, moreover, nevertheless, besides, furthermore, still, consequently, therefore, thus, likewise, similarly (*He got to the bank after four; nevertheless, the cashier attended to him*).

- **Interjection:** expresses **strong feeling, emotion or exclamation**; **no grammatical connection** to the other words; hardly used in formal writing: ouch, ha, oh, hey, eek, wow, oh no, watch it.
- **Phrase:** a group of words working in a sentence as a **single grammatical unit**; may be **just one word**. Each type is **named after the word class of its key word (head)**.

**8 types of phrase:**

1. **Noun phrase (NP):** head is a noun; does every job a noun does (*A heavy rain* fell: subject; Dami is *a very charming young man*: subject complement).
2. **Verb phrase (VP):** main (lexical) verb + auxiliaries (*will go, is going, may have been going*); shows time, mood, voice, number, person; acts as **predicator**.
3. **Prepositional phrase (PP):** preposition + its object (*in the fridge, under the bed, across the road*).
4. **Adjective phrase (AdjP):** head is an adjective; modifies a noun (*very happy* man, *extremely valuable* documents, *happily married* couple).
5. **Adverb phrase (AdvP):** head is an adverb (*fairly regularly, very recently, extremely warmly*).
6. **Appositive phrase:** a noun phrase next to another noun, identifying it (*The senator, Mr Malaki*).
7. **Gerund phrase:** gerund + its modifiers or complement (*I love chatting with my friends*).
8. **Participial phrase:** present or past participle + complement (*Sobbing uncontrollably, she tore up the pictures; Encouraged by her friends, she drove home*).

**Explain more**

- Trap: *nevertheless* is a **conjunctive adverb**, so it cannot introduce an adverb clause; *although, when, if* can.
- Trap: *so* is coordinating but **so that** is **subordinating** (complex, not compound, sentence). *Even...then* is **not** a correlative pair.
- A PP can act as an **adjective** (*The red book on the table is mine*) or an **adverb** (*The bus arrived during the day*), which is why *to Ibadan* is an adverb of place.
- Name a phrase by its **head**: *across the road* is a **PP** (head *across*) though *the road* inside it is an NP; in *because of the heavy rain*, *the heavy rain* alone is a **noun phrase** (head *rain*); *extremely valuable* is an **adjective phrase** (head *valuable*), not an adverb phrase.

### One-minute recap

- Grammar = study of sentence structure; morphology (word formation) + syntax (arrangement).
- Hierarchy: morpheme < word < phrase < clause < sentence. Free morphemes = lexical + functional; bound = affixes.
- Traditional: 8 parts of speech by meaning. Modern: 9 word classes (adds determiners) by notional, morphological and syntactic criteria.
- Nouns: 4 forms; plural /s/ /z/ /ɪz/; proper/common, count/non-count, concrete/abstract; 8 functions; no tense.
- Pronouns: 8 sub-classes, 3 cases; agree in number, gender, person, case. Determiners: articles, possessives, demonstratives, quantifiers, numerals.
- Adjectives: 9 sub-classes; attributive vs predicative; positive, comparative (two), superlative (three+); gradable vs non-gradable.
- Verbs: 5 forms (be has 8); transitive takes an object; auxiliaries = primary (have, be, do) + modal.
- Simple present = regularity; present continuous = now; present perfect = past linked to present.
- Adverbs: time, manner, place, degree, frequency; conjunctions: coordinating, correlative, subordinating, conjunctive adverbs.
- Phrase = single unit named after its head; 8 types.
