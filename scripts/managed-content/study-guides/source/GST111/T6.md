## Topic 6: Reading Skills III: Study Reading

**What you must know:** what reading is and why it is communication; what to read and when to read, including the conditions a serious student sets up; the four methods of study reading (SQ3R, PQRST, 3S3R, OK4R) plus flash cards, summarisation and mnemonics; the three types of memory and the three stages of storing information.

### Lesson 6.1: What reading is

**Must know**

- **Reading:** an activity which involves looking at words and understanding their meanings, either for pleasure or for serious purposes like an examination. Put another way, reading is the process of translating written or printed symbols and letters into words and sentences which convey meaning.
- Reading uses two things: the **eyes** and the **mental faculty** (the mind). It is understanding and interpreting written or printed words and signs.
- Reading is a **process of communication**. Most formal activities of learning take place through it.
- Reading is an important part of the **learning process**: it lets you garner (gather) the information you need for growth.
- Through reading you **enlarge your vocabulary** and so improve your listening and writing.
- **Saccadic movement:** when you read, your eyes move by little jumps forward, then stop, then jump forward again, alternately along the line. The eyes can also jump **backward** to words already read.

**Explain more**

- **Purposes of reading:** we read for pleasure, for examinations, for knowledge, for information. Reading is not for one purpose only. Trap: any option that says we read "only" for pleasure, "only" for exams or "only" for knowledge is false. If all the options carry "only", the correct answer is "none of the above".
- Reading is a **receptive** skill (you take in meaning), like listening. Speaking and writing are **productive** skills. Reading interprets written or printed symbols; listening interprets spoken words.
- Reading is basic to learning in a literate society.

### Lesson 6.2: The nature of reading: what and when to read

**Must know**

**What to read**

- Reading materials include **newspapers, magazines, phone directories and textbooks** on any subject.
- Different materials are read in **different ways**. Reading can involve **scanning**, **skimming** or normal **study reading**.
- Skimming is a mere **survey**. A textbook is read with **concentration and effort**.
- A newspaper is usually read through **once** and discarded. A textbook is read **over and over again**; ideas are questioned and recalled, possibly for examinations.
- Rule: **the nature of the reading material dictates how it is read** and the rigour attached to its reading. What you read determines how you read.

**When to read**

- You can read at **any time of the day**.
- The **place** you choose for reading usually determines the nature of what is read. A newspaper or magazine can be read **anywhere and at any time**. A mathematics textbook requires a **private, noise-free and distraction-free** area for adequate comprehension.
- Marks of a **serious student**:

1. Sets aside a **particular time** of the day for study.
2. Studies at a **regular place** designated for it.
3. Draws up a **reading timetable**.
4. **Prioritises** the reading schedule and excludes himself or herself from parties.
5. **Eliminates distractions** as much as possible.

**Explain more**

- Trap: "reading study materials anywhere" is **not** a mark of a serious student. Newspapers can be read anywhere; study materials need a fixed time and place.
- **Conditions that affect reading and listening.** Distractions fall into three groups. Know which is which:

| Condition | What it covers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | the environment around you | **noise**, a noisy or ill-ventilated room, music from radio, television, laptop or phone |
| **Physiological** | the body of the speaker or reader | speech defects, tiredness, illness, hunger |
| **Psychological** | the mind of the listener or reader | worries about love affairs, money or health; emotional state; prejudice |

- **Noise is a physical condition**, not psychological or physiological. There is no "spiritual" category.
- **Texts you read for a course:**
    - **Prescribed text:** the recommended main book for a course. It usually contains **most of the topics to be treated, or the basis for discussion in the lectures**. Trap: it does not contain *all* the topics, and it is not the lecturer's sole source.
    - **Supplementary texts:** given so that students can **acquire additional information** for their courses. "Supplement" means to add to.
- **Selective reading** is important because it may be **impossible, or a waste of time, to read everything in a text**. Depending on your purpose and how familiar the material is, you read selectively. Believing you must read every word is a misconception.
- Good study practice: generate guide questions as you read, adopt a **flexible reading speed**, and use **memory-enhancing devices** (mnemonics). **Not** recommended: reading with **pre-conceived ideas**. Prejudice and emotional attachment to a subject block understanding; keep an open, questioning mind.

### Lesson 6.3: Study reading and the SQ3R technique

**Must know**

- **Study reading:** the type of reading we engage in to gather information that will translate to knowledge on a specific area of study. It targets **detailed, sometimes critical, exploration and evaluation** of the content. To succeed you must read vigorously and widely and become an avid reader who can recall what has been read.
- There are many methods of study reading. **Four** are treated here: **SQ3R, PQRST, 3S3R and OK4R**.
- **SQ3R** = **S**urvey, **Q**uestion, and 3R = **R**ead, **R**ecall/Recite, **R**eview.

1. **S, Survey:** examine the **preliminary matters** of the book: the **title, preface, foreword and table of contents**. Read the **blurb** (it summarises what the book is about). Check the **publication date** to see whether the material is current. Ask whether the title is relevant to what you are searching for. To survey an **article or chapter**, check the **title**, the **first and concluding paragraphs**, and the **topic sentences** of each paragraph.
2. **Q, Question:** ask questions about what is being read. They direct and guide your reading and lead you to careful, serious study. Examples:
    - How correct is the idea or information in the book?
    - Does the book actually contain what it said it would in the introduction?
    - Does the book contradict your opinions on the subject?
    - What is the writer's aim in writing the book?
    - Does the writer present his or her views objectively or otherwise?
3. **R1, Read:** a **careful reading** of the text, examining its main ideas, illustrations and examples, and getting answers to the questions you asked. You may read the text **two or three times**. Read with a **critical mind**, clarify issues, accommodate new vocabulary and take notes.
4. **R2, Recall/Recite:** make an effort to remember from memory what you have read: definitions, keywords, new words, illustrations. Do this by **paraphrasing** or by using the **exact words** of the text; write the expressions down or have a **friend listen** while you recall them verbally. Go back to the text if you need to refresh your memory. Purpose: learn new ideas and add them to your knowledge base.
5. **R3, Review:** to review is to **assess**; it is synonymous with **revision**. You look again at everything you have done on the SQ3R scale, read the text again and make corrections, making sure **nothing has been forgotten**. It is more or less a **repetition of all the steps**.

**Explain more**

- Trap on the 3Rs: **Read, Recall, Review**. "Recite" is another name for Recall, but "Repeat", "Roll", "Replay" and "Rehash" are not part of the method.
- Trap on survey: the **blurb** belongs to the survey of a whole **book** (only books carry one). When surveying an **article or chapter**, you consider the title, the introduction and conclusion, and the topic sentences, not a blurb.
- "Survey" here has nothing to do with land surveyors or measuring the size of a book, and it is not an in-depth investigation of the content.
- **Seven reading strategies** that help you understand what you read at the Read stage:

1. **Reading with purpose and flexibility:** the nature of the material and your purpose dictate the thoroughness and speed of reading. Flexibility is your ability to **vary your reading speed** to suit the material. Skim, scan or study-read with your purpose and the difficulty of the material in mind.
2. **Pre-reading:** read the preliminary details such as the introductory paragraph, and the conclusion, before the actual reading. It helps you organise what is to be read.
3. **Identifying verbal and sense links:** words are strung into phrases, clauses and sentences. Look for cues and linkages; **keywords and topic sentences** help understanding.
4. **Adopting a proper mental posture:** be mentally alert and stable. To aid this: (a) have a **regular place of study** so you adapt to the environment; (b) **avoid distractions**, both physical (music from the radio, television, laptop or phone) and other forms (worries about love affairs, money or health).
5. **Dividing up the study material:** break the material into manageable smaller units and take one step at a time to aid assimilation.
6. **Adhering to the writer's leads:** follow the text's division into **introduction, main body and conclusion** as the writer intends.
7. **Being a participant reader:** take part in the process of communication by asking yourself important questions and finding the answers in the text; be critical of what you read.

- Trap: your mental posture is affected by music from gadgets, noise and worries about money or affairs. It is **not** affected by the **arrangement of keywords and topic sentences**; that is a reading strategy that helps you, not a distraction.

### Lesson 6.4: PQRST, 3S3R, OK4R and other study aids

**Must know**

- **PQRST** = **P**review, **Q**uestion, **R**ead, **S**ummary, **T**est. It gives priority to the information to be examined, especially in an examination.

1. **Preview:** examine the topic and the various headings and ideas in the course outline.
2. **Question:** ask the usual questions about what you are reading to aid understanding.
3. **Read:** carefully read all materials related to the topic and select ideas that answer the questions.
4. **Summary:** put all you have studied in summary form, taking account of illustrations and diagrams.
5. **Test:** test yourself by answering the questions you formulated earlier, focusing only on the topic.

- **3S3R:** 3S = **Survey, Study-read, Speech-read**; 3R = **Recite, Reflect, Recollect**.
- **OK4R:** **O**verview, **K**ey points; 4R = **Read, Recall, Reflect, Review**.
- 3S3R and OK4R are strategies for studying better; they teach effective **note-taking** and **mnemonic devices**.
- Other methods of study reading:

1. **Flash cards:** cards of **A5 size** carrying short notes for revision. They are usually **visual clues**.
2. **Summarisation:** read and recall what you have read in a short form using **keywords**; the keywords bring the ideas back in the examination hall.
3. **Mnemonic devices:** acronyms or simple phrases that help you memorise ideas for an examination. Example: **ROYGBIV** for the colours of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.

- **Advantages of study reading** (3): it increases better study habits; it boosts your effort at studying and passing examinations; it helps you to study smartly and pass well.

**Explain more**

- Trap: only **PQRST** is a real technique. STPQR, QRSTP and RSTPQ are scrambled letters, not methods.
- Compare the acronyms: SQ3R and OK4R both contain Read, Recall and Review (OK4R adds Reflect); PQRST is the only one that ends with a **Test**; 3S3R is the only one with Speech-read and Recollect.

### Lesson 6.5: Reading, retaining and remembering

**Must know**

- **Memory** controls the retention of what has been studied. The ability to remember and recollect what you have read is important to a student.
- **Three types of memory**, classified by **how long** information is kept:

1. **Sensory memory (SM):** linked to the **senses**: the ears, eyes, hands, tongue and nose. Information here is very **ephemeral** (short-lived). It is the first area of memory.
2. **Short-term memory (STM):** the second compartment. Ideas stay for a **short time**. Information is **organised** here; vital and relevant points are selected and irrelevant ones filtered out.
3. **Long-term memory (LTM):** a **permanent** room for storing information. It is **large and limitless**, and new ideas are allowed in.

- **Best way to remember** what you read: recall it **without using the exact words** of the textbook, that is, **paraphrase** in your own words. Another way is to **recite verbatim**; this suits lines in a play, words in a foreign language, or the sciences.
- **Three stages of storing information:**

1. **Encoding:** the ability to read, organise and internalise information.
2. **Storage:** the ability to retain the information.
3. **Retrieval:** remembering or recalling stored information. Aided by **attention**.

- Recall is aided by **attention, concentration and focus**, and by **repetition, recitation and review**.

**Explain more**

- Trap: the **brain** is not a sense organ, so it is **not** associated with sensory memory. The ears, eyes, hands, tongue and nose are.
- Trap: the classification based on **length of storage** is **long-term and short-term** (with sensory memory as the very brief first stage). "Transient and permanent", "visible and invisible" and "variable and invariable" are not the terms used.
- Trap: **reproduction** (simply producing information, as in an exam) does **not** enhance recall. Repetition, recitation and review do.
- Practical test of good study reading: read a passage at least twice, then answer questions on it without looking back.

### One-minute recap

- Reading = looking at words and understanding their meanings; eyes plus mind; a process of communication; saccadic eye movement.
- We read for pleasure, exams and knowledge; never "only" one.
- The nature of the material dictates how it is read: newspaper once, textbook over and over.
- Serious student: fixed time, regular place, timetable, no parties, no distractions. Noise is a **physical** condition; worries are psychological.
- Prescribed text covers most topics; supplementary texts add information; selective reading saves time.
- Four methods: **SQ3R** (Survey, Question, Read, Recall, Review), **PQRST** (Preview, Question, Read, Summary, Test), **3S3R**, **OK4R**.
- Survey a book: title, preface, foreword, contents, blurb, date. Survey a chapter: title, first and last paragraphs, topic sentences.
- Other aids: A5 flash cards, summarisation with keywords, mnemonics (ROYGBIV).
- Memory: sensory (senses, ephemeral), short-term, long-term (permanent, limitless). Stages: encoding, storage, retrieval.
- Recall is aided by attention, repetition, recitation and review; paraphrasing beats memorising exact words.
