## Topic 12: The Library, Information Resources and Use

**What you must know:** the library, its types and five functions; data, information and information literacy; the five characteristics of good information; the six forms and two sources (primary, secondary) of information; the University of Lagos Library's three resource categories, five print collections, eleven types of reference collection, and where each is housed.

### Lesson 12.1: The library, its functions, data and information

**Must know**

- **Library:** any **organised collection** for use by an individual or group of people; a collection of books gathered for purposes of **reading, study or reference**. Today it means a body of **recorded information** brought together for specific purposes, **organised for use** and **made available to users**.
- **Types of library (the 5 named):** public, academic, special, school, private, and so on. A **court library** is not one of the named types.
- **5 basic functions** of every library, whatever its type, for resources in **print and electronic** format:

1. **Selection**
2. **Acquisition** (acquiring print and non-print materials for users)
3. **Organisation** (organising resources for effective use)
4. **Preservation** (preserving the resources acquired)
5. **Dissemination** (making information available to users)

- **Book selling** is not a library function.
- **Data:** plural of *datum*; **facts** used in describing or discussing an item or set of items; also facts, figures or instructions in a form that can be comprehended and communicated by a human being or processed by a computer. Another word for data is **fact**.
- **Information:** facts, news, opinions, messages, symbols, signals and **processed data** capable of **improving the knowledge state** of a user. It is the **building block** in the creation of **knowledge and wisdom**.
- **Information literacy:** the ability to **locate, retrieve, evaluate and apply** information as you convert it into knowledge; it also includes the ability to **recognise when information is needed**. It stresses the skill of finding needed information and understanding how the library and its resources are organised.

**Explain more**

- Data and information are **not** the same. Data is raw facts; information is processed data that improves what you know. "Another word for data" is **fact**, not information.
- Information literacy distractors: "information knowledge", "information discovery" (only finding, not evaluating and applying), "information explosion" (the rapid growth in the amount of information), "information support", "information asset". None is the term.
- **Library sections and terms** you must recognise:
    - **Circulation section (circulation desk):** deals with the **borrowing and returning** of books; also where you ask for help locating a book (with the library catalogue and any library staff). Distributing books among affiliated libraries is inter-library lending, a separate service.
    - **Acquisitions section:** acquires new materials. **Cataloguing section:** catalogues and classifies acquired materials. Both are back-office units.
    - **Reference librarian:** the person who provides **reference services**. Not the Chief Librarian or University Librarian (head of the library) and not a general "library officer".
    - **Weeding:** **removing outdated** (damaged or no-longer-useful) books from the collection. Not dusting shelves, clearing tables or screening users.
    - **Catalogue:** gives the **bibliographic details** (author, title and **call number**, also called the **call mark**) needed to retrieve a book. Two forms: the **card catalogue** (author/title and subject) and the **Online Public Access Catalogue (OPAC)**.

### Lesson 12.2: Characteristics of good information

**Must know**

Information is good if it has these **5 characteristics** (attributes, qualities):

1. **Availability/Accessibility:** information is of no use if it is not within reach of its intended users; it should be **easy to obtain and access**. Accessibility is the **ease with which information can be obtained**.
2. **Accuracy:** information must be accurate for the purpose for which it is used.
3. **Reliability:** the **truthfulness** with which information is presented; ensured by its **authoritativeness and dependability**.
4. **Timeliness:** information must be provided **on time** for the purpose it is required; late information becomes **irrelevant**. Timeliness is a **sub-set of accessibility**.
5. **Relevance:** information must be relevant to the purpose and **suitable for the user**.

**Explain more**

- **Not** characteristics: **news** (a form information takes), **citation**, **printing** (things done with information), **non-reliability** (the opposite of a required quality).
- Example of accessibility: the University of Lagos Library is the **major organ of information provision** in the university community and beyond; it gives access to many types and formats of information through the traditional **catalogue** and the **OPAC**.
- Trap: "Where information is not provided on time it becomes ..." The answer is **irrelevant**, never "useful" or "relevant".

### Lesson 12.3: Types and sources of information

**Must know**

**6 types (forms) of information:**

1. **Oral:** spoken words.
2. **Textual:** text and words.
3. **Numeric:** numbers.
4. **Bibliographic:** the use of multi-diversified means of dissemination.
5. **Graphical:** graphs and charts.
6. **Multimedia:** a **diversified medium** of communication such as **audio, visual, audio-visual** and so on.

- Information can therefore be in the form of numbers, texts, graphs and charts (**all** of them).

**2 sources of information: primary and secondary.**

| | Primary source | Secondary source |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Stands on its own; gives a **first-hand** account of an event; provides the information the user needs **directly** | **Analyses, critiques, reviews or explains** a primary source |
| Who writes it | The original observer or creator | Scholars who studied the primary source and drew their own conclusions; often people **not present** when the event occurred |
| Literature | Novel, short story, poem | Criticism, reviews |
| History | Laws, letters, oral histories, diaries, newspaper articles on events | Books explaining the events |
| Science | **Reports of original research** (journal articles, theses) | Textbooks, encyclopaedias, digests |

- **Neither type is by nature more or less reliable** than the other. Depending on your topic, primary documents may be essential; some disciplines require the information to be **current**.

**Explain more**

- "The two sources of information" are **primary and secondary**. "Print and electronic" is a division by **format**, not by kind of source; "books and online" and "library and internet" are informal. **Tertiary** is not one of the two categories.
- Fill-in: "A secondary source is one that **analyses and explains** a primary source." "Disqualify" and "manipulates" are not part of the definition.
- A **thesis** is a **primary** source (original research). **Textbooks, encyclopaedias and digests** compile or explain other people's work, so they are **secondary**.
- **Journals** convey **recent original research and observation**; textbooks summarise established knowledge.
- "Multimedia ... diversified medium of communication such as ..." The answer is **audio-visual**, not text, graphs or numbers, which are single types.

### Lesson 12.4: Resources of the University of Lagos Library: print collections

**Must know**

- **3 categories of resources** in the University of Lagos Library: **Print, Non-print, Audio-visual** (all three).
- **A. Print resources (books):** general and special collections, made up of **5 collections**:

1. **Africana collection**
2. **Reference collection**
3. **Reserved collection**
4. **Serials collection**
5. **Recent accessions**

- There is **no "American collection"**.

**Africana collection**

- Print materials **published on Africa** by **African and non-African authors**; primarily **research** collections.
- Housed at the **Research and Bibliographic Department**, otherwise known as the **Gandhi Library**, at the **basement** of the main library.
- To use it, consult the **OPAC** or the **Card Catalogue** (author/title and subject) on the **ground floor** for the **author, title and call number (call mark)**.
- Run on a **closed access system**: users may **not** retrieve materials from the shelves; the **desk officer** retrieves them from the details supplied. Closed access means **restricted access**.
- Access is **strictly for researchers and postgraduate students**. Undergraduates enter only with a **letter from the head of department, supervisor or course lecturer**.
- Africana materials are **not loanable** and may not be taken out of the department. They are consulted within the department; **photocopying is allowed**. (True.)
- Other holdings: **special collections of notable individuals**; **newspapers, old and current, dating back to 1930** (the *West African Pilot*, founded and owned by Nnamdi Azikiwe); magazines; **PhD theses** from the university; government papers; NGO papers; **cartographic materials** (maps); the **University of Lagos archives**. A separate catalogue in the Gandhi Library lists these.

**Reference collection**

- Materials consulted to provide **specific information** whenever the need arises; also called **reference sources**; in **print and electronic** formats.
- Contain **facts and rarely opinions** (True). **Not read from cover to cover** like books. Entries (tables, diagrams, statistics, maps) are arranged in **alphabetical order**.
- Reference books **cannot be borrowed**; they are consulted in the **reference section**, on the **left-hand side of the ground floor**.
- **Notable reference collections:** dictionaries, encyclopaedias, biographies, indexes, abstracts, fact sources, geographical sources, bibliographical sources, books of quotation, handbooks and manuals. **Books** in general and **journals** (serials) are **not** reference materials.

**Reserved collection**

- **Valuable**, often **rare** materials cutting across all disciplines.
- Run on a **closed access system**: you place a request with the **bibliographic details**; the material is retrieved for you.
- Housed at the **Reserved Section**, at the **immediate right on the ground floor**.
- Must be **consulted within the reading room**; **not placed on long loan**; so reserved books **cannot be borrowed**, but you can use them and **photocopy**.
- Books are placed on reserve to **facilitate a rapid and wider circulation of scarce materials among users**, not to protect them from wear, keep them from unregistered users or save them for serious students only. They are for students, not staff only.

**Serials collection**

- A **serial** is any publication issued in **successive parts**, appearing at **regular intervals**, and as a rule **intended to be continued indefinitely**.
- Examples: newspapers, magazines, newsletters, **journals**, indexes, abstracts, reports, proceedings and transactions of societies.
- Very important because of the **currency** of their content. Holdings are recorded in a **kardex** (a card information system holding bibliographic details), updated as new volumes arrive. Serials also have an **index to magazines** used for term papers.
- **Article/journal searches** are done in the **Serials section** and the **E-library**, not in the Cataloguing or Acquisition sections.

**Explain more**

- Special collections in the Gandhi Library belong to: **Mahatma Gandhi** (Indian nationalist and elder statesman), **D.O. Fagunwa** (the "Shakespeare of Africa"), **Pa S.O. Solanke** (WASU Secretary), **Revd Awojobi**, **Professor Babalola**, **Yesufu Eke** (one-time bursar), **Bishop Kale** and **Professor Adetokunbo Sofoluwe** (former Vice-Chancellor).
- "The Research and Bibliographic section houses ..." The defining answer is **PhD theses**; old and current newspapers are also kept there. Law books belong to the general collection.
- Location map: **basement** = Gandhi Library (Africana); **ground floor** = card catalogue, reference section (left), reserved section (immediate right); **third floor** = Automation Section and E-Library classrooms.
- Open access (the opposite of closed access) lets users retrieve materials from the shelves themselves. The reserved and Africana systems are called **closed** access (not "restricted access system" or "reserve access system"), and what closed access **refers to** is **restricted access**.
- Newspapers go back to **1930**, not 1860, 1920 or 1960.

### Lesson 12.5: The eleven types of reference collection

**Must know**

1. **Dictionaries:** give information about **words**: spelling, meaning, pronunciation, usage, synonyms, etymology and grammatical function. **3 types:** **general** (Oxford English Dictionary, Chambers English Dictionary), **specialised** and **subject** dictionaries (Roget's International Thesaurus, Dictionary of American Slang, Dictionary of the Yoruba Language, Dictionary of Modern Politics). Arranged **alphabetically**; housed at the **reference section**. Dictionaries are used as quick **reference** sources. There is no "anthem dictionary".
2. **Encyclopaedias:** give **background and current information on any topic**; a book or set of books on **one or all branches of knowledge**. **General** (Encyclopaedia Britannica, Encyclopaedia Americana) or **subject-based** (Encyclopedia of Library and Information Science, of Linguistics and Languages, of Philosophy). Alphabetical; at the **reference section**.
3. **Fact sources:** provide **facts on a wide range of current issues**: **directories** (Nigeria Yellow Pages, Directory of African Universities), **handbooks** (Guinness World Records, Handbook of Statistical Tables), **yearbooks** (Europa World Yearbook, Africa South of the Sahara) and **almanacs**. Housed at the **reference section**. Yearbooks are also called **annuals**.
4. **Biographical sources:** information on **individuals**: date of birth, place of birth, education, career achievements (**all of these**). **2 categories:** **universal and current** (International Who's Who, Webster's Biographical Dictionary, Who's Who) and **national and local** (Africa Who's Who, Newswatch Who's Who). Reference section, some in the Gandhi Library.
5. **Bibliographical sources:** **lists** of articles in periodicals, books, conference proceedings, technical reports, theses and dissertations on a **subject** or by a **given author**; give quick access to knowledge in the shortest time. **National bibliography** (National Bibliography of Nigeria, British National Bibliography); **trade** (The Bookseller, Publishers' Trade List Annual); **subject** (Economic Development in Nigeria, Bibliographic Guide to Technology). Reference section; old ones in Gandhi.
6. **Index:** a **directional aid** (indicator or pointer) to already published materials in **serial form** within a period; a systematic guide to the contents of a book or text; arranged alphabetically by author, title and subject; published as part of a book or separately. **4 types:** **book indexes**, **index to collections** (Anthology of Shakespeare Plays), **periodical index** (Agricultural Index, British Education Index), **newspaper index**.
7. **Abstracts:** a **summary** of a publication or article with enough bibliographical detail to trace the original; brings quick information to users who may not have access to the original. **3 types:** **indicative, informative, evaluative**. Examples: Abstracts of English Studies, Nigerian Universities Dissertation Abstracts, Science Abstracts, Chemical Abstracts. Old ones (CAB) in Gandhi; recent ones at the reference section.
8. **Geographical sources:** information on **places** (towns, cities, states, countries) and **physical features** (mountains, valleys, rivers): **maps** (Map of Nigeria), **atlases** (The Times Atlas of the World), **gazetteers** (Federal Republic of Nigeria Gazette) and **travel guides**. Old ones in Gandhi; recent at the reference section.
9. **Manuals:** **instructions on how to do things** ("how to do", "how to perform", "make"). Example: product manuals.
10. **Books of quotation:** quotations on sets of subjects by certain authors, found through their indexes; consulted mostly by **speech makers and writers**. Example: Home Book of Quotations.
11. **Handbooks:** a **small ready reference source** that provides facts and information on places, techniques, institutions, subjects; used much like a manual. Examples: **Faculty and Departmental Handbooks** (True: a faculty handbook is a small ready reference source).

**Explain more**

- Pairs the exam swaps:
    - **Fact sources** give facts on current issues; **biographical sources** give facts about **individuals**.
    - **Bibliographical sources** list publications on a subject or by an author; an **index** points you to where items appear in serials or a book.
    - **Bibliographical** (about publications) is not **biographical** (about people).
    - **Geographical sources** are the answer for "places and physical features" and for "Map of Nigeria and travel guide"; "reference source" is too general there.
- "The encyclopaedia is ..." Choose **a book or set of books on one or all branches of knowledge**, not "on Britain", "on America" or "compiled by Chambers"; those are only titles or publishers.
- "Reference materials include all EXCEPT": **journals** (serials). "Notable reference collection includes all EXCEPT": **books**.
- Handbooks appear twice: as a type of **fact source** and as a reference collection on their own. Both are correct. "Encyclopedia" and "encyclopaedia" are the same word.

### Lesson 12.6: Non-print and audio-visual resources

**Must know**

**B. Non-print resources** (soft copies, accessed electronically on computers, laptops and phones):

- **Electronic books, electronic journals, electronic images, electronic texts/records** (full text from the internet), **servers** (library software), **CD-ROMs**, floppy and flash disks, hard disks. The main electronic materials in most libraries are **e-books and e-periodicals**.
- Non-print resources allow **multiple usage** of a material **at the same time** (a print book serves one person at a time).
- Managed by the **Automation Section**, on the **third floor**. Enquiries on how to access any resource go to the **Readers' Services** point.
- Access area: the **E-Library**: **two classrooms on the third floor** (undergraduate and postgraduate) plus the **MTN Connect Library** at the **Akintunde Ojo building**. The library provides **wireless access**, so you may use your own devices in other approved areas. E-resources cover all programmes of the university and beyond.
- **Remote access** to the library's online resources is through the **UNILAG Library website** (not a matric number, telephone directory or hall of residence).

**C. Audio-visual resources:** aids that make learning as real as possible, giving first-hand knowledge through **hearing and seeing**. **3 groups:**

| Group | Materials | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Audio aids** | Radio, tape-recorder, gramophone, audio cassette player | **Music Library** |
| **Visual aids** | Maps, pictures, textbooks, slide projector, transparencies, print materials | Research and Bibliographic Department and Automation Section |
| **Audio-visual aids** | LCD projector, **television**, computers, VCD player, multimedia, artefacts | Various sections and the **Museum** |

**Explain more**

- **Print versus non-print:** print is hard copy on the shelves; non-print is soft copy (e-books, e-journals) accessed only on electronic devices and usable by many at once.
- Trap: radio, tape-recorder, gramophone and cassette player are **audio** materials (sound only), not audio-visual and not simply "electronic". **Television** is the standard example of an **audio-visual** material. Electronic journals, electronic images, library software and CD-ROMs are **non-print**, not audio-visual.
- The **MTN Connect Library** is an e-library area; fact sources are at the **reference section**.
- If a friend cannot find a book: use the **library catalogue** (card catalogue or OPAC) or ask the officer at the **circulation desk** or any library staff.

### One-minute recap

- Library: 5 types (public, academic, special, school, private); 5 functions (selection, acquisition, organisation, preservation, dissemination).
- Data = facts; information = processed data; information literacy = locate, retrieve, evaluate, apply, and recognise when information is needed.
- 5 characteristics: availability/accessibility, accuracy, reliability, timeliness, relevance. Late information is irrelevant.
- 6 forms: oral, textual, numeric, bibliographic, graphical, multimedia. 2 sources: primary (first-hand; thesis) and secondary (analyses/explains; textbook, encyclopaedia).
- UNILAG Library: print, non-print, audio-visual. Print collections: Africana, Reference, Reserved, Serials, Recent accessions.
- Africana = Gandhi Library, basement, closed access, researchers and postgraduates, not loanable, newspapers from 1930, PhD theses.
- Reference = facts not opinions, not read cover to cover, not borrowable. Reserved = closed access, no long loan, rapid wider circulation of scarce materials.
- Serials = successive parts, regular intervals, indefinitely; journals carry recent original research.
- 11 reference types: dictionaries (general, specialised, subject), encyclopaedias, fact sources (yearbooks = annuals), biographical, bibliographical, index, abstracts, geographical, manuals, books of quotation, handbooks.
- Non-print = Automation Section, E-Library, multiple usage, remote access via library website; audio (Music Library), audio-visual (television).
