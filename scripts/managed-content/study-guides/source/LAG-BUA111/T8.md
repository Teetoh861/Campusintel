## Topic 8: Marketing System

**What you must know:** the meaning of a marketing system and a market system; the components of the marketing system; the simplest marketing system and its two flows; the simple versus the complex (modern) system and the five markets of a modern exchange economy; the actors (suppliers, intermediaries and their types, competitors, customers) and the utilities intermediaries create; marketing channels, channel levels, the contact computation, channel flows and channel functions; vertical, horizontal and multichannel systems and channel conflict; the systems view.

### Lesson 8.1: Meaning, components and the systems view

**Must know**

- **Marketing system:** an integrative set of **institutions, flows and activities** affecting the ability of an organisation to develop and maintain successful transactions with its market.
- **Exam-style definition:** a set of **interacting and interdependent** individuals and institutions (producers, suppliers, intermediaries and customers) linked together by **flows of goods, money and information**. Put another way, it is the **network of firms, suppliers, intermediaries and customers** linked by these flows.
- **Market system:** the network of buyers, sellers and other actors that come together to trade in a given product or service.

**3 groups of participants in a market system:**

1. **Direct market players:** producers, buyers and consumers who drive economic activity in the market.
2. **Suppliers of supporting goods and services:** finance, equipment, business consulting.
3. **Entities that influence the business environment:** regulatory agencies, infrastructure providers, business associations.

**Components of the marketing system** (the "integrative set of institutions, flows and activities"):

1. **The marketing organisation:** an organisation that places customers and the satisfaction of their needs and wants at the centre of its corporate thinking.
2. **The controllable elements of the marketing mix:** product, price, distribution, advertising and others.
3. **The target market.**
4. **The facilitating institutions:** wholesalers, retailers, agents and others for the **distribution** function; advertising agencies and media for **communication**; marketing research for **marketing information**.
5. **The uncontrollable environmental variables:** economic conditions, demographics, social and cultural values, competition, legal forces, technology, trade barriers and sources of raw materials.

- Viewed as actors, the firm's marketing system is: the **company**, **suppliers**, **marketing intermediaries**, **competitors** and **customers** (the end-user market), all operating inside the wider **environment**, and linked by flows of **goods, money and information**.

**Explain more**

- **Picture the system as rings.** At the centre sits the marketing organisation with its mix (product, price, distribution, sales promotion and personal selling, advertising). Around it is a ring of functions: **marketing information**, **communication**, **distribution**, and social-cultural value. The outer ring is the environment: physical, legal environment and competition, the economy and the international environment.
- **The systems idea:** the parts are **interdependent**, so a change in one part affects the others. If a supplier of wheat flour raises its price, the noodle maker's costs rise, the wholesaler's margin is squeezed, and the shop price paid by the student goes up. The system is not fixed; it changes over time. No participant operates in isolation, and the firm cannot ignore suppliers or intermediaries.
- **Glaser's view:** the marketing system covers a narrow, limited range of organisational activities; it is only a **sub-system** of a larger organisation and is joined to other organisational sub-systems (production, finance, personnel) and to the environment.
- The effectiveness of a firm's marketing activities **increases substantially** through proper **co-ordination** of the different parts to form a system.
- **Marketing systems of developing countries (LDCs):** one view (Sorenson; Bhandari and Vora) is that they are **resilient, adaptive** to economic conditions and fairly **low-cost**. The opposing view (Dholakia) is that they have been generally **incapable of major innovations** in products, customer services, marketing methods, communications media or research techniques, and have not produced market-expansion strategies at home or abroad. So the typical LDC marketing system merely performs a **system maintenance function**: it can neither spur rapid economic development nor generate products that benefit the mass of the population.
- **Traps:** "the uncontrollable forces outside the firm" describes the **environment**, not the marketing system. "The controllable variables blended to satisfy the target market" is the **marketing mix**. "Dividing a market into distinct groups" is **segmentation**. A firm's internal accounting depreciation method is **not** a component of its marketing system.
- In the evolution of marketing, the **stage of marketing system** came after barter: people tired of travelling to different places to exchange goods, so they began to use a particular place for exchange.

### Lesson 8.2: Simple and complex (modern) marketing systems

**Must know**

- **The simplest marketing system** has **two interacting elements:** the **marketing organisation** and its **target market**. They are connected by **two sets of flows:**
  1. **Product and payment flow:** the company distributes a product or service to customers in exchange for some kind of payment.
  2. **Information flow:** the company communicates with its market (sales force, advertising); the market returns **informational feedback** to the firm.

| Flow | Direction |
|---|---|
| Goods/services | Seller to buyer (market) |
| Communication | Seller to buyer (market) |
| Money (payment) | Buyer to seller |
| Information (feedback) | Buyer to seller |

- So **from seller to buyer: goods/services and communication**. **From buyer to seller: money and information.** Information therefore flows in **both directions**.
- **Complex (modern) marketing system:** links **many markets** through flows of resources, goods, money, taxes and services, rather than just one seller and its buyers.

**5 markets in a modern exchange economy:**

1. **Resource markets:** raw materials, labour, money.
2. **Manufacturer markets:** producers buy resources and turn them into goods and services.
3. **Intermediary markets:** buy from manufacturers and sell to consumers.
4. **Consumer markets:** households sell their labour for money, then spend it on goods and services.
5. **Government markets:** buy goods from resource, manufacturer and intermediary markets and pay them; **collect taxes** from these markets and from consumers; and **provide public services** in return.

**Explain more**

- **Simple system example:** a pepper seller at a village market in Ogun State sells her own produce straight to shoppers. Goods go from her to them; they pay her cash and tell her which peppers they prefer (information feedback). One seller, her buyers, two sets of flows: a **simple marketing system**.
- **Complex system example:** a Dangote factory buys gypsum, labour and bank loans in resource markets; it sells cement to distributors (intermediary market), who sell to builders and households (consumer market). Government buys cement for roads, collects companies income tax and VAT, and provides public services such as roads and security. The workers' wages go back into consumer spending. Every market depends on the others.
- **Traps:** the government is **inside** the modern system (it buys, taxes and provides services); it is not outside it and it is not just a retailer. The complex system does **not** consist only of direct sales, and it still has information flows. "Marketing mix market" is **not** one of the five markets.
- Do not mix up "simple marketing system" with the channel arrangements in Lesson 8.5 (vertical, horizontal, multichannel). A single seller dealing directly with her buyers is a simple system, not a vertical or horizontal one.

### Lesson 8.3: The actors in the marketing system

**Must know**

- **Supply side** of the micro environment (and of the marketing system): **suppliers, marketing intermediaries and competitors**. **Demand side:** **customers**.
- **Suppliers:** firms and individuals that provide the raw materials and other resources a company needs to produce its goods and services. The price of raw materials shapes the final price, so suppliers can affect profit; firms monitor them for shortages and price changes.
- **Marketing intermediaries:** independent businesses that help a company **link with its customers** by **promoting, selling and distributing** its products; they perform the activities needed to move products from manufacturers to ultimate consumers (wholesalers, retailers, agents, distributors). They are part of the firm's distribution channels.

**4 types of marketing intermediaries:**

1. **Resellers:** buy the products and sell them to customers (wholesalers, retailers).
2. **Distribution centres:** help the firm **store** goods (a warehouse).
3. **Marketing agencies:** promote products by making customers aware of their benefits (an advertising agency).
4. **Financial intermediaries:** finance business transactions (banks, credit organisations, insurance organisations).

- **Competitors:** organisations offering similar products and trying to win market share with different strategies; competition pushes a firm to **differentiate** its product.
- **Customers:** buy the product; customer satisfaction is the firm's main goal. **5 customer markets:** consumer, industrial (business), reseller, government and non-profit, international.

**5 utilities intermediaries create:**

1. **Place:** stocking products where customers are located.
2. **Time:** opening long hours so customers buy when convenient.
3. **Quantity:** offering amounts customers can afford to buy.
4. **Assortment:** gathering many products in one place for one shopping trip.
5. **Possession:** transferring the product to the customer through a successful transaction.

**Explain more**

- **Applications:** a warehouse operator near the Lagos-Ibadan expressway storing a drinks maker's stock is a **distribution centre**. An advertising agency running a radio campaign for a new detergent is a **marketing agency**. GTBank lending to a distributor is a **financial intermediary**. A Shoprite branch buying and reselling is a **reseller**.
- Two noodle brands (say Indomie and a rival) chasing the same students with different packs and flavours are each other's **competitors**, not suppliers or resellers.
- **Intratype competition:** between firms in the same basic activity (Nigerian Bottling Company versus Seven Up Bottling Company). **Intertype competition:** between different types of organisations (finance houses competing with banks for savers).
- "Right place, right time, in a form they can own" points to **place, time and possession utility**. **Form utility** is created by production, not by intermediaries.
- Distribution channels change slowly but constantly, like the **hour hand of a watch**: each movement is too small to see, yet the change over years can be massive.

### Lesson 8.4: Marketing channels, levels, flows and functions

**Must know**

- **Marketing (distribution) channel:** a set of **interdependent organisations** that help make a product or service available for use or consumption by the consumer or business user.

**Channel levels** (count the **intermediaries** only):

| Level | Structure |
|---|---|
| Zero-level (direct) | Producer to consumer |
| One-level | Producer to retailer to consumer |
| Two-level | Producer to wholesaler to retailer to consumer |
| Three-level | Producer to wholesaler to jobber (sub-wholesaler) to retailer to consumer |

- **Contact formula** (each intermediary cuts the number of transactions):
  - Direct contacts = number of producers × number of buyers
  - Contacts through one intermediary = number of producers + number of buyers
  - Contacts saved = (producers × buyers) − (producers + buyers)

**Channel flows:**

1. **Physical flow** of goods: forward, producer to consumer.
2. **Title (ownership) flow:** forward.
3. **Promotion flow:** forward.
4. **Payment flow:** backward, consumer to producer.
5. **Information flow:** both directions.

**8 channel functions:**

1. **Information:** gathering and spreading research and intelligence about the market.
2. **Promotion:** developing and spreading persuasive communication about an offer.
3. **Contact:** finding and communicating with prospective buyers.
4. **Matching:** shaping and fitting the offer to the buyer's needs (manufacturing, grading, assembling, packaging).
5. **Negotiation:** agreeing on price and other terms so ownership can be transferred.
6. **Physical distribution:** transporting and storing goods.
7. **Financing:** acquiring and using funds to cover the costs of channel work.
8. **Risk taking:** bearing the risks of carrying out channel work.

**Explain more**

- **Levels:** a Kano farmer selling yoghurt at his farm gate is **zero-level (direct)**. A soap maker selling to distributors, who sell to neighbourhood shops, who sell to households, has **two** intermediaries, so it is a **two-level** channel. Count only the middlemen, never the producer or the consumer.
- **Worked example 1:** 4 textile producers, 5 clothing shops.
  - Direct: 4 × 5 = **20 contacts**.
  - Through one wholesaler: 4 + 5 = **9 contacts**.
- **Worked example 2:** 6 pharmaceutical producers, 8 pharmacies.
  - Direct: 6 × 8 = 48. Through one distributor: 6 + 8 = 14.
  - Fall = 48 − 14 = **34 contacts**. (Options of 48 or 14 are the traps: the question asks for the fall.)
- **Worked example 3 (cost):** 5 producers, 12 retailers, each contact costs ₦2,000.
  - Direct: 5 × 12 = 60 contacts; 60 × ₦2,000 = ₦120,000.
  - Through one wholesaler: 5 + 12 = 17 contacts; 17 × ₦2,000 = ₦34,000.
  - Saving = ₦120,000 − ₦34,000 = **₦86,000** (43 fewer contacts).
- **Function applications:** a Lagos wholesaler that buys 5,000 cartons of tomato paste in advance and accepts losses if prices fall or stock expires is performing **risk taking**. Grading and packaging rice to suit buyers is **matching**. A distributor giving a retailer 30 days' credit is **financing**. Setting the national interest rate is **not** a channel function; that is the Central Bank of Nigeria's job.
- Information, promotion, contact, matching and negotiation help **complete transactions**; physical distribution, financing and risk taking help **fulfil** them.

### Lesson 8.5: Organising the channel: conventional, vertical, horizontal and multichannel systems

**Must know**

- **Conventional distribution channel:** an independent producer, wholesaler(s) and retailer(s), each a separate business seeking to maximise its own profit, even at the expense of the whole system; no member has much control over the others.
- **Vertical marketing system (VMS):** producers, wholesalers and retailers acting as a **unified system**. One member **owns** the others, has **contracts** with them, or has so much **power** that they must cooperate.

**3 types of VMS:**

1. **Corporate VMS:** successive stages of production and distribution under **single ownership**.
2. **Contractual VMS:** independent firms at different levels joined by **contracts**; the **franchise** organisation is the most common form (also wholesaler-sponsored voluntary chains and retailer cooperatives).
3. **Administered VMS:** leadership comes from the **size and power** of one dominant member, not from ownership or contracts.

- **Horizontal marketing system:** two or more companies **at the same channel level** join together to follow a new marketing opportunity.
- **Multichannel (hybrid) distribution system:** one firm sets up **two or more channels** to reach one or more customer segments.
- **Channel conflict:** disagreement among channel members over goals, roles and rewards.
  - **Vertical conflict:** between **different levels** of the same channel (manufacturer versus its own retailers).
  - **Horizontal conflict:** between firms **at the same level** (one dealer versus another dealer of the same brand).

**Explain more**

- A food company that owns its own flour mills, delivery trucks and retail outlets is a **corporate VMS**. A manufacturer tied to its dealers by franchise agreements, without owning them, is a **contractual VMS**. A giant brand whose size lets it dictate shelf space and promotions to independent retailers is running an **administered VMS**.
- A bank placing kiosks or ATMs inside a supermarket chain's stores is a **horizontal marketing system**: the two firms sit at the same level and combine resources; neither owns the other.
- A phone-accessories brand selling through its own website, Computer Village traders and supermarkets at once is a **multichannel (hybrid)** system. Many Nigerian brands add Jumia as an online channel alongside shops.
- **Trap:** a fight between a manufacturer and its own retailers over prices and margins is **vertical** conflict (different levels), even though "horizontal" sounds right to some students. Two retailers undercutting each other is horizontal conflict.
- **Trap:** "ownership" points to corporate, "contract or franchise" to contractual, "power" to administered.

### One-minute recap

- Marketing system = interacting, interdependent producers, suppliers, intermediaries and customers linked by flows of goods, money and information.
- Components: marketing organisation, controllable mix, target market, facilitating institutions, uncontrollable environment.
- Simplest system = marketing organisation + target market; goods and communication go to the market, money and information come back.
- Modern exchange economy: resource, manufacturer, intermediary, consumer and government markets; government buys, taxes and provides services.
- Supply side = suppliers, intermediaries, competitors; demand side = customers. 4 intermediaries: resellers, distribution centres, marketing agencies, financial intermediaries.
- Intermediaries create place, time, quantity, assortment and possession utility.
- Channel levels count middlemen: zero, one, two, three. Contacts: direct = P × B; via one intermediary = P + B.
- Goods and title flow forward, payment backward, information both ways. 8 functions: information, promotion, contact, matching, negotiation, physical distribution, financing, risk taking.
- VMS: corporate (ownership), contractual (franchise), administered (power). Horizontal = same level. Multichannel = several channels. Vertical conflict = different levels.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Three paint manufacturers sell to ten hardware shops. Each sales contact costs ₦1,500. How much does the industry save if all trade passes through one wholesaler?

*Solution:*

- Direct contacts = 3 × 10 = 30; cost = 30 × ₦1,500 = ₦45,000.
- Contacts through one wholesaler = 3 + 10 = 13; cost = 13 × ₦1,500 = ₦19,500.
- Saving = ₦45,000 − ₦19,500 = **₦25,500** (17 fewer contacts).
