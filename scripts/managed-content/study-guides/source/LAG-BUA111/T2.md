## Topic 2: Marketing Management

**What you must know:** the definitions of marketing management and why it is called demand management; the eight demand states and the marketing task that matches each; the marketing management process (analysis, planning, implementation, control); the marketing plan, marketing control and the marketing audit; appraising the marketing effort; the importance of marketing management.

### Lesson 2.1: Meaning of marketing management

**Must know**

- **Marketing management (Kotler and Keller):** the art and science of choosing target markets and **getting, keeping and growing customers** through creating, delivering and communicating superior customer value.
- **Marketing management (Kotler and Armstrong):** the art and science of choosing target markets and **building profitable relationships** with them.
- **Process definition:** the process of planning and executing the conception, pricing, promotion and distribution of ideas, goods and services **to create exchanges that satisfy individual and organisational objectives**. Note the four elements inside it: conception (product), pricing (price), promotion (promotion), distribution (place).
- **Demand management:** marketing management is described as demand management because the marketing manager's task is to **influence the level, timing and composition of demand** so that the firm meets its objectives. It is not only about pushing demand up.
- **Two basic questions** every marketing manager must answer:
  1. "What customers will we serve?" The answer is the **target market**.
  2. "How can we serve these customers best?" The answer is the **value proposition**: the set of benefits or values the firm promises to deliver to satisfy customer needs.
- **Art and science:** it uses systematic research and analysis (science) as well as creativity and judgement (art).
- **Scope:** relevant to **both profit-seeking businesses and non-profit organisations**, to goods and services, small and large firms, local and export markets.

**Explain more**

- Read the definitions carefully in the exam. If a gap follows "choosing target markets and \_\_\_\_ through creating, delivering and communicating superior customer value", the answer is "getting, keeping and growing customers". If the stem says "choosing target markets and building \_\_\_\_ with them", the answer is "profitable relationships".
- In the process definition, the purpose is **to create exchanges that satisfy individual and organisational objectives**. It is not about reducing government control, hiring more staff or manufacturing at the lowest cost.
- Trap on demand management: "always push demand up to the highest possible level" is wrong. Sometimes the manager must reduce demand (demarketing) or shift it in time (synchromarketing). "Leave demand to market forces" and "fix national prices" are also wrong.
- Example: MTN deciding to serve students (target market) with cheap night data bundles and fast campus coverage (value proposition) has answered both questions.
- Non-profit use: NAFDAC campaigns against fake drugs, a church growing its membership or a state immunisation drive all use marketing management.

### Lesson 2.2: Demand states and the types of marketing management

**Must know**

A firm can face any of **eight demand states**. Each has its own marketing task, and the task gives its name to a **type of marketing management**.

| Demand state | What it means | Marketing task (type) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Negative demand | A large part of the market dislikes the product and may even pay to avoid it | **Conversional marketing**: find out why people dislike it and change their attitude |
| 2. No demand | Target consumers are unaware of, or uninterested in, the product | **Stimulational marketing**: create awareness and interest; link the product to a need |
| 3. Latent demand | Many consumers share a strong need that no existing product satisfies | **Developmental marketing**: measure the potential market and develop products that satisfy the unmet need |
| 4. Falling (faltering, declining) demand | Demand for a product is dropping | **Remarketing**: revive demand by re-targeting, changing the product or the communication |
| 5. Irregular demand | Demand varies by season, day or even hour, causing idle or overworked capacity | **Synchromarketing**: match the timing of demand to supply through flexible pricing, promotion and incentives |
| 6. Full demand | The firm is satisfied with its current volume of business | **Maintenance marketing**: keep demand at that level despite changing preferences and rising competition |
| 7. Overfull demand | Demand is higher than the firm can or wants to handle | **Demarketing**: reduce demand temporarily or permanently, e.g. raise prices, cut promotion and service |
| 8. Unwholesome demand | Demand for products considered harmful (hard drugs, cigarettes, illegal guns) | **Countermarketing**: try to destroy the demand entirely |

- **Demarketing vs countermarketing:** demarketing **reduces** demand for an **acceptable** product; countermarketing tries to **destroy** demand for a **harmful** product.
- **Not demand states:** elastic, inelastic, derived, joint demand. These are economics or business-buying terms, not the eight demand states.

**Explain more**

- Memory chain (state, then task): Negative, Convert; No demand, Stimulate; Latent, Develop; Falling, Remarket; Irregular, Synchronise; Full, Maintain; Overfull, Demarket; Unwholesome, Counter.
- **Negative demand / conversional:** parents in a rural community avoid free immunisation because they fear side effects. The task is to learn the reason (fear) and change the attitude, e.g. through trusted community and religious leaders.
- **No demand / stimulational:** farmers who have never heard of a solar-powered irrigation pump show no interest. The task is to create awareness: demonstrations, free trials, extension workers.
- **Latent demand / developmental:** Lagos commuters want a fast, safe, affordable way to beat traffic that no service satisfies. The firm that measures this need and builds a fitting service (as the Lagos rail lines and ride-hailing firms tried) is doing developmental marketing.
- **Falling demand / remarketing:** a once-popular malt drink sees sales fall for three years; the company changes the packaging, adds a new flavour and targets new age groups. That is remarketing.
- **Irregular demand / synchromarketing:** a Lekki cinema is packed on Friday and Saturday nights but empty on weekday afternoons, so it sells half-price weekday tickets. Calabar hotels are full in December (carnival season) but empty the rest of the year: that state is **irregular demand**, not overfull or latent.
- **Full demand / maintenance:** a market leader such as Indomie keeps quality steady, keeps distribution wide and keeps advertising to hold its position.
- **Overfull demand / demarketing:** in a severe dry season a state water corporation asks residents to cut water use and charges more for very high use. The product (water) is good; the firm simply cannot meet all the demand.
- **Unwholesome demand / countermarketing:** a national agency (for example the NDLEA) runs school campaigns on the dangers of hard drugs to stop young people wanting them at all.
- Trap: in a "NOT correctly matched" question, look for a swap such as "Irregular demand: remarketing". Irregular goes with synchromarketing; remarketing goes with falling demand.
- Trap: conversional marketing is about **changing a negative attitude**, while stimulational marketing is about **creating awareness among people who have never heard of the product**. Reviving falling sales is remarketing; shifting demand between busy and quiet periods is synchromarketing.

### Lesson 2.3: The marketing management process: analysis and planning

**Must know**

- **The marketing management process (4 stages, in order):**
  1. **Marketing analysis**
  2. **Marketing planning**
  3. **Marketing implementation**
  4. **Marketing control**
- The same process is also written as: (1) analysing marketing opportunities, (2) selecting (researching and choosing) target markets, (3) developing the marketing mix (designing strategy and the 4Ps programme), (4) managing the marketing effort, which itself means **planning, implementation and control**. Both versions start with analysis and end with control.
- **Marketing analysis:** the **first** stage; a complete study of the company's situation: customers, competitors, the economy and the wider environment, and the firm's own strengths and weaknesses.
- **SWOT analysis:** the main tool of marketing analysis.
  - **Strengths and weaknesses:** **internal** capabilities and limitations within the firm.
  - **Opportunities and threats:** **external** factors the firm may exploit (opportunities) or must guard against (threats).
- **Marketing planning:** the process by which **marketing plans are developed for products** (brands, product lines). It decides marketing objectives and strategies and prepares the marketing plan.
- **Sections of a marketing plan (8):**
  1. Executive summary
  2. Current marketing situation
  3. Threats and opportunities analysis (with strengths and weaknesses)
  4. Objectives and issues
  5. Marketing strategy
  6. Action programmes
  7. Budgets
  8. Controls

**Explain more**

- Do not confuse the levels of planning. **Strategic planning** is company-wide and long-term: it sets the mission, objectives and business portfolio of the whole firm. **Marketing planning** works inside it and produces plans for each product, brand or market. Short-term and medium-term planning describe time spans, not the process of producing product plans.
- SWOT example: a Surulere bakery hears that a well-funded rival will soon open nearby selling cheaper bread. That rival is outside the bakery, so it is a **threat**. The bakery's loyal customers would be a strength; its old oven a weakness; a new estate opening next door an opportunity.
- Analysis example: before next year's plan, a telecom firm studies its customers, competitors and the economy and lists its strengths and weaknesses. That is **marketing analysis**, the first stage.
- Planning example: a drinks company decides it will launch a new juice in Abuja, aiming for 10% market share, positioned as a healthy school-lunch drink. Deciding the objective and strategy is planning.
- A marketing plan never contains things like each employee's personal tax records; that is not a marketing matter.

### Lesson 2.4: Implementation, control and the marketing audit

**Must know**

- **Marketing implementation:** the process that **turns marketing strategies and plans into marketing actions** in order to accomplish marketing objectives.
- **Planning vs implementation:** planning addresses the **what** and **why** of marketing activities; implementation addresses the **who, where, when and how**.
- A brilliant marketing strategy counts for little if the company fails to implement it properly. Results do not follow automatically from a written plan.
- **Marketing control:** measuring and evaluating the results of marketing strategies and plans and **taking corrective action** to ensure that objectives are achieved.
- **The control process (4 steps, in order):**
  1. **Set goals** (what the firm wants to achieve)
  2. **Measure performance** in the market
  3. **Evaluate the causes** of any gap between expected and actual performance
  4. **Take corrective action** to close the gap (this may mean changing the action programmes or even the goals)
- **Operating control:** checks ongoing performance **against the annual plan** and takes corrective action when needed (sales, market share, costs).
- **Strategic control:** checks whether the company's **basic strategies still fit its opportunities** in a changing environment.
- **Four types of marketing control:** annual-plan control, profitability control, efficiency control, strategic control.
- **Marketing audit:** a **comprehensive, systematic, independent and periodic** examination of a company's environment, objectives, strategies and activities to determine problem areas and opportunities and to recommend a plan of action. It is the main tool of strategic control.
- **Formula (control variance):** Variance = Target - Actual; Variance % = (Target - Actual) / Target x 100; Achievement % = Actual / Target x 100.

**Appraising the marketing effort**

- **Marketing effort:** the total resources a firm commits to its marketing mix to stimulate demand for its product. Appraisal checks how well the marketing mix was implemented.
- **2 ways to appraise:**
  - **Subjective evaluation:** based on rules of thumb and experience, not a set standard; inaccurate and biased, so not advisable.
  - **Objective evaluation:** uses the scientific (systematic) approach, grouped into 3 processes: problem identification, modelling (representing a real-life situation), implementation.
- **6 steps in objective appraisal:**
  1. Set the standard
  2. Collect information on performance in each marketing-mix area
  3. Collate the performance information
  4. Analyse the collated information
  5. Compare the results with the standard
  6. Take corrective action if needed
- Effective objective appraisal needs an efficient **marketing information system** giving authentic, adequate and timely feedback from all marketing-mix components.
- **2 appraisal strategies:** periodic marketing effort appraisal (**PMEA**) and continuous marketing effort appraisal (**CMEA**).
- **2 difficulties in appraisal:** it is hard to isolate and measure the impact of environmental forces; it is hard to measure intangible aspects such as consumer satisfaction, attitude and brand image.

**Explain more**

- Implementation example: a drinks company has approved its plan to launch a juice in Abuja. The team now assigns staff, books the launch venue, schedules radio adverts and arranges delivery to retailers. That is **implementation** (who, where, when, how).
- Control example: halfway through the year, a paint company's sales manager finds northern sales well below target (measure), discovers the cause is weak distributor support (evaluate), and adds distributor incentives (correct). That is **control**.
- Worked computation: a paint company's northern target for the half-year is ₦60,000,000; actual sales are ₦51,000,000.
  - Variance = ₦60,000,000 - ₦51,000,000 = ₦9,000,000 shortfall.
  - Variance % = 9,000,000 / 60,000,000 x 100 = 15%.
  - Achievement % = 51,000,000 / 60,000,000 x 100 = 85%.
  - The manager now finds the cause of the 15% gap and corrects it.
- Trap: the order of control is goals, measure, evaluate, correct. Any option starting with "measure" or "take corrective action" is wrong.
- Trap: operating control is about this year's plan; strategic control is about whether the whole strategy still fits. A marketing audit is not a SWOT analysis (SWOT is one part of analysis), not a sales forecast and not the marketing plan itself.
- Objective appraisal steps mirror the control steps: standard first, corrective action last.

### Lesson 2.5: Importance of marketing management

**Must know**

**To the organisation:**

1. Coordinates product, price, promotion and distribution decisions towards the firm's objectives.
2. Helps the firm match its offerings to market demand.
3. Guides decisions on target markets and the marketing mix.
4. Helps the firm identify and respond to changes in customer needs and the environment.
5. Generates revenue and profit and supports growth and survival.
6. Builds customer satisfaction, loyalty and long-term relationships.
7. Provides information for planning and decision-making.
8. Helps the firm meet competition.

**To society and the economy:**

1. Helps direct scarce resources towards the goods and services people want.
2. Raises the standard of living by making a wide variety of products available.
3. Creates employment in selling, advertising, distribution and research.
4. Supports economic growth by matching production with consumption.

**Explain more**

- Marketing management does **not** guarantee that a firm will never make a loss, that rivals will leave the market or that all firms will charge the same price. It does not remove the need for finance or production departments, nor for consumer protection laws such as the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 enforced by the FCCPC.
- It does not reduce product variety or let a firm ignore customer needs; those options describe the opposite of good marketing management.
- Example: Dangote Cement uses research on housing demand to plan where to build plants and depots, so resources flow to where cement is needed. That is resource direction (a benefit to society) and matching supply to demand (a benefit to the firm).

### One-minute recap

- Marketing management: the art and science of choosing target markets and getting, keeping and growing customers through superior customer value.
- It is demand management: influencing the level, timing and composition of demand.
- Two questions: whom to serve (target market) and how best to serve them (value proposition).
- Eight states and tasks: negative-conversional, no-stimulational, latent-developmental, falling-remarketing, irregular-synchromarketing, full-maintenance, overfull-demarketing, unwholesome-countermarketing.
- Demarketing reduces demand for a good product; countermarketing destroys demand for a harmful one.
- Process: analysis, planning, implementation, control.
- SWOT: strengths and weaknesses internal; opportunities and threats external.
- Planning is what and why; implementation is who, where, when and how.
- Control: set goals, measure, evaluate causes, correct. Marketing audit: comprehensive, systematic, independent, periodic.
- Marketing management serves profit and non-profit organisations alike.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A Lagos ferry operator's target for the first quarter was ₦12,000,000 in fares, but actual fares were ₦9,600,000. Boats are full at rush hours but nearly empty at midday. (a) Compute the shortfall and the achievement rate. (b) Name the demand state and the matching marketing task, and say which stage of the marketing management process the computation belongs to.

*Answer:*

- (a) Shortfall = ₦12,000,000 - ₦9,600,000 = ₦2,400,000. Shortfall % = 2,400,000 / 12,000,000 x 100 = 20%. Achievement = 9,600,000 / 12,000,000 x 100 = 80%.
- (b) Demand that swings by hour is **irregular demand**; the task is **synchromarketing**, e.g. cheaper midday fares. Comparing actual results with a target and correcting the gap is **marketing control**.
