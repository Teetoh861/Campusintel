## Topic 10: Marketing in the Digital and Social Media Era

**What you must know:** the meaning of digital marketing and e-marketing and how they differ from traditional marketing; the main digital channels (search, SEO and PPC, display, retargeting, content, email, mobile, affiliate, influencer, viral); social media marketing, its platforms, social commerce and e-commerce; paid, owned and earned media, user-generated content and eWOM; the advantages and challenges of digital marketing in Nigeria; and the six campaign metrics with their formulas.

### Lesson 10.1: Meaning of digital marketing and how it differs from traditional marketing

**Must know**

- **Digital marketing:** the use of digital channels and technologies, such as websites, search engines, social media, email and mobile apps, to promote products and engage customers.
- **E-marketing (internet or online marketing):** marketing products and services over the internet, through websites, online ads, email and similar tools. In an exam, treat it as the internet side of digital marketing.
- **Social media marketing:** using platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X (Twitter) and LinkedIn to build brand awareness, interact with customers and promote offers.
- **E-commerce:** buying and selling goods and services over the internet (e.g. Jumia, Konga, Jiji).
- **Online advertising:** paid messages shown on the internet, e.g. a banner ad for a hotel on a Nigerian news website.

| Feature | Traditional marketing | Digital marketing |
|---|---|---|
| Media | Print, radio, TV, billboards, handbills | Websites, search, social media, email, mobile |
| Direction | Mostly one-way mass communication | Two-way and interactive: customers reply, comment, share |
| Targeting | Broad, by medium and location | Precise: by location, age, interests, behaviour |
| Measurement | Slow and approximate | Precise and real-time: views, clicks, sales |
| Cost | Often high entry cost | Can start small; low cost per person reached |

**Explain more**

- **Is it digital or not?** A sponsored Instagram post, optimising a website to rank higher on search engines and an email newsletter are all digital marketing. Painting a shop's name on a roadside wall, a handbill at a bus stop, a jingle on a market loudspeaker and a billboard on Third Mainland Bridge are **not**; they are traditional (outdoor or print) methods.
- The single feature that most clearly separates social media marketing from newspaper or TV advertising is **two-way, interactive communication**. Options saying social media is "one-way", "cannot be measured" or "cannot be targeted" are always wrong.
- Technology is a fast-changing force in the marketing environment: it creates opportunities to win market share and makes older products obsolete. Electronic banking cut banks' costs and made services more convenient; GTBank now serves many customers through apps and USSD.
- **New business models:** technology firms such as Uber, Airbnb and Amazon (in Nigeria, Bolt, Jumia and Paystack) have reshaped whole industries. Marketers should think in terms of the value delivered to the client and experiment with new pricing structures.
- **360-degree marketing:** be present wherever customers look for help, which is increasingly online. Firms that blend online and offline methods grow fastest and are most profitable; assuming "my customers don't go online" is risky.

### Lesson 10.2: The main digital marketing channels

**Must know**

1. **Search engine optimisation (SEO):** improving a website so it ranks higher in the **free (organic)** search results. The firm does not pay per click.
2. **Pay-per-click (PPC) search advertising:** buying **sponsored listings** by bidding on keywords; the advertiser **pays each time the ad is clicked**. SEO plus paid search together are broadly called **search engine marketing (SEM)**, though some use SEM for paid search alone.
3. **Display (banner) advertising:** image or video ads on other websites and apps.
4. **Retargeting (remarketing):** showing ads to people who visited a site or viewed a product but left without buying, as they browse other websites and social media.
5. **Content marketing:** creating and sharing valuable, relevant material (blog posts, how-to videos, infographics) to attract and engage a clearly defined audience **rather than directly pushing a sales message**.
6. **Email marketing:** sending newsletters and offers by email. It should be **permission-based**.
7. **Mobile marketing:** messages, promotions and content delivered through mobile phones: **SMS**, in-app ads, mobile websites, and **location-based (geo-targeted)** offers sent when the phone is near a place.
8. **Affiliate marketing:** paying a **commission** to bloggers or website owners for **each sale** made through their special link or code.
9. **Influencer marketing:** partnering with people who have a large or highly engaged online following (content creators, celebrities) so they recommend the brand to their followers.
10. **Viral marketing:** a message so entertaining or useful that people pass it on rapidly online; the **digital version of word-of-mouth**.

- **Permission-based marketing:** sending messages only to people who have agreed (**opted in**), e.g. by subscribing to a newsletter.
- **Spam:** unsolicited, unwanted commercial messages sent in bulk to people who did not ask for them.
- **Micro-influencers:** smaller, more focused audiences that often show **higher engagement and trust** than celebrity ("mega") influencers.
- **Marketing automation:** software that automates large parts of marketing, such as follow-up emails, lead generation and lead nurturing.

**Explain more**

- **SEO versus PPC** is the favourite trap. SEO = organic, earned over time, no fee per click. PPC = paid, instant, charged per click. An option saying "SEO is paid per click" or "PPC is always free" is reversed. A Lagos furniture store bidding on "office chairs Lagos" so its sponsored link sits at the top, paying only when someone clicks, is **PPC**, not SEO.
- **Content marketing:** a Nigerian skincare brand posting free weekly videos on caring for skin in hot, humid weather, without asking viewers to buy, is content marketing. The sale comes later, from trust.
- **Mobile marketing in practice:** a supermarket texting customers who gave their numbers at checkout about a weekend discount is **SMS (mobile) marketing**. A restaurant in Ikeja whose app sends an offer only when the customer is within 1 km is **location-based (geo-targeted) mobile marketing**. MTN, Airtel and Glo also push offers by SMS and USSD.
- **Retargeting:** a student views a laptop on Jumia, leaves, and then sees the same laptop in ads on other sites for days. That is retargeting, not viral marketing or market testing.
- **Affiliate versus influencer:** the affiliate is paid **per sale** through a tracked link or code; the influencer is usually paid to **recommend**. "Commission per sale through a link" means affiliate.
- **Hyper-targeted advertising:** online ads can now reach specific companies, job roles or niche interests, and link to downloadable content that proves expertise. This suits professional service firms that traditional mass advertising served poorly.
- **Permission versus spam:** opt-in lists get better responses; spam damages trust. In Nigeria, personal data is protected by the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

### Lesson 10.3: Social media marketing, social commerce and types of media

**Must know**

- **Main platforms:** Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X (Twitter), LinkedIn (professional and B2B), YouTube, WhatsApp.
- **Social commerce:** buying and selling directly within social media platforms, e.g. a vendor showing products in a **WhatsApp Business catalogue** and taking orders in the chat, or selling through Instagram shops.
- **User-generated content (UGC):** photos, videos, reviews and posts about a brand **created and shared by customers**, not by the company.
- **Electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM):** positive or negative comments about products and brands that consumers share with each other through reviews, social media posts and online forums.

**The three types of media (POE):**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid** | Exposure the brand pays for | Boosted (sponsored) Instagram post, PPC ads, banner ads, paid influencer posts |
| **Owned** | Channels the brand itself controls | Its own website, Instagram page, company blog, app, email list |
| **Earned** | Free exposure others give the brand | An unpaid blogger's review, shares, mentions, press coverage, eWOM |

**Explain more**

- **Classify carefully.** A fashion brand paying to "boost" its Instagram post into the feeds of 50,000 targeted users = **paid**. The same brand's Instagram page itself = **owned**. A food blogger, **not paid**, posting a glowing review of a new Lekki restaurant that thousands share = **earned** for the restaurant. If money changes hands for the exposure, it is paid; if the brand runs the channel, it is owned; if others give it freely, it is earned.
- **Hashtag challenges:** a Nigerian telecoms brand that creates a branded hashtag and invites customers to post short dance videos, with data bundles as prizes, is aiming to **generate engagement and user-generated content that spreads the brand message**. It does not replace distribution channels or set sales quotas.
- **Why UGC and eWOM matter:** buyers trust fellow customers more than the firm's adverts, so reviews and posts shape the information-search stage of buying.
- **Social commerce is huge in Nigeria:** many small vendors run their whole business through Instagram and WhatsApp, taking payment by bank transfer or through Paystack or Flutterwave links and delivering by dispatch rider.
- **E-commerce** is all online buying and selling (Jumia, Konga, a brand's own store); social commerce is the part that happens **inside** social platforms.

### Lesson 10.4: Advantages and challenges of digital marketing in Nigeria

**Must know**

**Advantages:**

1. **Wide reach at relatively low cost;** a small business can start with a small budget.
2. **Precise targeting** by location, age, interests and behaviour.
3. **Measurable results:** clicks, views and sales can be measured precisely and quickly (real time).
4. **Two-way interaction:** quick conversation with customers; feedback and complaints are heard fast.
5. **Always on:** customers can see offers and buy 24 hours a day, from anywhere.
6. **Flexibility:** campaigns can be changed or stopped quickly based on results.
7. **Relationship building:** regular contact through email, social media and content builds loyalty.

**Challenges:**

1. **Cost of mobile data and unreliable power supply** limit how often some customers go online.
2. **Online fraud and lack of trust:** some consumers fear being scammed and hesitate to pay online or share personal details, which slows online sales.
3. **Negative messages spread fast** and are difficult for the firm to control.
4. **Ad clutter and ad avoidance:** too many ads lead people to install ad blockers or ignore banner ads.
5. **Uneven internet access and digital literacy,** especially in rural areas.
6. **Privacy and data protection concerns,** and spam annoying customers.
7. **Fast-changing platforms and algorithms,** which demand constant skills and effort; delivery and logistics problems for online orders.

**Explain more**

- **NOT an advantage trap:** "it guarantees that no customer will ever post a negative comment" is false. Social media gives customers a public voice, so firms must manage complaints. Reach, targeting and two-way interaction are real advantages.
- **Spread of bad news:** a customer posts a video complaint about a Lagos bakery's spoiled cake and within hours it is shared thousands of times. This shows that negative messages spread quickly and are hard to control. The right response is fast, polite, public handling of the complaint.
- **Trust and payment:** cash on delivery is common on Jumia and in social commerce because many buyers fear online fraud; trusted payment gateways and clear return policies build confidence.
- **Ad avoidance** is not price discrimination or segmentation; it is consumers tuning out because of too many ads.
- Options such as "online results can never be measured" or "no Nigerians use mobile phones" are false challenges.

### Lesson 10.5: Digital marketing metrics and computations

**Must know**

1. **Click-through rate (CTR)** = clicks / impressions x 100
2. **Cost per thousand impressions (CPM)** = total cost / impressions x 1,000
3. **Cost per click (CPC)** = total ad spend / number of clicks
4. **Conversion rate** = orders (conversions) / visitors (or clicks) x 100
5. **Engagement rate** = (likes + comments + shares) / followers x 100
6. **Return on ad spend (ROAS)** = revenue from ads / ad spend

- **Impression:** one showing of an ad on a screen. **Click:** one tap or click on it. **Conversion:** the visitor does what you want (buys, signs up).
- **Cost per acquisition (CPA)** = ad spend / number of orders: the cost of winning one customer.
- Other things firms track: reach, website traffic, followers, likes, shares, comments.

**Explain more**

**Worked example (one campaign, every metric).** A Lagos shoe store spends ₦60,000 on Instagram ads. The ads get 200,000 impressions and 1,500 clicks. 45 of the people who clicked place an order, at an average of ₦8,000 each.

- CPM = 60,000 / 200,000 x 1,000 = 0.3 x 1,000 = **₦300** per thousand impressions.
- CTR = 1,500 / 200,000 x 100 = 0.0075 x 100 = **0.75%**.
- CPC = 60,000 / 1,500 = **₦40** per click.
- Conversion rate = 45 / 1,500 x 100 = 0.03 x 100 = **3%**.
- CPA = 60,000 / 45 = **₦1,333.33** per order (rounded to the nearest kobo).
- Revenue = 45 x 8,000 = ₦360,000. ROAS = 360,000 / 60,000 = **6** (₦6 of sales for every ₦1 of ads).

**Engagement rate example.** A Nigerian creator with 40,000 followers gets 1,200 likes, 300 comments and 100 shares on a sponsored post. Total engagements = 1,200 + 300 + 100 = 1,600. Engagement rate = 1,600 / 40,000 x 100 = 0.04 x 100 = **4%**.

**Traps to avoid:**

- **CTR** divides clicks by **impressions**, never impressions by clicks. With 1,200 clicks from 80,000 impressions, CTR = 1,200 / 80,000 x 100 = **1.5%**; 66.67 is the upside-down answer and 0.015 forgets to multiply by 100.
- **CPM** must be multiplied by **1,000**. ₦90,000 for 600,000 impressions: 90,000 / 600,000 = ₦0.15 per impression, x 1,000 = **₦150**. ₦0.15 is the cost of one impression, not a thousand.
- **CPC** divides spend by clicks: ₦75,000 / 2,500 = **₦30**. Clicks / spend gives 0.033, which is meaningless.
- **Conversion rate:** 120 orders from 4,000 visitors = 120 / 4,000 x 100 = **3%** (not 30% or 0.03%).
- **Engagement rate** adds all three actions before dividing: (900 + 250 + 350) / 25,000 x 100 = 1,500 / 25,000 x 100 = **6%**. Using likes alone (3.6%) is wrong.
- **ROAS** is a ratio, not a percentage: ₦1,800,000 / ₦400,000 = **4.5**, meaning ₦4.50 of sales per ₦1 spent. The upside-down 0.22 is wrong, and 450 is not the ratio.

### One-minute recap

- Digital marketing = digital channels and technologies (websites, search, social media, email, mobile apps) to promote and engage; e-marketing = marketing over the internet.
- Digital beats traditional on targeting, real-time measurement and two-way interaction; a painted wall, billboard or handbill is not digital.
- SEO = free organic ranking; PPC = sponsored listing, pay per click; SEO + paid search = SEM.
- Content (value, not hard sell), email (permission, not spam), mobile (SMS, geo-targeted), affiliate (commission per sale), influencer (micro = higher engagement), viral (digital word-of-mouth), retargeting (follows people who left).
- Social commerce = selling inside social platforms (WhatsApp Business catalogue); UGC = customer-made content; eWOM = consumers sharing opinions online.
- Paid (boosted post), owned (own website, page, blog), earned (unpaid review, shares).
- Advantages: low cost, targeting, measurement, interaction. Challenges: data cost and power, fraud and trust, fast-spreading complaints, ad clutter and avoidance.
- CTR = clicks / impressions x 100; CPM = cost / impressions x 1,000; CPC = spend / clicks; conversion = orders / visitors x 100; engagement = (likes + comments + shares) / followers x 100; ROAS = revenue / ad spend.

### Theory practice

**Question:** An online store in Abuja spends ₦150,000 on a display campaign that delivers 500,000 impressions and 2,000 clicks. 80 of those visitors buy, producing ₦600,000 in sales. Calculate the CPM, CTR, CPC, conversion rate and ROAS.

*Solution:*

- CPM = 150,000 / 500,000 x 1,000 = 0.3 x 1,000 = **₦300**.
- CTR = 2,000 / 500,000 x 100 = 0.004 x 100 = **0.4%**.
- CPC = 150,000 / 2,000 = **₦75**.
- Conversion rate = 80 / 2,000 x 100 = 0.04 x 100 = **4%**.
- ROAS = 600,000 / 150,000 = **4** (₦4 of sales per ₦1 of advertising).
