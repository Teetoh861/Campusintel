## Topic 1: Meaning, Exchange Process and Functions of Marketing

**What you must know:** the main definitions of marketing and who gave them; needs, wants and demands; value and satisfaction; exchange, its five conditions, transactions, transfers and relationships; markets; the eight functions of marketing in three groups; utility; the importance of marketing to consumers, firms and society.

### Lesson 1.1: What marketing means

**Must know**

- The ultimate goal of any business is to **identify and satisfy customers**; that goal is reached only through effective **marketing** activities. Marketing is not merely selling, and it is not confined to one department: it is an attitude of mind and a business philosophy for the whole organisation.
- There is **no single, universally accepted definition**. Learn these:

| Defined by | Definition (key words) |
|---|---|
| Stanton (1981) | **Societal sense:** any exchange activity intended to satisfy human wants. **Business sense:** a system of business action designed to plan, price, promote and distribute want-satisfying goods and services to markets |
| Kotler (1988) | A **social and managerial process** by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating and exchanging products and value with others |
| Kotler and Armstrong | The process by which companies **create value for customers and build strong customer relationships** in order to **capture value from customers in return** |
| Kotler and Keller (shortest) | **Meeting needs profitably** |
| Chartered Institute of Marketing (CIM, UK) | The management process responsible for **identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer requirements profitably** |
| AMA (current) | The activity, set of institutions and processes for **creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging offerings** that have value for **customers, clients, partners and society at large** |
| AMA (1985) | The process of planning and executing the conception, pricing, promotion and distribution of ideas, goods and services to create exchanges that satisfy individual and organisational objectives |
| AMA (early, 1960) | The performance of business activities that **direct the flow of goods and services from producer to consumer or user** |
| Working definition | All activities aimed at **identifying and satisfying customer needs through beneficial exchanges with target markets** |

- The working definition requires 4 things: (1) finding out what customers need; (2) developing a product or service to satisfy those needs; (3) positioning it to satisfy the needs; (4) deciding the marketing mix to price, promote and distribute it.
- **Peter Drucker:** "the aim of marketing is to make selling superfluous", i.e. know the customer so well that the product fits and sells itself.
- Definitions fall into **3 groups**: marketing as a **process**; as a **concept or philosophy** of business; as an **orientation**.
- Marketing is **both a functional area of management and an overall business philosophy** which recognises that identifying, satisfying and retaining customers is the key to success. It **focuses the firm's or individual's attention on the needs and wants of the marketplace**.
- **Micromarketing (micro definition):** firm-oriented, narrow view: marketing activities from the point of view of the individual organisation (e.g. identifying, anticipating and satisfying needs profitably for the firm).
- **Macromarketing (macro definition):** broad, societal view: exchange activities and exchange systems in society, and how marketing systems relate to the rest of society.
- **Nonbusiness marketing:** marketing carried out by **non-profit organisations**.
- **What can be marketed:** goods, services, events, experiences, persons, places, properties, organisations, information and **ideas**.

**Explain more**

- **Narrow (traditional) concept:** physical transfer of goods from producer to consumer; product-oriented, exchange of goods for money. **Broad (modern) concept:** everything from before production to after-sales service; customer-oriented, earning profit through customer satisfaction.
- **Weakness of the early AMA (1960) definition:** it treats marketing as starting **only after production**, ignoring the study of customer needs. Marketing actually **starts before production** (finding out what customers need) and **continues after the sale** (customer service, relationship building). So "marketing begins after production and ends at the sale" is **false**. Selling and advertising are **only part** of marketing.
- Stanton's "**total system** of business activities designed to plan, price, promote and distribute want-satisfying products to target markets to achieve organisational objectives" stresses that marketing is **a set of related activities that work together**, not one activity.
- Kotler's "**societal process** by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating, offering and freely exchanging products and services of value" is a **social** definition. "Meeting needs profitably" and the CIM version are **managerial** definitions.
- The 2004 change to the AMA definition moved the emphasis from the 4Ps to **customers and customer relationships**. The current AMA wording, "creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging offerings with value", is the modern view that goes beyond a mere sale. The CIM definition is often called the most concise.
- The essence of every modern definition: **delivering superior value to customers while balancing profitability**. The scope of marketing covers ideation, value creation and strategic distribution, orchestrating value creation, delivery and customer engagement, not advertising alone. **Market segmentation and research** need the deepest understanding of consumer insights.
- A Lagos State ministry of health jingle urging handwashing to prevent cholera sells nothing, yet it **is** marketing: marketing applies to ideas and non-profit causes. Commercialised bodies such as NIPOST now also market actively.

### Lesson 1.2: Needs, wants, demands, value and satisfaction

**Must know**

- **Needs:** states of **felt deprivation** (hunger, thirst, the desire to belong). They are a basic part of human make-up; **marketers do not create needs**.
  1. **Physical needs:** food, clothing, warmth, **safety**.
  2. **Social needs:** **belonging and affection**.
  3. **Individual needs:** **knowledge and self-expression**.
- **Wants:** the form a need takes as it is **shaped by culture and individual personality**.
- **Demands:** wants **backed by buying power**. (The quantity of goods people are willing and able to buy in a given period.)
- **Market offering:** a combination of products, services, information or experiences offered to satisfy a need or want.
- **Customer perceived value:** the customer's evaluation of the difference between **all the benefits and all the costs** of an offering compared with competing offers.
  Customer perceived value = total customer benefit − total customer cost
- **Customer satisfaction:** depends on product **performance relative to expectations**:

| Performance vs expectations | Result |
|---|---|
| Falls short | Dissatisfied |
| Matches | Satisfied |
| Exceeds | Highly satisfied (delighted) |

**Explain more**

- Tunde at UNILAG is hungry (**need**). He specifically wants jollof rice with chicken (**want**). With ₦2,000 in his pocket and the willingness to buy, the want becomes a **demand**.
- One need, different wants: a hungry person in Ibadan wants amala and ewedu; one in Enugu wants abacha. Culture shapes the want.
- **Trap:** a desire with no money is still only a **want**. Ada earns ₦250,000 a month with no savings or loan access and desires an ₦80 million SUV. ₦80,000,000 ÷ ₦250,000 = 320 months of her **entire** pay (about 26.7 years, rounded). Her desire is **a want that is not yet a demand**.
- Worked value example: Bisi values the benefits of Phone A at ₦420,000; its total cost is ₦350,000 price + ₦20,000 transport and set-up = ₦370,000, so value = ₦420,000 − ₦370,000 = ₦50,000. Phone B: benefits ₦380,000, cost ₦300,000 + ₦10,000 = ₦310,000, so value = ₦380,000 − ₦310,000 = ₦70,000. She picks **Phone B**, which gives more value even though Phone A has more benefits.
- Chika expected her generator to run quietly for ten hours; it is noisy and stops after four. Performance fell short, so she is **dissatisfied**.

### Lesson 1.3: Exchange, transactions, relationships and markets

**Must know**

- **4 ways to obtain a product:** self-production, coercion, begging, **exchange**. Marketing is based on **exchange**.
- **Exchange:** the act of obtaining a desired object from someone by **offering something in return**. It is the process through which transfer of ownership and rights takes place. **5 conditions:**
  1. There are **at least two parties**.
  2. Each party has **something that might be of value** to the other.
  3. Each party is **capable of communication and delivery**.
  4. Each party is **free to accept or reject** the offer.
  5. Each party believes it is **appropriate or desirable** to deal with the other.
- Money is **not** a condition. Exchange is **value-creating**: it normally leaves **both parties better off**.
- Exchange is a **process** (parties negotiating towards agreement). When agreement is reached, a **transaction** takes place.
- **Transaction:** a **trade of values between two parties**; the basic unit of exchange. Elements: (1) at least two things of value; (2) agreed-upon conditions; (3) a time of agreement; (4) a place of agreement. No intermediary is required.
  - **Monetary transaction:** goods for money. **Barter transaction:** goods or services for other goods or services.
- **Transfer:** A gives to B but receives **nothing tangible in return** (gifts, donations, subsidies).
- **Relationship marketing:** building, maintaining and enhancing **long-term, mutually satisfying relationships** with customers, rather than single sales (transaction marketing).
- **Market:** the set of **all actual and potential buyers** of a product. (Traditionally also a physical place where buyers and sellers meet to exchange.)
- **Marketer:** one who seeks a response (attention, purchase, vote, donation) from another party, the **prospect**.

**Explain more**

- Robbers who take a traveller's phone and leave him some cash have not made an exchange: he was **not free to reject** the offer (coercion).
- A Kano tomato farmer and a Lagos restaurant owner who do not know of each other, with no way to send goods, lack condition 3, **communication and delivery**.
- Benue farmer gives ten tubers of yam for a bag of fertiliser: **barter**. Mrs Okafor pays ₦75,000 at Mile 12 for a bag of rice: **monetary transaction**. A company donates food to flood victims in Kogi State: **transfer**.
- **Evolution of marketing (6 stages):** (1) **self-sufficient** stage (people produced what they consumed); (2) **barter** stage (goods for goods, before money); (3) **marketing system** stage (a fixed place for exchange, to save travelling); (4) **monetary** stage (currency, first metal, solved barter's problems); (5) **town marketing** (transport, communication and industry grew); (6) **international marketing** (worldwide trade, supported by bodies such as GATT, now the WTO, UNCTAD and ITC).
- Relationship marketing builds the long-term customer base that keeps firms like MTN and GTBank growing in a dynamic economy.

### Lesson 1.4: Functions of marketing and utility

**Must know**

The traditional classification groups **8 functions** into **3**:

| Group | Functions |
|---|---|
| Exchange functions | 1. Buying 2. Selling |
| Physical supply (physical distribution) functions | 3. Transportation 4. Storage |
| Facilitating functions | 5. Financing 6. Risk bearing 7. Standardisation and grading 8. Market information |

- **Buying:** deciding what to buy, choosing suppliers and placing orders. **Selling:** finding buyers and persuading them to buy; transfers ownership.
- **Transportation:** moving goods from where they are produced to where they are wanted. **Storage:** holding goods from when they are produced until they are wanted.
- **Financing:** providing funds or credit to carry out marketing. **Risk bearing:** carrying the risk of loss (damage, theft, price falls), often through insurance. **Standardisation and grading:** setting standards and sorting products into classes by quality and size. **Market information:** gathering and analysing information on customers, competitors and the market (marketing research).
- **Utility** (want-satisfying power) created:

| Utility | Created by |
|---|---|
| Form | **Production** (marketing guides it through product planning and development) |
| Place | Transportation |
| Time | Storage |
| Possession | Exchange (selling, credit and instalment terms) |
| Information | Research and promotion |

**Explain more**

- Kebbi miller buys paddy at harvest and sells in the lean season: **storage** (time utility). Trucks moving tomatoes from Kano to Lagos: **transportation** (place utility).
- Ondo cocoa sorted into Grade I and Grade II before export: **standardisation and grading**. Lagos importer buying marine insurance: **risk bearing**. Distributor letting supermarkets pay in 30 days: **financing**. A noodles maker surveying Abuja shoppers and tracking rivals' prices: **market information**. Supermarket choosing soap brands and placing orders: **buying**.
- **Trap:** transportation is a **physical supply** function, not facilitating. Financing and risk bearing are facilitating, not exchange functions.
- An Ikeja shop letting customers take home a ₦450,000 sofa today and pay monthly creates **possession utility**. Form utility is the one mainly created by production, not marketing.

### Lesson 1.5: Importance of marketing

**Must know**

The basic role of marketing is to **enhance the growth of economies and the standard of living of individuals**. Marketing has been called "the creation and delivery of a standard of living to society".

1. **To consumers (4):** customer satisfaction; selection facility (wider choice); product information; higher standard of living.
2. **To the firm (4):** revenue generation (for survival and growth, by attracting and keeping customers); information for planning and decision-making; mass distribution; increase in goodwill (loyal, long-term customers).
3. **To society and the economy (5):** employment generation; poverty eradication; rise in standard of living; creation of utility; optimum utilisation of resources.

**Explain more**

- Jobs come from buying, selling, retailing, transport, advertising, warehousing and distribution. Good distribution systems for raw materials and finished goods speed up growth in developing countries.
- Marketing **bridges the gap between consumer needs and available solutions**, and creates societal value by aligning offerings with consumer needs and social welfare.
- Marketing does **not** guarantee a monopoly, remove all business risk, end the need for production planning, or fix one price everywhere.
- Firms such as Cadbury Nigeria and Nestlé Nigeria have stepped up marketing; formerly non-profit bodies such as NIPOST, once commercialised, now market to earn profit.

### One-minute recap

- No single definition; know Stanton, Kotler, Kotler and Armstrong, CIM, AMA and "meeting needs profitably".
- Micromarketing = firm view; macromarketing = societal view; nonbusiness marketing = non-profits.
- Needs (physical, social, individual) are not created by marketers; wants are shaped by culture; demands are wants plus buying power.
- Perceived value = total benefits − total costs; satisfaction = performance vs expectations.
- Exchange: 5 conditions, money not one; transaction = trade of values (barter or monetary); transfer = nothing tangible back.
- Market = all actual and potential buyers; marketer seeks a response from a prospect.
- 8 functions: exchange (buying, selling), physical supply (transportation, storage), facilitating (financing, risk bearing, standardisation and grading, market information).
- Form utility comes mainly from production; marketing adds place, time, possession, information.
- Marketing benefits consumers, firms and society; its basic role is economic growth and a higher standard of living.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Classify each activity into its marketing function and group: (i) a warehouse holds yam until the lean season; (ii) a bank gives a distributor a 60-day trade loan; (iii) a trader negotiates and hands over goods to a buyer; (iv) eggs are sorted by size into crates.

*Answer:* (i) Storage, a physical supply function (time utility). (ii) Financing, a facilitating function. (iii) Selling, an exchange function (possession utility). (iv) Standardisation and grading, a facilitating function.
