## Topic 9: Market Measurement and Forecasting

**What you must know:** what market measurement and forecasting are and why firms do them; the levels of a market; market demand, market minimum, market potential, company demand, sales forecast, quota and budget; market share, relative share and penetration; estimating current demand (n x q x p, market build-up, chain ratio, multiple-factor index); the forecasting methods; time series with moving averages and trend lines, all with computations.

### Lesson 9.1: Meaning, importance and managing measurement

**Must know**

- **Market measurement:** helps management understand the market and sales potential for a **specific area, time period, product and segment**. **"Ordinal analytics"** studies market share, size and growth by customer segment and product.
- **Forecasting:** giving managers reliable, early predictive information on emerging trends and "what if" scenarios so they can prepare and react. It uses business requirements, industry challenges, macroeconomic factors and statistical/econometric techniques, and is **re-run as new data arrive**.
- **Importance:** the main aim is to **support marketing decisions**. Decisions are only as good as their information, so measurement makes decisions more **objective and scientific**, reducing risk and guesswork. For minor, low-risk decisions it may not be worth it: weigh the decision's importance and risk against the **cost and time** of gathering information.
- **Managing measurement and forecasting** = planning, implementing and controlling these activities systematically. Steps:

1. **Describe the problem and information needs** (gathering information is costly, so a clear problem definition decides what data is relevant).
2. **Gather relevant information** (dual purpose: supplies data for the problem and guides the choice of method).
3. **Select the methods** of measurement and forecasting (start by checking there is enough data of the right quality, then match information needs to what each method can do).

- **5 factors in choosing a method:**

1. **Nature and availability of data** (enough suitable data? what pattern: **trends, seasonal fluctuations, conjuncture patterns**?)
2. **Accuracy required**
3. **Costs**
4. **Time**
5. **Application** (results must be usable and understandable by decision makers)

**Explain more**

- Think of Dangote Cement deciding whether to build a new plant in the north. A wrong demand estimate costs billions, so careful measurement pays. Choosing between two jingle colours does not justify an expensive study.
- More reliable methods usually cost more time and money; a perfect answer that arrives after the decision is useless.

### Lesson 9.2: Levels of the market and key demand concepts

**Must know**

- **Market:** the set of all actual and potential buyers of a product.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Potential market | consumers who profess a sufficient level of **interest** in an offer |
| Available market | consumers with **interest, income and access** to the offer |
| Qualified available market | available market who also **qualify** (e.g. legal age) |
| Target (served) market | the part of the qualified available market the company **decides to pursue** |
| Penetrated market | consumers who are **already buying** the product |

- **Market demand:** the **total volume** of a product that would be **bought** by a defined **customer group**, in a defined **geographical area**, in a defined **time period**, in a defined **marketing environment**, under a defined **marketing programme** (8 elements: product, total volume, bought, customer group, area, time, environment, programme).
- Market demand is **not a fixed number** but a function of conditions and industry marketing spending.
- **Market minimum:** sales that would occur **without any marketing expenditure** to stimulate demand.
- **Market potential:** the **limit approached by market demand as industry marketing expenditure approaches infinity**, for a given marketing environment. Also: the maximum possible demand for a **product type** in a whole market, for a given area, period and segment.
- **3 aspects in defining market potential:** (1) defined market environment, (2) marketing expenditure by the industry, (3) market demand.
- **Primary demand:** demand for the **product class** as a whole (e.g. bottled water in general). **Selective (brand) demand:** demand for **one brand** (e.g. Eva water).
- **Company demand:** the company's **estimated share of market demand** at alternative levels of its marketing effort in a given period. Formula: Company demand = market share x total market demand.
- **Company sales forecast:** the **expected** level of company sales based on a **chosen marketing plan** and an **assumed marketing environment**.
- **Company sales potential (sales potential):** the **limit** approached by company demand as its marketing effort increases **relative to competitors**; the maximum demand for **one firm's brand**, not the product type.
- **Sales quota:** a sales **goal** set for a product line, division or salesperson; usually set **slightly above** estimated sales to stretch effort.
- **Sales budget:** a **conservative** estimate of expected sales, used for purchasing, production and cash-flow decisions; usually set **slightly below** the forecast.
- **Potential market share:** the relation between market potential and sales potential; what part of the potential market the firm expects to win.
- **Effective demand:** **actual sales** of a product type in a given area, period and segment.

**Explain more**

- **Potential versus forecast (favourite trap):** sales potential is what the firm **could** reach if it pushed effort to the limit; the forecast is what it **expects** under the plan it has **actually chosen**. They are not the same; potential is normally the higher figure.
- Company sales potential equals market potential only if the company took **100%** of the market, which almost never happens.
- **Expansible market:** total size changes a lot with industry marketing spend (e.g. a new snack category). **Non-expansible:** spend barely changes it (e.g. salt); the gap between market minimum and market potential is small.
- A "Drink more water" campaign from a water producers' association builds **primary** demand; "Choose Nestlé Pure Life" builds **selective** demand.
- The forecast does **not** decide marketing spend; the plan comes first, and the forecast follows from it.
- All these measures can refer to the **present or the future**. Comparing them shows how well an offering does against rivals, the total market and market potential.

### Lesson 9.3: Estimating current demand and market share

**Must know**

- **Total market potential:** Q = n x q x p, where n = number of buyers, q = quantity bought by an average buyer per period, p = average price per unit.
- **Area market potential** (for a city, state or region), 2 main methods:
  1. **Market-buildup method:** identify **all potential buyers** in each market (mainly business buyers, e.g. every bakery in a state) and estimate their potential purchases, then **add up**.
  2. **Multiple-factor index method** (mainly consumer markets): weight several factors. The best known is the **buying power index (BPI)**: BPI = 0.5(area % of national disposable income) + 0.3(area % of national retail sales) + 0.2(area % of national population). Area potential = BPI% x national potential.
- **Chain-ratio method:** start with a **base number** (e.g. population) and multiply by a **series of percentages** that narrow it to likely buyers (then by usage per buyer if needed).
- **Market share** = (company sales / total market sales, including the company) x 100%.
- **Relative market share** = company's market share / largest competitor's share.
- **Market penetration index** = (current market demand / market potential) x 100%.
- **Sales forecast** = forecast market demand x expected market share.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (n x q x p).** 500,000 buyers each buy 6 tins a year at ₦800. Q = 500,000 x 6 x ₦800 = **₦2,400,000,000** a year.

**Worked example 2 (weekly data).** 20,000 households buy 2 tins of milk a week at ₦1,500. Weekly = 20,000 x 2 x ₦1,500 = ₦60,000,000. Annual (52 weeks) = **₦3,120,000,000**. Trap: forgetting to multiply by 52 (or by 12 for monthly data).

**Worked example 3 (market build-up).** A drinks distributor finds 250 restaurants in a state, each buying 30 crates a month at ₦5,000. Monthly = 250 x 30 x ₦5,000 = ₦37,500,000. Annual = x 12 = **₦450,000,000**.

**Worked example 4 (chain ratio).** A city has 2,000,000 people; 55% are adults; 30% of adults drink yoghurt; each drinks 24 cups a year. 2,000,000 x 0.55 = 1,100,000 adults; x 0.30 = 330,000 drinkers; x 24 = **7,920,000 cups** a year.

**Worked example 5 (BPI).** A state has 6% of national income, 4% of retail sales, 5% of population. BPI = 0.5(6) + 0.3(4) + 0.2(5) = 3 + 1.2 + 1 = 5.2%. If national potential is ₦200 billion, state potential = 0.052 x ₦200bn = **₦10.4 billion**.

**Worked example 6 (shares).**
- A paint firm sells ₦84m; the whole market (including it) sells ₦420m. Share = 84/420 x 100 = **20%**. Trap: dividing by sales of rivals only (84/336 = 25%) is wrong.
- Firm with 18% against a leader with 27%: relative share = 18/27 = **0.67** (2 d.p.). The leader's relative share = 27/18 = **1.5** (above 1 means you lead).
- Current demand 3m units, potential 12m: penetration = 3/12 x 100 = **25%** (a low index means room to grow).
- Market demand forecast ₦1.5 billion, expected share 8%: sales forecast = 0.08 x ₦1.5bn = **₦120 million**.

- Actual industry sales come from trade associations, government statistics and research firms; comparing your growth with the industry's tells you whether you are gaining or losing share.

### Lesson 9.4: Forecasting from what buyers, salespeople and experts say, and what buyers do

**Must know**

- Forecasts rest on 3 information bases: what people **say** (buyers' intentions, sales-force opinion, expert opinion), what people **do** (market test), what people **have done** (time-series and statistical analysis of past sales).
- Usual sequence: macroeconomic forecast, then industry forecast, then company sales forecast.

1. **Survey of buyers' intentions:** ask buyers what they plan to buy. Most useful when buyers are **few**, **cheap to reach**, have **clear intentions**, will **carry them out** and are **willing to disclose** them (strongest for industrial buyers and durables).
2. **Composite of sales-force opinions (sales-force composite):** each salesperson estimates sales in his or her **territory**; estimates are **added up** and adjusted by management.
3. **Expert opinion:** dealers, distributors, suppliers, consultants, trade associations. **Delphi method:** experts give estimates **anonymously**, receive a **summary** of the group's answers, and **revise over several rounds** until a **consensus** emerges.
4. **Market test method:** launch in a limited, representative area, record **actual purchases**, project to the full market. Best for **new products**, new channels or territories, or when buyers do not plan purchases.

**Explain more**

- **Sales-force composite strengths:** salespeople are closest to customers and spot trends; they trust quotas they helped set; estimates break down by product, territory, customer and salesperson. **Weaknesses:** bias (over-optimism or pessimism); salespeople may **deliberately underestimate so their quotas are set low**; they may not see wider economic developments.
- Buyers' intentions are weak when buyers are millions of scattered consumers who rarely plan, refuse to share plans, or cannot picture a brand-new product.
- An Ibadan-only launch of a new Lagos snack to project national sales is a **market test**, not a survey.

**Worked example 7 (buyers' intentions).** 100,000 households: 20% "definitely buy", 30% "probably buy". Past conversion: 70% of definites, 10% of probables. Definite buyers = 100,000 x 0.20 x 0.70 = 14,000. Probable buyers = 100,000 x 0.30 x 0.10 = 3,000. Expected buyers = **17,000**.

**Worked example 8 (sales-force composite).** Estimates 30,000 + 45,000 + 25,000 = 100,000 units. Management cuts 15% for over-optimism: 100,000 x 0.85 = **85,000 units**. (If the team usually underestimates, management raises the total instead.)

**Worked example 9 (weighted expert opinion).** Forecasts ₦100m, ₦140m, ₦120m with weights 0.2, 0.5, 0.3 (weights sum to 1). Forecast = 0.2(100) + 0.5(140) + 0.3(120) = 20 + 70 + 36 = **₦126 million**. A simple average would be ₦120m; adding the three (₦360m) is a trap.

**Worked example 10 (market test).** A test city holds 3% of the target population and sells 12,000 packs. National estimate = 12,000 / 0.03 = **400,000 packs**. Trap: multiplying by 3% instead of dividing.

### Lesson 9.5: Time-series analysis, moving averages and trend projection

**Must know**

- **Time-series analysis:** breaks past sales into **4 components**:

1. **Trend (T):** the long-run, underlying growth or decline (population, capital, technology).
2. **Cycle (C):** wave-like movement over **several years**, from general economic activity (booms, recessions).
3. **Season (S):** a **recurring pattern within the year** (weather, holidays, festivals).
4. **Erratic events (E):** **unpredictable** one-off shocks (strikes, fuel scarcity, riots, floods).

- **Elasticity is not a component.**
- **Simple moving average (SMA)** = sum of sales in the last n periods / n. Purpose: **smooth out short-term random fluctuations** so the underlying level is clearer.
- **Weighted moving average (WMA)** = sum of (weight x sales) / sum of weights; gives **different weights**, usually **more weight to recent** periods.
- **Trend projection:** assumes the **pattern of past sales will continue**. Trend line: Y = a + bX.
- **Least squares (X coded to sum to zero, e.g. -2, -1, 0, 1, 2):** a = sum of Y / n; b = sum of XY / sum of X^2. b is the **average yearly increase**.
- **Straight-line growth:** yearly increase = (last value - first value) / number of intervals.
- **Compound growth:** forecast = current sales x (1 + g)^t.
- **Statistical demand analysis:** statistical procedures (e.g. multiple regression) that find the real factors affecting sales (price, income, population, promotion) and their relative influence.

**Explain more**

- Umbrella sales rising every April to July in Lagos = **seasonal**. A two-week ticket slump from a sudden fuel scarcity = **erratic**. A recession that lowers car sales for three years = **cyclical**.

**Worked example 11 (SMA).** Monthly sales: 30, 34, 38, 36, 41 (thousand). 3-month SMA forecast = (38 + 36 + 41) / 3 = 115 / 3 = **38.33 thousand** (2 d.p.). Use only the **last** n periods.

**Worked example 12 (WMA).** Last three months (oldest first): 200, 220, 260; weights 1, 2, 3 (3 on the most recent). Forecast = (1x200 + 2x220 + 3x260) / (1 + 2 + 3) = (200 + 440 + 780) / 6 = 1,420 / 6 = **236.67 units** (2 d.p.). Trap: dividing by 3 instead of 6.

**Worked example 13 (given trend line).** Y = 120 + 8X, X = 1 for 2020. For 2027, X = 8 (2027 - 2020 + 1). Y = 120 + 8 x 8 = **184**.

**Worked example 14 (least squares).** Sales 2021 to 2025: 10, 13, 15, 18, 19 (₦m); X = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2.
- Sum of Y = 75; a = 75 / 5 = 15.
- XY: -20, -13, 0, 18, 38; sum = 23. Sum of X^2 = 4 + 1 + 0 + 1 + 4 = 10. b = 23 / 10 = 2.3.
- 2026 is X = 3: Y = 15 + 2.3 x 3 = **₦21.9 million**.
- (With an even number of years, code X as -3, -1, 1, 3, so each year is 2 units of X.)

**Worked example 15 (straight-line growth).** ₦60m in 2020, ₦90m in 2025. Intervals = 5 (not 6). Increase = 30 / 5 = ₦6m a year. 2026 = 90 + 6 = **₦96 million**.

**Worked example 16 (compound growth).** ₦80m now, 5% a year. In two years = 80 x 1.05^2 = 80 x 1.1025 = **₦88.2 million**. Trap: simple growth (80 + 4 + 4 = ₦88m) is wrong for compound growth.

### One-minute recap

- Market levels: potential, available (interest, income, access), qualified available, target (served), penetrated.
- Market demand = total volume bought by a defined group, area, time, environment and programme; market minimum = sales with no marketing spend; market potential = limit as industry spend approaches infinity.
- Primary demand = product class; selective = one brand.
- Company demand = share x market demand; sales forecast = expected sales under the chosen plan; sales potential = limit as company effort rises relative to rivals.
- Quota is set above, budget below, the forecast.
- Q = n x q x p; market build-up adds up all buyers; chain ratio multiplies a base by percentages; BPI = 0.5 income + 0.3 retail sales + 0.2 population.
- Market share = own sales / total market; relative share = own share / largest rival's; penetration = current demand / potential.
- Methods: buyers' intentions, sales-force composite, expert opinion (Delphi), market test, time series, statistical demand analysis.
- Time series: trend, cycle, season, erratic; SMA smooths, WMA weights recent periods more; least squares a = sum Y / n, b = sum XY / sum X^2.

### Theory practice

**Question:** In a city of 800,000 adults, 25% drink yoghurt and each drinker buys 40 cups a year at ₦500. (a) What is the annual market potential in naira? (b) If a firm expects a 15% share, what is its sales forecast?

**Solution:**
- (a) Drinkers = 800,000 x 0.25 = 200,000. Cups = 200,000 x 40 = 8,000,000. Value = 8,000,000 x ₦500 = **₦4,000,000,000** (₦4 billion). This is the chain-ratio method combined with Q = n x q x p.
- (b) Sales forecast = 0.15 x ₦4bn = **₦600 million**.
