## Topic 8: Probability Distributions

**What you must know:** discrete versus continuous distributions; Bernoulli and discrete uniform; binomial (conditions, formula, mean, variance, probabilities); Poisson (assumptions, formula, mean = variance, changing the interval, approximating the binomial); continuous uniform and exponential; the normal curve, the standard normal, z-scores, table look-ups, the empirical rule, reverse problems and the normal approximation with continuity correction.

### Lesson 8.1: Discrete and continuous distributions; Bernoulli and discrete uniform

**Must know**

- **Probability distribution:** a list, table, formula or curve giving every value of a random variable with its probability.
- **Discrete distribution:** the variable takes separate, countable values (0, 1, 2, ...). Probabilities sit on exact points, P(X = x), and they add up to 1. Examples: Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, discrete uniform.
- **Continuous distribution:** the variable can take **any value within a specified interval** (time, weight, height, revenue). It is described by a **probability density function (pdf), f(x)**. Examples: continuous uniform, exponential, normal.
- **3 properties of a pdf:**
  1. f(x) is never negative.
  2. The **total area under the curve is equal to 1**.
  3. Probability = area under the curve between two values; P(X = any exact value) = 0.
- **Bernoulli trial:** a single random experiment with exactly **two possible outcomes**, called success and failure. X = 1 for success, X = 0 for failure.
  - Mean = p. Variance = p(1 − p) = pq, where q = 1 − p.
- **Discrete uniform distribution (values 1 to n):** every value equally likely.
  - P(X = x) = 1/n. Mean = (n + 1)/2. Variance = (n² − 1)/12.

| Feature | Discrete | Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Values | Countable points | Any value in an interval |
| Probability of one exact value | Can be above 0 | Always 0 |
| Total | Sum of probabilities = 1 | Area under f(x) = 1 |
| Examples | Binomial, Poisson, discrete uniform | Normal, continuous uniform, exponential |

**Explain more**

- The Bernoulli trial is named after Jacob Bernoulli. Example: a customer entering a Yaba electronics shop buys with p = 0.3. Variance of X = 0.3 × 0.7 = **0.21** (not 0.3, which is the mean).
- **Fair die:** n = 6. Each face has probability 1/6. Mean = (6 + 1)/2 = 3.5. Variance = (6² − 1)/12 = 35/12 = **2.9167** (4 d.p.). The number shown on one roll of a fair die is the classic discrete uniform variable; the number of heads in 10 tosses is binomial, a student's height is continuous, accidents per day are Poisson.
- **Raffle:** tickets numbered 1 to 10, each with probability 1/10. P(number ≤ 4) = 4 × 1/10 = **0.4**.
- Traps: a pdf need **not** be symmetrical, can **never** be negative, and continuous outcomes are **not** restricted to positive integers.

### Lesson 8.2: The binomial distribution

**Must know**

- **Use it for:** the number of successes in n independent trials, each with the same probability of success.
- **4 conditions of a binomial experiment:**
  1. A **fixed number of trials, n**, decided in advance.
  2. Each trial has **exactly two outcomes** (success or failure).
  3. The probability of success **p is the same** on every trial (q = 1 − p).
  4. The trials are **independent** of one another.
- **Formula:** P(X = x) = nCx × p^x × q^(n − x), for x = 0, 1, 2, ..., n, where nCx = n! ÷ [x!(n − x)!].
- **Mean = np. Variance = np(1 − p) = npq. Standard deviation = √(npq).**
- **Shape:** symmetric when **p = 0.5**; positively skewed when p < 0.5; negatively skewed when p > 0.5.
- **Working backwards:** q = variance ÷ mean, then p = 1 − q, then n = mean ÷ p.

| Phrase | Meaning | How to compute |
|---|---|---|
| exactly k | X = k | formula once |
| at most k | X ≤ k | P(0) + ... + P(k) |
| at least k | X ≥ k | 1 − P(X ≤ k − 1) |
| fewer than k | X < k | P(0) + ... + P(k − 1) |
| more than k | X > k | 1 − P(X ≤ k) |
| at least one | X ≥ 1 | 1 − P(0) |

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (magazine sales):** p = 0.2, n = 6, so q = 0.8.

- P(0) = 6C0 × 0.2⁰ × 0.8⁶ = 1 × 1 × 0.262144 = **0.262**.
- P(1) = 6 × 0.2 × 0.8⁵ = 6 × 0.2 × 0.32768 = 0.393216.
- P(2) = 15 × 0.2² × 0.8⁴ = 15 × 0.04 × 0.4096 = 0.24576.
- P(3) = 20 × 0.2³ × 0.8³ = 20 × 0.008 × 0.512 = **0.08192 ≈ 0.082**.
- At least 3 = 1 − [P(0) + P(1) + P(2)] = 1 − 0.90112 = **0.09888 ≈ 0.099**.
- At most 3 = 0.90112 + 0.08192 = **0.98304 ≈ 0.983**.

**Worked example 2 (8% defective, n = 10):** q = 0.92.

- P(0) = 0.92¹⁰ = 0.434388. P(1) = 10 × 0.08 × 0.92⁹ = 0.377729.
- P(at most 1) = 0.434388 + 0.377729 = **0.8121** (4 d.p.).
- P(2) = 45 × 0.08² × 0.92⁸ = 45 × 0.0064 × 0.513219 = **0.1478**.
- Mean = 10 × 0.08 = **0.8**. SD = √(10 × 0.08 × 0.92) = √0.736 = **0.858** (0.736 is the variance, not the SD).

**More quick ones (check each):**

- Sales agent in Surulere, p = 0.4, n = 6: P(2) = 15 × 0.16 × 0.6⁴ = 15 × 0.16 × 0.1296 = **0.3110**.
- Mushin bakery, 10% cracked, n = 8: P(none) = 0.9⁸ = **0.4305**.
- POS failures at Balogun, p = 0.2, n = 5: P(at least one) = 1 − 0.8⁵ = 1 − 0.32768 = **0.6723**.
- Bulbs, p = 0.05, n = 10: P(0) = 0.95¹⁰ = 0.598737; P(1) = 10 × 0.05 × 0.95⁹ = 0.315125; P(at most 1) = **0.9139**.
- UNILAG student, p = 0.6, n = 5: P(4) = 5 × 0.6⁴ × 0.4 = 0.2592; P(5) = 0.6⁵ = 0.07776; P(at least 4) = **0.3370**.
- Four generators, p = 0.9: P(all 4) = 0.9⁴ = **0.6561**.
- Means and spreads: n = 10, p = 0.3 gives mean **3**. Loan rejections in Ibadan, n = 20, p = 0.15: mean = **3**. n = 50, p = 0.4: variance = 50 × 0.4 × 0.6 = **12**. n = 100, p = 0.2: SD = √16 = **4**. Loyalty cards, n = 40, p = 0.3: mean = 12, SD = √8.4 = **2.8983**.
- **Backwards:** mean 6, variance 4.2. q = 4.2 ÷ 6 = 0.7, p = 0.3, n = 6 ÷ 0.3 = 20. So **n = 20, p = 0.3**.
- Traps: variance is np(1 − p), **not** √[np(1 − p)] (the SD) or p(1 − p) (one Bernoulli trial). Counting underfilled bottles in a sample of 20 at a Lagos bottling plant is binomial (fixed n). "Counted over a continuous interval of time" is a Poisson feature, **not** a binomial condition.

### Lesson 8.3: The Poisson distribution

**Must know**

- **Use it for:** the **number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space** (calls per 10 minutes, claims per day, errors per page, breakdowns per day). Named after Siméon Denis Poisson.
- **4 assumptions:**
  1. Events occur at random and **independently** of one another.
  2. The **average rate (λ) is constant** over the interval.
  3. The probability of an occurrence is **proportional to the length of the interval**.
  4. Two events cannot happen at exactly the same instant.
- **Formula:** P(X = x) = e^(−λ) × λ^x ÷ x!, for x = 0, 1, 2, ... (e ≈ 2.71828).
- **Mean = variance = λ** (the distinctive property). SD = √λ.
- **Changing the interval:** scale λ in proportion. New λ = rate × length of new interval.
- **Poisson approximation to the binomial:** when **n is large and p is small**, use λ = np.

| | Binomial | Poisson |
|---|---|---|
| Parameters | n and p | λ only |
| Values | 0 to n | 0, 1, 2, ... no upper limit |
| Mean | np | λ |
| Variance | npq (less than the mean) | λ (equal to the mean) |
| Fixed number of trials? | Yes | No |

**Explain more**

**Worked example (insurance claims, λ = 5.1 per day):**

- P(0) = e^(−5.1) = 0.006097.
- P(1) = e^(−5.1) × 5.1 = 0.031093.
- P(2) = e^(−5.1) × 5.1² ÷ 2! = 0.006097 × 26.01 ÷ 2 = **0.079288 ≈ 0.079**.
- Fewer than 2 = P(0) + P(1) = **0.037190 ≈ 0.037**.
- 2 or more = 1 − 0.037190 = **0.962810 ≈ 0.963**.
- More than 2 = 1 − [P(0) + P(1) + P(2)] = 1 − 0.116478 = **0.883522 ≈ 0.884**.

"2 or more" includes 2; "more than 2" does not.

**More quick ones:**

- BRT breakdowns, λ = 2: P(0) = e^(−2) = **0.1353**.
- Typing errors, λ = 2: P(3) = 0.135335 × 8 ÷ 6 = **0.1804**.
- Surulere power outages, λ = 3: P(2) = e^(−3) × 9 ÷ 2 = 0.049787 × 4.5 = **0.2240**.
- Ketu pharmacy, λ = 1.5: P(0) = 0.223130, P(1) = 0.334695, P(2) = 0.251021. At most 2 = **0.8088**.
- Hostel complaints, λ = 0.8: P(at least one) = 1 − e^(−0.8) = 1 − 0.449329 = **0.5507**.
- **Scaling:** Ojota filling station, 6 cars per hour. For 20 minutes, λ = 6 × 20/60 = 2. P(1) = e^(−2) × 2 = **0.2707**. Oshodi business, 3 complaints per day; for a 5-day week λ = 3 × 5 = **15**.
- Variance 4 means mean 4 and SD √4 = **2**.
- **Approximation:** 200 parcels, p = 0.01. λ = 200 × 0.01 = 2. P(1) = 2e^(−2) = **0.2707**.
- Traps: a fixed number of trials n with probability p is the **binomial** set-up, **not** a Poisson assumption. Poisson variance is λ, not λ² or √λ. Calls arriving randomly at an Ikeja call centre at a constant average rate are Poisson, not exponential (exponential is the **time between** calls).

### Lesson 8.4: Continuous uniform and exponential distributions

**Must know**

- **Continuous uniform on a to b** (rectangular distribution): every interval of equal length is equally likely.
  - pdf height: f(x) = 1/(b − a); the graph is a **rectangle** of height 1/(b − a).
  - Mean = (a + b)/2. Variance = (b − a)²/12. SD = √variance.
  - P(c < X < d) = (d − c)/(b − a).
- **Exponential distribution:** models the **time (or distance) between events** in a Poisson process. If arrivals per hour are Poisson with rate λ, the time between arrivals is exponential.
  - Mean = 1/λ. Variance = 1/λ². P(X > t) = e^(−λt).
  - **Memoryless property:** the probability of waiting a further t minutes does not depend on how long you have already waited.
  - Shape: highest at 0 and decreasing steadily (positively skewed).

**Explain more**

- Registry processing time, uniform 10 to 30 minutes: height = 1/(30 − 10) = 1/20 = **0.05**.
- Kiosk sales, uniform ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 (20 to 50 thousand): mean = (20 + 50)/2 = **35**, that is ₦35,000.
- Uniform 0 to 12: variance = (12 − 0)²/12 = 144/12 = **12**.
- Uniform 2 to 8: variance = 6²/12 = 3, SD = √3 = **1.7321**.
- Akoka to Yaba, uniform 10 to 40 minutes: P(15 < X < 25) = (25 − 15)/(40 − 10) = 10/30 = **0.3333**.
- Keke wait, uniform 0 to 20 minutes: P(X > 15) = (20 − 15)/20 = **0.25**.
- Bank counter, 4 arrivals per hour: mean time between arrivals = 1/4 hour = **15 minutes**.
- Lekki toll gate: arrivals per hour Poisson, so time between arrivals is **exponential**.
- Traps: "every value equally likely" is uniform; "mean = variance" is Poisson; neither describes the exponential.

### Lesson 8.5: The normal distribution and z-scores

**Must know**

- **Normal (Gaussian) distribution**, linked with Carl Friedrich Gauss: the most important continuous distribution.
- **Properties:**
  1. **Bell-shaped**.
  2. **Symmetric** about its mean (not skewed, so a long right tail is not a property).
  3. **Mean = median = mode**, at the centre.
  4. **Asymptotic:** the tails get closer and closer to the horizontal axis but never touch it.
  5. Total area under the curve = 1; **0.5 lies on each side of the mean**.
  6. **Completely determined by its mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ)**.
  7. Mesokurtic (kurtosis = 3).
- **Larger σ, same mean:** the curve is **lower and more spread out**. Changing μ only shifts the curve.
- **Empirical rule (68-95-99.7):** about **68%** of values lie within 1 SD of the mean, **95%** within 2 SD, **99.7%** within 3 SD.
- **Standard normal distribution:** **mean 0, standard deviation 1**, variable Z.
- **z-score:** z = (x − μ) ÷ σ. It tells you **how many standard deviations** an observation lies **above (z > 0) or below (z < 0) the mean**.
- **Reverse formula:** x = μ + zσ.

**Explain more**

- Marks with mean 60 and SD 8: a mark of 72 has z = (72 − 60)/8 = 12/8 = **1.5**. The answer is 1.5, not 12 (12 is the raw difference in marks).
- An ACS exam z-score of −0.75 means **0.75 standard deviations below the mean**, not 0.75 marks below.
- **Empirical rule examples:** IQ with mean 100, SD 15: 70 to 130 is mean ± 2 SD, so about **95%**. Above mean + 1 SD: (100% − 68%)/2 = **16%** (not 32%, which is both tails together, and not 34%, which is mean to +1 SD).
- Any value above the mean, e.g. courier time above 47 minutes when μ = 47: probability **0.5000**. The area left of the mean is also **0.5**.
- Traps: 99.7% is within 3 SD, not 1 SD. The curve is not J-shaped, discrete or skewed.

### Lesson 8.6: Finding normal probabilities and the normal approximation

**Must know**

- **Method:** convert x to z, sketch and shade, read the table, add or subtract areas.
- **Two kinds of table** (questions give either):
  - **Cumulative:** P(Z < z). Then P(Z > z) = 1 − P(Z < z), and P(a < Z < b) = P(Z < b) − P(Z < a). For negative z, P(Z < −z) = 1 − P(Z < z).
  - **Mean-to-z:** P(0 < Z < z) = A. Then P(Z < z) = 0.5 + A and P(Z > z) = 0.5 − A for positive z. Between two z's on opposite sides of 0, add the areas; on the same side, subtract.
- **Reverse problems:** find z from the table, then x = μ + zσ.
- **Normal approximation to the binomial:** works when **n is large and p is not too close to 0 or 1** (rule of thumb: np ≥ 5 and nq ≥ 5). Use μ = np and σ = √(npq), with a **continuity correction** of 0.5:

| Binomial | Normal with correction |
|---|---|
| P(X ≤ 10) | P(Y ≤ 10.5) |
| P(X < 10) | P(Y ≤ 9.5) |
| P(X ≥ 10) | P(Y ≥ 9.5) |
| P(X > 10) | P(Y ≥ 10.5) |
| P(X = 10) | P(9.5 ≤ Y ≤ 10.5) |

**Explain more**

**Cumulative-table examples (courier, μ = 47, σ = 10):**

- Between 45 and 51: z₁ = (45 − 47)/10 = −0.2; z₂ = (51 − 47)/10 = 0.4. P = 0.6554 − 0.4207 = **0.2347**.
- Less than 48: z = (48 − 47)/10 = 0.1. P(Z < 0.1) = **0.5398**.

**Lagos supermarket revenue, μ = ₦5.2m, σ = ₦0.6m:**

- More than ₦6.0m: z = (6.0 − 5.2)/0.6 = 1.3333. P = 1 − 0.9088 = **0.0912**.
- Between ₦4.5m and ₦5.8m: z₁ = (4.5 − 5.2)/0.6 = −1.1667; z₂ = (5.8 − 5.2)/0.6 = 1.00. P(Z < −1.1667) = 1 − 0.8783 = 0.1217. P = 0.8413 − 0.1217 = **0.7196**.

**Mean-to-z examples:**

- Garri bags, μ = 50 kg, σ = 4 kg, more than 55 kg: z = 5/4 = 1.25. P = 0.5 − 0.3944 = **0.1056**.
- Phone bills, μ = ₦5,000, σ = ₦1,000, less than ₦5,800: z = 800/1,000 = 0.8. P = 0.5 + 0.2881 = **0.7881**.
- Factory output, μ = 70, σ = 10, between 60 and 90: z = −1 and 2 (opposite sides), so P = 0.3413 + 0.4772 = **0.8185**.
- Electricity, μ = 500 kWh, σ = 50 kWh, less than 425: z = −75/50 = −1.5. P = 0.5 − 0.4332 = **0.0668**.
- Deliveries, μ = 80, σ = 5, between 85 and 90: z = 1 and 2 (same side), so P = 0.4772 − 0.3413 = **0.1359**.
- Bulbs, μ = 800 hours, σ = 40, n = 1,000, more than 860 hours: z = 60/40 = 1.5. P = 0.5 − 0.4332 = 0.0668. Expected number = 1,000 × 0.0668 = 66.8, about **67**.

**Reverse examples:**

- Top 5% in an aptitude test, μ = 200, σ = 20, z = 1.645: x = 200 + 1.645 × 20 = 200 + 32.9 = **232.9**.
- Shortest-lasting 10% of batteries, μ = 50, σ = 5, z = −1.28: x = 50 − 1.28 × 5 = 50 − 6.4 = **43.6 hours**. The negative z gives a value below the mean.

- Approximation traps: n large and p **very small** calls for the **Poisson**, not the normal. For P(X ≤ 10) the correction adds 0.5 to give **10.5**; 9.5 would be used for P(X < 10).

### One-minute recap

- Discrete: probabilities on points summing to 1. Continuous: any value in an interval; area under f(x) = 1; P(X = exact value) = 0.
- Bernoulli: mean p, variance pq. Discrete uniform 1 to n: mean (n + 1)/2, variance (n² − 1)/12.
- Binomial: fixed n, two outcomes, constant p, independent trials; mean np, variance npq; symmetric at p = 0.5.
- Poisson: events per fixed interval; mean = variance = λ; scale λ with the interval; approximates binomial when n large, p small.
- Continuous uniform: height 1/(b − a), mean (a + b)/2, variance (b − a)²/12. Exponential: time between Poisson events, mean 1/λ, memoryless.
- Normal: bell-shaped, symmetric, asymptotic, mean = median = mode, fixed by μ and σ; 68-95-99.7.
- z = (x − μ)/σ; x = μ + zσ; standard normal has mean 0, SD 1.
- Normal approximation: n large, p not near 0 or 1; continuity correction of 0.5.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Faults on an internet provider's Lagos network occur at an average of 2.5 per week, following a Poisson distribution. Find the probability of at most 1 fault in a week, and state the variance of the weekly number of faults.

*Answer:* λ = 2.5. P(0) = e^(−2.5) = 0.082085. P(1) = e^(−2.5) × 2.5 = 0.205212. P(at most 1) = 0.082085 + 0.205212 = **0.2873** (4 d.p.). For a Poisson distribution variance = mean, so the variance is **2.5**.
