## Topic 9: Break-even (Cost-Volume-Profit) Analysis

**What you must know:** the concept of CVP and break-even; contribution and the C/S (P/V) ratio; break-even point in units and naira; margin of safety; target profit, with and without tax; effect of changes in price and cost; degree of operating leverage; break-even, contribution and profit-volume charts; importance, assumptions and limitations; decision problems (make or buy, indifference point, limiting factor, special orders).

### Lesson 9.1: The concept, contribution and the C/S (P/V) ratio

**Must know**

- **Cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis:** the study of how changes in cost, selling price and volume of activity affect profit. It rests on the split of costs into fixed and variable (marginal costing).
- **Break-even analysis:** a technique which uses cost behaviour to identify the activity level at which there is **neither profit nor loss**. It is the best-known part of CVP analysis.
- **Break-even point (BEP):** the level of activity at which **total revenue equals total cost**. At BEP, profit = 0 and **contribution = fixed cost**.
- **Contribution:** sales minus variable cost. It first "contributes" to covering fixed costs; whatever is left is profit.

Key formulas:

- Contribution per unit = Selling price per unit − Variable cost per unit
- Total contribution = Sales − Total variable cost = Fixed cost + Profit
- Profit = Total contribution − Fixed cost
- Sales = Variable cost + Fixed cost + Profit = Variable cost + Contribution
- **C/S ratio** (contribution to sales ratio, also called **P/V ratio**, profit-volume ratio, or contribution margin ratio) = Contribution ÷ Sales (per unit or in total; × 100 for a percentage)
- P/V ratio from two periods = Change in profit ÷ Change in sales
- Fixed cost = (Sales × C/S ratio) − Profit

What changes the P/V ratio:

| Change | Effect on P/V ratio | Effect on BEP |
|---|---|---|
| Selling price rises | Rises | Falls |
| Selling price falls | Falls | Rises |
| Variable cost per unit falls | Rises | Falls |
| Variable cost per unit rises | Falls | Rises |
| Fixed cost rises or falls | **No effect** | Rises / falls |

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (contribution and C/S ratio).** An Aba shoe factory sells a pair of shoes for ₦5,000. Variable cost is ₦3,000 a pair. Fixed costs are ₦1,200,000 a month and 900 pairs are sold.

1. Contribution per pair = ₦5,000 − ₦3,000 = **₦2,000**.
2. C/S ratio = ₦2,000 ÷ ₦5,000 = **0.40 (40%)**. Every ₦1 of sales brings 40 kobo of contribution.
3. Total contribution = 900 × ₦2,000 = ₦1,800,000.
4. Profit = ₦1,800,000 − ₦1,200,000 = **₦600,000**.

*Trap:* if a question also gives "fixed cost per unit", ignore it when working out contribution and the C/S ratio. Contribution uses only selling price and variable cost. For example, price ₦4.50, variable cost ₦2.50, fixed cost ₦5 a unit: contribution = ₦2.00 and C/S ratio = 2 ÷ 4.5 = 0.44 (to 2 decimal places). The ₦5 does not enter either figure.

**Worked example 2 (rearranging the contribution equation).** A firm earns a profit of ₦45,000. Total contribution is ₦120,000 and variable cost is ₦180,000.

1. Fixed cost = Contribution − Profit = ₦120,000 − ₦45,000 = **₦75,000**.
2. Sales = Variable cost + Contribution = ₦180,000 + ₦120,000 = **₦300,000**.

*Trap:* the options often include Contribution + Profit or Variable cost + Profit. Stick to the two equations above.

**Worked example 3 (P/V ratio from two periods, including a loss).** A Lagos printing firm reports: March sales ₦1,000,000, loss ₦200,000; April sales ₦3,000,000, profit ₦600,000.

1. Change in sales = ₦3,000,000 − ₦1,000,000 = ₦2,000,000.
2. Change in profit = ₦600,000 − (−₦200,000) = ₦800,000. A loss is a negative profit, so moving from a loss to a profit **adds** the two figures.
3. P/V ratio = ₦800,000 ÷ ₦2,000,000 = **40%**.
4. Fixed cost = (₦3,000,000 × 40%) − ₦600,000 = ₦1,200,000 − ₦600,000 = **₦600,000**.
5. Check with March: ₦1,000,000 × 40% = ₦400,000 contribution; ₦400,000 − ₦600,000 = −₦200,000 loss. Correct.

This works because fixed cost is the same in both periods, so any change in profit is a change in contribution. The same idea answers: "Sales rise from ₦40,000 to ₦60,000 and profit rises by ₦5,000": P/V = ₦5,000 ÷ ₦20,000 = 25%.

### Lesson 9.2: Break-even point and margin of safety

**Must know**

- BEP (units) = Fixed cost ÷ Contribution per unit
- BEP (₦ sales) = Fixed cost ÷ C/S ratio = BEP units × Selling price
- **Margin of safety (MOS):** the difference between actual (or budgeted) sales and break-even sales. It shows how far sales can fall before a loss is made.
- MOS (units) = Actual or budgeted units − BEP units
- MOS (₦) = Actual or budgeted sales − BEP sales
- MOS % = MOS ÷ Actual or budgeted sales × 100
- Profit = MOS (₦) × C/S ratio = MOS (units) × Contribution per unit
- Working backwards:
  1. Fixed cost = BEP units × Contribution per unit = BEP sales × C/S ratio
  2. BEP sales = Actual sales × (1 − MOS %)
  3. BEP units = BEP sales ÷ Selling price

**Explain more**

**Worked example 4 (BEP and MOS).** A Kano flour mill sells a bag of flour for ₦40,000. Variable cost is ₦28,000 a bag. Fixed costs are ₦18,000,000 a year. Budgeted sales are 2,000 bags.

1. Contribution = ₦40,000 − ₦28,000 = ₦12,000; C/S ratio = 12,000 ÷ 40,000 = 30%.
2. BEP units = ₦18,000,000 ÷ ₦12,000 = **1,500 bags**.
3. BEP sales = ₦18,000,000 ÷ 0.30 = **₦60,000,000** (check: 1,500 × ₦40,000 = ₦60,000,000).
4. MOS = 2,000 − 1,500 = **500 bags**, or 500 × ₦40,000 = **₦20,000,000**.
5. MOS % = 500 ÷ 2,000 = **25%**.
6. Profit = ₦20,000,000 × 30% = ₦6,000,000 (check: 2,000 × ₦12,000 − ₦18,000,000 = ₦6,000,000).

When you are given total sales and total variable cost instead of unit figures, find the C/S ratio from the totals. Example: expected sales ₦600,000, variable costs ₦300,000, fixed costs ₦177,500. C/S = (600,000 − 300,000) ÷ 600,000 = 50%; BEP sales = ₦177,500 ÷ 0.5 = **₦355,000**.

**Worked example 5 (fixed cost given per unit).** A Port Harcourt paint maker sells a tin for ₦800. Variable production cost is ₦300 and variable selling cost ₦100 a tin. Fixed production cost is ₦150 a tin and fixed selling cost ₦50 a tin, based on budgeted output of 20,000 tins.

1. Variable cost = ₦300 + ₦100 = ₦400; contribution = ₦800 − ₦400 = ₦400. Variable **selling** cost is still variable: include it.
2. Total fixed cost = (₦150 + ₦50) × 20,000 = **₦4,000,000**. Fixed cost per unit is only an average at the budgeted output; turn it into a total first.
3. BEP = ₦4,000,000 ÷ ₦400 = **10,000 tins**.

*Trap:* never deduct fixed cost per unit from price to get "contribution". That gives profit per unit at one output only.

**Worked example 6 (working backwards).** A firm sells at ₦50 a unit with a C/S ratio of 40%. Weekly break-even sales are ₦45,000.

1. BEP units = ₦45,000 ÷ ₦50 = 900 units.
2. Contribution per unit = 40% × ₦50 = ₦20.
3. Fixed cost = ₦45,000 × 40% = ₦18,000 (or 900 × ₦20).
4. Profit when 1,500 units are sold = 1,500 × ₦20 − ₦18,000 = ₦12,000 (or MOS 600 units × ₦20).

Other backward routes: if BEP is 6,000 units, price ₦90 and variable cost ₦40, then fixed cost = 6,000 × ₦50 = ₦300,000. If sales are ₦5,000,000 and MOS is 30%, BEP sales = ₦5,000,000 × 70% = ₦3,500,000; with a C/S ratio of 40%, profit = ₦1,500,000 × 40% = ₦600,000.

*Rounding:* when BEP is not a whole number (e.g. 10,909.09), exam options normally round to the nearest unit (10,909). Strictly, the firm must sell the next whole unit to avoid any loss, so state your rounding.

### Lesson 9.3: Target profit, tax, changes in price and cost, operating leverage

**Must know**

- Units for target profit = (Fixed cost + Target profit) ÷ Contribution per unit
- Sales (₦) for target profit = (Fixed cost + Target profit) ÷ C/S ratio
- **Effect of tax:** CVP formulas need profit **before** tax. Required profit before tax = Profit after tax ÷ (1 − tax rate). Then units = (Fixed cost + Profit before tax) ÷ Contribution per unit.
- **Changes in price or cost:** recompute the new contribution per unit and the new fixed cost, then apply the formulas again. To keep the same profit: new units = (New fixed cost + Original profit) ÷ New contribution per unit.
- % change in volume = (New units − Original units) ÷ Original units × 100
- **Degree of operating leverage (DOL)** = Contribution ÷ Operating profit (profit before interest and tax). It also equals 1 ÷ MOS % (as a decimal). A DOL of 2.5 means a 10% rise in sales gives a 25% rise in profit.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 7 (target profit and tax).** An Ibadan furniture maker sells a chair for ₦60,000. Variable cost is ₦36,000 a chair; fixed costs are ₦4,800,000 a year. Tax rate is 30%.

1. Contribution = ₦24,000; C/S ratio = 24,000 ÷ 60,000 = 40%.
2. BEP = ₦4,800,000 ÷ ₦24,000 = 200 chairs.
3. Target profit ₦2,400,000 (before tax): units = (₦4,800,000 + ₦2,400,000) ÷ ₦24,000 = ₦7,200,000 ÷ ₦24,000 = **300 chairs**.
4. Target profit **after tax** ₦2,100,000: profit before tax = ₦2,100,000 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = ₦3,000,000.
5. Units = (₦4,800,000 + ₦3,000,000) ÷ ₦24,000 = **325 chairs**; sales value = ₦7,800,000 ÷ 0.40 = ₦19,500,000 (check: 325 × ₦60,000).

*Trap:* dividing ₦2,100,000 by ₦24,000 directly, or multiplying by 1.3 instead of dividing by 0.7, gives a wrong option. ₦2,100,000 × 1.3 = ₦2,730,000, not ₦3,000,000.

When the variable cost is given as a list (materials, labour, variable overheads, direct expenses), add them all to get variable cost per unit first. Example: price ₦130; materials ₦22, labour ₦15, variable overheads ₦3, direct expenses ₦10 = ₦50; contribution ₦80; fixed cost ₦1,600,000; target ₦250,000: units = ₦1,850,000 ÷ ₦80 = 23,125.

**Worked example 8 (changes in price and cost).** An Abeokuta soap maker sells at ₦500 a carton, variable cost ₦300, fixed cost ₦120 a carton based on budgeted sales of 10,000 cartons.

1. Original: fixed cost = ₦120 × 10,000 = ₦1,200,000; contribution = ₦200; profit = 10,000 × ₦200 − ₦1,200,000 = ₦800,000. BEP = ₦1,200,000 ÷ ₦200 = 6,000 cartons.
2. Price rises 10% to ₦550; variable cost rises 5% to ₦315. New contribution = ₦235.
3. Units to keep profit at ₦800,000 = (₦1,200,000 + ₦800,000) ÷ ₦235 = 8,510.6, about 8,511 cartons.
4. Change in volume = (8,510.6 − 10,000) ÷ 10,000 = **14.9% decrease** (to 1 decimal place).
5. If fixed cost also rises 20% to ₦1,440,000, new BEP = ₦1,440,000 ÷ ₦235 = 6,127.7, about **6,128 cartons**.

*Trap:* apply each percentage only to the item named. A 10% rise in "variable production cost" leaves variable selling cost unchanged; a 25% rise in "fixed production cost" leaves fixed selling cost unchanged.

**Worked example 9 (DOL).** In the original budget of example 8: contribution ₦2,000,000, profit ₦800,000.

1. DOL = ₦2,000,000 ÷ ₦800,000 = **2.5**.
2. Check: MOS = (10,000 − 6,000) ÷ 10,000 = 40%; 1 ÷ 0.4 = 2.5.
3. A 10% rise in volume raises profit by 2.5 × 10% = 25% (₦800,000 to ₦1,000,000). High fixed costs mean high operating leverage and higher risk.

### Lesson 9.4: Break-even charts, P/V charts, importance, assumptions and limitations

**Must know**

**Break-even chart (single product):** a graph showing costs and revenue at different activity levels.

1. Horizontal axis: activity (units or sales value). Vertical axis: costs and revenue (₦).
2. **Fixed cost line:** horizontal, at the level of fixed cost.
3. **Total cost line:** starts at the fixed cost on the vertical axis and rises with variable cost.
4. **Total revenue (sales) line:** starts at the origin (zero sales, zero revenue).
5. **BEP:** where the revenue line crosses the total cost line. Area to the left = loss; to the right = profit.
6. **Margin of safety:** the distance on the horizontal axis between BEP and budgeted activity.
7. **Angle of incidence:** the angle at which the revenue line cuts the total cost line. A wide angle means profit grows fast once BEP is passed.

**Contribution chart:** like the break-even chart, but the **variable cost line** is drawn from the origin and fixed cost is added on top, so contribution shows as the gap between revenue and variable cost.

**Profit-volume (P/V) chart:** horizontal axis = sales or units; vertical axis = profit (above) and loss (below). One line starts at **−fixed cost** at zero sales and rises; it crosses the horizontal axis at BEP. The slope of the line is the P/V ratio.

**Importance (uses) of CVP analysis:**

1. Finds the break-even point and the margin of safety.
2. Shows the sales needed to earn a target profit.
3. Shows the effect on profit of changes in price, costs and volume.
4. Helps pricing decisions and setting selling prices in a downturn.
5. Helps decisions such as make or buy, special orders, product mix and choosing between production methods.
6. Helps budgeting and profit planning.
7. Presents cost and profit relationships simply to managers.

**Assumptions:**

1. All costs can be split into fixed and variable.
2. Fixed costs stay constant in total over the relevant range.
3. Variable cost per unit is constant (total variable cost is linear).
4. Selling price per unit is constant at all volumes.
5. Single product, or a constant sales mix.
6. Production equals sales (no change in stock).
7. Efficiency and productivity do not change.
8. Volume is the only factor that affects costs and revenue.

**Limitations:**

1. Costs are hard to split exactly into fixed and variable; semi-variable costs exist.
2. Fixed costs may step up at higher output.
3. Selling price often has to fall to sell more (discounts), so revenue is not a straight line.
4. Variable cost per unit changes (bulk-buying discounts, overtime).
5. Most firms sell several products and the mix changes.
6. Stock levels change, so production differs from sales.
7. It is valid only within the relevant range and is short-term.
8. It ignores other factors such as inflation, technology and capital employed.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 10 (reading a chart).** For the Kano flour mill in example 4, plot fixed cost at ₦18,000,000 (horizontal line). Total cost starts at ₦18m and at 2,000 bags reaches ₦18m + 2,000 × ₦28,000 = ₦74m. Revenue starts at zero and at 2,000 bags reaches ₦80m. The two lines cross at 1,500 bags (₦60m): the BEP. The gap from 1,500 to 2,000 bags is the MOS. On a P/V chart, the line starts at −₦18m and reaches +₦6m at ₦80m of sales, crossing zero at ₦60m.

*Traps:* the revenue line, not the total cost line, starts at the origin. On a P/V chart, the line starts at minus fixed cost, not at zero. A change in fixed cost moves the P/V line up or down without changing its slope; a change in price or variable cost changes the slope.

### Lesson 9.5: Decision-making problems

**Must know**

- **Basic rule:** in short-term decisions, compare **contribution** (or relevant variable cost). Fixed costs that do not change because of the decision are irrelevant.
- **Make or buy:** if fixed costs continue either way and there is spare capacity, buying is profitable only when the supplier's price is **lower than the variable (marginal) cost** of making. Compare with variable cost, not total cost including apportioned fixed overheads.
- **Indifference (cost) point:** the activity level at which two options cost the same.
  - Indifference point = Difference in fixed costs ÷ Difference in variable cost per unit
  - Below it, choose the option with lower fixed cost; above it, the one with lower variable cost.
- **Limiting (key) factor:** a scarce resource (labour hours, machine hours, materials) that limits output. Rank products by **contribution per unit of the limiting factor** and make the best first.
- **Special order:** accept if price is above variable cost, there is spare capacity, and regular sales and prices are not affected.
- **Dropping a product or closing a department:** keep it while it earns a positive contribution, unless its fixed costs would be saved by closing.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 11 (make or buy).** An Onitsha factory makes a component: materials ₦900, labour ₦600, variable overhead ₦300, so variable cost ₦1,800. Fixed overhead absorbed is ₦700, giving total cost ₦2,500. A supplier offers ₦2,100. Fixed overhead will not change.

1. Relevant comparison: variable cost ₦1,800 against buying price ₦2,100.
2. Making saves ₦2,100 − ₦1,800 = ₦300 a unit. **Make.**
3. *Trap:* comparing ₦2,100 with ₦2,500 wrongly suggests buying. The ₦700 is paid either way.

**Worked example 12 (indifference point).** A Lagos restaurant can buy rice packs from Supplier X at ₦3.50 a unit, or from Supplier Y at ₦3.20 a unit plus a fixed charge of ₦9,000.

1. Difference in fixed cost = ₦9,000 − 0 = ₦9,000.
2. Difference in variable cost = ₦3.50 − ₦3.20 = ₦0.30.
3. Indifference point = ₦9,000 ÷ ₦0.30 = **30,000 units**. Below 30,000 units choose X; above, choose Y.

The same formula chooses between production methods. Method A: fixed ₦2,000,000, variable ₦400; Method B: fixed ₦3,500,000, variable ₦250. Point = ₦1,500,000 ÷ ₦150 = 10,000 units (both cost ₦6,000,000 there).

**Worked example 13 (limiting factor).** A firm has 10,000 labour hours. Product A: contribution ₦600, 2 hours, demand 3,000 units. Product B: contribution ₦900, 4 hours, demand 2,000 units.

1. Contribution per hour: A = ₦600 ÷ 2 = ₦300; B = ₦900 ÷ 4 = ₦225. Rank A first, even though B has the higher contribution per unit.
2. Make 3,000 A using 6,000 hours. Remaining 4,000 hours ÷ 4 = 1,000 B.
3. Total contribution = 3,000 × ₦600 + 1,000 × ₦900 = **₦2,700,000**.

**Worked example 14 (special order).** A Lagos bakery with spare capacity is offered an order for 2,000 loaves at ₦700. Variable cost is ₦550 and full cost (with fixed overhead) is ₦850. Extra contribution = 2,000 × (₦700 − ₦550) = **₦300,000**, so accept, as long as regular customers do not demand the lower price.

### One-minute recap

- Break-even analysis uses cost behaviour to find the activity level with neither profit nor loss; at BEP, total revenue = total cost and contribution = fixed cost.
- Contribution = Sales − Variable cost = Fixed cost + Profit; ignore fixed cost per unit when finding contribution.
- C/S (P/V) ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales = Change in profit ÷ Change in sales; it rises if price rises or variable cost falls; fixed cost does not affect it.
- BEP units = Fixed cost ÷ Contribution per unit; BEP ₦ = Fixed cost ÷ C/S ratio.
- Margin of safety = Actual sales − BEP sales; Profit = MOS × C/S ratio.
- Target units = (Fixed cost + Target profit) ÷ Contribution per unit; after-tax target ÷ (1 − tax rate) first.
- DOL = Contribution ÷ Operating profit = 1 ÷ MOS %.
- Charts: revenue line from origin, total cost from fixed cost; P/V line from −fixed cost, slope = P/V ratio.
- Decisions: buy only if price < variable cost of making; indifference point = ΔFixed ÷ ΔVariable; rank by contribution per unit of limiting factor.

### Theory practice

**Question:** An Ilorin water factory sells a pack of bottled water for ₦1,500. Variable cost is ₦900 a pack and fixed costs are ₦3,600,000 a month. Budgeted sales are 9,000 packs a month. The tax rate is 30%. Calculate (a) the C/S ratio, (b) the break-even point in packs and naira, (c) the margin of safety in packs, naira and percentage, and (d) the packs needed to earn a profit after tax of ₦1,680,000.

*Solution:*
1. (a) Contribution = ₦1,500 − ₦900 = ₦600; C/S ratio = 600 ÷ 1,500 = **40%**.
2. (b) BEP = ₦3,600,000 ÷ ₦600 = **6,000 packs**; in naira, 6,000 × ₦1,500 = **₦9,000,000** (or ₦3,600,000 ÷ 0.40).
3. (c) MOS = 9,000 − 6,000 = **3,000 packs** = 3,000 × ₦1,500 = **₦4,500,000**; MOS % = 3,000 ÷ 9,000 = **33.3%** (to 1 decimal place).
4. (d) Profit before tax = ₦1,680,000 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = ₦2,400,000. Packs = (₦3,600,000 + ₦2,400,000) ÷ ₦600 = **10,000 packs**. (The budget of 9,000 packs earns only ₦1,800,000 before tax.)
