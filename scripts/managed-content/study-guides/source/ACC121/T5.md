## Topic 5: Labour Costing

**What you must know:** time and output records (clock cards, job cards, time sheets) and the standard hour; time rate, piece rate, guaranteed and differential piece rates and their effect on unit cost; premium bonus schemes (Halsey, Halsey-Weir, Rowan) and time saved; gross pay, payroll preparation, wages analysis and ghost workers; treatment of overtime premium, shift premium and idle time; labour turnover, its causes, costs and the three measurement methods.

### Lesson 5.1: Labour cost and time and output records

**Must know**

- **Direct labour** works on the product itself (an Aba shoe factory's stitchers); **indirect labour** supports production (storekeepers, supervisors) and is an overhead.
- **Time-keeping** records **attendance** (for payroll); **time-booking** records how that time is **spent** on jobs (for costing).
- **Main records:**
  1. **Clock card (attendance card):** stamped by a time-recording clock when the worker arrives and leaves; gives total hours **attended**, the basis for time-rate pay.
  2. **Time sheet (daily or weekly):** the worker's own record of time spent on various jobs.
  3. **Job card:** records the labour time spent on **one job**; it is then **costed and posted to the job cost sheet**.
  4. **Piece-work ticket (operation card):** records the **output** of a piece-rate worker, used to calculate piece-rate pay.
- **Standard hour:** a hypothetical hour representing the **amount of work that should be performed in one hour** under stated conditions. Output is often measured in standard hours.
- **Time saved = Time allowed (standard time) − Time taken.**
- **Idle time = clock-card hours − hours booked to jobs.**

**Explain more**

- The clock card shows how long a worker was **present**; the job card shows where that time **went**. Bin cards, stores requisitions and goods received notes are materials documents, not labour records.
- **Standard hour example:** at 20 units per standard hour, 300 units = 300 ÷ 20 = **15 standard hours**.
- **Time saved example:** standard time 0.75 hour per unit; a labourer works 30 hours and makes 60 units. Time allowed = 60 × 0.75 = 45 hours. Time saved = 45 − 30 = **15 hours**.

### Lesson 5.2: Time rate, piece rate and differential piece rates

**Must know**

- **2 major categories of remuneration:** **time-based** systems and **output-based (piece-rate)** systems. Premium bonus schemes combine the two.
- **Time rate (day rate):** pay = **Hours worked × Rate per hour**, whatever the output.
- **Straight piece rate:** pay = **Units produced × Rate per unit**.
- **Piece rate per unit = Hourly rate ÷ Standard output per hour.**
- **Standard output per hour = 60 minutes ÷ Standard minutes per unit.**
- **Piece rate with guaranteed day rate (guaranteed minimum):** the worker receives the **higher** of time-rate pay and piece-rate earnings.
- **Taylor's differential piece rate:** no minimum guarantee; **low rate = 83% of the normal piece rate** for output **below** standard, **high rate = 125% of the normal piece rate** at or above standard. Rewards the efficient and penalises the slow.
- **Merrick's multiple piece rate:** efficiency up to 83% = normal piece rate; above 83% up to 100% = **110%** of normal; above 100% = **120%** of normal. Slow workers are not penalised.
- **Efficiency % = Standard time for output ÷ Actual time taken × 100** (or actual output ÷ standard output × 100).

| Feature | Time rate | Piece rate |
|---|---|---|
| Pay depends on | Hours | Output |
| Labour cost per unit | Falls as output rises | Constant |
| Incentive to produce | None | Strong |

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (standard output, piece rate, effect on unit cost).** A Lagos plastics factory pays ₦50 per hour; standard time is 12 minutes per unit. In a 40-hour week Bambi makes 66 units, Feranmi 166, Elsie 200 and Tifeh 220.

1. Standard output per hour = 60 ÷ 12 = **5 units**.
2. Piece rate per unit = ₦50 ÷ 5 = **₦10.00**.
3. Time-rate pay for each = 40 × ₦50 = ₦2,000.

| Worker | Units | Piece pay (× ₦10) | Time pay | Time-rate cost per unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bambi | 66 | ₦660 | ₦2,000 | ₦30.30 |
| Feranmi | 166 | ₦1,660 | ₦2,000 | ₦12.05 |
| Elsie | 200 | ₦2,000 | ₦2,000 | ₦10.00 |
| Tifeh | 220 | ₦2,200 | ₦2,000 | ₦9.09 |

(Unit costs rounded to 2 decimal places.) Under time rate the cost per unit falls as output rises; under piece rate it stays at ₦10. With a guaranteed day rate, Bambi and Feranmi get ₦2,000 and Tifeh ₦2,200.

**Worked example 2 (piece rate from a "units per minutes" standard).** Normal day 12 hours, standard output 7 units per 20 minutes, rate ₦42 per hour; a worker makes 4,200 units in 12 hours.

1. Standard output per hour = 7 × (60 ÷ 20) = **21 units**.
2. Piece rate = ₦42 ÷ 21 = **₦2 per unit**.
3. Piece-rate pay = 4,200 × ₦2 = **₦8,400**.

**Worked example 3 (guaranteed day rate).** Bright works 10 hours at ₦360 per hour; standard output is 10 units per 5 minutes; he makes 5,600 units.

1. Standard output per hour = 10 × (60 ÷ 5) = 120 units; piece rate = ₦360 ÷ 120 = ₦3.
2. Piece earnings = 5,600 × ₦3 = ₦16,800. Guaranteed time pay = 10 × ₦360 = ₦3,600.
3. Pay = the higher = **₦16,800**.

**Worked example 4 (Taylor).** Normal piece rate ₦10; standard 50 units a day. Low rate = 83% × ₦10 = ₦8.30; high rate = 125% × ₦10 = ₦12.50. Musa makes 45 units: 45 × ₦8.30 = **₦373.50**. Ngozi makes 55 units: 55 × ₦12.50 = **₦687.50**. Under Merrick, Musa (90% efficiency) gets 45 × ₦11 = **₦495** and Ngozi (110%) 55 × ₦12 = **₦660**.

- **Traps:** a "units per 20 minutes" standard must be converted to units per **hour** before dividing. "Guaranteed day rate" means you compare and pay the **higher**, not add the two.

### Lesson 5.3: Premium bonus schemes: Halsey, Halsey-Weir and Rowan

**Must know**

- **Premium bonus scheme:** time rate is **guaranteed** and a bonus is paid for **time saved**; the gain from time saved is **shared** between employer and worker.
- **Halsey (50%):** Wages = **Time taken × Rate + 50% × Time saved × Rate**.
- **Halsey-Weir:** Wages = **Time taken × Rate + 33⅓% × Time saved × Rate**.
- **Rowan:** Wages = **Time taken × Rate + (Time saved ÷ Time allowed) × Time taken × Rate**.
- **Effective hourly rate = Total wages ÷ Time taken.**
- **Comparison:**
  1. Time saved **less than 50%** of time allowed: **Rowan** pays more.
  2. Time saved **exactly 50%**: both pay the **same**.
  3. Time saved **more than 50%**: **Halsey** pays more. Rowan's bonus never exceeds time-rate pay.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (Halsey).** Standard time 8 hours, actual 6 hours, rate ₦200.
Time saved = 8 − 6 = 2 hours. Wages = 6 × ₦200 + 50% × 2 × ₦200 = ₦1,200 + ₦200 = **₦1,400**.

**Worked example 2 (Halsey, time allowed from output).** Standard output 21 units per hour (7 per 20 minutes), rate ₦42; a worker makes 4,200 units in 12 hours.

1. Time allowed = 4,200 ÷ 21 = 200 hours. Time saved = 200 − 12 = 188 hours.
2. Basic = 12 × ₦42 = ₦504. Bonus = 50% × 188 × ₦42 = ₦3,948.
3. Gross pay = ₦504 + ₦3,948 = **₦4,452**.

**Worked example 3 (Halsey and Rowan side by side).** Time allowed 10 hours, rate ₦500.

| Time taken | Time saved | Halsey | Rowan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 hrs (20% saved) | 2 | 4,000 + 500 = ₦4,500 | 4,000 + (2/10 × 4,000) = ₦4,800 |
| 5 hrs (50% saved) | 5 | 2,500 + 1,250 = ₦3,750 | 2,500 + (5/10 × 2,500) = ₦3,750 |
| 4 hrs (60% saved) | 6 | 2,000 + 1,500 = ₦3,500 | 2,000 + (6/10 × 2,000) = ₦3,200 |

Halsey-Weir at 8 hours: 4,000 + ⅓ × 2 × 500 = **₦4,333.33** (rounded).

**Worked example 4 (working back from a Rowan effective rate).** A worker is allowed 60 hours, guaranteed ₦10 per hour, and earns an effective ₦12 per hour under Rowan. What would Halsey pay for the same time saved?

1. Under Rowan, effective rate = Rate × (1 + Time saved ÷ Time allowed). So 12 = 10 × (1 + S ÷ 60), giving S ÷ 60 = 0.2 and **S = 12 hours**; time taken = 60 − 12 = 48 hours.
2. Check: Rowan = 480 + (12/60) × 480 = ₦576; ₦576 ÷ 48 = ₦12.
3. Halsey = 48 × ₦10 + 50% × 12 × ₦10 = ₦480 + ₦60 = **₦540**.

- **Traps:** Rowan's fraction multiplies **time taken × rate**, not time saved × rate. ₦576 is the Rowan figure, not Halsey.

### Lesson 5.4: Payroll, wages analysis, overtime, shift premium and idle time

**Must know**

- **Gross pay = Basic wages + all allowances** (plus overtime, bonuses and premiums earned).
- **Net pay = Gross pay − Deductions.** Statutory deductions include **PAYE** income tax and the employee's **pension contribution**; voluntary ones include union dues, cooperative savings and loan repayments.
- **Payroll preparation:** collect clock cards and output records; compute gross pay, deductions and net pay; prepare the payroll and payslips; pay after authorisation, with a separate person checking.
- **Wages analysis:** sorting gross wages by job, department and type so that **direct labour** is charged to jobs or work-in-progress and **indirect labour** to production overhead.
- **Ghost workers:** employees who **do not exist but whose salaries are being paid**. Controls: verified staff records, surprise headcounts, separate hiring, timekeeping and paying duties.
- **Overtime premium:** the amount paid **above the normal rate** for hours worked beyond normal working time. Overtime pay = basic rate part + premium part.
- **Shift premium:** extra pay above the basic rate for working unsocial shifts (e.g. nights).
- **Idle time:** time paid for but not productive. **Idle time ratio = Idle hours ÷ Total hours paid × 100.**

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Overtime premium, general pressure of work | Production overhead (spread over all jobs) |
| Overtime premium, at a **customer's request** (early delivery) | Charged **directly to that customer's job** |
| Overtime premium, abnormal (e.g. poor planning) | Overhead or costing profit and loss account |
| Shift premium | Production overhead, unless one job requires the shift |
| **Normal** idle time | Production overhead (or built into an inflated hourly rate) |
| **Abnormal** idle time | **Costing profit and loss account** (not product cost) |

- **Normal idle time (unavoidable):** walking from the factory gate to the work station, time between finishing one job and starting the next, tea and lunch breaks, tool setting, adjusting machines.
- **Abnormal idle time (avoidable/controllable):** **machine breakdowns**, power failure, **non-availability of raw materials**, poor supervision, strikes and lockouts, lack of demand.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (overtime).** A Lagos company pays ₦200 per hour, overtime at time and a half (₦300 per overtime hour).

- Diablo works 42 hours **including** 20 overtime hours: normal 22 × ₦200 = ₦4,400; overtime 20 × ₦300 = ₦6,000; total **₦10,400**.
- Samson works 38 normal hours **plus** 12 overtime hours: 38 × ₦200 = ₦7,600; 12 × ₦300 = ₦3,600; total **₦11,200**.
- Diablo's overtime premium = 20 × ₦100 = ₦2,000. Read "including" against "plus" carefully: it changes the normal hours.

**Worked example 2 (idle time).** A Kano flour mill operative attends 45 hours at ₦400 per hour; job cards show 38 hours. Idle 7 hours: 3 hours normal (breaks, setting), 4 hours because wheat did not arrive. Direct labour = 38 × ₦400 = ₦15,200; overhead = 3 × ₦400 = ₦1,200; costing profit and loss = 4 × ₦400 = **₦1,600**. Idle time ratio = 7 ÷ 45 × 100 = 15.56% (rounded).

- **Control of idle time:** plan production, order materials on time, maintain machines and report idle time by cause.
- **Traps:** gross pay adds allowances to basic; subtracting deductions gives net pay. Abnormal idle time is neither direct cost nor factory overhead.

### Lesson 5.5: Labour turnover

**Must know**

- **Labour turnover:** the rate of change in the composition of a firm's workforce over a period; the rate at which workers leave and are replaced.
- **3 measurement methods** (average workforce = (opening + closing) ÷ 2):
  1. **Separation method** = Separations ÷ Average workforce × 100 (separations = leavers + discharged).
  2. **Replacement method** = Replacements ÷ Average workforce × 100. This is the classic "labour turnover rate".
  3. **Flux method** = (Separations + Accessions) ÷ Average workforce × 100 (accessions = all new recruits, including replacements). Some versions use (Separations + Replacements).
- **3 groups of causes:**
  1. **Personal:** a better job elsewhere, retirement through ill health or old age, domestic and family problems, discontent with the job.
  2. **Unavoidable:** seasonal business, shortage of raw materials or slack market, change of plant location, **disability making a worker unfit for work**, disciplinary dismissal, marriage.
  3. **Avoidable:** dissatisfaction with the job, pay, hours or conditions; strained relations with management or colleagues; **lack of training** and promotion; lack of recreational and medical facilities; **low wages and allowances**.
- **2 classes of turnover cost:**
  1. **Preventive costs** (keeping workers satisfied): medical services, personnel administration, welfare activities, pension and retirement schemes. Treated as overhead, apportioned on number of employees.
  2. **Replacement costs** (replacing leavers): recruitment, selection and training, lost output during vacancies, lower output and quality from new workers, scrap and breakages.
- **Remedies:** exit interviews, job analysis, scientific recruitment and promotion, training and grievance procedures, joint committees.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (Chinwe Limited).** 2,000 workers at the start, 2,100 at the end; 50 terminated, 35 left for unavoidable reasons; 180 employed, 105 of them replacements.

1. Average workforce = (2,000 + 2,100) ÷ 2 = 2,050. Separations = 50 + 35 = 85.
2. Separation = 85 ÷ 2,050 × 100 = **4.15%**.
3. Replacement = 105 ÷ 2,050 × 100 = 5.12%.
4. Flux = (85 + 180) ÷ 2,050 × 100 = **12.93%**. (Rates rounded to 2 decimal places.)

**Worked example 2 (Oshodi Manufacturing).** 500 at the start, 600 at the end; 5 left, 20 discharged, 75 recruited (10 replacements, 65 for expansion).

1. Average = 550. Separations = 25.
2. Replacement = 10 ÷ 550 × 100 = **1.82%**. Separation = 25 ÷ 550 × 100 = **4.55%**. Flux = (25 + 75) ÷ 550 × 100 = 18.18%.
3. Recruits for **expansion** are not replacements.

**Worked example 3 (working backwards, Lagos Textiles).** Quarterly rates: flux 20%, replacement 10%, separation 6%; 80 workers replaced.

1. Average workforce = 80 ÷ 10% = 800.
2. Separations = 6% × 800 = **48** workers left or were discharged.
3. Flux: (48 + A) ÷ 800 = 20%, so 48 + A = 160 and **A = 112** recruited (including replacements).

- **Traps:** use the **average** workforce, not the opening figure. Disability is an **unavoidable** cause; low wages and lack of training are avoidable.

### One-minute recap

- Clock card = attendance; job card = time on a job, posted to the job cost sheet; standard hour = work that should be done in one hour.
- Pay is time-based or output-based; piece rate = hourly rate ÷ standard output per hour; guaranteed day rate pays the higher.
- Taylor: 83% below standard, 125% at or above; Merrick: 100%, 110%, 120%.
- Halsey = TT × R + 50% × TS × R; Rowan = TT × R + (TS ÷ TA) × TT × R; Rowan better below 50% saved, Halsey above.
- Gross pay = basic + allowances; net pay = gross − deductions; ghost workers are non-existent staff being paid.
- Overtime premium: overhead normally, the customer's job if requested; abnormal idle time goes to costing profit and loss.
- Turnover: separation, replacement, flux, each over average workforce; costs are preventive and replacement.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Ada is allowed 12 hours for a job at ₦400 per hour and finishes in 9 hours. Calculate her total wages and effective hourly rate under (a) the Halsey (50%) plan and (b) the Rowan plan.

*Solution:* Time saved = 12 − 9 = 3 hours. Basic = 9 × ₦400 = ₦3,600.
(a) Halsey = ₦3,600 + 50% × 3 × ₦400 = ₦3,600 + ₦600 = **₦4,200**; effective rate = ₦4,200 ÷ 9 = **₦466.67** (rounded).
(b) Rowan = ₦3,600 + (3 ÷ 12) × ₦3,600 = ₦3,600 + ₦900 = **₦4,500**; effective rate = ₦4,500 ÷ 9 = **₦500**.
Rowan pays more because time saved (25%) is below 50% of time allowed.
