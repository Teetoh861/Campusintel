## Topic 1: Nature and Scope of Cost Accounting

**What you must know:** what cost, costing and cost accounting mean; the objectives of cost accounting; how it differs from financial accounting and relates to management accounting; the costing terms (cost object, cost unit, cost centre, profit, revenue and investment centres); the role of cost accounting in management; the purposes and qualities of cost information; and the scope of cost accounting.

### Lesson 1.1: Cost, costing and cost accounting

**Must know**

- **Cost:** the amount of expenditure (actual or notional) incurred on, or attributable to, a specified thing or activity. To the producer, cost is what was spent to make a product or give a service; to the customer, the figure paid is the **price**. Cost and price are not the same.
- **Basic formula:** Cost = Quantity × Price (rate) per unit. Example: 250 litres of diesel at ₦1,450 per litre cost 250 × ₦1,450 = ₦362,500.
- **Cost per unit** = Total cost ÷ Number of units produced (or service units given).
- **Costing:** the techniques and processes of **ascertaining cost**. "The process of ascertaining cost" = costing.
- **Cost accounting:** the wider process of **recording, classifying, accumulating, assigning (allocating), controlling and reporting** costs, and presenting suitably arranged information to management for control and guidance. It is a specialised branch of accounting.
- **Costing vs cost accounting:** they are **different**. Costing is the technique of ascertaining costs; cost accounting is the wider system of recording, classifying and reporting costs that provides the basis for costing.
- **Cost accountancy:** the application of costing and cost accounting principles, methods and techniques to the science, art and practice of cost control and the ascertainment of profitability, including presenting information for managerial decision making. It is the widest of the three terms.
- **Cost audit:** the verification of cost accounts and a check that the costing plan is followed.

**Objectives of cost accounting (learn the list):**

1. **Ascertain the cost** of each product, job, process, service or department (cost per unit).
2. **Control costs:** keep actual costs within budgets and standards.
3. **Reduce costs:** find real, lasting savings without lowering quality.
4. **Help fix selling prices** and prepare quotations and tenders.
5. **Value inventory** (stock) and measure profit for each period, product or department.
6. **Reveal waste and inefficiency** in materials, labour time, expenses and machines.
7. **Provide information for planning and decision making** (budgets, standards, make or buy, special orders, which products to push or drop).
8. **Present comparative cost data** for different periods and levels of output.

- **Not** objectives: helping shareholders decide whether to buy or sell the company's shares, computing tax, declaring dividends, drawing up the published balance sheet. Those belong to financial accounting and investors.

**Explain more**

**Worked example: cost per unit at a Lagos bakery.** Mama Tolu Bakery, Surulere, bakes 20,000 loaves in March. Costs: flour 40 bags at ₦52,000; sugar 10 bags at ₦38,000; other ingredients ₦300,000; bakers' wages ₦900,000; diesel 600 litres at ₦1,100; rent ₦250,000.

| Item | Working | ₦ |
|---|---|---|
| Flour | 40 × 52,000 | 2,080,000 |
| Sugar | 10 × 38,000 | 380,000 |
| Other ingredients | given | 300,000 |
| Wages | given | 900,000 |
| Diesel | 600 × 1,100 | 660,000 |
| Rent | given | 250,000 |
| **Total cost** | | **4,570,000** |

Cost per loaf = ₦4,570,000 ÷ 20,000 = **₦228.50**. Notice how each line uses Cost = Quantity × Price. If the bakery sells each loaf at ₦300, the ₦300 is the **price**; ₦228.50 is the **cost**.

- **Trap:** "Cost is quantity multiplied by…" Answer: **price (rate) per unit**, not units sold, total revenue or profit per unit.
- **Trap:** "What does cost accounting involve?" Answer: **ascertainment of cost**. Drawing up the balance sheet and preparing a statement of value added are financial-accounting tasks.
- **No single "true" cost.** Cost always depends on purpose and conditions. Much costing uses estimates (for example overheads are estimated in advance and shared over units), and different depreciation methods give different costs for the same product. Different costs for different purposes is a key idea in the nature of cost accounting.
- **Cost control vs cost reduction:** control keeps costs at the planned level (actual compared with budget or standard); reduction lowers the planned level itself, permanently, without hurting quality.
- **How it began:** cost accounting grew after the Industrial Revolution, when large factories needed to know the cost of each process to judge efficiency; later, cost-plus government contracts (price = cost + agreed profit) increased its importance.

### Lesson 1.2: Cost accounting, financial accounting and management accounting

**Must know**

- **Financial accounting:** records transactions of the whole business and prepares the published financial statements (statement of profit or loss, statement of financial position) for **external** users: shareholders, investors, lenders, government, tax authorities.
- **Cost accounting:** provides detailed, **tailor-made** information to **management** (internal users).
- **Management accounting:** the wider function of providing information to managers for planning, organising, directing, controlling and decision making. It uses cost-accounting data plus budgeting, financial analysis and forecasting. **Cost accounting is an integral part of management accounting.**

| Point | Financial accounting | Cost accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Main users | External (shareholders, lenders, government) | Internal (management) |
| Legal requirement | Compulsory for companies | Not compulsory; kept by choice |
| Rules | Must follow accounting standards (IFRS) and company law | No compulsory standards; format suits management |
| Coverage | The whole business as one | Products, jobs, processes, departments, cost centres |
| Period | Usually yearly (fixed periods) | As often as needed: daily, weekly, monthly |
| Data | Mainly historical, actual figures | Historical plus estimates, budgets and standards |
| Units | Money only | Money and physical units (kg, hours, litres) |
| Main purpose | True and fair view, stewardship | Ascertainment, control, pricing, decisions |
| Stock valuation | Cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower | Detailed cost of each item and stage |

- **Similarity:** both use the same basic transaction data, both are mostly expressed in money, and **the information of both is used for decision making**.

| Point | Cost accounting | Management accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Narrower: cost data | Wider: cost, financial and non-financial data |
| Focus | Ascertaining and controlling cost | Planning, control and all management decisions |
| Relationship | A part of management accounting | Includes cost accounting |

**Explain more**

- **Trap:** "Financial and cost accounting are similar because…" The answer is that **their information is used for decision making**. "Both required by law" is false (cost accounting is not compulsory). "Both subject to accounting standards" is false. "Both prepared for the whole business" is false (cost accounting breaks the business into products and departments).
- **Trap:** "Cost accounting mainly provides information for … users." Answer: **internal**. "Cost accounting provides … information to …": **tailor-made; management**. Not "generic", and not "investors".
- **Trap:** "Cost accounting is an integral part of…" Answer: **management accounting**, not financial, forensic or treasury accounting.
- **Why financial accounting is not enough for managers:** a Kano flour mill's annual report shows one total profit. It does not tell the managing director which product (semolina, wheat flour, bran) makes money, what one bag costs, where waste is, or what price to quote a bakery chain. Cost accounting fills these gaps.
- In Nigeria, ICAN (the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria) examines cost and management accounting as core professional subjects.

### Lesson 1.3: Costing terms: cost object, cost unit and responsibility centres

**Must know**

- **Cost object:** anything for which a separate measurement of cost is wanted: a product, service, job, batch, department, customer, project or activity.
- **Cost unit:** a unit of **product, service or time** (or combination) in relation to which costs are ascertained or expressed. It answers "cost per what?"
- **Composite cost unit:** combines two measures, for example passenger-kilometre, tonne-kilometre, kilowatt-hour, patient-day, room-day.
- **Cost centre:** a production or service **location, function, activity or item of equipment** (it can also be a person) for which costs are accumulated, and whose costs may then be attributed to cost units. The manager is responsible for **costs only**.
- **Revenue centre:** the manager is responsible for **revenue (sales) only**, e.g. a sales office.
- **Profit centre:** a part of the business for which **both costs and revenues** are ascertained; the manager is responsible for **generating revenue and controlling costs so as to earn profit**.
- **Investment centre:** a profit centre whose manager also controls the **capital invested**; judged on return on capital employed (ROCE).
- **Responsibility centres** = cost centres, revenue centres, profit centres and investment centres (the classic exam grouping is **cost, profit and investment centres**).

**Types of cost centre:**

1. **Production cost centre:** where goods are actually made (mixing, baking, cutting, assembly).
2. **Service cost centre:** supports production but makes no product (stores, maintenance, canteen, quality control).
3. **Personal cost centre:** a person or group of persons (e.g. a sales representative).
4. **Impersonal cost centre:** a location or item of equipment (e.g. an oven, a machine).

**Common cost units:**

| Business | Cost unit |
|---|---|
| Bicycle or car manufacturer | Per bicycle / per car |
| Brick making | Per 1,000 bricks |
| Cement, steel, sugar, flour | Per tonne (or per bag) |
| Hotel | Room-day (room-night), guest-night |
| Electricity supply | Kilowatt-hour (kWh) |
| Bus or rail transport | Passenger-kilometre |
| Haulage (goods transport) | Tonne-kilometre |
| Hospital | Patient-day, bed-night |
| School | Per student |
| Printing, shoe factory | Per job, per batch, per pair |
| Oil and water | Per litre, per barrel |

- **Formula:** ROCE = Profit ÷ Capital employed × 100.

**Explain more**

- **Cost unit vs cost centre (most-swapped pair):** a cost **unit** is the thing you cost (a loaf, a passenger-kilometre). A cost **centre** is where costs are collected (the baking section, an oven). "A machine in the factory" is a cost **centre**, **not** a cost unit. A definition with "location, person or item of equipment" describes a cost centre; "unit of product, service or time" describes a cost unit.
- **Choose the natural output.** For a bicycle maker the cost unit is **per bicycle**: "per part of a bicycle" is too small, "per tonne" and "per day" do not describe the product. Select units that are simple, clear and commonly used in that industry.
- **Trap:** "A location, function or activity for which **cost and revenue** can be ascertained" = **profit centre** (both cost and revenue). Costs only = cost centre. Revenue only = revenue centre. Return on capital = investment centre.

**Worked example 1: passenger-kilometre.** An Ibadan transport company runs 3 coaches on the Lagos–Ibadan route (128 km). Each coach makes 20 trips a month with an average of 18 passengers. Monthly cost is ₦6,912,000.

- Passenger-km = 3 × 20 × 18 × 128 = 138,240.
- Cost per passenger-km = ₦6,912,000 ÷ 138,240 = **₦50**.

**Worked example 2: room-night.** A 40-room hotel in Abuja is open 30 days with 75% occupancy. Monthly cost is ₦13,500,000.

- Room-nights sold = 40 × 30 × 75% = 900.
- Cost per room-night = ₦13,500,000 ÷ 900 = **₦15,000**.

**Worked example 3: profit and investment centres.** The Kaduna branch of a Kano flour mill earns revenue of ₦48,000,000 and incurs costs of ₦39,000,000; capital invested in it is ₦60,000,000.

- As a profit centre: profit = ₦48,000,000 − ₦39,000,000 = **₦9,000,000**.
- As an investment centre: ROCE = ₦9,000,000 ÷ ₦60,000,000 × 100 = **15%**.

### Lesson 1.4: Role of cost accounting in management, purposes, qualities and scope

**Must know**

- **Role in management:** cost accounting is a valuable aid to management in **planning, control and decision making**. It also helps organise (by dividing the business into cost centres), coordinate, and motivate (through budgets, standards and incentive schemes).

**Purposes of cost accounting information:**

1. **Inventory valuation and profit measurement:** cost of closing stock and work in progress; profit per product or period.
2. **Pricing:** setting selling prices, quotations and tenders.
3. **Planning:** preparing budgets and setting standard costs.
4. **Control:** comparing actual results with budgets or standards and acting on variances.
5. **Decision making:** make or buy, accept or reject a special order, drop or keep a product, choose between methods.
6. **Performance evaluation:** judging managers, departments and cost centres.

**Qualities of good cost accounting information:**

1. **Relevant** to the decision or user.
2. **Accurate** (reliable), accurate enough for its purpose.
3. **Timely:** available when needed.
4. **Complete** (no vital item left out).
5. **Clear and understandable** to the manager using it.
6. **Cost-effective:** the benefit is greater than the cost of producing it.
7. **Communicated to the right person** (the one who can act on it).
8. **Comparable** over periods and between centres.

- **Not** a quality: "tax consciousness".

**Scope of cost accounting:**

1. **Cost ascertainment:** collecting, classifying and analysing costs to find cost per unit, job or process (by post costing after the event or continuous costing as work goes on).
2. **Cost bookkeeping (recording):** keeping cost records and accounts.
3. **Cost control:** standards and budgets, comparison, corrective action.
4. **Cost reduction:** permanent savings.
5. **Cost reporting (presentation):** regular reports to management; matching costs with revenue for each period.
6. **Cost audit:** checking cost records.
7. **Aiding decision making:** cost data for choosing between alternatives.

**Explain more**

- **Trap:** "Cost accounting is a valuable aid to management in \_\_\_, \_\_\_ and \_\_\_." Answer: **planning, control and decision making**. Auditing, taxation and dividends; advertising and packaging; recruitment and promotion are wrong sets.
- **Trap:** cost-effective information means its value exceeds its cost to produce. Spending ₦2,000,000 a year on a reporting system that saves ₦500,000 fails this test.
- **Who else benefits:** managers most of all; also employees (steady jobs, fair incentive pay), lenders (better judgement of profitability) and the national economy (efficient firms, less waste, more government revenue).
- **How an Aba shoe factory uses it:** it finds the cost of each pair (ascertainment), keeps leather waste within the standard (control), compares this month with last month (comparison), quotes a school for 2,000 pairs of sandals (pricing), and decides whether to buy soles or make them (decision making).
- **Nature, in summary:** cost accounting is a specialised, internal, non-compulsory branch of accounting; it deals with both past and future (estimates, budgets, standards); it uses money and physical units; it gives different costs for different purposes; and it serves management.

### One-minute recap

- Cost = expenditure (actual or notional) incurred on, or attributable to, a specified thing or activity; Cost = Quantity × Price per unit.
- Costing = ascertaining cost; cost accounting = the wider recording, classifying and reporting of costs; they are different.
- Objectives: ascertain cost, control and reduce cost, fix prices, value stock, reveal waste, inform decisions. Helping shareholders trade shares is not one.
- Cost accounting: internal users, tailor-made information for management, not compulsory, no compulsory standards; similar to financial accounting because both inform decisions.
- Cost accounting is an integral part of management accounting.
- Cost unit = unit of product, service or time (bicycle, room-day, kWh, passenger-km); cost centre = location, function, person or equipment (a machine is a cost centre).
- Responsibility centres: cost (costs), revenue (revenue), profit (cost and revenue), investment (profit plus capital).
- Aid to management in planning, control and decision making; good information is relevant, accurate, timely, complete, clear and cost-effective.
- Scope: ascertainment, recording, control, reduction, reporting, audit, decision support.

### Theory practice

**Question:** An Aba shoe factory produced 2,500 pairs of shoes in June. It used 1,250 m² of leather at ₦4,800 per m² and 2,500 soles at ₦900 each; labour cost ₦3,000,000 and factory overheads ₦1,750,000. (a) State a suitable cost unit and one cost centre. (b) Calculate the cost per unit.

**Solution:**

(a) Cost unit: **one pair of shoes**. A cost centre: the cutting section (or the stitching section, or a stitching machine).

(b)

| Item | Working | ₦ |
|---|---|---|
| Leather | 1,250 × 4,800 | 6,000,000 |
| Soles | 2,500 × 900 | 2,250,000 |
| Labour | given | 3,000,000 |
| Factory overheads | given | 1,750,000 |
| **Total cost** | | **13,000,000** |

Cost per pair = ₦13,000,000 ÷ 2,500 = **₦5,200**.
