## Topic 6: Overhead Costing

**What you must know:** what overheads are and how they are classified; the main sources of overheads, including depreciation and the fixed asset register; production and service cost centres; allocation, apportionment and the usual bases; re-apportioning service centre costs by the direct, step-down, repeated distribution and algebraic methods; absorption bases and rates (blanket and departmental); predetermined rates; calculating and treating under- and over-absorption.

### Lesson 6.1: Nature, classification and sources of overheads

**Must know**

- **Overheads (indirect costs):** the aggregate of **indirect materials, indirect labour and indirect expenses**. They are all the costs that cannot be traced directly to a cost unit. Overheads are everything except prime cost (direct materials, direct labour, direct expenses).
- **Classification by element (3 groups):**
  1. **Indirect materials:** lubricating oil, cleaning materials, glue and thread in a shoe factory, small tools.
  2. **Indirect labour:** supervisors, storekeepers, maintenance staff, security, idle time pay, overtime premium (normally).
  3. **Indirect expenses:** factory rent, rates, insurance, power and lighting, depreciation, repairs, telephone.
- **Classification by function (4 groups):** production (factory) overheads; administration overheads; selling overheads; distribution overheads.
- **Classification by behaviour (3 groups):** fixed (rent, salaries, straight-line depreciation); variable (power for machines, indirect materials that rise with output); semi-variable (telephone, electricity with a standing charge).
- **Classification by controllability:** controllable (can be influenced by the manager of the cost centre) and uncontrollable.
- **Main sources of overhead figures:** invoices and the purchase journal (indirect materials and services), stores requisitions (indirect materials), payroll and wages analysis (indirect labour), cash book and petty cash (rent, rates, utilities), and the **fixed asset register** (depreciation and insurance values).
- **Depreciation:** the share of a fixed asset's cost charged to each period of its useful life. It is an overhead (an indirect expense).
  - Straight-line depreciation per year = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life in years.
  - Reducing balance depreciation = Rate % × Net book value at the start of the year.
  - Machine hour (production unit) depreciation per hour = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Total expected machine hours.
- **Fixed asset register:** a detailed record of every fixed asset: description and identification number, location (department or cost centre), date of purchase, cost, supplier, useful life, depreciation method and rate, accumulated depreciation, net book value, insurance value and disposal details.

**Explain more**

- Trap: "fixed overheads" is a **behaviour** group, not an element group. Asked which is NOT a group by element, choose fixed overheads; the three element groups are indirect materials, indirect labour and indirect expenses.
- Trap: overheads are **all** indirect costs (materials, labour and expenses), not only factory costs and not only indirect expenses. Prime cost is never overhead.
- Why the fixed asset register matters for costing: because it records each asset's **location** and **value**, it tells you which cost centre to charge depreciation to and gives the value base for apportioning machine insurance and depreciation.
- Worked example: a Kano flour mill buys a milling machine for ₦2,000,000, with a residual value of ₦200,000 and a 5-year life (expected to run 36,000 hours in total).
  - Straight line = (₦2,000,000 − ₦200,000) ÷ 5 = **₦360,000 a year**.
  - Reducing balance at 20%: Year 1 = 20% × ₦2,000,000 = **₦400,000**; NBV = ₦1,600,000; Year 2 = 20% × ₦1,600,000 = **₦320,000**.
  - Machine hour = ₦1,800,000 ÷ 36,000 = **₦50 per hour**; 8,000 hours in a year gives ₦400,000.
  - The register places the machine in Milling, so the depreciation is **allocated** to Milling.

### Lesson 6.2: Cost centres, allocation and apportionment

**Must know**

- **Production cost centre:** a department where the product is actually made or converted (cutting, stitching, assembly, moulding, milling).
- **Service cost centre:** a department that does not make the product but serves other centres (stores, maintenance, canteen, personnel, power house, quality control, internal transport).
- The overhead process has **3 stages**:
  1. **Allocation and apportionment** (primary distribution) of overheads to all cost centres.
  2. **Re-apportionment** (secondary distribution) of service centre costs to production centres.
  3. **Absorption** of production centre overheads into cost units.
- **Allocation:** charging a **whole item of cost** directly to one cost centre or cost unit, because that centre alone caused it (a storekeeper's salary to Stores; depreciation of a machine located in Cutting).
- **Apportionment:** charging a **proportion of a common item of cost** to two or more cost centres on some equitable (fair) basis. It is **sharing a common cost**.
- **Usual bases of apportionment:**

| Overhead | Basis |
|---|---|
| Factory rent, rates, building insurance, lighting, heating, cleaning, building repairs | Floor area (or volume) occupied |
| Insurance of machinery, depreciation of machinery | Value of machinery (plant) in each centre |
| Power for machines | Kilowatt-hours (or horsepower × machine hours) |
| Canteen, welfare, personnel department, supervision | Number of employees |
| Stores department | Number (or value) of materials requisitions |
| Maintenance department | Maintenance hours worked for each centre |
| Internal transport | Tonne-kilometres or vehicle hours |

**Explain more**

- Trap: allocation = **whole** item to one centre; apportionment = **shared** common cost; absorption = **charging overheads to cost units** (recovery of overhead). Exams swap these three words freely.
- Trap: factory rent goes on **floor space**, not on number of machines or employees. Machinery insurance goes on **value of machines**, not number of machines (a ₦5 million machine needs more cover than a ₦500,000 one). Personnel department cost goes on **number of employees**.
- Worked example (primary distribution): an Aba shoe factory has two production centres (Cutting, Stitching) and two service centres (Stores, Maintenance). Budgeted overheads for the year:

| Data | Cutting | Stitching | Stores | Maintenance | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floor area (m²) | 4,000 | 5,000 | 2,000 | 1,000 | 12,000 |
| Value of machinery (₦) | 400,000 | 300,000 | 50,000 | 50,000 | 800,000 |
| Employees | 20 | 30 | 5 | 5 | 60 |
| Kilowatt-hours | 5,000 | 2,000 | 500 | 500 | 8,000 |

  Overheads: indirect labour (allocated) Cutting ₦60,000, Stitching ₦70,000, Stores ₦40,000, Maintenance ₦20,000; rent ₦240,000; machinery insurance ₦40,000; machinery depreciation ₦80,000; welfare ₦90,000; power ₦80,000.

  - Rent ₦240,000 ÷ 12,000 m² = ₦20 per m²: Cutting 4,000 × ₦20 = ₦80,000; Stitching ₦100,000; Stores ₦40,000; Maintenance ₦20,000.
  - Insurance ₦40,000 ÷ ₦800,000 = 5 kobo per ₦1 of machinery: ₦20,000; ₦15,000; ₦2,500; ₦2,500.
  - Depreciation ₦80,000 ÷ ₦800,000 = ₦0.10 per ₦1: ₦40,000; ₦30,000; ₦5,000; ₦5,000.
  - Welfare ₦90,000 ÷ 60 = ₦1,500 per employee: ₦30,000; ₦45,000; ₦7,500; ₦7,500.
  - Power ₦80,000 ÷ 8,000 kWh = ₦10 per kWh: ₦50,000; ₦20,000; ₦5,000; ₦5,000.

| Overhead (₦) | Basis | Cutting | Stitching | Stores | Maintenance | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Rent | Floor area | 80,000 | 100,000 | 40,000 | 20,000 | 240,000 |

| Machinery depreciation | Machine value | 40,000 | 30,000 | 5,000 | 5,000 | 80,000 |

  Always check the column totals add back to ₦720,000.

### Lesson 6.3: Re-apportionment of service centre costs

**Must know**

- **Re-apportionment (secondary distribution):** moving the total overhead of each service centre to the production centres, because cost units pass only through production centres and can absorb overhead only there.
- **4 methods:**
  1. **Direct (one-step) method:** service costs go **straight to production centres only**; any services that service centres give each other are ignored.
  2. **Step-down (elimination, step) method:** the service centre that serves the most other centres (or has the largest cost) is closed first, to production centres **and** the remaining service centres; then the next service centre is closed to production centres and any centres not yet closed; once a centre is closed nothing comes back to it. The last service centre goes to production centres only.
  3. **Repeated distribution (continuous allotment) method:** each service centre's overhead is re-apportioned to **both production centres and other service centres** that use its services, again and again, until the amounts left are insignificant; the small remainder then goes to production centres.
  4. **Algebraic (simultaneous equation) method:** the total cost of each service centre (its own cost plus its share of the other's) is found with simultaneous equations, then shared out once.
- Methods 3 and 4 are the **reciprocal service** methods: they fully recognise that service centres serve each other, and they give the same answer (apart from rounding).

**Explain more**

- Trap: repeated distribution is a **re-apportionment** method, **not** a method of absorption.
- Continue the shoe factory: Stores (₦100,000) serves Cutting 50%, Stitching 30%, Maintenance 20%. Maintenance (₦60,000) serves Cutting 40%, Stitching 40%, Stores 20%.
- **Direct method:** ignore the 20% each service centre gives the other. Stores is shared in the ratio 50:30 and Maintenance in the ratio 40:40.
  - Stores: Cutting ₦100,000 × 50/80 = ₦62,500; Stitching ₦100,000 × 30/80 = ₦37,500.
  - Maintenance: Cutting ₦60,000 × 40/80 = ₦30,000; Stitching ₦30,000.
  - Cutting = ₦280,000 + ₦62,500 + ₦30,000 = **₦372,500**; Stitching = ₦280,000 + ₦37,500 + ₦30,000 = **₦347,500**. Check: ₦720,000.
- **Step-down method** (close Stores first, because it gives more to Maintenance than Maintenance gives to it):
  - Stores ₦100,000: Cutting ₦50,000; Stitching ₦30,000; Maintenance ₦20,000.
  - Maintenance now = ₦60,000 + ₦20,000 = ₦80,000, shared 40:40 (Stores is closed): Cutting ₦40,000; Stitching ₦40,000.
  - Cutting = ₦280,000 + ₦50,000 + ₦40,000 = **₦370,000**; Stitching = ₦280,000 + ₦30,000 + ₦40,000 = **₦350,000**.
- **Repeated distribution method** (₦):

| Step | Cutting | Stitching | Stores | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary totals | 280,000 | 280,000 | 100,000 | 60,000 |
| Stores (50/30/20) | 50,000 | 30,000 | (100,000) | 20,000 |
| Maintenance (40/40/20) | 32,000 | 32,000 | 16,000 | (80,000) |
| Stores | 8,000 | 4,800 | (16,000) | 3,200 |
| Maintenance | 1,280 | 1,280 | 640 | (3,200) |
| Stores | 320 | 192 | (640) | 128 |
| Maintenance | 51.20 | 51.20 | 25.60 | (128) |
| Stores remainder (50:30 to production only) | 16 | 9.60 | (25.60) | 0 |
| **Total** | **371,667.20** | **348,332.80** | 0 | 0 |

  Rounded: Cutting **₦371,667**, Stitching **₦348,333**.
- **Algebraic method:** let S = total Stores cost and M = total Maintenance cost.
  - S = 100,000 + 0.2M and M = 60,000 + 0.2S.
  - Substitute: S = 100,000 + 0.2(60,000 + 0.2S) = 112,000 + 0.04S, so 0.96S = 112,000 and S = ₦116,666.67.
  - M = 60,000 + 0.2 × 116,666.67 = ₦83,333.33.
  - Cutting = 280,000 + 0.5 × 116,666.67 + 0.4 × 83,333.33 = **₦371,666.67**; Stitching = 280,000 + 0.3 × 116,666.67 + 0.4 × 83,333.33 = **₦348,333.33** (to 2 decimal places). The same as repeated distribution.
- **Direct method with different bases** (a typical exam set-up): Ifeoma Limited has production centres Molding (₦920,000) and Assembly (₦1,080,000), and service centres Administrative (₦160,000, apportioned on employees) and Maintenance (₦240,000, apportioned on machine hours). Employees: Molding 2,500, Assembly 1,000, Maintenance 500. Machine hours: Molding 6,000, Assembly 12,000, Administrative 200, Maintenance 300.
  - Direct method uses **production centres' figures only**, so ignore Maintenance's 500 employees and the service centres' machine hours.
  - Admin: Molding ₦160,000 × 2,500/3,500 = ₦114,286; Assembly ₦160,000 × 1,000/3,500 = ₦45,714 (to the nearest naira).
  - Maintenance: Molding ₦240,000 × 6,000/18,000 = ₦80,000; Assembly ₦240,000 × 12,000/18,000 = ₦160,000.
  - Molding = ₦920,000 + ₦114,286 + ₦80,000 = **₦1,114,286**; Assembly = ₦1,080,000 + ₦45,714 + ₦160,000 = **₦1,285,714**. Check: ₦2,400,000.
  - Trap: options such as ₦194,286 or ₦205,714 are only the **service costs received**; the question asks for the department's **total** overhead.

### Lesson 6.4: Absorption bases and rates

**Must know**

- **Overhead absorption (recovery):** charging overheads to cost units, so each product or job carries a fair share. It is the costing technique for sharing production overheads among different products on a fair basis, reflecting the volume of output and effort each product uses.
- **Overhead absorption rate (OAR)** = Budgeted overhead ÷ Budgeted level of the absorption base.
- **6 methods (bases) of absorption:**
  1. **Percentage of direct material cost** = Overhead ÷ Direct material cost × 100.
  2. **Percentage of direct labour (wages) cost** = Overhead ÷ Direct labour cost × 100.
  3. **Percentage of prime cost** = Overhead ÷ Prime cost × 100.
  4. **Cost unit (rate per unit of output)** = Overhead ÷ Units produced. Suitable only when all units are identical.
  5. **Direct labour hour rate** = Overhead ÷ Direct labour hours. Best for labour-intensive departments.
  6. **Machine hour rate** = Overhead ÷ Machine hours. Best for machine-intensive departments.
- **Blanket (single, plant-wide) rate:** one overhead absorption rate used for the **whole factory**.
- **Departmental (separate) rates:** a different rate for each production centre, on the base that suits it. They are more accurate when products spend different amounts of time in different departments.
- **Non-production overheads:** administration overheads are commonly absorbed as a **percentage of works (factory) cost**; selling and distribution overheads as a percentage of **sales value** or of works cost or cost of production, or a rate per unit sold.

**Explain more**

- Worked example (six bases): a Lagos bakery budgets production overhead of ₦600,000, direct materials ₦1,200,000, direct labour ₦800,000 (prime cost ₦2,000,000), 20,000 units, 40,000 direct labour hours and 15,000 machine hours.

| Base | Calculation | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| % of direct materials | 600,000 ÷ 1,200,000 × 100 | 50% |
| % of direct labour | 600,000 ÷ 800,000 × 100 | 75% |
| % of prime cost | 600,000 ÷ 2,000,000 × 100 | 30% |
| Per unit | 600,000 ÷ 20,000 | ₦30 per unit |
| Direct labour hour | 600,000 ÷ 40,000 | ₦15 per DLH |
| Machine hour | 600,000 ÷ 15,000 | ₦40 per MH |

  An order of 500 units uses DM ₦30,000, DL ₦20,000, 900 DLH and 300 MH. Overhead charged: 50% × ₦30,000 = ₦15,000; 75% × ₦20,000 = ₦15,000; 30% × ₦50,000 = ₦15,000; 500 × ₦30 = ₦15,000; 900 × ₦15 = ₦13,500; 300 × ₦40 = ₦12,000. The base chosen changes the cost, so pick the one that best reflects what drives the overhead. Time-based rates (hours) are generally preferred because most overheads build up with time.
- **Working out the hours first:** products A and B use the same equipment. A: 5,000 units × 1 hour = 5,000 DLH; B: 7,000 units × 2 hours = 14,000 DLH; total 19,000 DLH. Overhead ₦285,000 ÷ 19,000 = **₦15 per DLH**. Trap: dividing by 12,000 units (₦23.75) ignores that B takes twice as long.
- **Blanket versus departmental:** in the shoe factory (direct method totals), Cutting ₦372,500 is absorbed on 18,625 machine hours = **₦20 per MH**; Stitching ₦347,500 on 27,800 DLH = **₦12.50 per DLH**. Cutting also uses 8,200 DLH, so a blanket rate = ₦720,000 ÷ (8,200 + 27,800) = ₦720,000 ÷ 36,000 = **₦20 per DLH**.
  - A pair of boots needs 10 MH and 1 DLH in Cutting and 4 DLH in Stitching.
  - Departmental: 10 × ₦20 + 4 × ₦12.50 = ₦200 + ₦50 = **₦250**.
  - Blanket: (1 + 4) DLH × ₦20 = **₦100**. The blanket rate badly under-costs this machine-heavy product.
- **Administration as % of works cost:** budgeted administration overhead ₦150,000 and budgeted works cost ₦3,000,000 give 5%. A job with works cost ₦40,000 is charged ₦2,000 administration overhead, so its cost of production is ₦42,000.

### Lesson 6.5: Predetermined rates, under- and over-absorption

**Must know**

- **Predetermined overhead absorption rate:** the rate determined **before** the accounting period from estimated (budgeted) overheads and the budgeted level of the chosen absorption basis.
- **Why predetermined rather than actual rates:** actual overheads and activity are known only at the end of the period; jobs need costing and pricing (quotations) as they are done; actual rates swing from month to month with seasonal costs and output; predetermined rates smooth this out.
- **Overhead absorbed** = Predetermined OAR × **Actual** level of the base.
- **Under- or over-absorption** = Overhead absorbed − Actual overhead.
  - **Over-absorbed** (over-recovered): absorbed overheads **exceed** actual overheads. Too much was charged; profit is understated, so the over-absorption is **added** to profit.
  - **Under-absorbed** (under-recovered): actual overheads **exceed** absorbed overheads. Too little was charged; the under-absorption is **deducted** from profit.
- **2 causes** (alone or together): actual overhead differs from budgeted overhead; actual activity (hours or units) differs from budgeted activity.
- **3 treatments:**
  1. **Write off to the costing profit and loss account** when the amount is small or arises from abnormal causes (normal treatment).
  2. **Supplementary rate:** when the amount is **significant**, adjust the cost of jobs (cost of sales), work in progress and finished goods with a supplementary rate (positive for under-absorption, negative for over-absorption).
  3. **Carry forward** to the next period when the difference is only seasonal within the year (interim accounts); it should even out by the year end.

**Explain more**

- Trap: over- and under-absorption compare **absorbed** with **actual** overhead. Budgeted overhead is used only to set the rate.
- Worked example 1: machine hours budgeted 11,250 with overhead ₦258,750; actual 10,980 hours and overhead ₦254,692.
  - OAR = ₦258,750 ÷ 11,250 = ₦23 per machine hour.
  - Absorbed = 10,980 × ₦23 = ₦252,540.
  - ₦252,540 − ₦254,692 = −₦2,152: **under-absorbed by ₦2,152**.
- Worked example 2: Daramola Enterprises budgets ₦270,000 for 30,000 machine hours; actual ₦400,000 and 40,000 hours.
  - OAR = ₦270,000 ÷ 30,000 = ₦9 per MH; absorbed = 40,000 × ₦9 = ₦360,000.
  - ₦360,000 − ₦400,000 = **₦40,000 under-absorbed**. Trap: ₦60,000 compares the wrong figures.
- Worked example 3 (working backwards): Mary Nigeria Limited has actual overhead ₦300,000, actual activity 120,000 direct labour hours and over-absorption ₦60,000.
  - Absorbed = Actual + Over-absorption = ₦300,000 + ₦60,000 = ₦360,000.
  - OAR = ₦360,000 ÷ 120,000 = **₦3 per direct labour hour** (the base is hours, so the rate is per DLH, not per unit).
  - For under-absorption you would use Absorbed = Actual − Under-absorption.
- Worked example 4 (shoe factory): Cutting worked 19,200 MH with actual overhead ₦375,000: absorbed 19,200 × ₦20 = ₦384,000, so **₦9,000 over-absorbed**. Stitching worked 26,000 DLH with actual overhead ₦352,000: absorbed 26,000 × ₦12.50 = ₦325,000, so **₦27,000 under-absorbed**.
- Supplementary rate: suppose Stitching's ₦27,000 is significant and the ₦325,000 absorbed sits in cost of sales ₦227,500 (70%), finished goods ₦65,000 (20%) and work in progress ₦32,500 (10%).
  - Supplementary rate = ₦27,000 ÷ ₦325,000 = 8.31% of overhead absorbed (to 2 decimal places).
  - Add ₦27,000 × 70% = ₦18,900 to cost of sales, ₦5,400 to finished goods and ₦2,700 to work in progress.

### One-minute recap

- Overheads = indirect materials + indirect labour + indirect expenses; "fixed overheads" is a behaviour group, not an element.
- Depreciation is an indirect expense; the fixed asset register gives each asset's location and value.
- Allocation = whole cost to one centre; apportionment = common cost shared on a fair basis; absorption = charging overheads to cost units.
- Rent on floor area; machinery insurance and depreciation on machine value; personnel and canteen on employees; power on kWh.
- Re-apportionment: direct (production only), step-down (close one by one), repeated distribution and algebraic (full reciprocal service).
- Absorption bases: % of materials, % of labour, % of prime cost, per unit, labour hour, machine hour. Blanket rate = one rate for the whole factory.
- Administration overhead is commonly absorbed as a % of works cost.
- OAR = Budgeted overhead ÷ Budgeted base; Absorbed = OAR × Actual base.
- Absorbed > Actual = over-absorbed; Actual > Absorbed = under-absorbed.
- Small differences go to costing profit and loss; significant ones use a supplementary rate; seasonal ones are carried forward.

### Theory practice

**Question:** An Ibadan furniture maker budgets production overhead of ₦480,000 and 24,000 machine hours for the year. Actual results were 25,500 machine hours and overhead of ₦498,000. Calculate (a) the overhead absorption rate, (b) the overhead absorbed and (c) the under- or over-absorption, and say how a small difference is treated.

*Solution:*
1. (a) OAR = ₦480,000 ÷ 24,000 = **₦20 per machine hour**.
2. (b) Absorbed = 25,500 × ₦20 = **₦510,000**.
3. (c) ₦510,000 − ₦498,000 = **₦12,000 over-absorbed**, because absorbed overhead exceeds actual overhead.
4. Being small, it is written off (credited) to the costing profit and loss account, increasing profit by ₦12,000.
