## Topic 7: Job and Batch Costing

**What you must know:** what job costing is and where it is used; the documents that feed a job; the job cost sheet built from direct materials, direct labour (including idle time), direct expenses and overheads; quotations, mark-up and margin; time and materials pricing; batch costing, cost per unit in a batch and the economic batch quantity.

### Lesson 7.1: The nature of job costing

**Must know**

- **Job costing:** a specific order costing method in which the elements of cost are collected separately for each job (work order) undertaken. It applies where work is done to **customer order**, usually inside a **factory or workshop**, and each order is of **short duration**. The job is the cost unit.
- **Job:** a single order, product, unit, batch or service that can be separately identified and must be separately costed.
- **Purpose:** to bring together all the costs of completing a job, so that its profit or loss can be found and quotations for similar work can be given.

**Features of job costing:**
1. Work is done against specific customer orders, to the customer's own specification.
2. Each job is different from others, so each is costed separately.
3. Direct costs are charged straight to the job because they can be traced to it.
4. Overheads are charged to the job on a suitable basis, usually a predetermined absorption rate.
5. Each job has its own number and its own job cost sheet.
6. Production is not continuous; work in progress may or may not exist at the end of a period.
7. Closer supervision and control are needed because standard controls are hard to apply.

**Where it is used:** printing presses, furniture makers, tailors, repair workshops and garages, engineering firms, auditing and consulting firms, building repairs.

**Main documents:**

| Document | What it records |
|---|---|
| Production (job) order | Authority to start the job: job number, customer, quantity, specification |
| Materials requisition note | Issue of direct material from stores **to a specific job** (job number on it) |
| Materials returned note | Surplus material sent back from the job to stores (reduces the job's material cost) |
| Materials transfer note | Material moved from one job to another |
| Job card / time sheet | Labour time spent on each job, later costed and posted to the job cost sheet |
| Job cost sheet (job account) | Running record of all costs charged to the job |

**Job vs other methods:**

| Method | Used where |
|---|---|
| Job costing | Customer orders, inside a factory or workshop, short duration |
| Batch costing | A group of identical units made together as one job |
| Contract costing | Large jobs, usually on site, lasting a long time (often over a year), e.g. building a bridge |
| Process costing | Continuous production of identical units through processes, e.g. a Kano flour mill |
| Unit (output) costing | A single standard product made continuously, e.g. a block-moulding yard |

**Explain more**

- Exams ask "which method applies where work is done to customer order, usually inside a factory or workshop, and each order is of short duration?" The answer is **job costing**. Contract costing is the trap: it is also order-based, but contracts are long and usually on site. Process costing is for continuous, identical output.
- The **materials requisition note** records the issue of direct material to a specific job. A purchase requisition is a different document: it asks the buying department to order goods from a supplier.
- **Batch costing is a form of job costing.** The job costing method can be applied to batches: the batch is treated as the job.
- **Advantages:** shows which jobs are profitable; gives cost records for future quotations; helps control because spoilage and defective work can be traced to a job; suits cost-plus pricing.
- **Limitations:** detailed record-keeping makes it time-consuming and expensive; costs of past jobs go out of date when prices change; inefficiencies may be charged to a job that did not cause them.
- Example: a Yaba print shop receives an order for 2,000 wedding programmes. It opens Job 318, records paper issued on requisitions marked "318", records operators' hours on job cards marked "318", and adds overheads at its predetermined rate. When the job is finished the cost sheet shows the total cost and the profit on the job.

### Lesson 7.2: The job cost sheet: materials, labour, expenses and overheads

**Must know**

**Job cost sheet layout:**

1. Direct materials (issues less returns)
2. Direct labour (hours × rate, per department)
3. Direct expenses (e.g. hire of special equipment for this job)
4. **= Prime cost**
5. Production overheads absorbed (predetermined rate × actual base)
6. **= Production (factory) cost**
7. Administration, selling and distribution overheads (often a % of production cost)
8. **= Total cost**
9. Profit
10. **= Selling price**

- A typical job cost contains **actual** costs for direct material and direct labour, but production overheads are **absorbed at a predetermined rate** (predetermined rate × actual hours), **not** actual overheads; **non-production overheads are often added as a percentage of total production cost**. So the statement "a typical job cost contains actual costs for material, labour and production overheads" is **false**.
- **Production overhead absorbed** = Predetermined rate × Actual base (machine hours, labour hours or % of direct labour cost).
- **Idle time:** paid hours in which no work is done. If idle time is a % of hours **paid**: Hours paid = Productive hours ÷ (1 − idle %). If idle time is a % of **productive** hours: Hours paid = Productive hours × (1 + idle %).
- **Labour cost of a job** = Hours paid × Rate per hour paid. **Rate per hour paid** = Total labour cost ÷ Hours paid.
- **Overtime premium:** charged to the job only if the customer asked for the rush work; otherwise it is a production overhead. Normal idle time is built into the job's labour cost or treated as overhead; abnormal idle time goes to costing profit and loss.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 1 (full job cost sheet).** An Ikeja furniture workshop completes Job 47, a boardroom table.
- Materials issued ₦185,000; surplus returned to stores ₦12,000.
- Labour: Cutting 40 hours at ₦1,500; Finishing 25 hours at ₦1,200.
- Hire of a special spray machine for this job: ₦8,000.
- Overheads: Cutting ₦800 per machine hour (job used 30 machine hours); Finishing 50% of direct labour cost.
- Administration and selling: 20% of production cost. Profit: 25% on total cost.

| Item | ₦ |
|---|---|
| Direct materials (185,000 − 12,000) | 173,000 |
| Direct labour: Cutting 40 × 1,500 | 60,000 |
| Direct labour: Finishing 25 × 1,200 | 30,000 |
| Direct expenses (spray machine hire) | 8,000 |
| **Prime cost** | **271,000** |
| Overhead: Cutting 30 × 800 | 24,000 |
| Overhead: Finishing 50% × 30,000 | 15,000 |
| **Production cost** | **310,000** |
| Admin and selling 20% × 310,000 | 62,000 |
| **Total cost** | **372,000** |
| Profit 25% × 372,000 | 93,000 |
| **Selling price** | **465,000** |

Note: materials returned reduce the job's cost; the machine-hour rate is applied to machine hours, not labour hours.

**Worked example 2 (idle time as a % of hours paid).** A job needs 1,800 productive hours. Idle time is expected to be 10% of hours paid. The rate is ₦1,000 per hour paid.
1. Productive hours are 90% of hours paid, so hours paid = 1,800 ÷ 0.90 = **2,000 hours**.
2. Labour cost = 2,000 × ₦1,000 = **₦2,000,000**.
3. Working backwards: if you are told the budgeted labour cost is ₦2,000,000 and asked the rate per hour paid, divide by hours **paid**: ₦2,000,000 ÷ 2,000 = **₦1,000**. Dividing by productive hours (₦2,000,000 ÷ 1,800 = ₦1,111.11) gives the cost per productive hour, not the rate paid.

**Worked example 3 (idle time as a % of productive hours).** Same job, but idle time is 25% **of productive hours**. Hours paid = 1,800 × 1.25 = 2,250; cost at ₦1,000 = ₦2,250,000. Read the wording carefully: "25% of hours paid" means divide by 0.75; "25% of productive hours" means multiply by 1.25.

- Traps: do not charge general overtime premium to whichever job happened to be worked in the evening; it is an overhead spread over all jobs. Do not include indirect materials (glue, sandpaper) on the requisition for a job; they are overheads absorbed through the rate.
- **Work in progress:** at the period end, the cost sheets of unfinished jobs give the value of work in progress. On completion the job's cost is transferred to finished goods (or cost of sales), and profit on the job = selling price − total cost.

### Lesson 7.3: Quotations, mark-up and margin

**Must know**

- **Quotation (estimate):** the price offered to a customer **before** the job is done, built from estimated costs on a job cost sheet plus the profit required.
- **Mark-up (profit on cost):** Selling price = Total cost × (1 + mark-up %). Total cost = Selling price ÷ (1 + mark-up %).
- **Margin (profit on sales):** Selling price = Total cost ÷ (1 − margin %). Total cost = Selling price × (1 − margin %).
- Mark-up 25% = margin 20%; mark-up 33⅓% = margin 25%. Margin % = Mark-up % ÷ (1 + mark-up %).
- **Prime cost** = Total cost − Overheads (when total cost = prime cost + overheads).

**Explain more**

**Worked example 4 (mark-up vs margin on the same cost).** Estimated total cost of a job is ₦80,000.
- 25% mark-up on cost: price = 80,000 × 1.25 = **₦100,000** (profit ₦20,000).
- 25% margin on sales: price = 80,000 ÷ 0.75 = **₦106,666.67** (profit ₦26,666.67, rounded to kobo). Check: 26,666.67 ÷ 106,666.67 = 25%.

**Worked example 5 (working back to prime cost).** An Aba shoe factory prices jobs by adding overheads to prime cost and then adding 30% of total cost as profit. Job K12 sold for ₦2,600 and was charged overheads of ₦450.
1. Price = Total cost × 1.30, so total cost = 2,600 ÷ 1.30 = **₦2,000**.
2. Prime cost = Total cost − Overheads = 2,000 − 450 = **₦1,550**.

Trap: treating the 30% as a margin gives total cost = 2,600 × 0.70 = ₦1,820 and prime cost ₦1,370, which is wrong here because the profit is a percentage of **cost**. Another trap is taking 30% off the selling price as if the profit were ₦780 and then forgetting to remove overheads. Always ask: "30% of what?"

- A quotation that is too high loses the order; one too low wins work that makes a loss. That is why accurate past job cost sheets matter.
- Quotations normally use **predetermined** overhead rates, because actual overheads are not known until the period ends.

### Lesson 7.4: Time and materials pricing

**Must know**

- **Time and materials pricing:** a pricing method used mainly by service and repair businesses (mechanics, plumbers, electricians, generator repairers). The customer is charged:
  1. a **labour (time) charge** = hours worked × charge-out rate per hour, where the rate covers wages, overheads and profit; plus
  2. a **materials charge** = cost of parts and materials used + a materials **handling (loading) mark-up** covering ordering, storage and profit on materials.
- **Price** = (Hours × Charge-out rate) + Materials cost × (1 + handling %).

**Explain more**

**Worked example 6.** A generator repair shop in Surulere charges ₦3,000 per labour hour and adds 15% to the cost of parts. A repair takes 6 hours and uses parts costing ₦40,000.
1. Time charge = 6 × ₦3,000 = ₦18,000.
2. Materials charge = ₦40,000 × 1.15 = ₦46,000.
3. Price = 18,000 + 46,000 = **₦64,000**.

- The charge-out rate is higher than the wage rate because it also recovers overheads and profit; the mechanic may earn only ₦1,000 an hour.
- Strength: easy to explain and fair when the time needed is uncertain. Weakness: a slow worker raises the bill, so customers often ask for an estimate first.

### Lesson 7.5: Batch costing

**Must know**

- **Batch:** a separate, readily identifiable group of identical units that keeps its identity throughout production.
- **Batch costing:** a form of job costing in which the **batch is treated as the job (cost unit)**. Costs are collected on a batch cost sheet with a batch number, then:
- **Cost per unit** = Total batch cost ÷ Number of units in the batch.
- **Used in:** garments, toys, shoes, tyres, drugs and tablets, printed exercise books, biscuits.

**Features:**
1. Identical units are made together, and costing each unit separately is uneconomical.
2. Each batch gets a number; materials, labour and overheads are recorded batch by batch.
3. **Set-up costs** are incurred once per batch, whatever its size, so bigger batches spread them over more units.

| Job costing | Batch costing |
|---|---|
| Cost unit is one job | Cost unit is a batch of identical units |
| Each job is different | Units in a batch are identical |
| Cost found per job | Cost found per batch, then per unit |

- **Economic batch quantity (EBQ):** the batch size that minimises set-up plus holding cost. In its simplest form (the whole batch goes into stock at once), EBQ = √(2 × D × S ÷ H), where D = annual demand, S = set-up cost per batch, H = holding cost per unit per year.

**Explain more**

**Worked example 7 (batch cost sheet).** A Lagos printer makes a batch of 500 branded exercise books: materials ₦150,000, direct labour ₦60,000, machine set-up ₦15,000, design ₦25,000. Production overhead is 40% of direct labour; admin and selling are 10% of production cost; profit is 20% of selling price.

| Item | ₦ |
|---|---|
| Materials + labour + set-up + design | 250,000 |
| Production overhead 40% × 60,000 | 24,000 |
| **Production cost** | **274,000** |
| Admin and selling 10% | 27,400 |
| **Total cost of batch** | **301,400** |
| Selling price = 301,400 ÷ 0.80 | 376,750 |

Cost per book = 301,400 ÷ 500 = **₦602.80**; price per book = 376,750 ÷ 500 = **₦753.50**.

**Worked example 8 (scaling a typical batch).** A typical batch of 100 bags costs: materials ₦6,000, labour ₦2,000, set-up ₦1,500 (once per batch). Design ₦3,000 is once per order. A customer orders 600 bags made in one run. Overhead is 20% of labour; admin 5% of production cost; profit 25% of sales.
1. Materials 6 × 6,000 = 36,000; labour 6 × 2,000 = 12,000; set-up 1,500 (one run); design 3,000. Prime cost = **₦52,500**.
2. Overhead 20% × 12,000 = 2,400; production cost = 54,900.
3. Admin 5% × 54,900 = 2,745; total cost = **₦57,645**.
4. Price = 57,645 ÷ 0.75 = **₦76,860**, i.e. ₦128.10 a bag.

Trap: multiplying the whole typical-batch cost by 6 (₦75,000 prime cost) wrongly repeats set-up and design.

**Worked example 9 (EBQ).** A Kano biscuit factory sells 36,000 packs a year. Each set-up costs ₦5,000; holding cost is ₦40 per pack per year.
1. EBQ = √(2 × 36,000 × 5,000 ÷ 40) = √9,000,000 = **3,000 packs**.
2. Batches a year = 36,000 ÷ 3,000 = 12; set-up cost = 12 × ₦5,000 = ₦60,000; holding cost = 3,000 ÷ 2 × ₦40 = ₦60,000. They are equal at the EBQ, as with EOQ.

### One-minute recap

- Job costing: customer order, inside a factory or workshop, short duration; each job costed separately. Contract costing is long and on site.
- Materials requisition note = direct material issued to a specific job; job cards record labour time.
- Job cost sheet: direct materials + direct labour + direct expenses = prime cost; + production overhead = production cost; + non-production overhead (often % of production cost) = total cost; + profit = price.
- Idle time % of hours paid: hours paid = productive hours ÷ (1 − idle %); rate per hour paid = labour cost ÷ hours paid.
- Mark-up: price = cost × (1 + %). Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − %). Prime cost = total cost − overheads.
- Time and materials: hours × charge-out rate + materials × (1 + handling %).
- Batch costing is job costing applied to batches; cost per unit = batch cost ÷ units. Set-up is once per batch.
- EBQ = √(2DS ÷ H).

### Theory practice

**Question:** A Lekki tailoring workshop is quoting for Job 92: materials ₦45,000; 120 labour hours at ₦500 an hour; production overhead ₦300 per labour hour; administration overhead 10% of production cost. The workshop wants a profit of 20% of the selling price. Calculate the quoted price.

*Solution:*
1. Direct labour = 120 × ₦500 = ₦60,000; prime cost = 45,000 + 60,000 = ₦105,000.
2. Production overhead = 120 × ₦300 = ₦36,000; production cost = ₦141,000.
3. Administration = 10% × 141,000 = ₦14,100; total cost = ₦155,100.
4. Profit is a margin on sales: price = 155,100 ÷ 0.80 = **₦193,875** (profit ₦38,775).
