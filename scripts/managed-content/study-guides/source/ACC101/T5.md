## Topic 5: Cash book and bank reconciliation

**What you must know:** what the cash book is and its types (single, two-column, three-column, petty cash) and the imprest system; contra entries; discounts allowed and received; the bank statement; the causes of differences between the two; the adjusted cash book; and both layouts of the bank reconciliation statement.

### Lesson 5.1: The cash book and its types

**Must know**

- **Cash book:** an asset account that keeps records of transactions through cash or through the bank. It is both a **journal** (book of original entry, recording transactions as they occur) and a **ledger** account (entries follow the double entry principle). It is **the book that shows receipts, payments and the balances of cash and bank**.
- **Debit side** = receipts of money (increases). **Credit side** = payments of money (decreases).
- **Contra entry:** an entry that appears on **both** the debit and credit sides of the cash book, so the whole double entry is completed inside the cash book. Examples: cash paid into the bank (Dr bank column, Cr cash column); cash withdrawn from the bank for office use (Dr cash, Cr bank). Marked "C" in the folio column.

**Types of cash book** (types 1 to 3 are the classification by the number of money columns on each side; the petty cash book is kept alongside them):

1. **Single column:** one column; a simple cash account or bank account.
2. **Double (two-column):** two columns, **cash** and **bank**.
3. **Three-column:** an extension of the two-column book with a **discount** column on each side.
4. **Petty cash book:** for small cash payments, usually run on the **imprest** system (Lesson 5.2).

- **Balances:** the cash column can only have a debit balance (you cannot pay out more notes than you hold). A **debit** balance in the bank column = money in the bank. A **credit** balance in the bank column = a **bank overdraft**.
- **Cash discount:** a reduction in the sum paid if payment is made **within an agreed period**, to encourage early settlement. Given to customers = **discount allowed** (an expense; debit side of the cash book). Received from suppliers = **discount received** (income; credit side).
- **Trade discount:** a reduction in list price for trade or bulk buying. It is never entered in the cash book or any account.

**Explain more**

- The discount columns are **memorandum** columns: they are not balanced. At an agreed period their totals are **transferred** to the ledger: total of the debit-side column → Dr discount allowed account; total of the credit-side column → Cr discount received account.
- **Not** recorded in the cash book: anything with no movement of money, such as **depreciation**, credit sales and purchases, or goods taken as drawings. Bank charges, salaries and receipts from debtors are recorded because money moves.
- **Discount calculation trap:** list price ₦50,000, 2.5% trade discount, 5% cash discount. Trade discount comes off first: ₦50,000 − ₦1,250 = ₦48,750 (invoice amount). Cash discount = 5% × ₦48,750 = **₦2,437.50**. Amount paid = **₦46,312.50**. ₦1,250 is the trade discount, not the cash discount.
- **Worked example: Femi Stores, three-column cash book, May 2026.** 1 May balances: cash ₦20,000, bank ₦150,000. 3 May: received a cheque from Ade ₦47,500 in full settlement of ₦50,000. 6 May: cash sales ₦35,000. 8 May: paid ₦30,000 cash into the bank. 10 May: paid Bello by cheque ₦76,000 in full settlement of ₦80,000. 12 May: paid wages in cash ₦12,000. 14 May: withdrew ₦10,000 from the bank for office use.

### Lesson 5.2: Petty cash book and the imprest system

**Must know**

- **Petty cash book:** records **small payments made in cash** (postage, transport, cleaning, refreshments, stationery). It is kept by a **petty cashier**, often a junior member of staff, and has a total column plus **analysis columns** for each type of expense.
- **Imprest system:** the petty cashier starts each period with an agreed **float** (the imprest). Each payment is supported by a signed **petty cash voucher**. At the end of the period (or when cash runs low) the main cashier **reimburses exactly the amount spent**, restoring the float to its agreed level.
- **Petty cash account is debited** when the fund is first **established** and every time it is **replenished** (reimbursed). It is **credited** each time money is spent, and when the float is reduced.
- At the end of the period the analysis column totals are **debited** to the expense accounts in the general (nominal) ledger.

**Explain more**

- Cash in hand + vouchers always equals the imprest, so a spot count quickly detects theft.
- **Example:** imprest ₦20,000. During the week: stamps ₦1,500, transport ₦3,200, cleaning ₦2,000, refreshments ₦4,800, stationery ₦2,500.

- Before reimbursement, cash in hand = ₦20,000 − ₦14,000 = ₦6,000. Reimbursement = amount spent = ₦14,000, restoring the float to ₦20,000.
- **Trap:** if the petty cashier also received money (say staff paid ₦1,000 for photocopying), the reimbursement is spending less that receipt.
- **Trap:** petty cash is cash, not bank. Errors in the petty cash book can **never** cause a difference between the cash book (bank column) and the bank statement.

### Lesson 5.3: The bank statement and causes of differences

**Must know**

- **Bank statement:** the record kept by the bank of its transactions with the customer; a summary of the account for the period. To the bank, the customer's money is a **liability** (the bank owes it), so the statement is the mirror image of the cash book:
  - **Credit** side = receipts into the account (lodgements, direct credits, dividends).
  - **Debit** side = payments out (cheques paid, standing orders, direct debits, bank charges, dishonoured cheques).
- A credit balance on the statement ("in favour") = money in the bank. A debit balance on the statement = overdraft.
- **Bank reconciliation statement (BRS):** a schedule that accounts for the differences between the balance in the **bank column of the cash book** and the balance on the **bank statement**.

**Reasons for bank reconciliation (4):**

1. It affords the opportunity to update the cash book.
2. It serves as a means of correcting errors.
3. Fraud can be detected and prevented.
4. It enables the business to highlight errors committed by the bankers.

**Causes of differences (3 groups):**

1. **In the cash book but not on the bank statement (timing differences):**
   - **Uncredited cheques (uncredited lodgements):** cheques received and paid in, debited in the cash book, not yet credited by the bank.
   - **Unpresented cheques:** cheques issued by the business and credited in the cash book, not yet presented to the bank for payment.
2. **On the bank statement but not in the cash book:**
   - **Direct debits:** standing orders, bank charges, interest on overdraft, dishonoured cheques.
   - **Direct credits:** credit transfers from customers, dividends received, interest received.
3. **Errors:** in the cash book, in the bank statement, or in both.

**Explain more**

- **Standing order:** a written instruction by a customer to the bank to pay a fixed sum regularly to a named payee (rent, insurance premium, club subscription). **Bank charges** (service charges, commission): the bank's fee for running a current account. **Credit (direct) transfer:** money paid by a payer straight into the business's account.
- **Dishonoured cheque** ("returned", "refer to drawer"): a cheque the paying bank refuses to honour. It is returned, so the bank debits the account to reverse the amount it had credited. Reasons (5):
  1. Insufficient funds in the drawer's account.
  2. Irregular signature, or no signature.
  3. Amounts in figures and in words do not agree.
  4. No date, or the cheque is post-dated.
  5. An alteration not endorsed by the drawer.
- **Other names:** unpresented = outstanding cheques; uncredited cheques = outstanding lodgements (deposits in transit).
- **Procedure (ticking):** check the casting of both. Tick cash book **debits** against statement **credits**, and cash book **credits** against statement **debits**. Then:
  - Unticked on the **credit** side of the cash book = **unpresented cheques**.
  - Unticked on the **debit** side of the cash book = **uncredited cheques**.
  - Unticked on the **credit** side of the statement = **direct credits** (debit them to the cash book).
  - Unticked on the **debit** side of the statement = **standing orders, bank charges, dishonoured cheques** (credit them to the cash book). A direct transfer is never here.
- If the opening balances of the two records differ, reconcile last period's outstanding items first. Unpresented cheques and lodgements from last period that clear this month are already explained.
- **Reasoning trap:** cash book debit balance ₦1,000, statement credit balance ₦900. The bank figure is lower, so the bank has taken out something not yet in the cash book (e.g. a **standing order**). Unpresented cheques, bank interest received and direct credits would all make the bank figure **higher**.

### Lesson 5.4: Adjusted cash book and the bank reconciliation statement

**Must know**

- The BRS can be prepared **straightaway** (from the original cash book balance) or after an **adjusted cash book**. Exams usually ask for both.
- **Adjusted cash book (5 kinds of items):** bank charges, standing orders (direct debits), credit transfers and direct receipts (dividend, interest), dishonoured cheques, errors made by the business.

| Debit (increase balance) | Credit (reduce balance) |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (if favourable) | Balance b/d (if overdraft) |
| Direct credits: credit transfers, dividends, interest received | Direct debits: standing orders, bank charges, dishonoured cheques |
| Errors that reduced the cash book balance | Errors that increased the cash book balance |
| | Balance c/d |

- **BRS after an adjusted cash book (3 items only):** uncredited cheques, unpresented cheques, errors in the bank statement.

| Starting from bank statement balance | Starting from (adjusted) cash book balance |
|---|---|
| **Add** uncredited cheques | **Add** unpresented cheques |
| **Less** unpresented cheques | **Less** uncredited cheques |
| Add/less bank errors | Less/add bank errors (reversed) |
| = Balance as per (adjusted) cash book | = Balance as per bank statement |

- **Straightaway BRS from the bank statement:** add uncredited cheques, direct debits and errors; less unpresented cheques, direct credits and errors; = balance as per cash book. From the cash book, every sign is reversed.
- Rules: use the balance the question gives; a balance that is not normal (credit in the cash book, debit on the statement) is an **overdraft**, shown in brackets; compare only the **bank column** with the statement.

**Explain more**

- **Worked example: Kemi Stores, 30 June 2026.** Cash book (bank column) shows a debit balance of ₦84,500; the bank statement shows ₦93,900 in favour. On comparison:
  1. Cheques issued not yet presented: Bello ₦23,000, Oni ₦12,500.
  2. A cheque of ₦18,000 paid in on 30 June not yet credited by the bank.
  3. Bank charges ₦1,500 and a standing order for insurance ₦6,000 appear only on the statement.
  4. A dividend of ₦9,000 was received directly by the bank.
  5. A cheque of ₦4,000 from Tunde, a customer, was dishonoured.
  6. A cheque payment of ₦7,300 to a supplier was entered in the cash book as ₦3,700.
  7. The bank debited a cheque of ₦2,000 belonging to another customer to Kemi's account in error.

**The same done straightaway, from the original cash book balance:** ₦84,500 + unpresented ₦35,500 + dividend ₦9,000 = ₦129,000; less uncredited ₦18,000, bank charges ₦1,500, standing order ₦6,000, dishonoured ₦4,000, cash book error ₦3,600, bank error ₦2,000 (total ₦35,100) = **₦93,900**, the bank statement balance.

- **Why the bank error is added to the bank balance:** the bank wrongly took ₦2,000 out, so the true bank figure is higher. A bank error is corrected only in the BRS, never in the cash book.
- **Overdraft example:** adjusted cash book overdraft ₦32,500, unpresented ₦65,900, uncredited ₦18,500. From the cash book: (₦32,500) + ₦65,900 = ₦33,400; − ₦18,500 = **₦14,900** in favour per bank statement. The overdraft b/d sits on the **credit** side of the adjusted cash book.
- **Traps:** a direct transfer goes to the **debit** side of the adjusted cash book; bank charges always **reduce** its balance. Items needing an adjusting entry in the cash book are those the bank has recorded first (interest, charges, standing orders, direct credits). Unpresented and uncredited cheques correct themselves when the bank processes them.

### One-minute recap

- Cash book: asset account, both journal and ledger; debit = receipts, credit = payments; contra entries appear on both sides.
- Types by number of columns (3): single column, two-column (cash and bank), three-column (adds discount); plus the petty cash book.
- Credit balance in the bank column = overdraft. Depreciation never passes through the cash book.
- Cash discount is for paying within an agreed period: discount allowed (debit side) and discount received (credit side), totals transferred to the ledger. Trade discount comes off first and is never recorded.
- Imprest: fixed float, vouchers, reimburse exactly what was spent; petty cash is debited when set up and when replenished.
- Bank statement is the mirror image: credit = lodgements, debit = withdrawals.
- Causes: uncredited and unpresented cheques; direct debits and direct credits; errors.
- Adjusted cash book takes bank charges, standing orders, direct credits, dishonoured cheques and cash book errors.
- BRS after adjustment takes only uncredited cheques, unpresented cheques and bank errors.
- From the bank statement: add uncredited, less unpresented. From the cash book: add unpresented, less uncredited.

### Theory practice

**Question:** On 31 March 2026 Tola's cash book shows an overdraft of ₦25,000. Bank charges of ₦2,000 are not in the cash book, and the bank received a dividend of ₦10,000 for Tola. Unpresented cheques total ₦30,000 and a lodgement of ₦12,000 is not yet credited. Prepare the adjusted cash book balance and a BRS starting from it.

*Worked solution:*

- Adjusted cash book: overdraft b/d ₦25,000 (credit side) + bank charges ₦2,000 (credit) − dividend ₦10,000 (debit) = **overdraft ₦17,000**, balance c/d on the debit side.
- BRS: balance as per adjusted cash book (₦17,000); add unpresented cheques ₦30,000 = ₦13,000; less uncredited lodgement ₦12,000 = **₦1,000** in favour, the balance as per bank statement.
