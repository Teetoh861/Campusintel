## Topic 8: Final accounts of a sole trader

**What you must know:** the trading account, profit and loss account (income statement) and balance sheet (statement of financial position); cost of sales, gross profit and net profit; carriage, returns and drawings; current and non-current items; working capital; preparing final accounts from a trial balance with adjustments.

### Lesson 8.1: The final accounts and what each one shows

**Must know**

- **Final accounts:** the statements prepared at the end of a period from the trial balance (after end-of-year adjustments) to show the result and position of the business.
- **3 final accounts of a sole trader:**

1. **Trading account:** compares net sales with the cost of the goods sold to find **gross profit**.
2. **Profit and loss account (income statement):** takes gross profit, adds other income, deducts expenses to find **net profit** (or net loss). It reports the part of the change in owner's equity that comes from **revenues and expenses** in the period.
3. **Balance sheet (statement of financial position):** lists assets, liabilities and capital to show the **financial position at a date**. Its structure follows the accounting equation **Assets = Liabilities + Capital (equity)**.

| | Trading, profit and loss account | Balance sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | A **period** ("for the year ended 31 December") | One **date** ("as at 31 December") |
| Shows | Performance: gross and net profit | Position: assets, liabilities, capital |
| Items | Revenue and expense (nominal) accounts | Asset, liability and capital (real and personal) accounts |
| After posting | Accounts are closed off | Balances are carried forward |

**Explain more**

- The trading account and the profit and loss account are usually presented together as one statement: the **trading, profit and loss account for the year ended ...**.
- **Trap:** the trial balance also balances, but it lists every ledger balance, including income and expenses. It is a check on arithmetic, **not** a final account. A cash flow statement shows cash movements, not the equation.
- **Income statement headings:** revenue (sales), cost of sales, gross profit, distribution (selling) costs, administrative expenses, operating profit or loss. "Trading account" is the bookkeeping account used to find gross profit; it is **not** a reporting heading of the statement.
- **Where net profit goes:** in a sole trader's books, net profit is added to **capital** (a net loss is deducted). In a company (corporation), net income is closed to **retained earnings**, the part of shareholders' (stockholders') equity that holds profits kept in the business; dividends are paid out of it.

### Lesson 8.2: The trading account, cost of sales and gross profit

**Must know**

- **Net sales (turnover)** = sales − returns inwards (sales returns).
- **Net purchases** = purchases + carriage inwards − returns outwards (purchases returns).
- **Cost of sales (cost of goods sold)** = opening stock + net purchases − closing stock.
- **Cost of goods available for sale** = opening stock + net purchases.
- **Gross profit** = net sales − cost of sales. If cost of sales is bigger, the result is a **gross loss**.

**Where the trading items go**

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Returns inwards (sales returns) | Deduct from **sales** |
| Returns outwards (purchases returns) | Deduct from **purchases** |
| Carriage inwards (on goods bought) | **Add** to purchases (part of cost of sales) |
| Carriage outwards (on goods sold) | **Expense** in the profit and loss account (selling cost) |
| Wages to get goods ready for sale (direct wages) | Add to cost of sales when the question places them there |
| Goods taken by the owner | Dr Drawings, Cr Purchases (at **cost**); never a sale |
| Closing stock | Deduct in cost of sales; current asset in the balance sheet |

**Gross profit percentages**

- **Margin** = gross profit ÷ sales × 100. **Mark-up** = gross profit ÷ cost of sales × 100.
- A 25% margin means cost is 75% of sales, so mark-up = 25/75 = 33⅓%.

**Explain more**

**Layout (vertical)**: Kola's trading account for the year ended 31 December 2025

| Sales | | 820,000 |
| Less: returns inwards | | (20,000) |

In the horizontal (T) form, opening stock, purchases, carriage inwards and gross profit sit on the **debit** side; sales and closing stock on the **credit** side, with returns shown as deductions from purchases and sales.

**Quick computations exams set**

- Opening stock ₦340,000, purchases ₦900,000, carriage inwards ₦100,000, carriage outwards ₦150,000, closing stock ₦420,000. Cost of goods sold = 340,000 + 900,000 + 100,000 − 420,000 = **₦920,000**. Carriage outwards is ignored (it is a P&L expense).
- Sales ₦8,200, opening stock ₦1,300, purchases ₦6,400, carriage inwards ₦200, closing stock ₦900. Cost of goods sold = 1,300 + 6,400 + 200 − 900 = **₦7,000**. Sales never enter cost of goods sold.
- Opening stock ₦8,500, purchases ₦30,700, wages ₦4,800, returns inwards ₦5,200, closing stock ₦9,000. Cost of goods sold = 8,500 + 30,700 + 4,800 − 9,000 = **₦35,000**. Returns inwards belong to sales, not cost of goods sold.
- Purchases ₦90,000, freight on them ₦6,000, unsold goods ₦12,000, sales ₦120,000. Cost of goods sold = ₦84,000; gross profit = **₦36,000**. Freight inwards is carriage inwards.
- Closing stock ₦1,000 higher than opening, net sales ₦25,000, purchases ₦14,000. Cost of goods sold = 14,000 − 1,000 = ₦13,000; gross profit = ₦12,000 = **48%** of net sales.

**Working backwards**

- Margin 20% of sales, cost of goods ₦120,000: sales = 120,000 ÷ 0.8 = ₦150,000; gross profit = **₦30,000** (not 20% of cost).
- Find closing stock: opening ₦990,000, purchases ₦3,740,000, carriage inwards ₦22,000, sales ₦5,280,000, margin 25%. Cost of sales = 75% × 5,280,000 = ₦3,960,000. Closing stock = 990,000 + 3,740,000 + 22,000 − 3,960,000 = **₦792,000**.
- Sales ₦300,000, margin 20%, purchases ₦240,000, opening stock ₦20,000. Cost of sales = ₦240,000, so closing stock = 20,000 + 240,000 − 240,000 = **₦20,000**.

**Errors and gross profit**

- Closing stock **overstated** → cost of sales too low → gross profit **overstated**.
- Opening stock, purchases or carriage inwards **overstated** → cost of sales too high → gross profit **understated**.
- **Contra (reduction) accounts:** returns inwards reduce sales; returns outwards reduce purchases; accumulated depreciation and provision for bad and doubtful debts reduce assets. **Carriage inwards is not** a contra account; it adds to cost.

### Lesson 8.3: The profit and loss account and net profit

**Must know**

- **Net profit** = gross profit + other income − expenses. If expenses exceed gross profit plus other income, the result is a **net loss**.
- **Other income (credit side):** discount received, commission received, rent received, interest received, profit on disposal, decrease in provision for bad debts.
- **Expenses (debit side):** salaries and wages, rent and rates, insurance, lighting and heating, carriage outwards, advertising, postage, stationery, general expenses, discount allowed, bad debts, increase in provision for bad debts, provision for discount on debtors, depreciation, loan interest, loss on disposal.
- **Drawings are not an expense.** Cash or goods taken by the owner are deducted from **capital** in the balance sheet, so they never reduce net profit.

**How each adjustment hits both statements**

| Adjustment | Profit and loss account | Balance sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Closing stock | Deducted in cost of sales | Current asset |
| Accrued (owing) expense | Add to the expense | Current liability |
| Prepaid expense | Deduct from the expense | Current asset |
| Accrued income (in arrears) | Add to the income | Current asset |
| Income received in advance | Deduct from the income | Current liability |
| Depreciation for the year | Expense | Added to accumulated depreciation; NBV = cost − accumulated depreciation |
| Bad debts written off | Expense | Deduct from debtors |
| Provision for bad debts | Only the **increase** is an expense (a decrease is income) | Full new provision deducted from debtors |
| Goods taken by owner | Reduce purchases | Add to drawings |

**Explain more**

- **Increase in provision:** debtors after write-off ₦400,000, provision 5% = ₦20,000, old provision ₦15,000. Charge only ₦5,000 to profit and loss; show debtors ₦400,000 − ₦20,000 = ₦380,000.
- **Provision for discount on debtors** is worked on debtors **after** deducting the provision for bad debts; it is charged to profit and loss and also deducted from debtors.
- **Drawings of goods:** the owner takes goods costing ₦15,000. Dr Drawings ₦15,000, Cr Purchases ₦15,000. Purchases in the trading account fall by ₦15,000 and drawings in the balance sheet rise by ₦15,000.
- **Trap:** carriage outwards, discount allowed and bad debts are profit and loss expenses, not trading items. Discount received is income, not a reduction of purchases.

### Lesson 8.4: The balance sheet

**Must know**

- **Non-current (fixed) assets:** held for use over more than one year, not for resale.
  - **Tangible:** land and buildings, plant and machinery, motor vehicles, furniture and fittings, equipment. Shown at cost less accumulated depreciation (net book value).
  - **Intangible:** long-term assets with **no physical existence** whose value lies in rights: goodwill, patents, trademarks, brands.
  - **Long-term investments** are shown separately.
- **Current assets:** cash or items expected to be turned into cash or used **within one year**: stock (inventory), debtors less provision (trade receivables), prepayments, accrued income, cash at bank, cash in hand.
- **Current liabilities:** due within one year: creditors (trade payables), accrued expenses, income received in advance, bank overdraft.
- **Non-current (long-term) liabilities:** due after one year: long-term bank loan, debentures.
- **Capital section:** opening capital + net profit (− net loss) + further capital − drawings = **closing capital**.
- **Working capital (net current assets)** = current assets − current liabilities.
- **Balance check:** net assets (total assets − all liabilities) = closing capital.

**Explain more**

**Layout (vertical)**: the order of the headings

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Each at cost, accumulated depreciation, net book value |
| Current assets | Stock, debtors less provision, prepayments, bank, cash |
| Less: current liabilities | Creditors, accruals, bank overdraft |
| Working capital | Current assets − current liabilities |
| Less: non-current liabilities | Long-term loan |
| **Net assets** | Must equal the capital total below |
| Financed by: capital | Opening capital + net profit − drawings |

- **Trap:** working capital uses only current items. "Current assets over fixed assets" or "fixed assets over current liabilities" are wrong.
- **Missing figure:** fixed assets are double current assets (₦300,000) and half of capital; investments ₦400,000; no other items. Fixed assets = ₦600,000; capital = ₦1,200,000; total assets = 600,000 + 300,000 + 400,000 = ₦1,300,000; current liabilities = 1,300,000 − 1,200,000 = **₦100,000**.

**Must know**

**Steps**

1. Read every adjustment and note its **two** effects (Lesson 8.3 table).

3. Profit and loss account: gross profit + other income − adjusted expenses = net profit.
4. Balance sheet: adjusted assets and liabilities; capital + net profit − drawings.

### One-minute recap

- Three final accounts: trading account (gross profit), profit and loss account (net profit), balance sheet (position at a date, built on Assets = Liabilities + Capital).
- Cost of sales = opening stock + purchases + carriage inwards − returns outwards − closing stock.
- Gross profit = net sales (sales − returns inwards) − cost of sales.
- Carriage inwards adds to cost of sales; carriage outwards is a P&L expense.
- Net profit = gross profit + other income − expenses; drawings are deducted from capital, not expenses.
- Overstated closing stock overstates gross profit.
- Current assets turn into cash within a year; intangible assets have no physical form.
- Working capital = current assets − current liabilities.
- Every adjustment has two effects: one in profit and loss, one in the balance sheet.
- Closing capital = opening capital + net profit − drawings, and it equals net assets.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Distinguish between gross profit and net profit. Then compute both from: sales ₦500,000, returns inwards ₦20,000, opening stock ₦60,000, purchases ₦300,000, carriage inwards ₦10,000, carriage outwards ₦8,000, closing stock ₦70,000, rent ₦40,000, drawings ₦25,000.

*Worked solution:* Gross profit is net sales less cost of sales (found in the trading account); net profit is gross profit plus other income less all expenses (found in the profit and loss account). Net sales = 500,000 − 20,000 = ₦480,000. Cost of sales = 60,000 + 300,000 + 10,000 − 70,000 = ₦300,000. Gross profit = **₦180,000**. Net profit = 180,000 − 8,000 (carriage outwards) − 40,000 (rent) = **₦132,000**. Drawings of ₦25,000 are deducted from capital, not from profit.
