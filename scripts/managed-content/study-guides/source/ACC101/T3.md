## Topic 3: The accounting process

**What you must know:** the accounting sequence; source documents and what each records (especially credit and debit notes); the books of original entry and how their totals are posted; the journal's layout and uses; the ledger, the three types of account and the four types of ledger.

### Lesson 3.1: The accounting sequence and source documents

**Must know**

- **The accounting sequence (5 stages):** business transaction (source document) → books of original entry (journal entries) → ledger → trial balance → financial statements.
- **Source documents (accounting documents):** the source of all original information on a business's financial transactions. No transaction is recorded in the books unless a source document evidences it.

**Need for source documents (4):**

1. They serve as evidence of financial transactions, guarding against fraud.
2. They serve as evidence of financial transactions, making audit possible.
3. They are usually signed by the parties, so the transaction cannot easily be denied.
4. Because they are signed by the parties, it is almost impossible to alter them or collude to defraud.

**Main source documents (11):**

| Document | Issued by / purpose | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales invoice** | Seller to buyer (usually credit sales), asking for payment for goods | Sales day book |
| **Purchase invoice** | The same invoice, received by the buyer (same details) | Purchases day book |
| **Credit note** | Seller to buyer, reducing what the buyer owes (goods returned or overcharge); made out in **red** | Returns inward book (issued) / returns outward book (received) |
| **Debit note** | Buyer to supplier asking for a credit note (goods returned); or seller to customer to **increase** the amount owed (undercharge) | Returns outward book (buyer's returns) |
| **Bills** | Issued for **services** rendered on credit (electricity, water rate, telephone) | The expense concerned |
| **Payment voucher** | Authorises payment of an expense; must be authorised before cash is paid | Cash book (credit) |
| **Bank pay-in slip** | Evidence of cash and cheques paid into the bank | Cash book, bank column (debit) |
| **Cheque counterfoil (stub)** | Evidence of payments to creditors by cheque and withdrawals | Cash book, bank column (credit) |
| **Receipt** | Issued for cash received; original to buyer, duplicate kept by seller | Cash book |
| **Bank statement** | Sent by the bank, showing the customer's account and balance | Checking the cash book |
| **Store issue voucher** | Storekeeper's evidence of items issued from store | Store ledger |

**Explain more**

- **Credit note:** issued because goods were damaged before delivery, were of the wrong specification, or the buyer was **overcharged**. Credit note issued = returns on **sales**; credit note received = returns on **purchases**. If returned goods are **replaced**, no credit note is needed.
- **Debit note:** goods returned to a supplier go with a debit note, telling the supplier you have charged (debited) their account in your books; the supplier's credit note then confirms it. A seller who **undercharged** a customer, or left an expense off the invoice, also sends a debit note.
- **Trap:** overcharge = **credit note**; undercharge = **debit note**. A **remittance advice** goes with a payment, a **proforma invoice** is a quotation-style invoice sent before a sale, and a **promissory note** is a written promise to pay.
- **Trade discount** is a reduction in the list price given to trade customers. It appears **only on the invoice**; the books record the net amount. It is never posted to any account, so there is no "trade discount account".
  - Example: goods listed at ₦1,700,000 less 3.5% trade discount. Discount = ₦59,500; net amount recorded and paid = ₦1,640,500.
- **Cash discount** (for prompt payment) is different: it is recorded in the cash book as discount allowed or received (Topic 5).

### Lesson 3.2: Books of original entry (day books)

**Must know**

- **Books of original entry:** also called **day books, journals, books of prime entry** and **subsidiary books**. Transactions are first recorded here daily, then transferred (posted) to the ledger periodically: weekly, fortnightly or monthly, depending on the nature and volume of transactions. Ledger entries are posted **from the subsidiary books**.

**Need for books of original entry (4):**

1. They record the total of transactions in one place rather than in individual accounts.
2. They give an explanation of each transaction (the complete story in one place).
3. They record transactions in chronological order.
4. They help to prevent error: the book's total can be reconciled with the individual accounts.

**Main books of original entry (7):**

| Book | Records | Source document | Posting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales day book** | Credit sales | Sales invoice (duplicate) | Dr each customer (sales ledger); Cr **total** to sales account |
| **Purchases day book** | Credit purchases of goods | Purchase invoice (incoming) | Cr each supplier (purchases ledger); Dr **total** to purchases account |
| **Returns inward (sales returns) book** | Goods returned by customers | Credit note issued | Dr total to returns inward account; Cr each customer |
| **Returns outward (purchases returns) book** | Goods returned to suppliers | Credit note received (with the debit note sent) | Cr total to returns outward account; Dr each supplier |
| **General journal (journal proper)** | Items that are not credit sales or purchases | Various | See Lesson 3.3 |
| **Cash book** | Cash and bank transactions | Receipts, pay-in slips, cheque stubs | Itself part of the ledger |
| **Petty cash book** | Small cash payments | Petty cash vouchers | Itself part of the ledger |

**Explain more**

- The **cash book** (and petty cash book) has a **dual role**: it is a book of prime entry **and** part of the ledger. Its cash and bank columns are the cash and bank accounts themselves, so it is the one subsidiary book where entries are already one side of the double entry.
- The purchases day book is for **goods bought for resale**. Furniture bought on credit **for resale** (a furniture dealer's stock) goes to the purchases day book; furniture bought on credit **for use** goes to the journal proper.
- The sales day book does not describe the goods (the invoice does); the sales returns book does analyse what came back. In a computerised system the books may be stored in computer memory.

**Worked example.** Tola Stores, March 2026:

- Mar 2: Sold goods on credit to Ade Ventures, list price ₦400,000 less 10% trade discount (invoice 101).
- Mar 4: Sold goods on credit to Bisi Ltd ₦250,000 (invoice 102).
- Mar 5: Bought goods on credit from Kalu & Sons ₦300,000 (invoice K45).
- Mar 8: Bisi Ltd returned goods ₦30,000 (credit note CN01 issued).
- Mar 10: Returned goods to Kalu & Sons ₦20,000 (credit note KC7 received).
- Mar 12: Bought goods on credit from Femi Ltd ₦180,000 (invoice F09).

| Sales day book | Inv. | Customer | ₦ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2 | 101 | Ade Ventures | 360,000 |
| Mar 4 | 102 | Bisi Ltd | 250,000 |
| | | **Total (Cr sales account)** | **610,000** |

| Purchases day book | Inv. | Supplier | ₦ |
|---|---|---|---|

### Lesson 3.3: The general journal

**Must know**

- **General journal (journal proper):** records transactions that do not fit the other subsidiary books. Each entry shows (1) the **date**, (2) the **accounts debited and credited** with amounts, (3) a **narration** (brief explanation giving the purpose and authority of the transaction).
- **Layout:** Date | Particulars | Dr | Cr. The account **debited is written first**, the account credited below it (often "To: …"), then the narration ("Being …"). Leave a blank line after each entry.

**Uses of the journal (5):**

1. Opening and closing entries.
2. Transfers from one account to another.
3. Purchases and sales of fixed (non-current) assets on credit.
4. End of period adjustments (Topic 7).
5. Correction of errors (Topic 6).

**Explain more**

**Opening entries** find the opening capital: A = C + L, so **C = A − L**. Seyi's business on 1 January 2026: cash ₦50,000, bank ₦420,000, inventory ₦300,000, furniture ₦230,000, payables ₦120,000.

| Date | Particulars | Dr ₦ | Cr ₦ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/1/26 | Cash in hand | 50,000 | |
| | Cash at bank | 420,000 | |
| | Inventory | 300,000 | |
| | Furniture and fittings | 230,000 | |
| | Payables | | 120,000 |
| | Capital | | 880,000 |
| | *Being opening capital* | **1,000,000** | **1,000,000** |

**Other uses** (Orire Ltd, June 2026):

| Date | Particulars | Dr ₦ | Cr ₦ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/6 | Machinery | 186,000 | |
| | To: Jerry Enterprises (payable) | | 186,000 |
| | *Being machinery bought on credit* | | |
| 8/6 | Jebeleje (receivable) | 360,000 | |

- Reasoning: asset up = Dr, asset down = Cr; liability up = Cr, liability down = Dr.
- **Debt transferred between customers:** Okunade owes ₦600,000 and Bature takes it over: **Dr Bature, Cr Okunade** ₦600,000. Only the journal's narration explains such a transfer clearly.
- **Trap:** equipment bought **on credit** goes to the journal proper, not the purchases day book (not goods for resale) and not the cash book (no cash moved).

### Lesson 3.4: The ledger, types of account and types of ledger

**Must know**

- **Ledger:** the book containing a group of accounts; the permanent record of the assets, liabilities, income, expenses and capital of a business. Entries are posted to it from the subsidiary books. Also called the **principal book of account** (book of final entry).
- **Account:** a record of increases and decreases in one item, in T form: **title**, **debit (left) side**, **credit (right) side**. Each side has columns for Date, Particulars (the other account), **Folio** and Amount.
- **Folio column:** for **reference**: it shows the page or account where the other side of the entry is found.
- **Chart of accounts:** the list of all accounts (names and code numbers) available for posting transactions.

**Importance of the ledger (5):**

1. It keeps permanent records of assets, liabilities, income and expenses.
2. It provides the information needed to prepare the income statement and the balance sheet (statement of financial position).
3. It gives the origin of every transaction and the parties involved.
4. It shows the details of movements in each account (e.g. what was deposited in or withdrawn from the bank, and why).
5. The trial balance is extracted from it at the end of the period.

**Types of account (3):**

| Type | Relates to | Examples | Group |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real** | Tangible things that can be seen, touched and moved (assets) | Cash, motor vehicles, goods, buildings | Impersonal |
| **Personal** | Persons, companies, partnerships | Debtors (receivables), creditors (payables) | Personal |
| **Nominal** | Gains, losses, incomes and expenses | Rent, salaries, electricity, discounts, drawings, sales | Impersonal |

- Principally, accounts are **personal** or **impersonal**; impersonal accounts are **real** or **nominal**.

**Types of ledger (4):**

1. **Sales ledger (receivables or debtors ledger):** personal accounts of credit customers.
2. **Purchases ledger (payables or creditors ledger):** personal accounts of credit suppliers.
3. **General ledger (nominal ledger):** all remaining accounts: nominal accounts (wages, rent, sales, purchases, bad debts, income) and real accounts (land and buildings, motor vehicles, inventory, plant and machinery). The trial balance is extracted from it.
4. **Cash book and petty cash book.**

**Explain more**

- **Real accounts:** debit when more of the item is acquired; credit when it is disposed of (sold, damaged beyond use, given away). Real accounts arise from cash and credit transactions; personal accounts exist only where there are **credit** transactions.
- **Salary account = nominal, impersonal.** Motor vehicle account = real, impersonal. Debtors and creditors = personal.
- **Trap:** the sales **ledger** holds customers' accounts; the sales **journal** (day book) only lists credit sales first. The sales and purchases **accounts** themselves are in the general ledger, not the sales and purchases ledgers.
- **Balancing an account:** the balance is the difference between the debit and credit totals. Write it on the smaller side as "balance c/d", then on the opposite side as "balance b/d" for the next period. Example, Bisi Ltd:

| Dr | Folio | ₦ | Cr | Folio | ₦ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 4 Sales | SDB1 | 250,000 | Mar 8 Returns inward | RIB1 | 30,000 |
| | | | Mar 31 Balance c/d | | 220,000 |
| | | **250,000** | | | **250,000** |
| Apr 1 Balance b/d | | 220,000 | | | |

### One-minute recap

- Sequence: source document → books of original entry → ledger → trial balance → financial statements. No entry without a source document.
- Credit note = goods returned or overcharge (red); debit note = sent with goods returned to a supplier, or for an undercharge.
- Trade discount appears only on the invoice and is never posted; cash discount goes to the cash book.
- 7 books of original entry: sales, purchases, returns inward, returns outward, general journal, cash book, petty cash book.
- Day book totals go to the general ledger (sales Cr, purchases Dr, returns inward Dr, returns outward Cr); individual items go to personal accounts.
- Journal: debit first, credit second, narration; 5 uses including credit purchases and sales of non-current assets.
- Cash book is both a book of prime entry and part of the ledger.
- Accounts: real, personal, nominal (personal vs impersonal). Ledgers: sales, purchases, general, cash book.
- Folio = cross-reference column; chart of accounts = list of accounts available for posting.

### Theory practice

**Question:** On 1 May 2026, Dupe Enterprises (a provisions trader) (a) bought goods for resale on credit from Musa Ltd, list price ₦500,000 less 4% trade discount, and (b) bought a delivery van on credit from Lagos Motors for ₦900,000. State the book of original entry for each and the double entry.

*Worked solution:*

- (a) Trade discount = 4% × ₦500,000 = ₦20,000; net = ₦480,000. Book: **purchases day book** (source: purchase invoice). Dr purchases ₦480,000 (as part of the day book total); Cr Musa Ltd ₦480,000 in the purchases ledger. The ₦20,000 is not recorded.
- (b) Book: **general journal**, since it is a non-current asset bought on credit. Dr delivery van ₦900,000; Cr Lagos Motors ₦900,000. Narration: "Being delivery van bought on credit from Lagos Motors."
