## Topic 6: The Trial Balance and Correction of Errors

**What you must know:** what a trial balance is, its purpose, uses, importance and limits; how to prepare one; the six errors that do not affect its agreement; the errors that do; the suspense account; and how to correct errors with journal entries.

### Lesson 6.1: What a trial balance is and why we prepare it

**Must know**

- **Trial balance:** a list or schedule of all the debit and credit balances extracted from the ledger accounts. It is also called a **summary of ledger balances**. It is prepared at the **end of an accounting period**. Debit balances go in the debit column, credit balances in the credit column, and **the totals of both columns must agree**.
- **Purpose:** to ensure that all entries made in the general ledger are properly balanced, that is, that total debits equal total credits. It lists the ending balance on each general ledger account.

**5 uses of a trial balance**

1. **Ensures accuracy in postings:** helps the accountant verify that the balances from the ledger (the principal book of account) were extracted accurately.
2. **Provides an overview:** a summary sheet giving a bird's eye view of the business's transactions.
3. **Aids auditors:** auditors analyse the trial balance before scrutinising the ledgers further.
4. **Basis for preparing the final accounts:** the income statement (profit and loss account) and the balance sheet (statement of financial position) are prepared from it.
5. **Offers confidence in the ledger:** a trial balance that agrees shows the ledger balances can be relied upon.

**6 points on the importance of a trial balance**

1. It helps in checking the **arithmetical accuracy** of the ledger accounts.
2. It makes the preparation of the final accounts easy.
3. It helps in rectifying errors in the ledger accounts.
4. It helps in the preparation of the audit report.
5. It aids the preparation of budgets and decision making.
6. It helps the organisation make comparative analysis.

**Explain more**

- If the totals do **not** agree, an error was made in one of three places: (1) **posting** transactions to the ledger, (2) **balancing** the ledger accounts, or (3) **transferring** the balances from the ledger to the trial balance (or adding up its columns).
- **Limit:** a trial balance that agrees does **not** prove the books are free of errors. Six kinds of error leave it in agreement (Lesson 6.3).
- Trap: the best answer to "a trial balance is used to..." is **check the accuracy of postings to the ledger** (its arithmetical accuracy). It does **not correct** errors by itself, it does **not give details of transactions** (the journal and ledger do), and it detects only **some** errors.
- Trap: a trial balance is a **list (statement)**, not an account. Do not confuse it with the **journal** (a book of original entry), the **balance sheet** (reports financial position), a **chart of accounts** (a list of account names only), the **suspense account** (holds a difference temporarily) or the **trading** and **profit and loss accounts** (use some of the balances to work out profit).
- It is dated **"as at"** one day, because balances are taken at a point in time.

### Lesson 6.2: Preparing a trial balance

**Must know**

**Posting rules** (where each balance goes):

| Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|
| **Assets** (cash, bank, plant, trade receivables (debtors), prepayments) | **Liabilities** (trade payables (creditors), loans, accrued expenses) |
| **Expenses** (all of them, e.g. salaries, rent, repairs) | **Income** (sales, revenue, commission received, interest received) |
| **Drawings** | **Capital** |
| Purchases, returns inwards, discount allowed, opening inventory | Returns outwards, discount received, accumulated depreciation, provision for bad and doubtful debts |

**Steps**

1. Balance off every ledger account; the figure you use is the **balance brought down (b/d)**.
2. List each account with a debit balance in the debit column and each with a credit balance in the credit column.
3. Total both columns. They must agree.

**Explain more**

**Worked example.** Prepare an unadjusted trial balance from these ledger balances at 31 December 2023: cash ₦5,000; accounts receivable ₦8,000; accounts payable ₦7,000; salary payable ₦3,000; revenue ₦12,000; advertising expenses ₦11,000; plant ₦20,000; prepaid insurance ₦4,000; capital ₦27,500; repair expenses ₦1,500.

**Trial balance as at 31 December 2023**

| Account | Debit (₦) | Credit (₦) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Capital | | 27,500 |
| Cash | 5,000 | |
| Accounts receivable | 8,000 | |
| Accounts payable | | 7,000 |
| Salary payable | | 3,000 |
| Revenue | | 12,000 |
| Advertising expenses | 11,000 | |
| Plant | 20,000 | |
| Prepaid insurance | 4,000 | |
| Repair expenses | 1,500 | |
| **Total** | **49,500** | **49,500** |

- Why each goes where it does: cash, receivables, plant and prepaid insurance are **assets** (debit); advertising and repairs are **expenses** (debit); payables and salary payable are **liabilities** (credit); revenue is **income** (credit); capital is **credit**.
- Trap: "salary payable" is a liability (credit), but "salary expenses" is an expense (debit). Read the word after the account name. Likewise "accumulated depreciation" is a credit, but "depreciation expense" is a debit; "dividend" (like drawings) is a debit.
- Trap: **all expenses** sit on the **debit side**, never on both sides.

### Lesson 6.3: The six errors that do not affect the agreement of the trial balance

**Must know**

In each of these errors an equal debit and credit were still made, so the trial balance still agrees. It cannot reveal them.

1. **Error of omission:** a transaction is **completely left out** of the books (neither side recorded).
2. **Error of commission:** an entry is made in the **wrong account of the same class**, e.g. the wrong customer's account or the wrong expense account.
3. **Error of principle:** an entry is made in the **wrong class of account**, e.g. capital expenditure treated as revenue expenditure (a non-current asset debited to an expense or to purchases).
4. **Error of original entry:** the **wrong amount** is recorded in the book of original entry, so **both** the debit and the credit use the same wrong figure.
5. **Error of complete reversal:** the correct accounts and amount are used, but the account that should be debited is **credited** and vice versa.
6. **Compensating error:** two or more errors of the **same amount on opposite sides** cancel each other out.

**Explain more**

| Error | Example | Correcting journal entry |
|---|---|---|
| Omission | Credit sale of ₦20,000 to Tunde not recorded at all | Dr Tunde ₦20,000; Cr Sales ₦20,000 |
| Commission | ₦15,000 received from Ade credited to Adams' account | Dr Adams ₦15,000; Cr Ade ₦15,000 |
| Principle | Motor van bought for ₦500,000 debited to motor expenses | Dr Motor van ₦500,000; Cr Motor expenses ₦500,000 |
| Original entry | Sales invoice to Kemi for ₦8,400 entered in the sales day book as ₦4,800 | Dr Kemi ₦3,600; Cr Sales ₦3,600 |
| Complete reversal | ₦7,000 cash paid to supplier Musa: debited cash, credited Musa | Dr Musa ₦14,000; Cr Cash ₦14,000 |
| Compensating | Rent account overcast by ₦1,000 and sales account overcast by ₦1,000 | Dr Sales ₦1,000; Cr Rent ₦1,000 |

- Trap (reversal): the correction is **double** the amount. ₦7,000 must first cancel the wrong entry, then ₦7,000 more makes the right one.
- Trap (original entry): correct only the **difference** (₦8,400 − ₦4,800 = ₦3,600). A **transposition** of digits (84 to 48) that is made in the day book and carried to both sides is an error of original entry.
- **Commission vs principle:** same class of account (customer to customer, expense to expense) = commission; different class (asset to expense) = principle. Commission does not change profit; principle does (the van wrongly made profit ₦500,000 lower).
- Posting the **same transaction twice** to both accounts (duplication) also leaves the trial balance in agreement.
- None of these errors involve the suspense account when corrected.

### Lesson 6.4: Errors that affect agreement, and the suspense account

**Must know**

These errors make the debit and credit totals **differ**:

1. **Single entry:** only one side of a transaction is posted.
2. **Two debits or two credits:** both entries are posted to the same side.
3. **Unequal amounts:** the debit and credit are posted with different figures (e.g. a transposition in one account only).
4. **Casting errors:** a ledger account is **undercast** (added too low) or **overcast** (added too high).
5. **Wrong balance:** an account is balanced off wrongly.
6. **Trial balance errors:** a balance is left out of the trial balance, copied wrongly, put on the wrong side, or the trial balance columns are added wrongly.

- **Suspense account:** a temporary account opened to hold the difference on a trial balance that does not agree, until the errors are found and corrected.
- **Which side?** Put the difference on the **smaller** side. Debit total short = **debit** balance on suspense; credit total short = **credit** balance on suspense.

**Explain more**

- The suspense account lets work continue, but every effort must be made to find the errors. When all of them are corrected, the suspense account closes with **no balance**.
- **The test:** if an error affected the agreement of the trial balance, its correction **involves the suspense account**; if it did not (the six errors in Lesson 6.3), suspense is **not** used.
- Example: debit total ₦77 less than credit total. Open suspense with a **debit** of ₦77 so both totals agree.
- Trap: the suspense account is not an error type, and it does not list all balances. It only holds a difference.

### Lesson 6.5: Correcting errors with journal entries

**Must know**

- Errors are corrected **through the journal first**, then posted to the ledger. Correction of errors is one of the uses of the (general) journal.
- Each journal entry shows the date, the account debited, the account credited, the amounts and a **narration** explaining the error.
- **3-step method:**
  1. What **was** recorded?
  2. What **should** have been recorded?
  3. Write the entry that turns step 1 into step 2. If only **one** account is wrong, the other side of the entry is **suspense**.

**Explain more**

**Worked example.** The trial balance of Adaeze Stores at 31 December 2025 had debit total ₦1,056,800 and credit total ₦1,050,000. The difference of ₦6,800 was credited to a suspense account (credit side was short). These errors were then found:

1. The purchases day book total of ₦63,500 was posted to the purchases account as ₦65,300.
2. The sales account was undercast by ₦10,000.
3. ₦5,000 paid to Bayo, a supplier, was entered in the cash book but not in Bayo's account.
4. Repairs to the motor van, ₦8,000, were debited to the motor van account.
5. ₦6,000 taken by the owner for private use was debited to wages.
6. A credit sale of ₦12,000 to Chika was omitted from the books.

**Journal**

| | Suspense | | 5,000 |
| | *(Payment to Bayo posted to cash book only)* | | |
| 4 | Repairs | 8,000 | |
| | Motor van | | 8,000 |
| | *(Error of principle: revenue expense debited to an asset)* | | |
| 5 | Drawings | 6,000 | |
| | Wages | | 6,000 |
| | *(Error of principle: private withdrawal debited to wages)* | | |
| 6 | Trade receivables (Chika) | 12,000 | |
| | Sales | | 12,000 |
| | *(Error of omission)* | | |

**Suspense account**

| Dr | ₦ | Cr | ₦ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Purchases | 1,800 | Difference per trial balance | 6,800 |
| Sales | 10,000 | Trade payables (Bayo) | 5,000 |

- **Effect on net profit:** purchases down ₦1,800 (+), sales up ₦10,000 and ₦12,000 (+), repairs up ₦8,000 (−), wages down ₦6,000 (+). Net profit rises by **₦21,800**. Error 3 affects only the balance sheet (payables); in error 5 only the wages side affects profit, while drawings reduce capital.
- Trap: never "correct" by rubbing out or changing the wrong figure. Always make a new journal entry with a narration.

### One-minute recap

- A trial balance is a list of all debit and credit balances from the ledger at a date; its totals must agree.
- Main use: to check the arithmetical accuracy of postings to the ledger; it is also the basis of the final accounts.
- Debit: assets, expenses, drawings. Credit: liabilities, income, capital.
- Agreement does not prove accuracy: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal and compensating errors stay hidden.
- Complete reversal is corrected at double the amount; original entry by the difference only.
- One-sided errors, unequal amounts, casting errors and trial balance slips cause disagreement.
- A suspense account holds the difference on the smaller side until the errors are found.
- Correct errors by journal first; only errors that affected agreement involve suspense.

### Theory practice

**Question:** The debit total of a trial balance exceeds the credit total by ₦900. Two errors are found: (i) electricity of ₦2,100, correctly entered in the cash book, was posted to the electricity account as ₦1,200; (ii) discount received of ₦1,800 was entered in the supplier's account but omitted from the discount received account. Give the correcting journal entries and the suspense account.

*Worked solution:* The credit side was short, so suspense opens with a **credit** of ₦900.
(i) Dr Electricity ₦900, Cr Suspense ₦900 (electricity under-posted).
(ii) Dr Suspense ₦1,800, Cr Discount received ₦1,800 (one side omitted).
Suspense: Dr discount received ₦1,800 = Cr (difference ₦900 + electricity ₦900) ₦1,800. The account clears to nil.
