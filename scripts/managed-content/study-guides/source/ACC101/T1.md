## Topic 1: The Nature and Scope of Accounting

**What you must know:** the history of accounting; what accounting, financial accounting and bookkeeping mean and how they differ; the objectives of financial accounting and the accounting cycle; the elements of financial statements; the users and the qualitative characteristics of accounting information; types of organisation; the branches of accounting; manual and electronic recording.

### Lesson 1.1: History, definitions and bookkeeping vs accounting

**Must know**

**History in order:**

1. **Babylon:** money served as a medium of exchange; businessmen recorded sales and lending on **clay tablets**. As trade grew, servants found it hard to give a report of **stewardship**, so records became necessary.
2. **Egypt:** used **papyrus** to show tax collection.
3. **Greece:** accounts were **engraved on stones** and displayed in public.
4. **Luca Pacioli** (Italian mathematician), the **father of accounting**: his 1494 book, known as the *Treatise of Book Keeping*, was the first documented work describing double entry.
5. **Industrial Revolution (Great Britain):** professional bodies such as the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of England and Wales (ICAEW)** developed accounting theory, methods and practice. The basic rule of double entry **remained unchanged**.
6. **Nigeria:** the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria (ICAN)** was set up by **Act of Parliament No. 15 of 1965**; it sets the code of ethics and regulates the profession. After the **indigenisation decrees of 1975 and 1979**, ICAN members ran most enterprises, so the **Nigerian Accounting Standards Board (NASB)** was set up in **1982** to standardise practice. It is now the **Financial Reporting Council of Nigeria (FRCN)**.
7. **Today:** information technology has improved the speed and quality of accounting, still using double entry.

- **Accounting:** identifying, collecting, **recording**, classifying, analysing, summarising, **interpreting** and **communicating** financial transactions to users for decisions. Three aspects: **recording, reporting and forecasting**. Every definition must include **identifying and recording the transactions** of a business.
- **Financial accounting:** the process of **collecting, recording, analysing and summarising** financial transactions of a business and **interpreting** the information for the various users of financial statements. Transactions are recorded in the **books of original entry**, analysed and **posted to the ledgers**, then summarised in the **financial statements**, in line with accounting concepts, standards and the law.
- **Bookkeeping:** the aspect of accounting that covers the **collection and accurate recording** of financial transactions. It is a **recording** activity.

| Point | Bookkeeping | Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Narrow; one part of accounting | Wider; includes bookkeeping |
| Main task | Collecting and recording | Analysing, summarising, interpreting, preparing financial statements |
| Stage | Comes first | Starts where bookkeeping ends |
| Judgement | Routine, little judgement | Needs judgement (e.g. when to recognise a transaction) |
| End product | Books of prime entry, ledgers, trial balance | Financial statements and their interpretation |
| Done by | Bookkeeper (clerical) | Accountant (professional) |

**Similarities:** both deal with **financial transactions measured in money**; both follow **double entry** and the same accounting concepts; both rely on the **same source documents and records**; both aim at **accurate** financial information; both serve the **same business entity**.

**Explain more**

- **Trap:** "Bookkeeping is a ____ activity" = **recording**. Interpreting, reporting and analysing belong to accounting.
- **Trap:** accounting serves **many users**, not "management only", and deals mainly with **quantitative** (money) data, not only qualitative data.
- Financial accounting **does record** transactions, is **historical** (past events), **reports on a business entity** and treats the business as **separate** from its owner. "It does not record financial transactions" is false.
- Leonardo da Vinci and Adam Smith (an economist) are **not** the father of accounting.

### Lesson 1.2: Objectives, the accounting cycle and the elements

**Must know**

- **Two primary objectives of a business:** to **earn a profit** and to **remain solvent** (able to pay debts as they fall due).
- **Main objective of financial statements:** to provide information about the **financial position**, performance and changes in position of an enterprise that is **useful to a wide range of users in making economic decisions**.

**Objectives of financial accounting:**

1. Keep a **systematic record** of all transactions.
2. Find the **profit or loss** for a period (income statement).
3. Show the **financial position** at a date (balance sheet).
4. Provide **information to users** for economic decisions.
5. Show **stewardship**: how well managers have used the owners' resources.
6. Protect and **control** the business's assets.
7. Meet **legal** and tax requirements.

**The financial accounting cycle (in order):**

1. Transaction occurs; **source document** raised (invoice, receipt, cheque counterfoil).
2. Record in the **books of original (prime) entry** (day books, cash book, journal).
3. **Post** to the **ledger** accounts.
4. **Balance** the accounts and extract a **trial balance**.
5. Make **end-of-year adjustments** (accruals, prepayments, depreciation, bad debts).
6. Prepare the **financial statements**.
7. **Close** the income and expense accounts; the cycle starts again for the next period.

**Five elements of financial statements:**

| Element | Meaning | Where shown |
|---|---|---|
| Assets | Resources the business owns | Balance sheet |
| Liabilities | Amounts owed to outsiders | Balance sheet |
| Capital (owner's equity) | Owner's interest in the business | Balance sheet |
| Revenue (income) | Earnings from sales and services | Income statement |
| Expenses | Costs used up in earning revenue | Income statement |

- **Balance sheet accounts** = asset, **liability** and capital (stockholders' or owner's equity) accounts.
- **Main financial statements:** (1) **income statement** (profit or loss account), (2) **statement of owner's equity** (changes in equity), (3) **balance sheet** (statement of financial position), (4) **statement of cash flows**. They let users judge **profitability** and **solvency**. There is **no** "expenditure statement".

**Explain more**

**One transaction through the cycle.** Tolu sells goods on credit to Bayo for ₦80,000.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Source document | Tolu issues sales invoice No. 041 for ₦80,000 |
| Book of prime entry | Entered in the sales day book |
| Ledger | Debit Bayo's account ₦80,000; credit sales account ₦80,000 |
| Trial balance | Bayo ₦80,000 in the debit column; sales ₦80,000 in the credit column |
| Financial statements | Sales in the income statement; Bayo as a receivable (debtor) in the balance sheet |

**Elements in the statements.** Ada Stores, year to 31 December 2025:

| Income statement | ₦ | ₦ |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (sales) | | 850,000 |
| Less expenses: rent | 120,000 | |
| wages | 230,000 | 350,000 |
| Net profit | | 500,000 |

| Balance sheet as at 31 December 2025 | ₦ |
|---|---|
| Equipment | 600,000 |
| Inventory | 150,000 |
| Cash | 400,000 |
| **Total assets** | **1,150,000** |
| Capital at start | 500,000 |
| Add net profit | 500,000 |
| Capital at end | 1,000,000 |
| Trade payables (creditors) | 150,000 |
| **Capital and liabilities** | **1,150,000** |

- Profit (revenue less expenses) increases capital.
- **Trap:** "earn a profit and **create wealth**" or "earn a profit and **prepare financial statements**" are wrong pairs. Preparing statements is an accounting task that *measures* the objectives.

### Lesson 1.3: Users and qualitative characteristics

**Must know**

**Nine users of accounting information:**

| User | What they want to know |
|---|---|
| 1. Managers | Current and expected position, to manage efficiently and make effective decisions |
| 2. Shareholders (owners) | How well management performs; profitability; how much profit can be withdrawn |
| 3. Trade contacts | Suppliers: ability to pay debts. Customers: a secure source of supply, no danger of closing down |
| 4. Providers of finance (banks, lenders) | Ability to pay interest and repay loans and overdrafts |
| 5. Taxation authorities | Profit, to assess tax payable |
| 6. Employees | Job security, wages and salaries, future careers |
| 7. Financial analysts and advisers | Information for clients: stockbrokers advise investors, credit agencies advise suppliers, journalists inform readers |
| 8. Government and agencies | Allocation of resources; national statistics |
| 9. The public | Employment, use of local suppliers, effect on the environment (pollution) |

- **Internal user:** management (three levels: **strategic** = board, long-term decisions; **tactical** = middle managers, short to medium-term targets; **operational** = day-to-day, often non-money units such as hours worked). **External users:** everyone else.

**Qualitative characteristics** (qualities information must have before users can rely on it):

1. **Relevance:** enough facts to meet users' needs and the decision in hand.
2. **Reliability:** source verifiable; one source of evidence corroborates another.
3. **Comparability:** no change in the basis of preparation from period to period, so results can be compared.
4. **Timeliness:** available early enough to use; late information is useless.
5. **Objectivity:** no bias, window dressing or subjective judgement; traceable to documents; complies with regulations.
6. **Comprehensiveness:** just enough detail, neither too little nor too much.
7. **Title:** clearly named so the user knows the report, the entity and the period it covers.
8. **Volume:** a manageable quantity of information for the user.
9. **Channel of communication:** sent through a suitable medium to the person who will act on it.

The international framework also lists **understandability** (easily understood by users) and **completeness**; reports should also be **fair** (not favour any user group).

**Explain more**

- **Trap:** "**Monetary**" is **not** a quality of financial reports. It belongs to the money measurement concept (Topic 4), which decides what is recorded. Understandable, relevant, reliable, fair, comparable and timely **are** qualities.
- Match the user to the need: interest and loan repayment = **lender**; ability to pay for goods supplied = **supplier**; tax = **tax authorities**; pollution = **public**; dividends and profitability = **shareholders**.

### Lesson 1.4: Types of organisation and branches of accounting

**Must know**

**Two types of organisation:**

**A. Profit-making** (primary aim: profit on goods and services):

1. **Sole proprietorship:** one-man business; the owner is **fully liable** for debts and takes **all the profit**.
2. **Partnership:** **two or more** persons, **jointly and fully liable**; profit shared in **agreed ratios**.
3. **Company:** a **legal entity**, an **artificial person** distinct from its owners. The **company** is liable for its debts; owners act on its behalf and all transactions are in the company's name. Private (Ltd) or public (Plc).

**B. Non-profit-making** (services at affordable cost, not for profit):

1. Government ministries
2. Non-governmental organisations (NGOs)
3. Clubs and societies
4. Charity organisations

**Branches of accounting:**

| Branch | What it does |
|---|---|
| Financial accounting | Records transactions and prepares financial statements, mainly for **external** users, by set rules, at set intervals |
| Cost accounting | Finds and controls the cost of products, jobs and services |
| Management accounting | Information for **managers** to plan, evaluate and control; format chosen by management |
| Financial management | Raising funds and deciding how to invest and use them |
| Taxation | Computing tax liabilities and tax planning |
| Auditing | Independent examination to confirm the statements give a **true and fair** view |

Other branches you may meet: **public sector (government) accounting** and **forensic accounting** (investigating fraud).

**Explain more**

| Point | Financial accounting | Management accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Users | Mainly external | Internal (managers) |
| Frequency | Usually annual | Usually monthly or more often |
| Rules | Must follow standards and law | No set format |
| Detail | Summarised | Detailed (e.g. manufacturing costs) |
| Audit | Audited externally | Not externally audited |

- **Trap:** financial statements are **not** "more accurate" than management accounts; that is not the difference.
- **Trap:** a sole proprietor is **fully** liable for business debts; in a company the **company itself** is liable.

### Lesson 1.5: Manual and electronic recording

**Must know**

- **Manual recording:** transactions are written by hand in **bound books, loose-leaf binders or cards** (day books, cash book, journal, ledgers).
- **Electronic (computerised) recording:** accounting software keeps the books **in computer memory**, not printed form. It performs the **same bookkeeping tasks**; the **double entry system must still be kept** and controls are still needed.

**Benefits of computerised accounting:**

1. **Speed** of input and processing.
2. Speed and **flexibility** in producing information.
3. Handles **large numbers** of accounts and transactions.
4. **Improved accuracy.**
5. **Automatic update** of all related accounts from a single entry.
6. **Less storage space.**
7. **Input checks** on data: dates (31 November rejected), limits, ranges.

- **Biggest drawback:** the user **cannot physically see** what is happening, so errors and omissions are not readily spotted. A "self-balancing" system can make users assume everything is right.
- **Configurations:** (1) **mainframe** with minicomputer and PCs; (2) **networked** computers, files held centrally; (3) **stand-alone** computers. All need security and **segregation of duties**.
- **Coding:** each account gets a unique code. Good codes are **unique, useful, compact, meaningful, self-checking, expandable, of standard size**.

| Point | Manual | Computerised |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Slow | Fast |
| Arithmetic errors | Common | Rare |
| Posting | Each entry posted by hand | One entry updates all related records |
| Storage | Bulky books | Little space |
| Visibility | Entries can be seen and traced | Hard to see what happened |
| Cost | Cheap to start | Hardware, software, training |
| Risks | Loss, fire, damage | Viruses, power failure, unauthorised access |

**Explain more**

**Account coding example.** Main codes: assets 1, liabilities 2, capital 3, expenses 4, revenues 5. Current assets = 13. A six-digit expense code: digit 1 = function (1 production), digits 2-3 = cost centre (10 machining), digits 4-6 = expense (202 unskilled wages). So **110202** means unskilled-labour wages in the machining centre of production.

- The trial balance must still agree, and adjustments still go **through the journal**.
- **Trap:** computers do **not** remove the need for double entry or for checking. In exam questions, assume a **manual** system unless told otherwise.

### One-minute recap

- History: clay tablets, papyrus, stones; Pacioli 1494; ICAN 1965; NASB 1982, now FRCN.
- Bookkeeping = collecting and recording; accounting is wider: analyses, summarises, interprets, reports.
- Business aims: earn a profit and remain solvent. Financial statements give useful information for economic decisions.
- Cycle: source document, prime entry, ledger, trial balance, adjustments, financial statements, closing.
- Five elements: assets, liabilities, capital, revenue, expenses. Balance sheet accounts = assets, liabilities, equity. No "expenditure statement".
- Nine users, from managers to the public; management is the internal user.
- Nine qualities: relevance, reliability, comparability, timeliness, objectivity, comprehensiveness, title, volume, channel; plus understandability and fairness. "Monetary" is not one.
- Profit-making: sole proprietor, partnership, company. Non-profit: ministries, NGOs, clubs, charities.
- Branches: financial, cost, management accounting, financial management, taxation, auditing.
- Computers are faster and more accurate but still use double entry; errors are harder to see.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Distinguish between bookkeeping and accounting, and state three similarities between them.

*Answer outline:* Define bookkeeping (collection and accurate recording of financial transactions) and accounting (recording plus analysing, summarising, interpreting and communicating to users). Differences: scope (narrow vs wide), task (recording vs interpreting and reporting), stage (first vs follows on), judgement (routine vs professional), end product (ledgers and trial balance vs financial statements). Similarities: both deal with money transactions, both use double entry and the same concepts, both use the same source documents and records.
