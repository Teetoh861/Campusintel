## Topic 6: Governance in the Public Services

**What you must know:** what governance and corporate governance mean; the Four P's and the four principles; the eight characteristics of good governance; the public sector, its three Es and how public governance differs from private; why good public governance matters; accountability; and the hindrances to good governance in Nigeria.

### Lesson 6.1: Governance, corporate governance and the Four P's

**Must know**

- **Origin:** "governance" comes from the Greek verb **kubernaein** (kubernao), meaning **to steer**. Governance is not new; it is as old as human civilisation.
- **Governance (simple):** the process of decision-making **and** the process by which decisions are implemented (or not implemented). Also: the system by which a firm is controlled and operates, and the mechanisms that hold the company and its people accountable.
- **Fuller meaning:** the whole framework of processes, functions, structures, rules, laws and norms arising from relationships, power and communication; it manages, allocates and mobilises resources to address collective needs.
- **5 contexts:** corporate, institutional, national, local, international governance. (Levels also named: public, global, non-profit, corporate and project governance.)
- **Corporate governance:** the structures, processes, cultures and systems that make business organisations operate successfully; the rules, practices and processes by which a company is **directed and controlled** to balance the interests of its stakeholders.
- **Stakeholders in corporate governance:** shareholders, board of directors (elected by shareholders to oversee management), management, employees, customers, suppliers and partners, regulators.
- **Four P's of governance:** **Purpose, People, Process, Performance**. Profit is **not** one.

**Four principles of good corporate governance:**

1. **Accountability:** the board answers to shareholders and other stakeholders.
2. **Transparency:** timely and proper disclosure on all material matters, including financial situation, performance and ownership.
3. **Fairness:** all shareholders, including minority and foreign shareholders, get fair treatment.
4. **Responsibility:** the organisation complies with laws and regulations and considers the interests of all stakeholders.

**Explain more**

- Good governance is **not** about ticking boxes or adding layers of bureaucracy; it promotes an environment that drives growth, resilience and long-term success.
- **What fuelled the corporate governance debate:** fraud, corruption, opportunistic business leaders, agency problems, bankruptcies, internationalisation, deregulation and integration of financial markets, complex financial instruments and their risks, changing social expectations, inadequate reporting and failing auditors, and excessive directors' remuneration. Rising literacy is **not** one of them.
- Traps: "fair treatment of minority shareholders" is **fairness**, not responsibility; "complying with laws and considering all stakeholders" is **responsibility**; "disclosure of financial results and ownership" is **transparency**.

### Lesson 6.2: The eight characteristics of good governance

**Must know**

Good (effective) governance has **8** major characteristics:

1. **Participation:** by both men and women, either **directly** or through legitimate intermediate institutions or representatives.
2. **Rule of law:** fair legal frameworks **enforced impartially**, with full protection of human rights, particularly those of **minorities**. All individuals and institutions are subject to laws that are publicly known, fairly enforced and independently adjudicated.
3. **Transparency:** decisions and their enforcement **follow rules and regulations**, and information is **freely available and directly accessible** to those affected.
4. **Responsiveness:** institutions and processes try to serve **all stakeholders within a reasonable timeframe**.
5. **Consensus oriented:** **mediation** of the diverse interests in society to reach a **broad consensus** on what is best for the whole community, with a broad, long-term perspective.
6. **Equity and inclusiveness:** all members feel they have a **stake** and are not excluded; all groups, **especially the most vulnerable**, have opportunities to take part and improve or maintain their well-being.
7. **Effectiveness and efficiency:** procedures and institutions produce **results that meet the needs of society** while making the **best use of resources**.
8. **Accountability:** institutions answer to the public and their stakeholders. Accountability can only be enforced with **transparency and the rule of law**.

Not characteristics: centralisation, exclusion of minorities, nepotism, secrecy, restriction of civil liberties.

**Explain more**

- A federal agency takes three years on a pension claim that rules say must be decided in 90 days: **responsiveness** is lacking.
- The Federal Government spends months negotiating the minimum wage with labour unions, employers and state governments until a broadly acceptable figure emerges: **consensus orientation**.
- Student bodies such as the BSA and MSA giving members a say are examples of **participation**.
- Related ideas you will meet in questions:
    - **Legitimacy** rises when procedures are seen as **fair**.
    - Transparency mechanisms involve **public** access to records; transparency helps prevent misuse of **public authority**.
    - Clear **service standards** improve **predictability**; predictability and fairness build **public trust**.
    - **Policy coherence** means government actions are **aligned and mutually reinforcing** across sectors.
    - **Regulatory governance** ensures rules are **fairly implemented**, not selectively applied.
    - **Professionalism** means adherence to **established** standards.
    - Balancing today's service delivery with future sustainability shows **strategic vision**.

### Lesson 6.3: The public sector, the three Es and public vs private governance

**Must know**

- **Public sector:** government organisations and agencies responsible for delivering **public goods and services** (ministries, public schools, health facilities, law enforcement).
- **Private sector:** businesses owned and controlled by individuals or entities outside government; profit-oriented (SMEs, corporations, start-ups, family businesses, partnerships).
- **Three Es** (general objectives of public sector organisations):
    1. **Economy:** value for money; delivering on budget, on time, within resource limits.
    2. **Efficiency:** acceptable return on resources. **Efficiency = work output ÷ work inputs.**
    3. **Effectiveness:** the extent to which the organisation delivers what it is intended to deliver.

| Basis | Public sector enterprise | Private sector enterprise |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership (the **major** difference) | Owned and operated by government | Owned by individuals or shareholders |
| Objective | Public needs, general welfare | Maximise profit and shareholder value |
| Decision-making | Bureaucratic, several levels of approval | Flexible, responds quickly |
| Accountable to | Government and the public | Mainly shareholders |

- **Public sector governance:** ensures public sector entities meet their objectives and intended outcomes for citizens and operate ethically, transparently, efficiently and effectively. They are accountable to their **ultimate owners, the citizens**.
- **Objective:** to **serve the public interest, provide essential services and ensure national stability and well-being**.
- **Nigeria:** citizens' oversight is exercised through the **National and State Assemblies** and, at local level, **councillors**. Policy guideline example: the **Corporate Governance Manual for Governing Boards/Councils of Federal Parastatals, Agencies and Commissions**.
- Public governance differs from private mainly by its emphasis on **public interest, equity, accountability and democratic participation**. Transparency rules are **stricter** in public governance because it uses **public resources and authority**.

**Explain more**

- **Worked example (₦):** a state licensing office has a budget of ₦32,000,000, spends ₦30,000,000 on time, and issues 12,000 licences against a target of 15,000.
    - Economy: spent ₦30,000,000 of ₦32,000,000, on time, so it was economical.
    - Efficiency: cost per licence = ₦30,000,000 ÷ 12,000 = **₦2,500**; or 12,000 ÷ 30 = **400 licences per ₦1 million**.
    - Effectiveness: 12,000 ÷ 15,000 × 100 = **80%** of the intended output.
- A health board builds every clinic on time and within budget, but child deaths do not fall because there are no trained staff: **economy** is fine, **effectiveness** is lacking.
- **Challenges in public (listed) company governance (4):** managing diverse shareholder interests; pressure for short-term results versus long-term strategy; regulatory scrutiny and compliance burdens; transparency and disclosure requirements.
- **Agency relationship:** managers do not own the business but run it for the owners (principals) and owe them a **fiduciary duty**.
- **Challenges in private company governance (4):** lack of transparency and public accountability; succession planning and family-business dynamics; limited access to capital markets; balancing growth with control. Managing a large, diverse shareholder base is a **public** company problem.
    - An Onitsha family firm whose children quarrel over who takes over: **succession planning**.
    - A Lagos tech start-up fearing outside investors will dilute the founders' say: **balancing growth with control**.
- **Other differences:** public company boards are **larger**, mix executive and non-executive directors and stress **independence**; private boards are smaller and close to the owners. Listed companies face heavy disclosure (financial performance, executive pay, material events) and tend to be more **risk-averse** because of regulator and public scrutiny. Public companies often focus on shareholder value; private ones have more room for a stakeholder approach. Both face growing pressure on CSR.
- Public governance also needs mechanisms to manage **uncertainty and risk**, procedures to resolve **administrative conflicts**, and must balance **efficiency with equity**.

### Lesson 6.4: Importance of good public governance and accountability

**Must know**

**Importance of good public sector governance:**

1. Central to the effective operation of all organisations, stakeholders and government.
2. Encourages **global investors** to invest in the economy.
3. Improves the efficiency and effectiveness of organisations.
4. Sets clear standards for management practice and decision-making, giving better services and outcomes.
5. Promotes transparency, accountability and responsible decisions, raising **public trust and confidence** in government institutions.
6. Assures citizens that their welfare is the government's primary concern and that the public service will be run efficiently and effectively.
7. Creates an enabling environment where citizens **voluntarily participate** in governance and contribute to national development.
8. Encourages innovation and creativity.
9. Assures stakeholders that those who **mismanage or abuse trust will be sanctioned** under the law.

- Main reason it matters: it ensures the **efficient, transparent and accountable delivery of services that meet citizens' needs**. It promotes **fairness and equity** in allocating public resources and reduces **corruption and misuse of power**.
- **Accountability:** the obligation of leaders and institutions to **explain their actions, accept responsibility for decisions and face consequences** when performance falls short; officials must answer to the public and oversight bodies.
- **4 accountability mechanisms:** performance monitoring, legal oversight, public reporting, institutional checks and balances.

**Explain more**

- Good governance does **not** remove the need for laws, protect government from criticism or raise civil servants' pay.
- Accountability deters misconduct by raising the likelihood of **detection**. Executive immunity from all review is **not** an accountability mechanism.

### Lesson 6.5: Hindrances to good governance in the public services (Nigeria)

**Must know**

**5 leadership challenges (hindrances) in governance:**

| Hindrance | Meaning | Effects | Leadership response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corruption and misuse of power | Using authority for personal gain (bribery, embezzlement, nepotism, abuse of resources) | Weakens institutions, cuts trust, limits development, undermines justice | Strengthen accountability, promote transparency, enforce ethics |
| Lack of transparency | Opaque decisions, no access to information | Suspicion, low participation, room for corruption | Open communication, public reporting, inclusive policy talks |
| Weak institutions | Lacking structure, capacity or independence to enforce laws | Poor service delivery, poor enforcement, reliance on informal networks | Institutional reform, capacity building, stronger governance frameworks |
| Political interference | Outsiders steer decisions for personal or partisan gain | Less autonomy, policy inconsistency, lower standards | Professional ethics, institutional safeguards, merit-based decisions |
| Low citizen trust | Citizens lose confidence and disengage | Resistance to policy, civic withdrawal | Show integrity, deliver tangible results |

- Low citizen trust is caused by corruption scandals, poor service delivery and lack of accountability.
- **Weak governance results from (4):** passive followership, civic apathy, lack of accountability mechanisms, weak institutional structures.

**Explain more**

- A board overruled by politicians who demand jobs and contracts for party loyalists is **political interference**.
- The major hindrance in African public services is **systemic corruption, weak institutions and lack of accountability mechanisms**. An independent judiciary, a free press, decentralisation and merit-based recruitment **support** good governance; they are not hindrances.
- **Low voter turnout** (for example, under one in four registered voters) reduces leadership **accountability and the legitimacy** of governance; it does not show satisfaction.
- Other Nigerian public-service problems you should recognise: nepotism and favouritism in hiring, conflict of interest, absenteeism and low work ethic, poor record-keeping and limited access to information.
- **Active** followership strengthens governance; only **passive** followership weakens it.

### One-minute recap

- Governance comes from Greek kubernaein, "to steer": deciding and implementing decisions.
- Four P's: Purpose, People, Process, Performance. Four principles: accountability, transparency, fairness, responsibility.
- Eight characteristics: participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus oriented, equity and inclusiveness, effectiveness and efficiency, accountability.
- Accountability needs transparency and the rule of law.
- Three Es: economy (value for money), efficiency (output ÷ input), effectiveness (delivers what was intended).
- Public vs private: ownership is the major difference; public answers to government and citizens.
- Nigeria: oversight by National and State Assemblies and councillors.
- Five hindrances: corruption, lack of transparency, weak institutions, political interference, low citizen trust.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A federal agency budgeted ₦50,000,000 to process 20,000 passport applications. It spent ₦48,000,000 and processed 16,000. Compute its cost per application and the share of its target achieved, and say which of the three Es each figure measures.

*Solution:* Cost per application = ₦48,000,000 ÷ 16,000 = **₦3,000**; this measures **efficiency** (input per unit of output). Target achieved = 16,000 ÷ 20,000 × 100 = **80%**; this measures **effectiveness**. Spending ₦48,000,000 against a ₦50,000,000 budget shows **economy**.
