## Topic 7: Open Government Partnerships

**What you must know:** what the Open Government Partnership (OGP) is, when and where it began and its eight founders; its aims, core values and the Open Government Declaration; how it is run; how a country qualifies and joins; national action plans; benefits and challenges; and Nigeria's journey in OGP.

### Lesson 7.1: What OGP is and how it began

**Must know**

- **Open Government Partnership (OGP):** a **voluntary** partnership of **governments and civil society** that secures **concrete commitments** from governments to make governance more **open, accountable and responsive** to citizens. It is not a UN treaty, an African Union agency or a company, and it imposes no sanctions or loans.
- **Open government:** a government whose activities and information are open to citizens, who can take part in decisions and hold it to account.
- **Launched:** **20 September 2011**, on the sidelines of the **United Nations General Assembly in New York**.
- **8 founding governments:** **Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico, Norway, the Philippines, South Africa, the United Kingdom, the United States**. The only African founder is **South Africa**. Nigeria was **not** a founder.
- Memory hook: "**B**ig **I**deas **M**ake **N**ations **P**rogress: **S**outh **A**frica, **U**K, **U**S."

**Explain more**

- OGP brings governments and civil society organisations (CSOs) to the same table as **equal partners**; this is its distinctive feature.
- It has grown from 8 founders to a membership of dozens of countries plus a **local programme** for states, provinces and cities (subnational governments).
- Trap: 2011 is the launch year; **2016** is the year Nigeria joined.

### Lesson 7.2: Aims, core values and the Open Government Declaration

**Must know**

**Core aim:** to secure concrete commitments from governments to **promote transparency, empower citizens, fight corruption and harness new technologies** to strengthen governance.

**4 core values (principles) of OGP:**

1. **Transparency:** government information is open, complete, timely and freely available.
2. **Civic (citizen) participation:** citizens take part in debate and decisions and contribute ideas.
3. **Public accountability:** rules and mechanisms that make officials justify their actions and accept consequences.
4. **Technology and innovation** for openness and accountability.

**Open Government Declaration:** the document every participating government must **endorse**. Its **4 commitments:**

1. Increase the **availability of information** about government activities.
2. Support **civic participation**.
3. Implement the **highest standards of professional integrity** throughout government (aimed at **fighting corruption**).
4. Increase **access to new technologies** for openness and accountability.

Restricting civil society is **not** a commitment.

**Explain more**

- **Technology** is central because it **facilitates transparency, citizen participation and service delivery innovation**. It is not for surveillance, replacing civil servants or centralising all communication.
- **Open government data** aims to make government datasets **publicly accessible** to promote **innovation, accountability and civic engagement**, not to sell or hide data.
- Meaningful participation must be supported by **access to relevant information**; without information, consultation is only symbolic.
- Phrases to recognise:
    - OGP supports **value-driven governance** (reforms aligned with public values).
    - It links openness to better **developmental** outcomes for society.
    - It uses transparency to build **trust** and enhance **administrative efficiency**.
    - It improves policy quality by **incorporating diverse inputs**.
    - It strengthens **administrative accountability**, letting citizens challenge administrative decisions.
    - It promotes **comparative accountability**: governments benchmark progress against peers.
    - It treats **learning from failure** as a way to **improve future reforms**.
    - It normalises **transparent decision-making**, reducing public suspicion.
    - It encourages **publishing performance indicators** for public services, **regulatory transparency** in enforcement, and **partnership transparency** in public-private partnerships.
- A state publishes its full budget and monthly spending online and CSOs use the data to question a road contract: OGP's aim of **using transparency to empower citizens to hold government accountable**.

### Lesson 7.3: How OGP is run

**Must know**

| Body | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Steering Committee** | Executive body; **equal numbers of government and civil society** representatives |
| **Co-chairs** | Leadership rotates **every year** between **one government co-chair and one civil society co-chair** |
| **Support Unit** | Small **permanent secretariat**: institutional memory, communications, partner relationships; a neutral party between governments and CSOs |
| **Independent Reporting Mechanism (IRM)** | Independent reports assessing how each member **develops and implements** its action plan |
| **International Experts Panel (IEP)** | **Oversees the IRM** and the quality of its reports |

**Explain more**

- The IRM matters for **credibility**: it gives an **independent check** on whether governments actually deliver what they promise. Governments do not grade themselves.
- Equal representation on the Steering Committee mirrors OGP's principle that government and civil society are partners.

### Lesson 7.4: Eligibility, joining and national action plans

**Must know**

**4 eligibility areas** (each scored up to **4 points**, so maximum **16**):

1. **Fiscal transparency:** timely publication of the executive's budget proposal (2 points) and the audit report (2 points).
2. **Access to information:** **4** points for an access to information (freedom of information) **law in force**; 3 for a constitutional guarantee; 1 for a draft law.
3. **Asset disclosure** by public officials: **4** points when the law requires disclosure **and the information is public**; 2 when disclosure is required but not public; 0 with no law.
4. **Citizen engagement:** based on a civil liberties score.

- **Threshold:** at least **75%** of applicable points (**12 of 16**, or 9 of 12 if one area cannot be measured). GDP, military strength or NATO membership do **not** count.
- **Values Check:** a further test of whether **civil society can operate freely** (civic space).
- **To join:** endorse the **Open Government Declaration** and submit a **letter of intent** from a minister confirming agreement with the Declaration and intent to participate, naming a **lead agency** and a **point of contact**.
- **National action plan (NAP):** the document setting out the specific commitments a member will implement over a fixed period. Commitments are **co-created** by government and civil society.
- **Commitment:** a specific, time-bound reform in a NAP, e.g. publishing all public contracts online.
- **Co-creation:** government and civil society designing the commitments together.

**Explain more**

- **Worked example:** a country scores fiscal transparency 2 (budget published, audit not), access to information 4 (FOI law in force), asset disclosure 2 (required but not public), citizen engagement 3.
    - Total = 2 + 4 + 2 + 3 = **11 points**. Share = 11 ÷ 16 × 100 = **68.75%**, below 75%, so **not eligible**.
    - If it begins publishing its audit report on time, fiscal transparency becomes 4 and the total becomes 13. Share = 13 ÷ 16 × 100 = **81.25%**: **eligible**, subject to the Values Check.
    - Required minimum: 0.75 × 16 = **12** points.
- Commitments are put into practice through NAPs, not UN treaties, OGP loans or an OGP parliament. The IRM then assesses them.

### Lesson 7.5: Benefits, challenges and Nigeria in OGP

**Must know**

**Benefits:**

1. Greater citizen **access to government information and services**, and greater ability to **hold leaders accountable**.
2. Stronger public accountability through **independent assessment** (IRM).
3. **Peer learning** from other governments.
4. Better policies from diverse inputs; higher **public trust**.
5. Reduced corruption through open contracting, asset disclosure and beneficial ownership transparency.
6. Use of technology for better service delivery.

OGP does **not** cancel foreign debts or transfer public assets to investors.

**Challenges:** weak political will and changes of government; slow or partial implementation of commitments; limited funding and capacity; shrinking civic space; low public awareness and digital access; resistance from officials used to secrecy; commitments that are vague or not ambitious.

**Nigeria:**

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Joined OGP | **2016** |
| Event before joining | President Buhari reiterated commitments at the **London Anti-Corruption Summit**, May 2016 |
| First NAP | **2017-2019** |
| Later NAPs | 2019-2022; 2023-2025; the fourth, a four-year plan, 2026-2029 |
| First Nigerian state in local programme | **Kaduna State**, **2018** |
| Supporting law | **Freedom of Information Act 2011** |

- Nigeria's NAP commitments focus mainly on **fiscal transparency, extractive industries transparency, anti-corruption and access to public information**.

**Explain more**

- **2016 commitments linked to OGP:**
    - A public central register of **beneficial ownership**: shows the **real people who ultimately own or control companies**.
    - Full implementation of the **Open Contracting Data Standard**: publishing information and data about public contracts across the **whole procurement process**.
    - Disclosure by companies of payments to government for oil, gas and minerals, complementing the **Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (EITI)**; Nigeria's arm is NEITI.
- The **FOI Act 2011**, signed in May 2011 under President Goodluck Jonathan, gives Nigeria the maximum 4 points for access to information.
- A Nigerian CSO coalition that helps draft NAP commitments and later monitors delivery shows that **government and civil society work as partners** in designing and monitoring reforms.
- **The action plan cycle in order:** (1) government and civil society **co-create** the plan; (2) the government **implements** the commitments over the plan period; (3) the **IRM** assesses design and results; (4) lessons feed into the **next** plan. This is why OGP treats learning from failure as a route to better future reforms.
- **How the challenges show up:** a commitment can be written but never funded; a new administration may lose interest in its predecessor's plan; citizens without internet access or awareness cannot use published data; and officials used to secrecy may delay releasing records even where an FOI law exists. Benefits come only when commitments are specific, funded and monitored.
- Trap: OGP is **voluntary**. It has no power to punish a government; its pressure comes from public commitments, peer comparison and independent IRM reports.

### One-minute recap

- OGP: voluntary partnership of governments and civil society for concrete open-government commitments.
- Launched 20 September 2011 at the UN General Assembly, New York, by 8 founders; South Africa the only African founder.
- Four values: transparency, civic participation, public accountability, technology and innovation.
- Members endorse the Open Government Declaration (4 commitments).
- Steering Committee (equal government and CSOs), rotating co-chairs, Support Unit, IRM overseen by the International Experts Panel.
- Eligibility: 4 areas × 4 points = 16; need 75% (12 points), plus the Values Check.
- Commitments sit in co-created national action plans.
- Nigeria: joined 2016 after the London Anti-Corruption Summit; first NAP 2017-2019; Kaduna joined the local programme in 2018.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A country cannot be measured on fiscal transparency, so it is scored on the other three areas only. It earns 4, 2 and 3 points. Is it eligible on the core criteria?

*Solution:* Applicable maximum = 3 × 4 = 12 points. Score = 4 + 2 + 3 = 9. Share = 9 ÷ 12 × 100 = **75%**, which meets the 75% threshold (9 of 12). It is eligible on the core criteria, provided it also passes the Values Check.
