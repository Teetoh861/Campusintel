## Topic 8: Public Sector Reforms

**What you must know:** what public sector reforms are and why governments undertake them; their types, drivers and objectives; the steps to initiate a reform; the features of a successful reform and the reform models (NPM, NPG, PDIA); supporting conditions and obstacles; tools to measure effectiveness; and Nigeria's main reforms.

### Lesson 8.1: Meaning, reasons, types and objectives

**Must know**

- **Public sector reforms:** **deliberate, multifaceted initiatives** designed to improve the **efficiency, effectiveness, transparency and accountability** of government machinery, to boost **service delivery and institutional performance**. They often involve restructuring agencies, digitalisation (e-governance), anti-corruption measures and privatisation.
- **Primary objective:** to improve the efficiency, effectiveness, accountability and responsiveness of government institutions and services. Not to abolish social programmes, replace elected officials or privatise everything.
- Success depends heavily on **implementation and political will**.

**Reasons for reform:** poor service delivery; corruption and leakages; ghost workers and a bloated wage bill; high cost of governance; rigid, slow bureaucracy; weak institutions; citizens' demands for accountability; new technology; fiscal pressure.

**Major types of reform initiative:** administrative/civil service restructuring; digital transformation (e-governance); strengthening financial management (e.g. IFMIS); anti-corruption measures; public-private partnerships. Abolishing the civil service is **not** one.

**3 performance drivers:**

1. **Transparency and accountability:** "due process" in procurement and anti-graft agencies curb corruption.
2. **Digitalisation:** systems such as IPPIS improve efficiency.
3. **Capacity building:** re-professionalisation and training of public employees.

**4 key objectives:**

1. **Enhancing service delivery:** from bureaucratic to citizen-centred, efficient services.
2. **Combating corruption:** more transparency, fewer chances for graft through technology and oversight.
3. **Economic modernisation:** aligning public services with **market demands** and **reducing the cost of governance**.
4. **Strengthening institutions:** robust, accountable, professional institutions.

**Explain more**

- A reform to stop officials inflating contract prices and diverting funds: **combating corruption**. Cutting passport waiting time from six weeks to one week: **enhancing service delivery**. That is a reduction of 5 ÷ 6 × 100 = **83.3%** (rounded to one decimal place).
- **Why each reason drives reform:** ghost workers inflate the wage bill, so payroll reform (IPPIS) follows; revenue kept in many commercial bank accounts invites leakages, so the TSA follows; inflated contracts drain budgets, so due process in procurement follows; slow, paper-based offices frustrate citizens, so digital services and one-stop shops follow. Every reform should begin from a clear problem like these.
- **Types versus drivers versus objectives:** types are *what* is changed (restructuring, e-governance, financial management, anti-corruption, PPPs); drivers are *how* performance improves (transparency and accountability, digitalisation, capacity building); objectives are *what for* (service delivery, anti-corruption, modernisation, institutions). Exams swap these lists, so learn which is which.
- Reforms aim to create a modern, professional, performance-oriented public sector; impact on financial performance is often positive, while service delivery gains are sometimes limited by poor coordination and lack of a coherent strategy.
- **Regulatory reform** simplifies rules to reduce **administrative burden and compliance costs**. Climate-related reform supports **sustainability and resilience**. Asset management reform improves **tracking and utilisation of public assets**.

### Lesson 8.2: Steps to initiate a public sector reform

**Must know**

1. **Diagnosis and problem identification** (the **first** step), including stakeholder consultation.
2. Collect **baseline data** on current performance.
3. Secure a senior political **reform champion** (political will and leadership).
4. Set clear goals, a **strategy** and **realistic timelines**.
5. Set up a **central reform secretariat** for day-to-day coordination and reporting.
6. **Communicate** reasons for change and **engage staff, unions and citizens early**.
7. **Pilot** in a few agencies, learn, fix, then scale up.
8. Implement with training and resources.
9. **Monitor and evaluate** against the baseline and targets; adjust.

**Explain more**

- **Baseline data** lets later results be compared with the starting position to show whether the reform worked.
- **Piloting** tests the reform and fixes problems before scaling up, as Nigeria did when IPPIS began with **seven** MDAs.
- A state merging two agencies faces staff fear of job losses: the remedy is to **communicate the reasons clearly and engage staff and unions early**, not to announce it after the fact.
- A **champion** supplies the **political will** needed to drive the reform and overcome resistance.
- Unrealistic, undefined or arbitrary timelines reduce effectiveness; **realistic** ones raise it. Hiring foreign consultants first, or restructuring all ministries at once, is not the first step.

### Lesson 8.3: Features of a successful reform and reform models

**Must know**

Successful reforms show a shift towards **citizen-centric service delivery**, more transparency and efficiency. **7 features:**

1. **Citizen-centric focus and improved service delivery:** user-friendly services, e.g. **one-stop shops** such as **Kenya's Huduma Centres**.
2. **Transparency and accountability:** public reporting, due process, less corruption.
3. **New Public Management (NPM) techniques:** private-sector practices: results-based management, **performance contracting**, contracting out for competition.
4. **Digital transformation:** analogue to digital (e-government).
5. **Organisational restructuring and right-sizing:** eliminating **ghost workers**, rationalising agencies, streamlining processes.
6. **Professionalisation and performance management:** retraining, **merit** hiring, open performance appraisals.
7. **Problem-Driven Iterative Adaptation (PDIA):** solving specific **local** problems step by step through **experimentation**, rather than copying "best practice" blueprints.

Also named: **decentralisation**; strong political will, clear leadership, broad stakeholder buy-in and sustained commitment. Retaining rigid, rule-bound procedures is **not** a feature.

| Model | Core idea |
|---|---|
| Traditional (Weberian) bureaucracy | Rules, hierarchy, central control |
| **New Public Management (NPM)** | Apply **private-sector management techniques** for efficiency |
| **New Public Governance (NPG)** | **Collaborative networks** of public, private and civil society actors in service delivery |
| **PDIA** | Local problem-solving by iteration and experimentation |

**Explain more**

- **Ghost worker:** a name on the payroll that does not match a real, working employee.
- **Performance contracting:** directors sign agreements with measurable annual targets; continued appointment depends on meeting them.
- One centre where citizens register businesses, renew licences and get birth certificates illustrates **citizen-centric one-stop shops**.
- A state ministry tests three small changes in a few schools to cut teacher absenteeism, keeps what works and adjusts the rest: **PDIA**.
- Successful reforms emphasise **performance** management, aligning staff performance with reform goals.

### Lesson 8.4: Requirements, supporting conditions and obstacles

**Must know**

**5 core components (requirements) of reform:**

1. **Digital transformation and efficiency:** IPPIS and GIFMIS to reduce fraud and raise transparency.
2. **Financial management and accountability:** the **Treasury Single Account (TSA)** to centralise revenue and reduce leakages.
3. **Structural and institutional reforms:** restructuring agencies, privatising public enterprises, establishing anti-corruption bodies (**EFCC, ICPC**).
4. **Administrative and human resource reforms:** strengthening capacity, enforcing ethics, performance management systems.
5. **Policy and regulatory improvements:** stronger regulators, **due process** in procurement, more transparency.

**Supporting conditions:** sustained **political will** and commitment; a **politically neutral, capable civil service** with reform capacity; **adequate funding and human capacity**; clear strategy and **stakeholder buy-in**; stable leadership; legal backing; technology infrastructure; peer learning.

**Obstacles:** political instability; lack of clear strategies; **resistance to change**; **implementation gaps** from lack of political will and poor planning; resource constraints; need for continuous training.

**Explain more**

- Frequent changes of reform leadership and strategy are **not** a supporting condition; strong political will is **not** an obstacle.
- Suppressing unions or monopolising the agenda in the presidency undermines buy-in.

### Lesson 8.5: Measuring effectiveness and Nigerian reforms

**Must know**

Measuring effectiveness in Nigeria means assessing improvements in **transparency, financial accountability and service delivery**. **4 categories of indicator:**

1. **Financial performance and accountability:** revenue collected, payroll fraud cut via IPPIS, budgetary control via GIFMIS.
2. **Service delivery and efficiency:** speed, quality and accessibility of services; impact of due process in procurement.
3. **Institutional strengthening:** effectiveness of EFCC and ICPC; implementation of **IPSAS** (International Public Sector Accounting Standards).
4. **Economic impact:** contribution to growth, stability and development.

**Tools:**

- **KPIs**, independently verified, with **service delivery benchmarks** for objective assessment.
- **Citizen report cards** (citizen satisfaction surveys): citizens rate availability, quality and responsiveness.
- **Benchmarking:** comparing indicators with similar agencies or good-practice standards.
- **Performance (value-for-money) audit:** often by the Auditor-General; checks economy, efficiency and effectiveness.
- **Public Expenditure Tracking Survey (PETS):** follows funds from the centre to schools and clinics to see how much arrives.
- **Management information systems** such as dashboards; **outcome reporting** of achieved results against targets.

**Nigerian reforms:**

| Reform | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **IPPIS** (Integrated Payroll and Personnel Information System) | Accurate personnel and payroll records; eliminates **ghost workers**. Live **April 2007** with **7** pilot MDAs; managed by the **Office of the Accountant-General of the Federation** since **October 2008** |
| **GIFMIS** (Government Integrated Financial Management Information System) | **Budgetary control** and financial management |
| **TSA** (Treasury Single Account) | Consolidates federal revenues into one account at the **Central Bank of Nigeria**; piloted 2012; full adoption by all federal MDAs mandated in **2015** under **President Buhari**; credited with boosting revenue (IGR) |
| **BPSR** (Bureau of Public Service Reforms) | Set up in **2004**; to **initiate, coordinate, monitor and ensure full implementation** of reform policies |
| **Public Procurement Act 2007** | Created the **Bureau of Public Procurement**; put **due process** on a statutory footing |
| **BVN** (Bank Verification Number) | Biometric identity for bank customers, introduced by the CBN in 2014 |
| **Freedom of Information Act 2011** | Public right of access to government records |
| **EFCC, ICPC** | Anti-corruption bodies (structural and institutional reform) |

**Explain more**

- Pairing trap: IPPIS = **payroll fraud**; GIFMIS = **budgetary control**. IPPIS is sometimes written "Integrated Personnel and Payroll Information System"; both name the same system.
- **Worked example (₦):** a ministry's monthly wage bill is ₦7,200,000,000 for 48,000 names. After IPPIS enrolment, 1,200 fictitious names are removed.
    - Average monthly pay = ₦7,200,000,000 ÷ 48,000 = **₦150,000**.
    - Monthly saving = 1,200 × ₦150,000 = **₦180,000,000**; annual saving = ₦180,000,000 × 12 = **₦2,160,000,000**.
    - Payroll cut = 1,200 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = **2.5%**.
    - Comparing before and after payroll figures measures **financial performance and accountability**.

### One-minute recap

- Reforms: deliberate initiatives to raise efficiency, effectiveness, transparency and accountability; success hinges on implementation and political will.
- Drivers: transparency and accountability, digitalisation, capacity building.
- Objectives: service delivery, combating corruption, economic modernisation, strengthening institutions.
- First step: diagnosis; then baseline, champion, secretariat, communication, pilot, monitoring.
- Features: citizen-centric, transparency, NPM, digital, right-sizing, professionalisation, PDIA.
- NPM = private-sector techniques; NPG = networks; PDIA = local experimentation.
- Measures: financial, service delivery, institutional, economic; tools include KPIs, citizen report cards, benchmarking, VFM audit, PETS.
- Nigeria: IPPIS (2007, OAGF), GIFMIS, TSA (CBN, 2015), BPSR (2004), PPA 2007, BVN, FOI Act 2011.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Before a reform, a state paid ₦900,000,000 a month to 6,000 names. After IPPIS, 300 ghost workers were removed. Find the yearly saving and say which indicator category it reflects.

*Solution:* Average pay = ₦900,000,000 ÷ 6,000 = ₦150,000. Monthly saving = 300 × ₦150,000 = ₦45,000,000. Yearly saving = ₦45,000,000 × 12 = **₦540,000,000**. This is a **financial performance and accountability** indicator.
