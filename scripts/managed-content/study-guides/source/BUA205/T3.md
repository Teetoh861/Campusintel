## Topic 3: Leadership, Followership, Citizenship and Governance

**What you must know:** leadership as a relational process and its five core components; leadership challenges in governance; followership and Kelley's five follower types; importance and characteristics of effective followers; citizenship, its rights, responsibilities and modes of acquisition; civil society, its examples and functions; how citizens, followers and civil society shape governance in Nigeria.

### Lesson 3.1: Leadership as a relationship

**Must know**

- **Leadership:** the **process of influencing** others to achieve **shared goals** for collective development; it mobilises people, shapes behaviour and coordinates resources.
- Leadership is a **relational process**, not a one-person activity: it emerges from interaction between leaders and followers and depends on **trust, communication, legitimacy** and shared commitment.
- Effective governance depends on the interaction of **leaders, followers, citizens and civic institutions**.
- In democracies citizens play **dual roles**: **followers of leaders** and **active participants in governance** through civic engagement.

**5 core components of leadership:**
1. Providing direction (vision, objectives, roles).
2. Motivating followers (to be effective, innovative, committed).
3. Making decisions.
4. Maintaining ethical standards.
5. Managing change and conflict.

**6 characteristics of effective leaders:** vision and strategic thinking; integrity and ethical responsibility; communication skills; emotional intelligence; adaptability and innovation; accountability and responsibility.

**5 leadership challenges in governance:**

| Challenge | Meaning | Leadership response |
|---|---|---|
| Corruption and misuse of power | Authority used for personal gain | Accountability systems, transparency, ethical standards |
| Lack of transparency | Closed decisions, no access to information | Open channels, public reporting, inclusive discussions |
| Weak institutions | No structure, capacity or independence to enforce laws | Institutional reform, capacity building |
| Political interference | Outsiders sway decisions for partisan interest | Professional ethics, safeguards, merit-based decisions |
| Low citizen trust | Citizens disengage after scandals or poor service | Integrity, tangible results, citizen participation |

**Explain more**

- **Emotional intelligence:** understanding and managing your own emotions while recognising others'.
- Leadership effectiveness **improves** with **transparent** communication and **declines** when followers suffer **chronic disengagement**. Effective leaders recognise that followers have **valuable skills and insights**.
- "Good leaders must first be good followers": leadership development **requires understanding followers' perspective, needs and challenges** before leading.
- Leadership style depends on **4 factors: the environment, the leader, the followers and the objective or goal**; not the leader's ethnic origin, age or salary. A manager who instructs new interns step by step but lets senior engineers decide, with the same goal and workplace, is adjusting to **the followers**.

### Lesson 3.2: Followership and Kelley's model

**Must know**

- **Followership:** the **active role** individuals play in **supporting, influencing and sometimes challenging** leaders to achieve shared objectives. Also: the behaviour of people who back leaders while actively contributing to organisational or societal goals.
- It emphasises **collaboration, critical thinking and ethical responsibility**.
- **Modern view:** followers are **partners** in the leadership process, not passive recipients of instructions (the traditional view).
- **Leader-follower relationship:** **dynamic and reciprocal**; each role influences and depends on the other.

**Kelley's followership model** (Robert **Kelley**, 1992) classifies followers on **independent (critical) thinking** and **level of engagement** (active or passive):

1. **Exemplary (star):** active **and** independent thinkers. The **most effective**.
2. **Conformist:** active but **unquestioning** ("yes-people").
3. **Passive:** **dependent on leaders** for direction; no initiative.
4. **Alienated:** **critical but disengaged**.
5. **Pragmatic:** **flexible and situational**; shifts with whatever the situation rewards.

| Type | Thinking | Engagement |
|---|---|---|
| Exemplary | Independent | Active |
| Conformist | Dependent | Active |
| Alienated | Independent | Passive |
| Passive | Dependent | Passive |
| Pragmatic | Middle | Middle |

**6 importance of followership in governance:** prevents authoritarian leadership and abuse; encourages democratic participation; enhances institutional performance; strengthens accountability mechanisms; supports policy implementation; encourages organisational stability.

**6 characteristics of effective followers:** critical thinking; responsibility; ethical awareness; courage to question unethical practices; teamwork and cooperation; commitment to institutional values.

**Explain more**

- Scenarios: an accounts officer who works hard but objects, with reasons, to a ₦15 million payment lacking documents = **exemplary**. A worker who obeys every order, even doubtful ones = **conformist**. A clerk who waits to be told everything = **passive**. An officer who criticises privately but refuses to help = **alienated**. A union officer who changes stance with the mood = **pragmatic**.
- "Charismatic followers" is **not** a Kelley type. Kelley's dimensions are **not** task structure and position power (Fiedler), concern for people and production (Managerial Grid), or ability and willingness (Hersey and Blanchard).
- Followership supports leadership while keeping **personal integrity**, not blind loyalty. In democracies followers have a duty to **scrutinise** leadership actions.
- Effective followership helps organisations because competent followers **co-create outcomes and give critical feedback** leaders need. Active followership **maintains accountability and prevents misuse of power**.
- Followership never "guarantees that leaders never make mistakes", and unquestioning loyalty is **not** a mark of an effective follower.
- If staff watch their director divert ₦60 million and stay silent, passive followership **lets misuse of power continue and weakens governance**.

### Lesson 3.3: Citizenship

**Must know**

- **Citizenship:** an individual's **legal membership in a country**, involving **rights, duties and participation in governance and development**. Also: the legal and social relationship between an individual and a state.
- **3 elements of citizenship:** legal membership in a country; political participation (voting, advocacy); social responsibilities toward community development.
- Citizenship lets people **claim protection under national law**.

**5 rights of citizens:**
1. Right to vote and political participation.
2. Freedom of speech and association.
3. Access to justice.
4. Right to education.
5. Equal protection under the law.

**6 responsibilities of citizens:**
1. Obeying laws.
2. Paying taxes.
3. Voting responsibly.
4. Participating in community activities.
5. Promoting social harmony.
6. Holding leaders accountable.

**Types (modes of acquiring) legal citizenship in Nigeria** (1999 Constitution, Chapter III): by **birth**, by **registration**, by **naturalisation**.

**Explain more**

- **Registration** covers, for example, a foreign woman married to a Nigerian man. **Naturalisation** is for foreigners who meet residence and other legal conditions. Long residence **alone** does not make anyone a citizen.
- **Dual citizenship:** a Nigerian **by birth** may hold another citizenship; a citizen by registration or naturalisation who acquires or retains another citizenship forfeits the Nigerian one.
- **Active citizenship:** citizens **participating meaningfully** in governance, policy-making and community affairs and holding leaders accountable. **Passive citizenship** is mere compliance. Holding office or simply paying tax is not the definition of active citizenship.
- **Civic responsibility** includes protecting **public resources**, not elite interests.
- **Citizenship education** equips people with knowledge of **rights, responsibilities and civic participation skills**; it does not teach blind obedience.
- Trap: paying taxes and holding leaders accountable are **responsibilities**; equal protection under the law is a **right**.

### Lesson 3.4: Civil society

**Must know**

- **Civil (civic) society:** voluntary, **non-governmental** organisations and institutions that **represent citizens' interests and promote accountability**, transparency and citizens' well-being. It is the arena of voluntary associations **outside government and business**, operating **between the state and the private sector**.
- It acts as a **bridge between citizens and the state** and depends on **voluntary support** for its sustainability.

**6 examples:** NGOs; religious bodies; professional associations; labour unions; student organisations; media institutions. Community-based organisations also count.

**6 functions of civil society:**
1. Advocacy and policy influence.
2. Civic education.
3. Election monitoring.
4. Human rights protection.
5. Public accountability promotion.
6. Community development.

**Explain more**

- Nigerian examples: the **Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC)** campaigning on wages and policy; the **National Association of Nigerian Students (NANS)**; the Nigerian Bar Association; religious bodies; the press.
- A federal ministry is a **public sector entity**, not civil society. Enacting laws (legislature), commanding the armed forces and collecting taxes are **not** civil society functions.
- NGOs sending observers to polling units and publishing an irregularities report = **election monitoring**. An NGO radio programme in Hausa and Yoruba on constitutional rights = **civic education**.

### Lesson 3.5: Citizenship and governance

**Must know**

- **Governance:** the **systems and processes through which authority is exercised and decisions are made** in public or organisational institutions. It **extends beyond government** to private organisations, civil society actors and community groups.

**7 elements of good governance:** transparency; accountability; rule of law; participation; equity and inclusiveness; responsiveness; efficiency and effectiveness.

**5 roles of citizens in governance:** voting and electoral participation; public consultations and policy discussions; community advocacy; monitoring public officials; civic activism.

**5 roles of followership in governance:** supporting ethical leaders; resisting corruption; participating in decision-making; providing feedback on public policies; upholding institutional standards.

**Effective governance occurs when (4):** leaders act ethically and responsibly; followers actively engage and give constructive feedback; citizens participate in democratic processes; civil society promotes accountability and transparency.

**Weak governance results from (4):** passive followership; civic apathy; lack of accountability mechanisms; weak institutional structures.

- **Accountability:** the obligation of leaders to explain their actions, accept responsibility and face consequences; it ensures **answerability**. **Mechanisms (4):** performance monitoring, legal oversight, public reporting, institutional checks and balances.
- **Transparency:** openness in decisions, access to information, clear communication. **Promotes:** trust in institutions, reduced corruption, public participation, effective monitoring of leaders. Without it governance is open to **secrecy, inefficiency and abuse of power**.
- **Democratic participation forms (5):** voting, public consultations, civic activism, community engagement, advocacy and policy dialogue. It keeps leaders **responsive** to citizens and policies in line with public interest.

**Explain more**

- The link between citizenship and good governance: an **informed, engaged citizenry holds leaders accountable** and promotes transparency. Citizens influence governance between elections too, not only at election time. Participation enhances **policy responsiveness**.
- Governance quality reflects the **collective actions of leaders and followers**, not the head of government alone.
- **Nigerian applications (5):**
  1. **Electoral participation:** low voter turnout **reduces leadership accountability and legitimacy**.
  2. **Youth participation:** activism, policy advocacy and community development.
  3. **Student governance:** responsible student leaders and followers improve university administration (a union president who consults students before negotiating hostel fees).
  4. **Community development associations:** collective, grassroots action (Abeokuta residents paying ₦5,000 per household to repair a culvert).
  5. **Media and civic advocacy:** investigative journalism exposing diverted funds (₦2.3 billion meant for clinics), followed by campaigns for prosecution.
- Enugu residents tracking a ₦120 million constituency project = **monitoring public officials**. A clerk refusing ₦2 million to push a file = **resisting corruption**. Students answering a survey on a draft hostel policy = **feedback on public policies**. Staff association members on a welfare committee = **participating in decision-making**.
- Do not confuse lists: "preventing authoritarian leadership, encouraging stability..." is the **importance** of followership; "voting responsibly, obeying laws, paying taxes..." are citizens' **responsibilities**; "advocacy, civic education, election monitoring..." are civil society **functions**. Bribing officials is **not** democratic participation.

### One-minute recap

- Leadership = influencing others towards shared goals; a relational process with 5 core components.
- Followership = active support, influence and challenge; followers are partners.
- Kelley (1992): exemplary, conformist, passive, alienated, pragmatic; dimensions are independent thinking and engagement.
- Citizenship = legal membership with rights, duties and participation; Nigeria: birth, registration, naturalisation.
- 5 rights (vote, speech, justice, education, equal protection); 6 responsibilities (laws, taxes, voting, community, harmony, accountability).
- Civil society = voluntary non-governmental bodies between state and private sector; 6 functions.
- Governance = systems and processes for exercising authority; 7 elements; extends beyond government.
- Weak governance: passive followership, civic apathy, no accountability, weak institutions.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Name Kelley's five follower types and say which type governance needs most, with a reason.

*Answer:* Kelley (1992) classifies followers by independent thinking and engagement: exemplary (active, independent), conformist (active, unquestioning), passive (dependent on leaders), alienated (critical but disengaged) and pragmatic (flexible, situational). Governance needs **exemplary** followers most, because they support leaders actively while questioning unethical orders, which strengthens accountability and prevents misuse of power.
