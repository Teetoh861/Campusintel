## Topic 5: Stakeholder and Public Governance

**What you must know:** public governance; stakeholders (meaning, types, shareholders vs stakeholders); Freeman's stakeholder theory and its governance implications; the power/interest grid and the salience model; stakeholder engagement (process, levels, principles, benefits, challenges); crisis (meaning, characteristics, types, stages) and crisis communication management.

### Lesson 5.1: Public governance and stakeholders

**Must know**

- **Public governance:** the process by which **public institutions manage resources, implement policies and deliver services to citizens**. Effective public governance depends mainly on **stakeholder participation**.
- **Stakeholder:** any **individual, group or institution that can affect or is affected by** an organisation's or government's actions, decisions and outcomes. In public governance: those who **gain from public policy or bear its consequences and demand accountability**.
- **Examples in public governance:** citizens, community leaders, traditional rulers, pressure groups, NGOs, labour unions, the press, civil servants, student unions, religious bodies.
- **Corporate governance:** the **rules, practices and processes by which a company is directed and controlled**, balancing the interests of its stakeholders.

**Key stakeholders in corporate governance (7):**

1. **Shareholders:** owners who invest capital and expect returns.
2. **Board of directors:** elected by shareholders to **represent their interests and oversee management**.
3. **Management:** executives running **day-to-day operations**.
4. **Employees:** the workforce carrying out operations.
5. **Customers:** consumers of the goods or services.
6. **Suppliers and partners:** provide goods, services or other resources.
7. **Regulators:** **government bodies that enforce laws and regulations** on corporate governance.

**Types of stakeholders:**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | direct, formal stake; essential to survival | employees, customers, owners, suppliers |
| **Secondary** | influence or are affected, but no contract or ownership link | media, NGOs, pressure groups, community activists |
| **Internal** | inside the organisation | employees, managers, owners/board |
| **External** | outside the organisation | customers, depositors, suppliers, government, host community |

**Explain more**

- **Every shareholder is a stakeholder, but not every stakeholder is a shareholder.** Employees, customers and host communities are stakeholders who own no shares.
- An environmental NGO in Port Harcourt campaigning against gas flaring with no contract or ownership link is a **secondary** stakeholder. A bank's **employees** are internal; the **CBN**, depositors and host community are external.
- Firms that sell limestone, bags and diesel to a cement company are its **suppliers and partners**.
- Residents who will live beside a new landfill in Ogun State, bear its smell and health risks and demand explanations are **stakeholders** (affected by the decision).
- **Why public governance looks to the long term:** public policies shape **intergenerational welfare** (roads, education, debt affect future generations). It strengthens service delivery by **aligning processes with objectives**.

### Lesson 5.2: Stakeholder theory

**Must know**

- **Stakeholder theory:** a management framework holding that organisations, institutions and governments should **not be run for the benefit of one group** but should recognise and respond to **all groups and individuals who affect or are affected by** their decisions.
- Popularised by **R. Edward Freeman (1984)** in *Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach*. Long-term success depends on the ability to **identify, understand and manage the interests of multiple stakeholders**.
- **Shareholder-primacy view** (associated with Milton Friedman): the main responsibility of business is to **maximise returns to its owners within the law**. Stakeholder theory says managers should **weigh the interests of all affected groups, not only owners' returns** (it does not say profit should be ignored).

**Key governance implications of stakeholder theory (5):**

1. **Public interest** is a central priority.
2. **Inclusiveness** in decision-making.
3. **Ethical responsibility** to society.
4. **Accountability and transparency**.
5. **Sustainable development** and implementation of policy.

**Three perspectives** (Donaldson and Preston, 1995):

| Perspective | Says |
|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | explains how organisations **actually behave** towards stakeholders, without judging |
| **Instrumental** | attending to stakeholders **helps the firm perform better** |
| **Normative** | firms have a **moral duty** to consider stakeholders |

**Explain more**

- **Theory vs engagement:** stakeholder theory supplies the **"why"** (why stakeholders matter); stakeholder engagement is the **"how"** (how they are actually involved).
- "Concentrating decisions in a small elite", "keeping policy information confidential" or "profit as the only goal" are **not** implications of stakeholder theory.
- Stakeholder-based governance enhances accountability by **increasing mutual monitoring**: many groups watch one another and the government.
- Trap: Hobbes, Kelley and Weber are distractors; **Freeman** is the name, **1984** the year (1997 belongs to the salience model).

### Lesson 5.3: Stakeholder mapping and salience

**Must know**

- **Stakeholder mapping:** identifying stakeholders and **positioning them by attributes such as power and interest** to plan how to engage each group. The first task in any stakeholder analysis is **identifying all groups that can affect or be affected**.
- **Power/interest grid** (Mendelow): two dimensions, **power** and **interest**.

| | Low interest | High interest |
|---|---|---|
| **High power** | **Keep satisfied** | **Manage closely** (key players, fully engage) |
| **Low power** | **Monitor** (minimum effort) | **Keep informed** |

- **Salience model** (**Mitchell, Agle and Wood, 1997**): stakeholders are prioritised by **power, legitimacy and urgency**.
  - **Power:** ability to impose its will on the organisation.
  - **Legitimacy:** the perception that a claim or action is **desirable, proper or appropriate**.
  - **Urgency:** the degree to which a claim is **time-sensitive and critical**, calling for **immediate attention**.

| Attributes held | Type | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Power only | **Dormant** | Latent |
| Legitimacy only | **Discretionary** | Latent |
| Urgency only | **Demanding** | Latent |
| Power + legitimacy | **Dominant** | Expectant |
| Power + urgency | **Dangerous** | Expectant |
| Legitimacy + urgency | **Dependent** | Expectant |
| All three | **Definitive** | Most salient |

**Explain more**

- Lagos traders' association worried about a BRT corridor but with little influence (low power, high interest) → **keep informed**.
- A federal ministry that must approve funds but ignores details (high power, low interest) → **keep satisfied**. Risk of neglecting it: it may **become interested and use its power to block** your plans.
- Flood victims in Kogi State with a legitimate, urgent claim but little power → **dependent**. A large union that can strike but has no pending claim (power only) → **dormant**.
- Prioritisation is **not** by financial contribution, proximity or media visibility.
- Memory aid: the three latent types start with **D-D-D** (dormant, discretionary, demanding); "dependent" depends on others' power to act.

### Lesson 5.4: Stakeholder engagement

**Must know**

- **Stakeholder engagement:** the process by which organisations or government **identify stakeholders, keep communication open, understand their interests and involve them in decisions**, so that policy is implemented successfully. In public governance: involving those who affect or are affected by decisions **in shaping and implementing** them.

**Process of stakeholder engagement (5 steps, in order):**

1. **Stakeholder identification** (list everyone affected).
2. **Stakeholder analysis** (study each group's interests, influence, concerns).
3. **Engagement planning** (choose the method of engagement).
4. **Implementation and communication**.
5. **Monitoring and evaluation** (final step).

**Levels of engagement** (public participation spectrum, least to most public influence):

1. **Inform** (lowest) 2. **Consult** 3. **Involve** 4. **Collaborate** 5. **Empower** (final decision in the public's hands).

**Importance of stakeholder engagement in governance (5):**

1. Enhances **policy acceptance**.
2. Promotes **transparency and accountability** (citizens can demand explanations).
3. Improves **service delivery**.
4. Strengthens **trust and legitimacy**.
5. Reduces **conflicts and resistance**.

**Principles of effective engagement:** **inclusiveness**, **transparency**, **early and timely** involvement, **two-way communication**, **respect**, **responsiveness and feedback**, **meaningful** participation (views can actually change decisions).

**Challenges (4):** **political interference**, **resistance to change**, **poor communication systems**, **low literacy level**.

**Explain more**

- **Other benefits** examiners phrase in their own words: it adds **diverse perspectives** (adaptability), encourages **collaborative problem-solving** (innovation), enables **continuous feedback**, **integrates user feedback into service design**, strengthens **transparency norms** and trust, **broadens participation channels** (democracy). Open consultation forums mainly strengthen **participation**. It is closely linked to the principle of **transparency**.
- It does **not** guarantee every stakeholder gets exactly what it wants, does not skip consultation, and does not shift accountability from government to citizens.
- Effective engagement needs **inclusive, transparent and meaningful participation of all relevant parties**, not experts only, not telling people after decisions are made.
- **Poor engagement** leads to **resistance, low compliance and conflict** over a policy.
- **Classify the case:**

| Case | Answer |
|---|---|
| Lagos LG chairman shows how a ₦200 million road budget was spent at a town hall | Transparency and accountability |
| Kaduna team chooses between town halls, Hausa radio jingles, online surveys | Engagement planning |
| Agency studies each group's interests and influence over a toll policy | Stakeholder analysis |
| Ogun council publishes a draft budget and collects comments before finalising | Consult |
| Printed notices to farmers who cannot read them | Low literacy level |
| Politician invites only his supporters to consultations | Political interference |
| Ibadan motor park talks with union leaders only, leaving out traders, commuters, persons with disabilities | Inclusiveness breached |

### Lesson 5.5: Crisis: meaning, characteristics, types and stages

**Must know**

- **Crisis:** a **sudden, unexpected event** threatening an organisation's operations, reputation, stakeholders or the sustainability of a decision, forcing decisions under **time pressure and uncertainty**. Marks: **public panic, violent protests, destruction of property, loss of lives**.
- **Characteristics:** serious **threat**, **surprise**, **uncertainty**, **little time** for decisions. A crisis is **not** routine or fully predictable.
- **Types of crisis:** natural disasters (floods); technological (system failures, explosions); confrontation (protests, boycotts, strikes); malevolence (terrorism, kidnapping, product tampering, cyber attacks); organisational misdeeds (fraud, deception, management misconduct); workplace violence; rumours and misinformation; financial/economic crises; health crises (epidemics).

**Steps of crisis management (3, in order):**

1. **Getting prepared for crisis** (pre-crisis: prevention and preparedness). This is the **first** stage.
2. **Quick response and communication** (crisis/response stage).
3. **Recovery strategy** (post-crisis: recovery, evaluation, learning).

**Explain more**

- **Pre-crisis activities:** scanning for early warning signs, preparing a crisis communication plan, training spokespersons, drafting pre-approved message templates.
- **Response:** within an hour of a tanker explosion at an Onitsha market, the government issues a statement, opens a hotline and sends fire and medical teams → **quick response and communication**. Issuing a holding statement belongs here.
- **Post-crisis:** months after a market fire in Ibadan, stalls are rebuilt and traders get soft loans → **recovery strategy**. Evaluating how the crisis was handled and updating the plan, and rebuilding reputation, are post-crisis.
- Other models: **Fink's four stages:** prodromal (warning), acute, chronic, resolution. **Mitroff's five phases:** signal detection, probing and prevention, damage containment, recovery, learning.
- **Crisis management plan contains:** crisis team members and roles, up-to-date contacts for stakeholders and media, pre-approved templates and procedures. Never "a promise that the organisation will never face a crisis".
- **Crisis team:** senior decision-makers, a communications lead, legal advice and heads of affected operations.

### Lesson 5.6: Crisis communication management

**Must know**

- **Crisis communication:** the **collection, processing and sharing of information** needed to deal with a crisis, with affected stakeholders and the public.
- **Why it is a governance priority:** **unmanaged information gaps intensify public uncertainty and distrust**; **timely, accurate and empathetic** communication maintains public trust and enables effective response.
- **Holding statement:** a short statement issued **very quickly** that acknowledges the incident, expresses concern and promises updates before all facts are known.

**Principles of crisis communication:**

1. Communicate **early** (say what is known so far).
2. Be **accurate** and **honest**.
3. Be **consistent**: **one designated spokesperson**, coordinated verified messages.
4. Show **empathy** for those affected.
5. Use **two-way communication** (hotlines, feedback).
6. **Inform employees early**.
7. **Monitor social media** and correct false claims quickly through official channels.

**Response strategies** (Coombs' situational crisis communication theory):

| Group | Strategies |
|---|---|
| Deny | attack the accuser, denial, **scapegoating** (blaming an outside party) |
| Diminish | excuse, justification |
| Rebuild | compensation, **apology** |
| Bolster | reminding of past good works, ingratiation, victimage |

**Explain more**

- **Poor communication** in a security crisis leads to **public panic, spread of misinformation and erosion of institutional credibility**.
- **Bad practice:** "no comment", silence until attention fades, denying the crisis, letting agencies release different messages, waiting until every fact is confirmed, attacking those who reported it.
- **At fault for a preventable crisis:** **accept responsibility, apologise and take corrective action** to protect long-term trust.
- Three Bauchi officials give three death tolls for a cholera outbreak → needed **a single spokesperson and coordinated, verified messages**.
- Contaminated yoghurt batch in Lagos → **promptly recall and tell the public honestly** what is known, what is being done and how to get refunds.
- False WhatsApp claims that Abuja filling stations will shut → **monitor social media and correct quickly** with verified information. A toll-free outbreak hotline shows **two-way communication**.
- Employees should hear first because they act as **informal spokespersons**; learning facts from the media damages morale.

### One-minute recap

- Public governance = public institutions managing resources, implementing policy, delivering services; depends on stakeholder participation.
- Stakeholder = can affect or is affected; every shareholder is a stakeholder, not the reverse.
- Freeman (1984): stakeholder theory is the "why"; engagement is the "how".
- Grid: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor.
- Salience (Mitchell, Agle and Wood, 1997): power, legitimacy, urgency; all three = definitive.
- Engagement: identification, analysis, planning, implementation and communication, monitoring and evaluation.
- Levels: inform, consult, involve, collaborate, empower. Challenges: politics, resistance, poor communication, low literacy.
- Crisis steps: prepare, respond and communicate, recover. Communicate early, accurately, consistently, with empathy.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Classify each stakeholder using the salience model: (a) a regulator with power and a legitimate but not urgent claim; (b) protesters blocking a road with an urgent but illegitimate claim and power to disrupt; (c) a charity with a legitimate claim only.

*Solution:* (a) Power + legitimacy = **dominant**. (b) Power + urgency = **dangerous**. (c) Legitimacy only = **discretionary**. Add urgency to (a) and it becomes **definitive**.
