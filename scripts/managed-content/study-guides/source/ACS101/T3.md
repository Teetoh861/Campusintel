## Topic 3: Actuarial Symbols, Notations and Life Tables

**What you must know:** the general principles of the international actuarial notation (the four spaces around a symbol); the interest symbols i, i^(m), d, d^(m), v, δ and how they link; the mortality, annuity and assurance letters and their marks; the life table columns l\_x, d\_x, q\_x, p\_x and their properties; multi-year probabilities \_np\_x, \_nq\_x, m|q\_x; survival functions; expectation of life; select and ultimate tables.

### Lesson 3.1: How the international actuarial notation is built

**Must know**

- **Origin:** based on George King's "Key to the Notation"; set out in an explanatory statement adopted **unanimously** at the **Second International Actuarial Congress, London, May 1898**. Dr Sprague's rearranged statement followed at the **Third Congress, Paris, 1900** (no real change to the symbols beyond two slight additions). The **Eleventh Congress, Paris, 1937** appointed a committee (met Brussels 1938 and 1939); the planned 1940 Lucerne adoption was stopped by the war, so the **Institute of Actuaries and the Faculty of Actuaries** adopted the agreed changes in **1949**, effective from the May 1950 examinations.
- **General principle:** each principal (fundamental) letter carries signs in four positions:

| Position | Reserved for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Lower **left** | **duration** of the operation and its **position in time** | n in \_np\_x; m\| in m\|a\_x (deferment) |
| Lower **right** | **ages** of the lives and the **order of succession** of events | x in q\_x, l\_x, a\_x |
| Upper **right** | **periodicity** (frequency) of events | (m) in i^(m), a^(m)\_x |
| Upper **left** | **free**: signs for any other notion | |

- **Two conventions:** (x) in brackets = **a person aged x**. A number in a right angle, n| or 15|, = a **term-certain** of n years.
- **Deferment:** m| in the lower left means "deferred m years", e.g. m|a\_x = life annuity on (x) deferred m years; m|q\_x = probability (x) survives m years then dies in the next year.

**The 1949 changes** (know them, and know what was **not** adopted):

1. i^(m) replaced j(m) for the nominal rate of interest convertible m times a year (local use of j(m) still allowed).
2. ä (trema) adopted for the annuity-due (first payment at once); this allowed the new symbol s̈\_n| for the accumulated amount of an annuity-due at the end of the term.
3. Q (probability of dying within more than 1 year) was dropped.
4. D introduced in compound symbols for **decreasing** benefits.
5. Old commutation-column definitions abolished.

- **U for surrender value was NOT adopted** as an international symbol (only agreed that U should be chosen if a symbol were needed).

**Explain more**

- In \_5p\_30 the 5 (lower left) is a duration and the 30 (lower right) an age; in i^(12) the 12 (upper right) is how often interest is convertible.
- Trap: options swap "lower left" (duration/time) and "lower right" (ages/order). Memory aid: **left = length of time, right = the life**.
- Trap: the 1898 Congress was in **London**; 1900 and 1937 were in **Paris**; no Lucerne congress took place in 1940.

### Lesson 3.2: Interest symbols and their relationships

**Must know**

- **i:** the **effective rate of interest**: the total interest earned on 1 in a year, assuming any interest received more often than yearly is reinvested at once on the same terms as the original principal.
- **i^(m):** the **nominal rate of interest convertible m times a year** (m-thly).
- **d:** the **effective rate of discount**: the discount on 1 due a year hence.
- **d^(m):** the **nominal rate of discount convertible m times a year**.
- **v:** the **present value of 1 due a year hence** (discount factor).
- **δ (delta):** the **force of interest**: the instantaneous rate of growth, like a nominal rate compounded continuously; δ = lim i^(m) as m → ∞.

**Relationships (all equivalent at the same rate):**

1. v = 1/(1 + i) = (1 + i)^-1
2. d = i/(1 + i) = iv = 1 - v
3. i = d/(1 - d)
4. 1 + i = (1 + i^(m)/m)^m, so i^(m) = m[(1 + i)^(1/m) - 1]
5. 1 - d = (1 - d^(m)/m)^m
6. 1 + i = e^δ, δ = ln(1 + i), v = e^-δ
7. Present value of 1 due in n years = v^n; 1 grows to (1 + i)^n.

**Explain more**

Worked example at i = 8% (rounded to 5 decimal places):

- v = 1/1.08 = **0.92593**
- d = 0.08/1.08 = **0.07407** (check: 1 - v = 0.07407, iv = 0.08 × 0.92593 = 0.07407)
- δ = ln 1.08 = **0.07696**
- i^(12) = 12 × (1.08^(1/12) - 1) = **0.07721**
- v^5 = 1.08^-5 = **0.68058**, so ₦1,000,000 due in 5 years is worth ₦680,583.20 now.

More conversions:

- Nominal i^(4) = 10% convertible quarterly: i = (1 + 0.10/4)^4 - 1 = 1.025^4 - 1 = **0.10381** (10.381%).
- d = 6%: i = 0.06/0.94 = **0.06383**.
- δ = 0.05: v = e^-0.05 = **0.95123**.

- A Lagos bank quoting "12% per annum convertible monthly" is quoting **i^(12) = 0.12**, not i = 0.12 and not δ. Its effective rate is higher: 1.01^12 - 1 = 12.683%.
- Ordering at any positive rate: d < d^(m) < δ < i^(m) < i. Discount rates are always smaller than the matching interest rates.
- Trap: d (interest) is the discount on 1 due a year hence; d\_x (life table) is the number dying.

### Lesson 3.3: Mortality, annuity and assurance symbols

**Must know**

**Fundamental letters (mortality tables):**

| Letter | Meaning | Letter | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| l | number living | a | present value of an annuity |
| d | number dying | s | **amount** (accumulated value) of an annuity |
| p | probability of living | e | expectation of life |
| q | probability of dying | A | present value of an assurance |
| µ (mu) | force of mortality | E | present value of an endowment |
| m | central death rate | P | premium per annum (net premium) |
| V | policy value | W | paid-up policy |

**Life-table symbols:** l\_x = number who attain age x; d\_x = l\_x - l\_(x+1) = number who die between x and x + 1; p\_x = probability (x) lives 1 year; q\_x = probability (x) dies within 1 year; µ\_x = force of mortality at age x; m\_x = central death rate for the year of age x to x + 1; e\_x = **curtate** expectation of life (average after-lifetime) of (x).

**Annuities-certain (term n|):**

| Symbol | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| a\_n\| | PV of 1 a year for n years, paid at **end** of each year (annuity-immediate) | (1 - v^n)/i |
| ä\_n\| | PV of the same, paid at the **beginning** of each year (annuity-due) | (1 - v^n)/d = (1 + i)a\_n\| = 1 + a\_(n-1)\| |
| s\_n\| | **amount** (accumulated value at end of term), payments at end of year | ((1 + i)^n - 1)/i |
| s̈\_n\| | amount at end of term of the annuity-due | (1 + i)s\_n\| |

**Life contingency symbols on (x):**

1. **A\_x:** whole life assurance (paid at end of year of death).
2. **A^1\_x:n|** (1 over the x): n-year **term** assurance; pays only if (x) dies within n years.
3. **A\_x:n|:** n-year **endowment assurance**: pays at end of year of death within n years, or at end of n years if (x) is alive.
4. **\_nE\_x** (also A\_x:n| with 1 over the n|): pure endowment; pays only if (x) survives n years.
5. **a\_x:** whole life annuity, payments at end of each year while (x) lives.
6. **a\_x:n|:** **temporary** life annuity on (x) for n years. **ä\_x:n|:** the same as an annuity-due.

**Marks on the principal letter:**

- **Bar** (ā\_x, Ā\_x): **continuous** (momently) annuity, or assurance payable at the **moment of death**.
- **Small circle** (e̊\_x): **complete** rather than curtate.
- **Diaeresis or trema** (ä, s̈): **acceleration** of payments (paid at the beginning).
- **Square brackets** on the age, l\_[x]+t: **selection**; selected at age x, t years ago, now aged x + t. a\_[x] = annuity on a life now aged x and select now; a\_[x-n]+n = annuity on a life now aged x, selected n years ago at age x - n.

**Several lives in the suffix:**

- Letters with no mark (a\_xyz): **joint lives**; lasts while **all** survive.
- **Colon** (a\_x:n|) separates items to avoid confusion with **decimals** when numbers are involved.
- **Vertical bar** (a\_y|x): status after the bar follows the one before; **reversionary annuity** to (x) after the death of (y).
- **Horizontal bar over the suffix:** last **survivor**. A number r above it = **at least r** survivors; **[r]** = **exactly r**; no number = at least one.
- **Numerals over or under** a life give the **order of failure**: A^1\_xy (1 over x) pays on death of (x) only if (x) dies **first**; A^2\_xyz (2 over x) pays if (x) dies second.

**Premiums and compound symbols:**

- P = net premium; **P'** = a **valuation premium** other than the net premium (policy value V'); **P''** = the **office (commercial) premium** (paid-up policy W''). P^[m] = m-thly premium continuing to the end of the year of death.
- **(Ia)** = annuity starting at 1 and **increasing by 1** a year; (IA) = increasing assurance; **D** = decreasing (e.g. starting at n, falling by 1 a year); **v** in (va) = varying.

**Explain more**

- **e\_x and a\_x:** a\_x = Σ v^k × \_kp\_x, and e\_x = Σ \_kp\_x. So e\_x is a\_x valued at **i = 0**; likewise e̊\_x is ā\_x at i = 0.
- **Nigerian example:** a 15-year policy for a 35-year-old paying ₦2,000,000 at the end of the year of death within 15 years, or at the end of 15 years if alive, is an endowment assurance: **A\_35:15|**. If it paid only on death within 15 years it would be A^1\_35:15|; only on survival, \_15E\_35.
- **Annuity example** at i = 8%, n = 5 (rounded to 4 dp): v^5 = 0.68058; a\_5| = (1 - 0.68058)/0.08 = **3.9927**; ä\_5| = 1.08 × 3.9927 = **4.3121** (check: 1 + a\_4| = 1 + 3.3121 = 4.3121); s\_5| = (1.08^5 - 1)/0.08 = **5.8666**; s̈\_5| = 1.08 × 5.8666 = **6.3359**. So ₦50,000 at the end of each year for 5 years is worth 50,000 × 3.99271 = ₦199,635.50 now; paid at the start of each year, 50,000 × 4.31213 = ₦215,606.34.
- **Using ä\_n| = 1 + a\_(n-1)|:** if a\_9| = 7.1078, then ä\_10| = 1 + 7.1078 = **8.1078**.
- Trap: s is the **amount** (accumulated value) of an annuity, never its present value; a is the present value.

### Lesson 3.4: The life table and its characteristics

**Must know**

- **Mortality:** the incidence of death in a population, often measured by the probability that a randomly selected individual would die in some period of time.
- **Life (mortality) table:** a table showing, for each age x, how many of a starting group are still alive (l\_x) and how many die (d\_x), with the probabilities q\_x and p\_x. It is used for **analysing mortality and survival patterns**.
- **Radix:** the starting number l\_0 (or l at the youngest age), a convenient round figure such as 100,000 or 1,000.
- **Limiting age (ω):** the age beyond which no one survives; l\_ω = 0.

**Formulas:**

1. d\_x = l\_x - l\_(x+1)
2. q\_x = d\_x/l\_x
3. p\_x = l\_(x+1)/l\_x = 1 - q\_x
4. p\_x + q\_x = 1
5. l\_(x+1) = l\_x × p\_x = l\_x × (1 - q\_x); d\_x = l\_x × q\_x
6. Expected survivors or deaths in a group = group size × probability.

**Characteristics of a standard life table:**

1. l\_x **decreases** (never increases) as age rises.
2. d\_x = l\_x - l\_(x+1) at every age.
3. p\_x + q\_x = 1 at every age; both lie between 0 and 1.
4. The sum of d\_x from age x to the end of the table equals l\_x (everyone eventually dies).
5. It starts from a radix and ends at the limiting age.

**Select and ultimate:** newly accepted (medically selected) lives die at lower rates than others of the same age. A **select table** shows mortality by age at selection [x] and duration since selection; after the **select period** the effect wears off and **ultimate** rates (depending on attained age only) apply. A table without selection is an aggregate table. **Selection** is also the process by which an insurer decides whether to accept a risk and which set of probabilities to assign it.

**Uses:** pricing life assurance and annuities (premiums), reserves and policy values, pension and social security costs, demographic study of a population.

**Explain more**

- **Construction:** observe a large group (a national population from census and death registration, or an insurer's own policyholders), count exposed lives and deaths at each age to get crude q\_x, smooth them (graduation), then start from the radix and apply l\_(x+1) = l\_x × (1 - q\_x).
- **Nigeria:** a 2022 review of the Nigerian actuarial market noted there are **no local actuarial tables**. Nigerian life insurers (supervised by NAICOM) and pension schemes (supervised by PenCom) therefore base their q\_x and l\_x on tables built from **other populations**.
- The actuary pricing a one-year term assurance for a 45-year-old needs **q\_45** directly.
- Trap: l\_x is the **number alive at exact age x**, not a probability and not the number dying.

### Lesson 3.5: Worked life table and multi-year probabilities

**Must know**

- **\_np\_x** (often printed npx or 3p50): probability (x) survives n years = l\_(x+n)/l\_x.
- **\_nq\_x:** probability (x) dies within n years = 1 - \_np\_x = (l\_x - l\_(x+n))/l\_x.
- **m|q\_x:** (x) survives m years then dies in the next year = d\_(x+m)/l\_x = (l\_(x+m) - l\_(x+m+1))/l\_x.
- **m|nq\_x:** (x) survives m years then dies within the next n years = (l\_(x+m) - l\_(x+m+n))/l\_x.
- **Chain rule:** \_np\_x = p\_x × p\_(x+1) × ... × p\_(x+n-1) = (1 - q\_x)(1 - q\_(x+1))...(1 - q\_(x+n-1)).
- Deaths between x and x + n = l\_x - l\_(x+n) = d\_x + d\_(x+1) + ... + d\_(x+n-1).

**Explain more**

**Worked example** (probabilities rounded to 5 dp):

| x | l\_x | d\_x = l\_x - l\_(x+1) | q\_x = d\_x/l\_x | p\_x = l\_(x+1)/l\_x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 95,000 | 190 | 0.00200 | 0.99800 |
| 31 | 94,810 | 200 | 0.00211 | 0.99789 |
| 32 | 94,610 | 215 | 0.00227 | 0.99773 |
| 33 | 94,395 | 235 | 0.00249 | 0.99751 |
| 34 | 94,160 | 260 | 0.00276 | 0.99724 |
| 35 | 93,900 | | | |

1. \_3p\_30 = l\_33/l\_30 = 94,395/95,000 = **0.99363**.
2. \_5p\_30 = 93,900/95,000 = **0.98842**; \_5q\_30 = 1 - 0.98842 = 0.01158. Check: 190 + 200 + 215 + 235 + 260 = 1,100 = 95,000 - 93,900.
3. \_2q\_31 = 1 - l\_33/l\_31 = 1 - 94,395/94,810 = 415/94,810 = **0.00438**.
4. 2|q\_30 = d\_32/l\_30 = 215/95,000 = **0.00226**.
5. 1|2q\_30 = (l\_31 - l\_33)/l\_30 = (94,810 - 94,395)/95,000 = 415/95,000 = **0.00437**. Note the divisor is l\_30, not l\_31: that is why it differs from \_2q\_31.
6. 500 lives aged exactly 30: expected alive at 35 = 500 × 0.988421 = **494.21**, about 494.
7. A Lagos insurer covers 2,000 lives aged 32, each for ₦1,000,000 for one year: expected deaths = 2,000 × 215/94,610 = **4.54**; expected claims = 4.54497 × ₦1,000,000 ≈ **₦4,544,974**.

**Building a table from q\_x:** radix l\_50 = 20,000, q\_50 = 0.010, q\_51 = 0.012, q\_52 = 0.015.

- d\_50 = 20,000 × 0.010 = 200; l\_51 = 19,800.
- d\_51 = 19,800 × 0.012 = 237.6; l\_52 = 19,562.4.
- d\_52 = 19,562.4 × 0.015 = 293.436; l\_53 = **19,268.964** (19,268.96 to 2 dp).
- \_3p\_50 = 0.990 × 0.988 × 0.985 = **0.96345** = 19,268.964/20,000. Check: \_3q\_50 = 0.03655.

- From d\_x alone: if l\_70 = 5,000 and d\_70, d\_71, d\_72 are given, l\_73 = 5,000 - (d\_70 + d\_71 + d\_72); then q\_71 = d\_71/l\_71 with l\_71 = 5,000 - d\_70.
- Trap: \_nq\_x is **not** the sum of the q's, and \_np\_x is **not** l\_(x+n) alone. Always divide by l at the **starting** age.

### Lesson 3.6: Survival functions and expectation of life

**Must know**

- **Survival function s(x):** probability a newborn survives to age x; s(x) = l\_x/l\_0, s(0) = 1, falls to 0 at the limiting age.
- l\_x = l\_0 × s(x).
- \_np\_x = s(x + n)/s(x); \_nq\_x = 1 - s(x + n)/s(x).
- Probability a **newborn** dies between ages a and b = s(a) - s(b).
- **Curtate expectation of life:** e\_x = (l\_(x+1) + l\_(x+2) + ... )/l\_x = Σ \_kp\_x (k = 1, 2, ...): average number of **complete** future years lived by (x).
- **Complete expectation of life e̊\_x:** average total future lifetime including fractions; approximately e̊\_x ≈ e\_x + 0.5 (deaths spread evenly over each year).

**Explain more**

**Example:** s(x) = 1 - x/100, 0 ≤ x ≤ 100.

- Newborn survives to 25: s(25) = 1 - 0.25 = **0.75**.
- \_25p\_40 = s(65)/s(40) = 0.35/0.60 = **0.5833** (4 dp).
- \_30q\_40 = 1 - s(70)/s(40) = 1 - 0.30/0.60 = **0.50**.
- Newborn dies between 20 and 50: s(20) - s(50) = 0.80 - 0.50 = **0.30**.
- Radix l\_0 = 10,000: l\_60 = 10,000 × 0.40 = **4,000**.

**Square-root example:** s(x) = √(1 - x/100). s(19) = √0.81 = 0.9; s(51) = √0.49 = 0.7; a life aged 19 survives to 51 with probability 0.7/0.9 = 7/9 = **0.7778**. The same method works for any s(x), such as (1 - x/100)^2: divide s at the later age by s at the current age.

- Trap: "a life aged 0 dies between 16 and 43" uses s(16) - s(43); "a life aged 16 survives to 43" uses s(43)/s(16). Watch who the life is now.

**Expectation example:** l\_90 = 500, l\_91 = 300, l\_92 = 150, l\_93 = 60, l\_94 = 15, l\_95 = 0.

- e\_90 = (300 + 150 + 60 + 15)/500 = 525/500 = **1.05** years.
- e\_92 = (60 + 15)/150 = **0.50** years.
- e̊\_90 ≈ 1.05 + 0.5 = **1.55** years.
- Trap: the sum starts at l\_(x+1), **not** l\_x. Including l\_x adds exactly 1.

### One-minute recap

- Corners of a symbol: lower left = duration/time (and deferment m|), lower right = ages and order, upper right = periodicity, upper left = free. (x) = a person aged x; n| = term-certain.
- Adopted London 1898; revised 1949 (i^(m), ä and s̈, D, Q dropped); U not adopted.
- v = 1/(1 + i), d = iv = 1 - v, i = d/(1 - d), 1 + i = (1 + i^(m)/m)^m = e^δ.
- a = PV of annuity, s = amount, A = assurance, E = endowment, P' valuation premium, P'' office premium, W paid-up.
- Bar = continuous, circle = complete, trema = acceleration (due), [x] = select.
- A\_x whole life, A^1\_x:n| term, A\_x:n| endowment assurance, a\_x:n| temporary annuity, ä\_x:n| its annuity-due.
- d\_x = l\_x - l\_(x+1); q\_x = d\_x/l\_x; p\_x = 1 - q\_x; l\_x always falls; radix = l\_0.
- \_np\_x = l\_(x+n)/l\_x; m|q\_x = d\_(x+m)/l\_x; s(x) = l\_x/l\_0.
- e\_x = (l\_(x+1) + l\_(x+2) + ...)/l\_x = a\_x at i = 0; e̊\_x ≈ e\_x + 0.5.
- Nigeria has no local actuarial tables; insurers rely on foreign-population tables.

### Theory practice

**Question:** In a life table l\_40 = 90,000, q\_40 = 0.003 and q\_41 = 0.004. Find l\_41, d\_41, l\_42 and \_2p\_40.

*Solution:*

- l\_41 = 90,000 × (1 - 0.003) = 90,000 × 0.997 = **89,730**.
- d\_41 = l\_41 × q\_41 = 89,730 × 0.004 = **358.92**.
- l\_42 = 89,730 - 358.92 = **89,371.08**.
- \_2p\_40 = 89,371.08/90,000 = **0.993012** (check: 0.997 × 0.996 = 0.993012). So \_2q\_40 = 0.006988.
