## Topic 2: History and Professional Structure of Actuarial Science

**What you must know:** the people, works and dates that built actuarial science (1654 to 1825); the Equitable Society and the first use of the title "actuary"; the world's actuarial bodies and when they began; the Nigerian bodies and what each does; the student, associate and fellow grades and the IFoA, SOA and CAS exam pathways; professional standards and ethics; career outlook, supply and demand.

### Lesson 2.1: Probability and the first mortality tables

**Must know**

- **1654, Blaise Pascal and Pierre de Fermat:** letters on the gambler's "problem of points". Treated as the start of **modern probability theory**.
- **1662, John Graunt:** a London draper (cloth tradesman). Published *Natural and Political Observations ... upon the Bills of Mortality*, based on the **weekly London Bills of Mortality**. Showed deaths follow predictable patterns in a population. Main contribution: the **first attempt at a life table** from death records.
- **1693, Edmond Halley:** the astronomer famous for predicting the return of the comet named after him. Built a life table from the **parish records of Breslau** and showed how to **price a life annuity** from it. Practical use: life annuities sold by the British government should be priced **according to the buyer's age**.
- **1713, Jacob Bernoulli:** *Ars Conjectandi*, published after his death. Contains an early version of the **law of large numbers**.
- **1725, Abraham de Moivre:** *Annuities upon Lives* (London). Methods for valuing annuities on one or more lives, using a **postulated uniform rate of mortality** together with a **constant rate of interest**.

**Law of large numbers / statistical regularity:** if similar independent trials are repeated under the same conditions, the proportion of times an event happens settles down (stabilises) as the number of trials grows. Pooling many similar risks makes the insurer's results predictable.

**Explain more**

- Do not swap the cities: **Graunt = London** (Bills of Mortality); **Halley = Breslau**. Northampton belongs to Richard Price (Lesson 2.2); Equitable records belong to neither.
- Halley's table showed that a young buyer of a life annuity should pay more than an old buyer, because the young buyer is expected to collect payments for longer. A flat price for all ages is wrong.
- Law of large numbers in ₦ terms: a Lagos motor insurer with only 200 policies may see 4 accidents one year (2%) and 14 the next (7%). With 50,000 similar policies the yearly rate stays close to 4%, about 2,000 accidents. The insurer can then set a premium with confidence. This is **statistical regularity**, not adverse selection or the time value of money.

### Lesson 2.2: The birth of the profession, 1750s to 1825

**Must know**

- **James Dodson** (died **1757**): pioneered the **level premium system** for long-term life assurance. His work led to the founding of the Equitable.
- **1762, the Society for Equitable Assurances on Lives and Survivorships** (the **Equitable**, London): the first life office to charge **scientifically calculated premiums** on long-term policies.
- **Edward Rowe Mores**, a founder of the Equitable, specified around 1762 that its chief official be called the **actuary**: the first use of the title for a business position. Before then, an actuary (Latin *actuarius*) was a **clerk who kept the records of a court or senate**.
- **William Morgan:** appointed actuary of the Equitable in **1775**, held the post until **1830**; often called the **father of the actuarial profession**.
- **Richard Price:** *Observations on Reversionary Payments* (**1771**); compiled the **Northampton mortality table**, a British standard for about a century. Weakness: it **overstated mortality**, so **annuities priced on it were sold too cheaply** (and life cover was overpriced).
- **1825, Benjamin Gompertz:** law of mortality based on the idea that a person's **resistance to death declines** with age, so the force of mortality rises geometrically: μ\_x = B × c^x (B > 0, c > 1).

| Premium type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Level premium** | The same premium every year for the policy's life; early years overpay, later years underpay |
| **Natural (yearly risk) premium** | Each year's premium matches that year's risk, so it rises with age |
| **Single premium** | One payment at the start covers the whole policy |

**Explain more**

- **Level premium example:** Bola, aged 30, pays ₦60,000 every year on a whole life policy, and still pays ₦60,000 at 60 although her death risk is now far higher. This is a **level premium**. Her early overpayments build a reserve that meets the shortfall in later years.
- Trap: **Gompertz is not linked with the level premium**; that is Dodson. Gompertz = 1825 law of mortality.
- Trap: Mores named the post; **Morgan held it** for 55 years. Price was Morgan's uncle and adviser.

**Timeline to memorise:** 1654 Pascal and Fermat · 1662 Graunt · 1693 Halley · 1713 Bernoulli · 1725 de Moivre · 1757 Dodson dies · 1762 Equitable and "actuary" · 1771 Price · 1775 to 1830 Morgan · 1825 Gompertz · 1848 Institute of Actuaries.

### Lesson 2.3: Actuarial bodies around the world

**Must know**

| Body | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Institute of Actuaries** | Founded **1848**, London |
| **Faculty of Actuaries** | Founded **1856**, Scotland (Edinburgh); Royal Charter **1868**, the **first actuarial body granted one** |
| **Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (IFoA)** | The two merged on **1 August 2010**; offices in London, Oxford, Edinburgh, Beijing, Malaysia and Singapore |
| **Society of Actuaries (SOA)** | Formed **1949** by merging the **Actuarial Society of America** (1889) and the **American Institute of Actuaries** (1909); Chicago, Illinois area |
| **Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS)** | Founded **November 1914** as the **Casualty Actuarial and Statistical Society of America**; present name **1921**; Arlington, Virginia (Washington, DC area). Began with **workers' compensation** insurance |
| **International Actuarial Association (IAA)** | Origin: committee of **1895**, when the first **International Congress of Actuaries** met in **Brussels**. Restructured in **1998** at the Congress in **Birmingham**: its full members are **national actuarial associations** (an association of associations) |
| **Actuarial Society of South Africa (ASSA)** | Cape Town |

- Letters: **FIA** / **FFA** = Fellow of the Institute / **Fellow of the Faculty of Actuaries**; **AIA** = Associate of the Institute. **ASA / FSA** = SOA; **ACAS / FCAS** = CAS.
- The IAA's **International Standards of Actuarial Practice (ISAPs)** are **model standards**: member associations are encouraged to align their own standards with them. They are not binding world rules.

**United States professional bodies**

1. **American Academy of Actuaries** (1965): its **MAAA** designation must be held to **sign statements of actuarial opinion** in the US.
2. **Actuarial Standards Board (ASB):** promulgates the **Actuarial Standards of Practice (ASOPs)**, which say what an actuary should **consider, document and disclose** on an assignment.
3. **Actuarial Board for Counseling and Discipline (ABCD):** reviews complaints that an actuary breached the code of conduct and can recommend discipline.

**Practice areas by body**

| Area | IFoA | SOA | CAS | ASSA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Life | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Pensions/retirement | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Health | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| General insurance (P&C) | Yes | No | Yes | Yes |

**Explain more**

- **CAS = property and casualty only**: motor (auto) pricing, liability ratemaking and property reserving are CAS work; **life assurance and annuities are not**.
- Chinedu, a general insurance actuary in Lagos, completing CAS Associate requirements writes **ACAS** (not ASA, which is SOA, and not FCAS, which is Fellow).
- Year traps: 1848 Institute; 1856 Faculty; 1868 Faculty's charter; 1889 Actuarial Society of America; 1762 Equitable.
- When these bodies were founded in the mid-19th century, one main purpose besides supporting actuaries was **protecting the public** by ensuring members' competence and ethical standards. They never set every insurer's premiums, collected taxes or sold policies.
- All bodies' syllabuses cover financial mathematics, probability, statistics, accounting, corporate finance, loss and mortality models, reserving, data ethics and model validation. Banking, ERM and microinsurance are notable exclusions from the practice areas above.

### Lesson 2.4: The profession and regulators in Nigeria

**Must know**

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Nigerian Actuarial Society (NAS)** | **Professional body for actuaries** in Nigeria |
| **National Insurance Commission (NAICOM)** | Government agency established under the **National Insurance Commission Act 1997**; **regulates and supervises insurers** |
| **National Pension Commission (PenCom)** | Established by the **Pension Reform Act 2004**; **regulates and supervises pensions** |
| **Chartered Insurance Institute of Nigeria (CIIN)** | Professional body for insurance practitioners (not for actuaries) |
| **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** | Regulates the capital market (not insurance) |

- **January 2024:** the University of Lagos MSc Actuarial Science programme was accredited by the **IFoA**, earning its graduates exam exemptions.

**Current landscape in Nigeria (2022 figures)**

1. **Supply:** 19 Fellows, 8 Associates, 70+ students; 5 universities, including UNILAG.
2. **Demand:** 56 insurance companies, life and non-life (limited in pensions); requirement soon to keep an actuarial function in-house; IFRS 17 and innovations.
3. **Public policy and regulation:** 3 actuarial staff within NAICOM but no qualified actuaries; no clarity on whether an appointed actuary must be an associate or a fellow; **no local actuarial (mortality) tables**; plans for risk-based capital requirements and IFRS 17.

**Explain more**

- Memory hook: **N**AICOM = i**N**surance regulator; **P**enCom = **P**ensions regulator; NAS = the actuaries' own society. Exams love swapping these three.
- The figures show demand far ahead of supply: 56 insurers but only 19 Fellows in the whole country. That gap is the career opportunity for a UNILAG actuarial student.

### Lesson 2.5: Professional structure and how to qualify

**Must know**

- **Grades:** **Student → Associate → Fellow**.
- You qualify by passing a **series of professional exams, usually while working** ("earn while you learn"). Exams are held several times a year. A degree alone does not qualify you.
- **SOA/CAS:** **Associate (ASA, ACAS)** and **Fellow (FSA, FCAS)**.
  - **ASA:** has shown knowledge of the fundamental concepts and techniques for modelling and managing risk through required exams and learning modules, and completed a professionalism course.
  - **FSA:** has shown knowledge in **all major practice areas**, with **further concentration in one or more** of them, plus further professionalism education.
- **SOA/CAS joint preliminary exams:** **P/1** Probability; **FM/2** Financial Mathematics; **MFE/3F** Financial Economics; **C/4** Actuarial Models. Then SOA's **MLC** (Life Contingencies) or CAS's **3L** (Life Contingencies and Statistics).
- **SOA upper-level areas:** Retirement Benefits; Group and Health; Individual Life and Annuities; Investments. **CAS:** P&C insurance/pricing; reserving; accounting/annual statement; advanced financial management; advanced pricing.

**IFoA pathway**

1. **Core Principles (7):** CS1, CS2 Actuarial Statistics; CM1, CM2 Actuarial Mathematics; CB1 Business Finance; CB2 Business Economics; CB3 Business Management (Professional Skills Stage 1 needed before CB3).
2. **Core Practices (3):** **CP1 Actuarial Practice**, CP2 Modelling Practice, CP3 Communication Practice.
3. **Associate** = Core Principles + Core Practices + **2 years of PPD** (Personal and Professional Development) + **Professional Skills Stages 1 and 2**.
4. **Specialist Principles (choose 2 of 8):** SP1 Health and Care, SP2 Life Insurance, SP4 Pensions and Other Benefits, SP5 Investment and Finance, SP6 Financial Derivatives, SP7 General Insurance Reserving and Capital Modelling, SP8 General Insurance Pricing, SP9 Enterprise Risk Management.
5. **Specialist Advanced (choose 1, the final exam):** SA1 Health and Care, SA2 Life Insurance, SA3 General Insurance, SA4 Pensions and Other Benefits, SA7 Investment and Finance.
6. **Fellow** = 2 SP + 1 SA + **3 years of PPD in total** (one more year after Associate).

**Ethics and standards**

- **Code of Professional Conduct:** sets out what it means for an actuary to act as a professional; responsibilities to **the public**, to **clients and employers**, and to **the actuarial profession**. It brings accountability, responsibility and trust.
- **Professionalism:** professional status, methods, character or standards; adherence to the Code.
- **Continuing professional development (CPD):** qualified members keep learning and record it each year to stay competent.

**Explain more**

- Trap: competing firms are **not** among the groups the Code protects.
- Trap: "Associate" needs all Core exams but **no** Specialist exams; "Fellow" adds 2 SP, 1 SA and the third PPD year. CP1 is Core Practices; CM1 and CB2 are Core Principles; SP2 is Specialist.
- **Tax Auditing is not** an IFoA Specialist Principles subject.
- **Skills needed:** mathematical and analytical skill, project management and problem solving, communication (oral and written), business sense, self-motivation (you pass exams while working), teamwork, creativity and independence.

### Lesson 2.6: Specialisations and career outlook

**Must know**

**8 specialisations:**

1. Health and Care
2. Life Insurance
3. Pensions
4. Investment and Finance
5. Financial Derivatives
6. General Insurance: Reserving
7. General Insurance: Pricing
8. Enterprise Risk Management

- **Career outlook:** the **2002 Jobs Rated Almanac** ranked actuary **number 2** in America (number 1 biologist, 3 financial planner, 5 accountant, 9 statistician).
- **Pay:** US starting salaries of about 46,000 to 65,000 US dollars (one exam passed, 0 to 0.5 years' experience; base pay only, 2009 survey), rising with merit, with **each exam passed** and on reaching each designation.

**10 reasons to become an actuary:** (1) competitive salary and benefits; (2) suits the self-motivated with strong maths and communication; (3) a choice of job offers; (4) earn while you learn; (5) job security through economic cycles; (6) skills transferable across industries; (7) early advancement is largely exam-based, so you control it; (8) dynamic, challenging work; (9) a professional title without being a doctor, lawyer or accountant; (10) continual learning. Also: international opportunities and globally recognised qualifications.

**Explain more**

- **Pensions example:** Ngozi, at an Abuja consulting firm, projects staff salaries and retirement ages and estimates how much to set aside today for future retirement benefits. That is **pensions** work. If the fund's benefits are ₦500 million due in 10 years and the fund can earn 10% a year, the amount to set aside now is ₦500,000,000 × (1.10)^-10 = ₦192,771,645 (to the nearest naira).
- Health and care prices medical cover (for example HMO premiums); financial derivatives values options; general insurance pricing sets motor and home premiums.

### One-minute recap

- 1654 Pascal and Fermat (probability); 1662 Graunt, London Bills (first life table attempt); 1693 Halley, Breslau (annuity price by age).
- 1713 Bernoulli (law of large numbers); 1725 de Moivre (uniform mortality plus constant interest).
- Dodson (died 1757) = level premium; 1762 Equitable = first scientific premiums; Mores named the "actuary" (once a court clerk); Morgan, actuary 1775 to 1830, father of the profession.
- Price's Northampton table overstated mortality, so annuities were too cheap; Gompertz 1825 law of mortality.
- Institute 1848; Faculty 1856, charter 1868; IFoA 1 August 2010; SOA 1949; CAS 1914 (named 1921); IAA from 1895 Brussels, association of associations since 1998.
- NAS = actuaries; NAICOM (1997 Act) = insurers; PenCom (Pension Reform Act 2004) = pensions.
