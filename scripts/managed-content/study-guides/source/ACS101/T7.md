## Topic 7: Annuities and Annuity Functions

**What you must know:** the types of annuity and their symbols; a\_n|, s\_n|, ä\_n|, s̈\_n| and how they are linked; loans and savings funds; deferred annuities and perpetuities; m-thly and continuous annuities; increasing, decreasing and geometric annuities.

### Lesson 7.1: Types of annuity and their symbols

**Must know**

- **Annuity:** a series of payments made at regular intervals of time. As a contract, an annuity is an agreement with an insurance company that provides a series of payments at regular intervals for a number of years or over a lifetime.
- **Annuity-immediate:** payments at the **end** of each period (times 1, 2, ..., n). Also called an **ordinary annuity** or an **annuity in arrears**.
- **Annuity-due:** payments at the **start** of each period (times 0, 1, ..., n - 1). Also called an **annuity in advance**.
- **Deferred annuity:** payments do not begin immediately, or in the first period, but only after a period of postponement. Deferred m periods: payments at times m + 1, ..., m + n.
- **Perpetuity:** an annuity payable for an unlimited term; payments continue for ever, with no termination date.
- **Annuity-certain:** pays for a fixed term whatever happens.
- **Life (contingent) annuity:** pays only while a named life survives.
    - **Whole-life annuity:** pays for as long as the person lives; payments stop at death.
    - **Temporary life annuity for n years:** continues during the joint duration of the life of (x) and a term of n years certain, so it stops at death or after n years, whichever comes first.
    - **Reversionary annuity:** an annuity on the life of (x) after the death of (y).

**The symbols (International Actuarial Notation)**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| a\_n\| | present value of an annuity-immediate of 1 per period for n periods |
| s\_n\| | accumulated value (amount) at time n of that annuity-immediate |
| ä\_n\| (a-double-dot) | present value of an annuity-due |
| s̈\_n\| (s-double-dot) | accumulated value at time n of an annuity-due |
| ā\_n\| (a-bar) | present value of a continuous annuity (payable continuously) |
| m\|a\_n\| | present value of an annuity deferred m periods |
| a\_∞\| , ä\_∞\| | perpetuity-immediate, perpetuity-due |
| a^(m)\_n\| , ä^(m)\_n\| | annuity of 1 per period paid m-thly, in arrears or in advance |
| (Ia)\_n\| , (Da)\_n\| , (va) | increasing, decreasing and varying annuities |

- **a** = present value of an annuity; **s** = amount of an annuity; **A** = present value of an assurance (not an annuity).
- The **diaeresis** (two dots) over a or s is the symbol of **acceleration of payments**: payments at the beginning of each period.
- A **bar** over the principal symbol means **continuous** (momently) payment.
- Sometimes the rate is shown as a subscript: a\_n|i.

**Explain more**

- Trap: "annuity-due" is the odd one out. Annuity in arrears, ordinary annuity and annuity-immediate all mean end-of-period payments.
- A tenant in Yaba paying rent of ₦400,000 at the beginning of each year for 3 years, starting today, is a 3-year annuity-due from the landlord's side.
- Preference shares paying a fixed dividend every year with no redemption date are valued as a **perpetuity**.
- A retiree's pension of ₦150,000 every month until death is a **whole-life (contingent) annuity**. Under the Nigerian Contributory Pension Scheme, regulated by PenCom, a retiree can use the balance in a Retirement Savings Account to buy a life annuity from a life insurer.

### Lesson 7.2: Annuity-immediate: a\_n| and s\_n|

**Must know**

With v = 1/(1 + i):

- a\_n| = v + v^2 + ... + v^n = (1 - v^n)/i = [1 - (1 + i)^-n]/i
- s\_n| = (1 + i)^(n-1) + ... + (1 + i) + 1 = [(1 + i)^n - 1]/i
- s\_n| = (1 + i)^n × a\_n|
- Level payment P: PV = P × a\_n|, FV (future value) = P × s\_n| = P[(1 + i)^n - 1]/i.
- **Loan instalment** (first payment one period after the loan): X = Loan / a\_n|.
- **Savings (sinking) fund** target F, deposits in arrears: X = F / s\_n|.
- **Value at time t** (0 ≤ t ≤ n, just after the t-th payment) = s\_t| + a\_(n-t)| = (1 + i)^t a\_n|.
- **Outstanding loan** just after the t-th instalment = X × a\_(n-t)| (PV of the remaining instalments).
- **Total interest** on a loan = n × X - Loan.

**Explain more**

**Example 1 (PV and FV).** ₦100,000 at the end of each year for 5 years at 9%.

- v^5 = 1.09^-5 = 0.649931, so a\_5| = (1 - 0.649931)/0.09 = 3.8897 (4 d.p.).
- PV = 100,000 × 3.889651 = **₦388,965**.
- s\_5| = (1.538624 - 1)/0.09 = 5.9847; FV = **₦598,471**. Check: 388,965 × 1.09^5 = 598,471.

**Example 2 (loan).** A loan of ₦2,000,000 is repaid by 4 equal annual instalments in arrears at 12%.

- a\_4| = (1 - 1.12^-4)/0.12 = (1 - 0.635518)/0.12 = 3.0373.
- X = 2,000,000/3.037349 = **₦658,469**.
- Just after the 1st instalment, the outstanding loan = X × a\_3| = 658,468.87 × 2.401831 = **₦1,581,531**.
- Total interest = 4 × 658,468.87 - 2,000,000 = **₦633,875**.

**Example 3 (savings fund).** A firm wants ₦1,000,000 in 6 years, depositing at the end of each year at 8%. s\_6| = (1.586874 - 1)/0.08 = 7.3359. Deposit = 1,000,000/7.335929 = **₦136,315**.

**Example 4 (how many payments).** ₦300,000 pays ₦40,000 at the end of each year for as long as possible at 6%. Need a\_n| = 300,000/40,000 = 7.5. At 6%, a\_10| = 7.3601 and a\_11| = 7.8869, so **10 full payments** (a\_11| is too big). The fund left at time 0 is 300,000 - 40,000 × 7.360087 = 5,596.52; at time 10 this is 5,596.52 × 1.06^10 = 5,596.52 × 1.790848 = **₦10,023**, the extra amount paid with the 10th payment.

**Example 5 (finding the rate).** a\_10| = 7.3601 at some rate. Try values: at 6%, a\_10| = 7.3601. So i = **6%**. In a CBT, test each option in (1 - (1 + i)^-n)/i; a higher rate gives a smaller a\_n|.

**Example 6 (value at an intermediate time).** A 5-year annuity-immediate of 1 at 9%, valued just after the 2nd payment: s\_2| + a\_3| = 2.0900 + 2.5313 = **4.6213** (equal to 1.09^2 × 3.889651).

- If a\_n| is given, v^n = 1 - i × a\_n|, and s\_n| = (1 + i)^n × a\_n| = a\_n|/v^n. Example: a\_n| = 10.5631 at 4% gives v^n = 1 - 0.04 × 10.5631 = 0.5775.
- If only v is given, a\_n| = v + v^2 + ... + v^n. With v = 0.95: a\_4| = 0.95 + 0.9025 + 0.857375 + 0.814506 = **3.5244**.
- Trap: [1 - (1 + i)^-n]/i is a PV; [(1 + i)^n - 1]/i is an FV. Expressions with (1 + i)^n in place of (1 + i)^-n in a PV are wrong.

### Lesson 7.3: Annuity-due and the key relationships

**Must know**

With d = i/(1 + i) = 1 - v:

- ä\_n| = 1 + v + ... + v^(n-1) = (1 - v^n)/d
- s̈\_n| = [(1 + i)^n - 1]/d = (1 + i)^n × ä\_n|
- ä\_n| = (1 + i) × a\_n|  and  s̈\_n| = (1 + i) × s\_n| (each payment is one period earlier)
- ä\_n| = 1 + a\_(n-1)| (a payment now plus an (n - 1)-payment annuity-immediate)
- s\_(n+1)| = s̈\_n| + 1 (an n-payment annuity-due from time 1 plus a final 1 at time n + 1)
- ä\_n| - a\_n| = 1 - v^n
- **Splitting a term:** a\_(m+n)| = a\_m| + v^m a\_n|;  s\_(m+n)| = (1 + i)^n s\_m| + s\_n|.
- For a positive rate, **ä\_n| > a\_n|** because each payment is received one period earlier; likewise s̈\_n| > s\_n|.

**Explain more**

**Example 1.** At 5%, a\_5| = 4.3295, so ä\_6| = 1 + a\_5| = **5.3295**. Check: (1 - 1.05^-6)/d with d = 0.05/1.05 = 0.047619 gives 5.3295.

**Example 2.** At 5%, s̈\_6| = (1.340096 - 1)/0.047619 = 7.1420, so s\_7| = s̈\_6| + 1 = **8.1420**.

**Example 3 (rent in advance).** A shop is let for 6 years at ₦250,000 at the beginning of each year, 5%. PV = 250,000 × 5.329477 = **₦1,332,369**.

**Example 4 (d given).** d = 5%, so v = 0.95. ä\_4| = (1 - 0.95^4)/0.05 = (1 - 0.814506)/0.05 = **3.7099**.

**Example 5 (lease or deposit in advance).** Equal instalments at the start of each year: X = PV / ä\_n|. A fund target F with deposits at the start of each year: X = F / s̈\_n|, where s̈\_n| measures the balance at time n, one year after the last deposit.

**Example 6 (splitting).** At 10%, a\_2| = 1.7355, a\_3| = 2.4869, v^2 = 0.826446: a\_5| = a\_2| + v^2 a\_3| = 1.7355 + 0.826446 × 2.4869 = **3.7908**.

- Traps: s̈\_n| = s\_n|/(1 + i) is **wrong** (it is multiplied, not divided). ä\_n| = 1 + a\_n| is wrong (it is 1 + a\_(n-1)|). a\_(m+n)| = a\_m| + a\_n| is wrong (the second block must be discounted by v^m).
- Difference between due and immediate for ₦100,000 a year for 5 years at 10%: 100,000 × (1 - 1.1^-5) = 100,000 × 0.379079 = **₦37,908**.

### Lesson 7.4: Deferred annuities and perpetuities

**Must know**

- **Deferred annuity-immediate** (payments at m + 1, ..., m + n):
  m|a\_n| = v^m a\_n| = a\_(m+n)| - a\_m| = (v^m - v^(m+n))/i.
- **Deferred annuity-due** (payments at m, ..., m + n - 1): m|ä\_n| = v^m ä\_n|.
- If the first payment is at time k (in arrears), the deferment is m = k - 1.
- **Perpetuity-immediate:** a\_∞| = 1/i. Reason: v < 1, so v^n tends to 0 as n tends to infinity in a\_n| = (1 - v^n)/i.
- **Perpetuity-due:** ä\_∞| = 1/d = (1 + i)/i = 1 + 1/i.
- **Deferred perpetuity** (first payment at time m + 1): v^m/i.
- Payments from time n + 1 onwards: 1/i - a\_n| = v^n/i.
- **Rate from a perpetuity:** i = payment / price (in arrears). If in advance, price = X + X/i, so i = X/(price - X).

**Explain more**

**Example 1.** 2|a\_3| at 10% (payments at times 3, 4, 5): v^2 a\_3| = 0.826446 × 2.4869 = **2.0552**. Check: a\_5| - a\_2| = 3.7908 - 1.7355 = 2.0553 (difference from rounding; 2.0552 to 4 d.p.).

**Example 2 (gift).** ₦100,000 at the end of each year for 3 years, the first at the end of year 3, at 10%: deferment m = 2, PV = 100,000 × 2.055250 = **₦205,525**.

**Example 3 (perpetuities at 12%, ₦90,000 a year).**

- In arrears: 90,000/0.12 = **₦750,000**.
- In advance (first payment today): 90,000 × 1.12/0.12 = **₦840,000**.
- First payment at the end of year 5 (m = 4): 750,000 × 1.12^-4 = 750,000 × 0.635518 = **₦476,639**.

**Example 4 (rate).** ₦600,000 buys ₦45,000 a year in arrears for ever: i = 45,000/600,000 = **7.5%**. ₦1,500,000 buys ₦100,000 a year with the first payment today: i = 100,000/1,400,000 = **7.14%** (2 d.p.).

**Example 5.** At 10%, payments of 1 from year 9 onwards: 1/0.1 - a\_8| = 10 - 5.3349 = **4.6651**.

- If d is given, perpetuity-due = 1/d and perpetuity-immediate = 1/i = 1/d - 1. With d = 0.04: 25 and 24.
- Trap: a\_(m+n)| + a\_m| is **not** a deferred annuity value; the correct form subtracts.

### Lesson 7.5: Annuities payable m-thly and continuously

**Must know**

- **Payment period = interest period:** use the rate per payment period and the number of payments. 24% convertible monthly gives 2% a month.
- **Periods differ:** first find the **effective rate j per payment period**, then use a\_n|j.
    - Payments less often than interest (e.g. half-yearly payments, interest quarterly): j = (1 + i^(4)/4)^2 - 1.
    - Payments more often than interest (e.g. quarterly payments, interest half-yearly): j = (1 + i^(2)/2)^(1/2) - 1.
- **m-thly formulae** (1/m paid every 1/m of a year, total 1 a year, i effective annual):
    - a^(m)\_n| = (1 - v^n)/i^(m), where i^(m) = m[(1 + i)^(1/m) - 1]
    - ä^(m)\_n| = (1 - v^n)/d^(m), where d^(m) = m[1 - (1 + i)^(-1/m)]
    - s^(m)\_n| = [(1 + i)^n - 1]/i^(m); a^(m)\_n| = (i/i^(m)) a\_n|.
- **Continuous annuity:** ā\_n| = (1 - v^n)/δ, with δ = ln(1 + i).
- Order for the same i > 0 and 1 a year: a\_n| < a^(m)\_n| < ā\_n| < ä^(m)\_n| < ä\_n|.

**Explain more**

**Example 1 (monthly).** ₦8,000 at the end of each month for 2 years at 12% convertible monthly. j = 1% a month, 24 payments: a\_24| = (1 - 1.01^-24)/0.01 = 21.2434. PV = **₦169,947**. Monthly loan instalments work the same way: X = Loan / a\_(12n)| at the monthly rate.

**Example 2 (payments less often).** ₦50,000 at the end of each half-year for 3 years at 12% convertible monthly. j = 1.01^6 - 1 = 0.061520 per half-year. PV = 50,000 × a\_6|j = 50,000 × 4.8939 = **₦244,696**.

**Example 3 (payments more often).** ₦30,000 at the end of each quarter for 2 years at 12% convertible half-yearly. j = 1.06^(1/2) - 1 = 0.029563 per quarter. PV = 30,000 × a\_8|j = 30,000 × 7.0327 = **₦210,980** (to the nearest naira, using unrounded factors).

**Example 4 (formula).** ₦60,000 a year paid as ₦5,000 at the end of each month for 5 years, 8% effective. i^(12) = 12(1.08^(1/12) - 1) = 0.077208; 1 - v^5 = 0.319417. a^(12)\_5| = 0.319417/0.077208 = 4.1371. PV = 60,000 × 4.137075 = **₦248,225**. Paid at the **start** of each month: d^(12) = 12(1 - 1.08^(-1/12)) = 0.076715, ä^(12)\_5| = 4.1637, PV = **₦249,822**. Note: multiply by the **annual** amount.

**Example 5 (continuous).** ā\_5| at 8%: δ = ln 1.08 = 0.076961, ā\_5| = 0.319417/0.076961 = **4.1504**. It lies between a\_5| = 3.9927 and ä\_5| = 4.3121.

- Trap: a^(m)\_n| uses v^n (years) on top, not v^(mn), and i^(m) underneath, not i.

### Lesson 7.6: Increasing, decreasing, geometric and simple-interest annuities

**Must know**

- **Increasing annuity-immediate** (1 at time 1, 2 at time 2, ..., n at time n):
  (Ia)\_n| = (ä\_n| - n v^n)/i.  Increasing annuity-due: (Iä)\_n| = (1 + i)(Ia)\_n|.
- **Decreasing annuity-immediate** (n at time 1, n - 1 at time 2, ..., 1 at time n):
  (Da)\_n| = (n - a\_n|)/i.
- **Arithmetic** (P at time 1, rising by D each period): PV = P a\_n| + D(a\_n| - n v^n)/i.
- **Geometric** (1 at time 1, rising by k each period, k ≠ i): PV = [1 - ((1 + k)/(1 + i))^n]/(i - k). Multiply by the first payment.
- **Varying annuity** (va): payments of any amounts; value each payment separately.
- **Simple interest:** PV of 1 due at t is 1/(1 + rt), so a\_n| = sum of 1/(1 + rt) for t = 1 to n. There is no neat formula. At the same rate it is **higher** than the compound value, because simple interest discounts later payments less.

**Explain more**

**Example 1.** ₦10,000, ₦20,000, ₦30,000, ₦40,000 at the ends of years 1 to 4, at 10%. ä\_4| = 3.4869, 4v^4 = 2.7321. (Ia)\_4| = (3.4869 - 2.7321)/0.1 = 7.5480. PV = **₦75,480**. Check: 10,000(1 × 0.9091 + 2 × 0.8264 + 3 × 0.7513 + 4 × 0.6830) agrees.

**Example 2.** ₦40,000, ₦30,000, ₦20,000, ₦10,000 at 10%: (Da)\_4| = (4 - 3.1699)/0.1 = 8.3013. PV = **₦83,013**.

**Example 3 (arithmetic).** ₦50,000 at time 1, rising by ₦10,000 a year, 4 payments, 10%: 50,000 × 3.1699 + 10,000 × (3.1699 - 2.7321)/0.1 = 158,493 + 43,781 = **₦202,274**.

**Example 4 (geometric).** ₦200,000 at time 1, rising 4% a year, 5 payments, 10%: (1.04/1.1)^5 = 0.755446; PV = 200,000 × (1 - 0.755446)/0.06 = **₦815,181**.

**Example 5 (simple interest).** a\_3| at 10% simple: 1/1.1 + 1/1.2 + 1/1.3 = 0.9091 + 0.8333 + 0.7692 = **2.5117**; at 10% compound a\_3| = 2.4869, so the simple value is higher.

- Trap: (Ia)\_n| has ä\_n| (the due factor) in its formula; (Da)\_n| has a\_n| and n. Do not swap them.

### One-minute recap

- Annuity = regular payments. Immediate (ordinary, in arrears) pays at period ends; due (in advance) pays at period starts.
- a\_n| = (1 - v^n)/i; s\_n| = [(1 + i)^n - 1]/i; ä\_n| = (1 - v^n)/d; s̈\_n| = [(1 + i)^n - 1]/d.
- ä\_n| = (1 + i)a\_n| = 1 + a\_(n-1)|; s\_(n+1)| = s̈\_n| + 1; ä\_n| > a\_n|.
- Two dots = acceleration (in advance); bar = continuous; m| = deferred m periods.
- Loan instalment = Loan/a\_n|; fund deposit = Target/s\_n|; outstanding loan = X × a\_(remaining)|.
- m|a\_n| = v^m a\_n| = a\_(m+n)| - a\_m|; perpetuity 1/i in arrears, 1/d in advance.
- Different payment and interest periods: find the effective rate per payment period first.
- a^(m)\_n| = (1 - v^n)/i^(m); ā\_n| = (1 - v^n)/δ.
- (Ia)\_n| = (ä\_n| - n v^n)/i; (Da)\_n| = (n - a\_n|)/i; geometric [1 - ((1 + k)/(1 + i))^n]/(i - k).
- Certain annuities run a fixed term; life (contingent) annuities stop at death.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A company promises a manager ₦400,000 at the end of each year for 12 years, the first payment one year after she retires in 5 years' time. It funds this with 5 equal payments at the **beginning** of each year over the 5 years before retirement. At 7% effective, find the annual funding payment.

*Solution:*

1. Value of the pension at retirement (time 5): 400,000 × a\_12| = 400,000 × 7.942686 = ₦3,177,075.
2. Bring it to today: 3,177,075 × 1.07^-5 = 3,177,075 × 0.712986 = ₦2,265,210.
3. Funding is an annuity-due of 5 payments: ä\_5| = (1 - 1.07^-5)/d = 4.387211.
4. X = 2,265,210/4.387211 = **₦516,321** (to the nearest naira, using unrounded factors).
