## Topic 6: Present and Accumulated Values of Payment Streams

**What you must know:** the cash flows of common securities; present value and accumulated value of a stream of equal or unequal payments; moving a value to any date; equations of value (unknown payment, time or rate); changing rates of interest; net present value, internal rate of return (yield) and discounted payback.

### Lesson 6.1: Cash flows and the securities that produce them

**Must know**

- **Cash flow:** a payment (in or out) at a stated time. Valuing a contract means listing its cash flows and their times, then applying interest.
- **Bond:** a certificate of debt issued by a government or company, promising periodic interest payments and the return of the original amount on a stated date.
- **Coupon:** the interest payment on a bond. **Redemption:** the return of the investor's capital at the end.
- **Zero-coupon bond:** pays **no coupons**; makes a **single payment at maturity** whose money amount is known in advance.
- **Fixed-interest security:** pays regular **fixed coupons plus a redemption payment**. Ignoring default, the money amounts and dates are fixed at issue. In actuarial and UK usage it is also called **loan stock** (or simply **stock**); a UK government bond is a **gilt**. **Unsecured loan stock:** long-term company debt not secured on any specific assets.
- **Index-linked security:** coupons and redemption are **adjusted in line with an index** (usually inflation), so the money amounts are **not** known in advance; the dates are.
- **Call deposit:** money in a bank account that can be withdrawn on demand; the interest rate can change.
- **Equity (ordinary share):** pays dividends that depend on the company's profits and the directors' decisions; no redemption date.

| Contract | Amount known in advance? | Timing known in advance? |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-coupon bond | Yes | Yes |
| Fixed-interest security | Yes | Yes |
| Index-linked security | No | Yes |
| Call deposit | No | No |
| Equity | No | No |

**Index-linked payment formula:**

Payment actually received = nominal payment × (index at payment date ÷ index at purchase date)

**Explain more**

*Worked example.* An investor buys a two-year index-linked security on 1.1.2021. Without inflation it would pay ₦10,000 at the end of each year and ₦200,000 on redemption at 1.1.2023. Index: 1.1.2020 = 100; 1.1.2021 = 150; 1.1.2022 = 165; 1.1.2023 = 180.

- Base is the index on the **purchase date**, 150 (not 100).
- Payment on 1.1.2022 = 10,000 × 165/150 = **₦11,000**.
- Payment on 1.1.2023 = 10,000 × 180/150 = **₦12,000**.
- Redemption on 1.1.2023 = 200,000 × 180/150 = **₦240,000**.
- If the index falls below the purchase value, the payment falls below the nominal amount (for example an index of 135 would give 10,000 × 135/150 = ₦9,000).

Traps: a zero-coupon bond **does** pay something (once, at maturity); "regular coupons plus redemption" describes a fixed-interest security. For an equity, the dividend amount is never known in advance; dividends are usually paid at regular intervals but neither the date nor the amount is guaranteed. In Nigeria, FGN bonds are fixed-interest securities and Nigerian Treasury Bills work like zero-coupon instruments; pension funds supervised by PenCom and insurers supervised by NAICOM hold both.

### Lesson 6.2: Present value and accumulated value of a stream

**Must know**

- **Present value (PV) of a payment stream:** the single value at time 0 found by discounting every payment to time 0 at a given rate of interest and adding the results.
- **Accumulated value (AV) at time n:** the total of all payments rolled forward with interest to time n.
- **Value at time t:** every payment is moved to time t (accumulated if it is earlier, discounted if it is later) and the results are added.

With payments C\_1, C\_2, ..., C\_n at times t\_1, t\_2, ..., t\_n and v = 1/(1 + i):

- PV = C\_1 v^(t\_1) + C\_2 v^(t\_2) + ... + C\_n v^(t\_n)
- AV at time n = sum of C\_k (1 + i)^(n − t\_k)
- **Moving a value:** value at time t = PV × (1 + i)^t; PV = (value at time t) × v^t. So AV at n = PV × (1 + i)^n.
- Times need not be whole numbers: v^0.5 = (1 + i)^(−0.5).

**Discount factor for each way of quoting interest:**

| Basis given | Discount factor for time t |
|---|---|
| Effective rate i per year | v^t = (1 + i)^(−t) |
| Nominal rate i^(m) convertible m-thly | (1 + i^(m)/m)^(−mt); use i^(m)/m per period and count periods |
| Force of interest δ | v^t = e^(−δt) |
| Effective rate of discount d | v = 1 − d, so v^t = (1 − d)^t |
| Simple interest at r | 1/(1 + rt) |

**Level payments** (from Topic 7, restated): ₦1 at the end of each year for n years has PV a\_n| = (1 − v^n)/i and AV s\_n| = ((1 + i)^n − 1)/i. Paid at the start of each year: ä\_n| = (1 + i) a\_n| and s̈\_n| = (1 + i) s\_n|.

**Explain more**

*Worked example 1 (unequal payments).* ₦30,000 at time 1, ₦40,000 at time 2 and ₦50,000 at time 4, at 8% effective.

- PV = 30,000 × 1.08^(−1) + 40,000 × 1.08^(−2) + 50,000 × 1.08^(−4)
- = 27,777.78 + 34,293.55 + 36,751.49 = **₦98,822.82**
- AV at time 4 = 30,000 × 1.08^3 + 40,000 × 1.08^2 + 50,000 = 37,791.36 + 46,656 + 50,000 = **₦134,447.36**
- Check: 98,822.82 × 1.08^4 = ₦134,447.36.
- Value at time 2 = 30,000 × 1.08 + 40,000 + 50,000 × 1.08^(−2) = 32,400 + 40,000 + 42,866.94 = **₦115,266.94** (also 98,822.82 × 1.08^2).

*Worked example 2 (other bases).*

- Fractional time: ₦50,000 at time 0.5 at 10% effective: 50,000 × 1.1^(−0.5) = 50,000 × 0.953463 = **₦47,673** (nearest naira).
- Nominal rate: ₦20,000 at 6 months and ₦20,000 at 18 months, at 10% convertible half-yearly. Rate per half-year = 5%; the payments are 1 and 3 half-years away: 20,000/1.05 + 20,000/1.05^3 = 19,047.62 + 17,276.75 = **₦36,324.37**.
- Force: ₦100,000 at time 2 with δ = 0.1: 100,000 × e^(−0.2) = **₦81,873** (nearest naira).
- Discount rate: ₦40,000 at times 1 and 2 with d = 10%: v = 0.9, PV = 40,000 × 0.9 + 40,000 × 0.81 = **₦68,400**.
- Simple interest at 10%: ₦22,000 at time 1 and ₦24,000 at time 2: 22,000/1.1 + 24,000/1.2 = 20,000 + 20,000 = **₦40,000**.

*Worked example 3 (level payments).* ₦10,000 at the end of each year for 5 years at 8%: a\_5| = (1 − 1.08^(−5))/0.08 = 3.99271, so PV = **₦39,927**; s\_5| = 5.86660, so AV at the time of the 5th payment = **₦58,666**. Paid at the start of each year (times 0 to 4): PV = 39,927.10 × 1.08 = **₦43,121**, and AV at time 5 = 58,666.01 × 1.08 = **₦63,359**. If a question gives you a table value, such as a\_4| = 3.2397 at 9%, simply multiply: ₦40,000 × 3.2397 = ₦129,588.

*Worked example 4 (gaps and growth).* ₦10,000 at the ends of years 3, 4 and 5 only, at 8%: 10,000 × (1.08^(−3) + 1.08^(−4) + 1.08^(−5)) = **₦22,094**, equal to 10,000 × (a\_5| − a\_2|). A year with no payment simply contributes nothing. Payments of ₦50,000, ₦55,000 and ₦60,500 (growing 10% a year) at times 1, 2, 3 at 10%: each discounts to ₦45,454.55, so PV = **₦136,364**.

Traps: the AV of an end-of-year series is measured "at the time of the last payment" (so the last payment earns no interest); for start-of-year deposits valued one year after the last deposit, every deposit earns interest. The PV is always less than the plain total of future positive payments, and the PV does **not** equal the AV at the last date (they differ by the factor (1 + i)^n).

### Lesson 6.3: Equations of value

**Must know**

- **Equation of value:** an equation stating that, at a chosen date (the **comparison date** or focal date), the total value of money paid out equals the total value of money received, at a given rate of interest.
- General form: sum of (outgo values) = sum of (income values), all at the same date. At time 0: sum of C\_t v^t = 0 with outflows negative.
- Under **compound interest** any comparison date gives the **same answer**, because moving from time 0 to time T multiplies every term by (1 + i)^T. Time 0 or the date of the unknown payment is usually easiest.
- Uses: (1) find an unknown payment; (2) replace debts by one payment at a new date; (3) find an unknown time; (4) find an unknown rate (yield); (5) loan repayments and saving targets.
- Equal loan repayment: loan = R × a\_n|, so R = loan ÷ a\_n|. Saving target: target = D × s\_n| (end of year) or D × s̈\_n| (start of year).
- Unknown time: rearrange to (1 + i)^t = k, then t = ln k ÷ ln(1 + i).

**Explain more**

*Replacing debts.* Debts of ₦80,000 due at time 1 and ₦120,000 due at time 4 are replaced by one payment at time 2, at 10%. At time 2: X = 80,000 × 1.1 + 120,000 × 1.1^(−2) = 88,000 + 99,173.55 = **₦187,174** (nearest naira). The earlier debt is accumulated, the later one discounted. A Kano debtor and creditor are equally well off because both sides are valued at the same date and rate.

*Unknown final payment.* A loan of ₦300,000 is repaid by ₦100,000 at time 1 and X at time 2, at 12%. At time 2: 300,000 × 1.12^2 = 100,000 × 1.12 + X, so X = 376,320 − 112,000 = **₦264,320**.

*Payments in a ratio.* A loan of ₦300,000 is repaid by X at time 1 and 2X at time 3, at 8%: 300,000 = X(1.08^(−1) + 2 × 1.08^(−3)) = X × 2.513590, so X = **₦119,351**.

*Two equal payments.* A debt of ₦400,000 due at time 3 is replaced by X at time 1 and X at time 5, at 10%: X(1.1^(−1) + 1.1^(−5)) = 400,000 × 1.1^(−3), so X × 1.530012 = 300,525.92 and X = **₦196,421**.

*Loan and saving.* ₦500,000 borrowed, repaid by 4 equal year-end payments at 10%: R = 500,000 ÷ 3.169865 = **₦157,735**. To have ₦600,000 at time 5 by 5 equal deposits at 7%: end of each year, D = 600,000 ÷ s\_5| = 600,000 ÷ 5.750739 = **₦104,334**; start of each year, D = 600,000 ÷ 6.153291 = **₦97,509**.

*Unknown time (equated time).* ₦60,000 due at time 1 and ₦60,000 at time 5 are replaced by ₦120,000 at time t, at 6%:

- 120,000 v^t = 60,000(v + v^5), so v^t = (0.943396 + 0.747258)/2 = 0.845327
- (1.06)^t = 1.182974, t = ln 1.182974 ÷ ln 1.06 = 0.168032 ÷ 0.058269 = **2.88 years** (2 d.p.), earlier than the simple average of 3 years.

*Time to reach a target.* ₦50,000 now and ₦50,000 at time 1, at 10%: when is the fund ₦150,000? 50,000(1.1)^t + 50,000(1.1)^(t − 1) = 150,000, so (1.1)^t × (1 + 1/1.1) = 3, (1.1)^t = 1.571429, t = 0.451985 ÷ 0.095310 = **4.74 years**.

*Unknown rate.* ₦100,000 deposited at times 0 and 1 gives ₦231,000 at time 2. Let x = 1 + i: 100,000x^2 + 100,000x = 231,000, so x^2 + x − 2.31 = 0, x = (−1 + √10.24)/2 = (−1 + 3.2)/2 = 1.1, so **i = 10%**. With answer options, you can also test each rate.

### Lesson 6.4: Changing rates and sensitivity to the rate

**Must know**

- When the rate changes over time, accumulate (or discount) **period by period**, using the rate in force in each period: AV of ₦1 over two years at i\_1 then i\_2 = (1 + i\_1)(1 + i\_2).
- Discount factor to time 2 when year 1 is at i\_1 and year 2 at i\_2: 1/[(1 + i\_1)(1 + i\_2)].
- A later deposit earns only the rates that apply **after** it is made.
- **Higher rate, lower PV:** each v^t gets smaller, so the PV of fixed positive future payments **falls** when i rises (and the AV rises).
- Payments due **later** are hit harder: long-dated streams lose more value than short-dated ones when rates rise.
- A naira due later adds less to the PV than a naira due sooner.

**Explain more**

*Changing rates, accumulation.* ₦100 for ten years at 5% for 3 years, 6% for 3 years and 7% for 4 years: 100 × 1.05^3 × 1.06^3 × 1.07^4 = **₦180.73**. With a second deposit: ₦200,000 now and ₦100,000 at time 2; 6% for years 1-2, then 9% for years 3-5. Value at time 2 = 200,000 × 1.06^2 + 100,000 = 224,720 + 100,000 = 324,720. At time 5: 324,720 × 1.09^3 = 324,720 × 1.295029 = **₦420,522**. Order matters: swapping which rate applies first changes the answer when deposits are made in between.

*Changing rates, discounting.* 10% in year 1 and 12% in year 2; ₦100,000 at times 1 and 2: PV = 100,000/1.1 + 100,000/(1.1 × 1.12) = 90,909.09 + 81,168.83 = **₦172,078** (nearest naira). Do **not** discount the second payment at 1.12^2.

*Withdrawals.* ₦100,000 deposited now, ₦30,000 withdrawn at time 2, 9%: balance at time 4 = 100,000 × 1.09^4 − 30,000 × 1.09^2 = **₦105,515**.

*Choice depends on the rate.* ₦100,000 at time 1 against ₦130,000 at time 4:

| Rate | PV of ₦100,000 at time 1 | PV of ₦130,000 at time 4 | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | ₦95,238 | ₦106,951 | later sum |
| 10% | ₦90,909 | ₦88,792 | earlier sum |

*Sensitivity.* ₦100,000 at the end of each of 3 years: PV is 100,000 a\_3| = ₦267,301 at 6% but ₦240,183 at 12%, a fall of ₦27,118. Two streams equal in value at one rate are no longer equal at a higher rate: the one paying mostly later now has the **lower** PV. Comparing values at a later date: if two streams have PVs of P\_1 and P\_2, their values at time t differ by (P\_1 − P\_2)(1 + i)^t.

### Lesson 6.5: Net present value, internal rate of return and payback

**Must know**

- **Net present value (NPV):** PV of inflows minus PV of outflows at a chosen rate: NPV = C\_0 + C\_1 v + C\_2 v^2 + ... + C\_n v^n, outflows negative.
- **Decision rule:** NPV > 0, accept (it earns more than the required rate); NPV < 0, reject (it earns less than the required rate); NPV = 0, it earns exactly that rate.
- **Internal rate of return (IRR), or yield:** the discount rate at which NPV = 0 (the equation of value is satisfied). For a bond, the yield to redemption (redemption yield) is the IRR of buying it.
- **Hurdle rate:** the minimum IRR required on a project to compensate for its risk. Accept if IRR exceeds the hurdle rate.
- If NPV is positive at i\_1 and negative at i\_2, the IRR lies **between** i\_1 and i\_2. Estimate by linear interpolation: IRR ≈ i\_1 + (i\_2 − i\_1) × NPV\_1 ÷ (NPV\_1 − NPV\_2).
- **Payback period:** time needed to recover the cost from undiscounted cash flows; it ignores the time value of money.
- **Discounted payback period:** the number of years needed to recover the initial outlay after each cash flow has been discounted; the first year in which the cumulative discounted cash flow becomes positive.

**Explain more**

*NPV.* A Lagos firm considers a machine costing ₦800,000 that returns ₦300,000 at the end of each of 3 years. At 10%: NPV = −800,000 + 300,000 × a\_3| = −800,000 + 300,000 × 2.486852 = **−₦53,944**. Reject: the project earns less than 10%, even though plain inflows (₦900,000) exceed the cost. With mixed flows, for example −₦300,000 at time 0 and −₦100,000 at time 1, discount every outflow too; an outflow at time 1 becomes −100,000/1.1.

*IRR by quadratic.* A project costs ₦100,000 and returns ₦60,000 at time 1 and ₦72,000 at time 2. NPV = 0 gives 72,000v^2 + 60,000v − 100,000 = 0. Divide by 4,000: 18v^2 + 15v − 25 = 0. v = (−15 + √(225 + 1,800))/36 = (−15 + 45)/36 = 0.833333 (take the positive root). i = 1/v − 1 = **20%**.

*IRR by testing options.* If ₦100,000 now buys ₦55,000 at time 1 and ₦60,500 at time 2, test 10%: 55,000/1.1 + 60,500/1.21 = 50,000 + 50,000 = 100,000. NPV = 0, so the IRR is **10%**.

*IRR by bracketing.* Cost ₦400,000; returns ₦150,000, ₦200,000 and ₦120,000 at times 1, 2, 3. NPV at 8% = +₦5,617; at 10% = −₦8,189. So the IRR lies between 8% and 10%. Interpolation: 8% + 2% × 5,617/(5,617 + 8,189) = **8.81%** (true value 8.80%).

*Discounted payback.* Cost ₦600,000; ₦250,000 at the end of each year; 12%.

| Year | Discounted inflow | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 223,214 | −376,786 |
| 2 | 199,298 | −177,487 |
| 3 | 177,945 | +458 |

Discounted payback = **year 3** (first positive cumulative). The ordinary payback is 600,000/250,000 = 2.4 years, shorter, because it ignores interest.

*Largest loan.* A Nigerian microfinance bank charging 18% will lend at most the PV of what the borrower can repay. A borrower who can pay ₦100,000 at each of times 1 and 2 can be lent 100,000 × (1.18^(−1) + 1.18^(−2)) = **₦156,564** (nearest naira).

### One-minute recap

- Zero-coupon: one known payment at maturity. Fixed-interest (loan stock): known coupons plus redemption. Index-linked: amounts move with an index (base = index at purchase).
- PV = sum of C\_t v^t; AV at n = sum of C\_t (1 + i)^(n − t); value at t = PV × (1 + i)^t.
- Convert the rate to the payment period, or use e^(−δt), (1 − d)^t or 1/(1 + rt) as given.
- Equation of value: outgo = income at one date; under compound interest any date gives the same answer.
- Solve for X directly, for t with logs, for i by quadratic or by testing options.
- Changing rates: multiply period by period; later deposits earn only later rates.
- Higher rate, lower PV; long-dated streams fall most.
- NPV > 0 accept; IRR = yield = rate making NPV zero; NPV changing sign brackets the IRR.
- Discounted payback: first year cumulative discounted cash flow is positive.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A debt of ₦200,000 is due at time 3. It is to be settled by X at time 1 and X at time 2. At 10% per annum effective, find X.

*Solution:* Equation of value at time 0: X(1.1^(−1) + 1.1^(−2)) = 200,000 × 1.1^(−3). Left factor = 0.909091 + 0.826446 = 1.735537. Right side = 200,000 × 0.751315 = 150,262.96. X = 150,262.96 ÷ 1.735537 = **₦86,580** (nearest naira).
