## Topic 5: Time Value of Money: Compound Interest and Discounting

**What you must know:** the time value of money; simple interest and simple discount; compound interest, accumulation and discounting with v; the effective rate of discount d; nominal rates i^(m) and d^(m); the force of interest δ; the relationships among i, d, v and δ; changing rates; equations of value; solving for time and rate.

### Lesson 5.1: Time value of money, simple interest and simple discount

**Must know**

- **Time value of money (TVM):** money available today is worth more than the same amount in the future, because money received now can be invested to earn interest. So ₦100 today is worth more than ₦100 a year from now.
- **Principal (P):** the amount originally invested, which attracts interest.
- **Accumulation (compounding):** moving money forward in time (finding a future or accumulated value). **Discounting:** moving money back in time (finding the present value of future cash flows).
- The future value of an investment depends on **all three**: the principal, the interest rate and the time.
- **Simple interest:** interest is earned only on the original principal, so the interest is the **same every year**. At rate r per annum, 1 grows to **1 + rt** after t years.
  - A = P(1 + rt) and interest I = Prt.
  - Present value under simple interest: P = A/(1 + rt).
  - Time in months: t = months/12.
- **Simple discount:** at rate of simple discount d per annum, the value now of an amount A payable at time t is **P = A(1 − dt)**. So an amount P invested now accumulates to **A = P/(1 − dt)**, and 1 invested for one year accumulates to **1/(1 − d)**.
- Solving simple interest for the rate: r = (A/P − 1)/t. For the time: t = (A/P − 1)/r.

**Explain more**

- **Simple interest, worked:** ₦50,000 at 12% simple interest for 3 years. I = 50,000 × 0.12 × 3 = ₦18,000; A = ₦68,000. Equally, ₦1,000 at 8% simple for 5 years: A = 1,000(1 + 0.08 × 5) = ₦1,400.
- **Part of a year:** ₦60,000 at 10% for 8 months: I = 60,000 × 0.10 × 8/12 = ₦4,000.
- **Finding the rate:** ₦30,000 grows to ₦39,000 in 4 years. r = (39,000/30,000 − 1)/4 = 0.30/4 = **7.5%**.
- **Finding the time:** ₦40,000 grows to ₦52,000 at 6%. t = (1.3 − 1)/0.06 = **5 years**.
- **Present value at simple interest:** a debt of ₦20,000 is due in 4 years and you can invest at 5% simple. Invest 20,000/(1 + 0.05 × 4) = 20,000/1.2 = **₦16,666.67** now.
- **Simple discount, present value:** a bill of ₦80,000 due in 1.5 years at 8% simple discount: P = 80,000(1 − 0.08 × 1.5) = 80,000 × 0.88 = **₦70,400**.
- **Simple discount, accumulation:** ₦1,000 invested for 5 years at 8% simple discount accumulates to 1,000/(1 − 0.4) = **₦1,666.67** (2 d.p.). ₦5,000 for 2 years at 10% gives 5,000/0.8 = ₦6,250. One naira for one year at 10% simple discount gives 1/0.9 = 1.1111.
- **Trap:** under simple discount you **divide** by (1 − dt) to accumulate and **multiply** by (1 − dt) to find the value now. Do not use 1 + dt.
- Nigerian Treasury Bills are sold at a discount to their face value, so the buyer pays less now and receives the full face value at maturity.

### Lesson 5.2: Compound interest, accumulation and the discount factor v

**Must know**

- **Compound interest:** interest is earned on the principal **and** on interest already earned (interest is added and itself earns interest).
- **Effective rate of interest i:** the total interest earned on 1 in a period (usually a year), assuming interest is reinvested as it falls due.
- **Accumulation factor:** (1 + i)^n = the accumulated value at time n of 1 invested at time 0.
  - **AV = P(1 + i)^n**; interest earned = P(1 + i)^n − P.
- **Discount factor:** **v = (1 + i)^−1 = 1/(1 + i)**, the present value of 1 due one period hence.
  - **v^n = (1 + i)^−n** = the present value of 1 due at the end of n years.
  - **PV = FV/(1 + i)^n = FV × v^n**.
- **Moving a payment:** value at time t of X due at time s = X(1 + i)^(t − s). If t is earlier than s, this is X × v^(s − t). E.g. value at time 3 of a payment due at time 7 = payment × v^4.
- The later a payment, the **smaller** its present value, because v^n falls as n increases.
- n need not be whole: 2 years 6 months means n = 2.5.

| Feature | Simple interest | Compound interest |
|---|---|---|
| Interest earned on | principal only | principal and past interest |
| 1 grows to | 1 + rt | (1 + i)^t |
| Growth pattern | linear (straight line) | geometric (exponential) |
| Interest each year | the same | rises every year |

- At the same rate: **t = 1 year**, both give 1 + i (equal); **t > 1**, compound gives more; **t < 1**, compound gives **less** than simple.

**Explain more**

- **Year-by-year table:** ₦100 at 5% compound for 3 years (2 d.p.).

| Year | Principal at start | Interest at end | Accumulation at end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100.00 | 5.00 | 105.00 |
| 2 | 105.00 | 5.25 | 110.25 |
| 3 | 110.25 | 5.51 | 115.76 |

Check: 100 × 1.05^3 = 115.7625 = ₦115.76. Interest rises each year because it is earned on a growing balance. A thrift cooperative that credits interest yearly and lets it earn interest is using compound interest.
- **Accumulated value:** ₦120,000 at 9% effective for 5 years. 1.09^5 = 1.53862395; AV = **₦184,634.87**; interest earned = ₦64,634.87.
- **Present value:** how much must be invested now to accumulate ₦200 in 3 years at 5%? X × 1.05^3 = 200, so X = 200/1.157625 = **₦172.77**.
- **Value at an intermediate date:** ₦400,000 is due at the end of year 6; at 8%, its value at the end of year 2 is 400,000 × v^4 = 400,000 × 0.73502985 = **₦294,011.94**.
- **Fractional period:** ₦20,000 for 3 years 9 months at 10%: 20,000 × 1.1^3.75 = **₦28,592.53**.
- **Compound versus simple over 2 years:** ₦200,000 at 15%. Compound interest = 200,000(1.15^2 − 1) = ₦64,500; simple interest = ₦60,000. Compound exceeds simple by ₦4,500 (= P × i^2 for 2 years).
- **Under one year simple wins:** ₦100,000 for 3 months at 20%. Simple: 100,000(1 + 0.2 × 0.25) = ₦105,000. Compound: 100,000 × 1.2^0.25 = ₦104,663.51. Simple gives ₦336.49 more.
- **Interest-table values** (learn to get these on your calculator): at 6%, (1.06)^10 = 1.79085 and v^10 = 0.55839; at 3.5%, (1.035)^10 = 1.41060.
- **Trap:** PV = FV(1 + i)^n is accumulation, not discounting; PV = FV/(1 + ni) is simple interest. The compound-interest PV is FV/(1 + i)^n.
- Actuaries valuing pension liabilities (supervised by PenCom) and insurance reserves (supervised by NAICOM) discount the future payments to today with v^n.

### Lesson 5.3: The effective rate of discount d and the links among i, d and v

**Must know**

- **Effective rate of discount d:** the discount on 1 due a year hence; that is, interest for the year paid at the **start** of the year. (i is interest paid at the **end** of the year.)
- Correct formulae (learn all of them):
  1. **d = i/(1 + i)**
  2. **d = iv**
  3. **d = 1 − v**, so **v = 1 − d**
  4. **i = d/(1 − d)**
- **d < i** whenever i > 0.
- PV of X due in n years at discount rate d: **X(1 − d)^n = X v^n**.
- **Amount of discount** on a bill of face value X due in one year = X − Xv = **X × d**.
- **Not** correct: d = i/(1 − i); v = 1 + d.

**Explain more**

- **From i to d:** i = 8%. d = 0.08/1.08 = 0.074074 = **7.41%** (2 d.p.). Likewise i = 5% gives d = 0.047619 and i = 2.5% gives d = 2.44%.
- **From d to i:** d = 6%. i = 0.06/0.94 = 0.063830 = **6.38%**.
- **PV using d:** ₦300,000 due in 3 years at d = 4%: 300,000 × 0.96^3 = **₦265,420.80**.
- **Discount on a bill:** a bill of ₦250,000 due in one year, i = 10%. d = 0.1/1.1 = 0.090909; discount = 250,000 × 0.1/1.1 = **₦22,727.27**; present value = 250,000/1.1 = ₦227,272.73. Check: 227,272.73 + 22,727.27 = 250,000.
- **Trap:** the amount of discount is face × d, not face × i (₦25,000 here would be wrong).

### Lesson 5.4: Nominal rates of interest and discount; equivalent rates

**Must know**

- **Nominal rate of interest i^(m):** a rate per annum **convertible (compounded, payable) m times a year**. The effective rate per m-th of a year is **i^(m)/m**.
  - m = 2 half-yearly, 4 quarterly, 12 monthly, 365 daily.
- **Equivalent rates:** two rates are equivalent when they give the **same accumulated value** from the same principal over the same period.
- Key equation: **1 + i = (1 + i^(m)/m)^m**
  - Nominal to effective: **i = (1 + i^(m)/m)^m − 1**
  - Effective to nominal: **i^(m) = m[(1 + i)^(1/m) − 1]**
- **AV with a nominal rate:** A = P(1 + i^(m)/m)^(mn), n in years. **PV:** P = A(1 + i^(m)/m)^−(mn).
- **Effective rate per sub-period** from an annual effective rate: (1 + i)^(1/m) − 1. From one period to a longer one: e.g. monthly j to quarterly = (1 + j)^3 − 1.
- **Nominal rate of discount d^(m):** discount convertible m times a year; **1 − d = (1 − d^(m)/m)^m = v**.
- For a fixed nominal rate, the effective rate **rises** as m rises, towards a limit e^(i^(m)) − 1.

**Explain more**

- **Nominal to effective:** 6% p.a. payable half-yearly. ₦1,000 after 2 years: 1,000(1 + 0.06/2)^4 = 1,000 × 1.03^4 = **₦1,125.51**. Effective annual rate: (1.03)^2 − 1 = **0.0609 = 6.09%**.
- 12% convertible quarterly: i = 1.03^4 − 1 = **12.55%**. 24% convertible monthly: 2% per month, i = 1.02^12 − 1 = **26.82%** (not 24%).
- **Table for 10%** (5 d.p.):

| m | Effective i when i^(m) = 10% | Nominal i^(m) when i = 10% |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.10000 | 0.10000 |
| 2 | 0.10250 | 0.09762 |
| 4 | 0.10381 | 0.09645 |
| 6 | 0.10426 | 0.09607 |
| 12 | 0.10471 | 0.09569 |

- **More frequent compounding, same nominal 6%:** ₦10,000 for 10 years (to the nearest kobo): m = 1: ₦17,908.48; m = 2: ₦18,061.11; m = 4: ₦18,140.18; m = 12: ₦18,193.97; m = 365: ₦18,220.29. Note 6% "compounded annually" is just 1.06^10.
- **Effective to nominal:** i = 21% gives i^(2) = 2(1.21^0.5 − 1) = 2(0.1) = **20%**.
- **Sub-period rates:** i = 12% a year gives (1.12)^(1/12) − 1 = 0.9489% ≈ **0.95% per month**. 2% per month gives 1.02^3 − 1 = **6.12% per quarter**.
- **Nominal to nominal:** i^(4) = 8% means 2% a quarter. Half-yearly: (1.02)^2 = 1.0404, so i^(2) = 2 × 0.0404 = **8.08%**.
- **AV:** ₦150,000 for 2 years at 12% convertible monthly: 150,000 × 1.01^24 = **₦190,460.20**. 18 months would be 1.01^18.
- **PV:** ₦600,000 due in 3 years at 8% convertible quarterly: 600,000 × 1.02^−12 = **₦473,095.91**.
- **Nominal discount:** d^(2) = 10% means 5% each half-year. PV of ₦400,000 due in a year = 400,000 × 0.95^2 = **₦361,000**. d^(4) = 8% gives d = 1 − 0.98^4 = **7.76%** (lower than 8%).
- **Solving for a nominal rate:** ₦60,000 grows to ₦75,000 in 3 years, monthly compounding. (1 + j)^36 = 1.25, so j = 1.25^(1/36) − 1 and i^(12) = 12j = **7.46%**.
- **Comparing offers:** Bank A pays 12% convertible monthly, i = 1.01^12 − 1 = 12.68%; Bank B pays 12.5% effective. Bank A is better. Always compare **effective** rates.
- **Trap:** "24% per annum convertible monthly" is i^(12) = 24%, 2% per month. It is not an effective 24%, a force of interest, or a discount rate.

### Lesson 5.5: The force of interest δ

**Must know**

- **Force of interest δ (delta):** the instantaneous rate of growth; the limit of the nominal rate i^(m) as m tends to infinity, i.e. a nominal rate compounding continuously ("compounded continuously" means a force of interest).
- In international actuarial notation the Greek letter **δ** denotes the force of interest.
- Relationships:
  1. **1 + i = e^δ**
  2. **δ = ln(1 + i)**
  3. **v = e^−δ**
  4. **d = 1 − e^−δ**
  5. i^(m) = m(e^(δ/m) − 1)
- **AV = P e^(δn)**; **PV = A e^−(δn)**.
- **Not** correct: δ = e^i − 1.
- **Ordering** for any positive i: **d < d^(m) < δ < i^(m) < i**; in detail d < d^(2) < d^(12) < δ < i^(12) < i^(2) < i.

**Explain more**

- **Effective to force:** i = 10%: δ = ln 1.1 = **0.09531**. Half-yearly nominal 10%: (1.05)^2 = 1.1025, δ = ln 1.1025 = 0.09758. 10% effective per half-year: (1.1)^2 = 1.21, δ = ln 1.21 = 0.19062 p.a. Similarly 8% effective gives δ = 0.0770, and 5% per quarter gives δ = 4 ln 1.05 = 0.1952 p.a.
- **Force to others:** δ = 8%. i = e^0.08 − 1 = **8.33%**; v = e^−0.08 = 0.9231; d = 1 − 0.9231 = 7.69%. δ = 10% gives i^(12) = 12(e^(0.1/12) − 1) = 10.04%.
- **AV:** ₦300,000 for 3 years at δ = 10% (or 10% compounded continuously): 300,000 e^0.3 = **₦404,957.64**.
- **PV:** ₦500,000 due in 4 years at δ = 6%: 500,000 e^−0.24 = **₦393,313.93**.
- **Dates:** a loan from 1 March 2020 to 1 March 2025 runs exactly 5 years, so use n = 5.
- **Ordering check at i = 10%:** d 0.09091 < d^(2) 0.09307 < d^(12) 0.09493 < δ 0.09531 < i^(12) 0.09569 < i^(2) 0.09762 < i 0.10000.
- A fixed nominal 10% with m rising gives effective rates rising towards e^0.1 − 1 = 10.52%.
- **Trap:** δ is the natural log (ln) of 1 + i, not of i, and it is always below i.

### Lesson 5.6: Changing rates, equations of value, solving for time and rate

**Must know**

- **Changing rates:** multiply the accumulation factors for each period. 1 invested for 3 years at i\_1 then 2 years at i\_2 grows to (1 + i\_1)^3(1 + i\_2)^2. Discount by dividing by the same factors.
- For a **single sum**, the **order** of the rates does not matter (multiplication). With **further payments** at later dates, the order matters.
- **Equation of value:** set the value of what is paid in equal to the value of what is paid out, **with every payment valued at the same date**. Under compound interest **any comparison date** gives the same answer.
- **Equivalent single payment** at time t: accumulate earlier payments to t and discount later ones to t.
- **Solving for time:** n = ln(A/P)/ln(1 + i). Under a force δ: n = ln(A/P)/δ. Doubling time: n = ln 2/ln(1 + i), or ln 2/δ.
- **Solving for the rate (one payment):** i = (A/P)^(1/n) − 1. With two payments a year apart, solve a **quadratic** in x = 1 + i.

**Explain more**

- **Changing rates, two investments:** ₦100 now and ₦100 at time 3, accumulated to time 10.
  - 5% for 3 years, 6% for 3, 7% for 4: (100 × 1.05^3 + 100) × 1.06^3 × 1.07^4 = **₦336.84**.
  - 6%, then 5%, then 7%: (100 × 1.06^3 + 100) × 1.05^3 × 1.07^4 = **₦332.47**. Different, because the second ₦100 only meets the later rates.
- **Single sum:** ₦50,000 for 5 years at 5% for 2 years then 8% for 3: 50,000 × 1.05^2 = 55,125; × 1.08^3 = **₦69,441.62**. Reversing the rates gives the same answer.
- **PV with changing rates:** ₦80,000 due at year 5; 10% for years 1 and 2, 9% for years 3 to 5. PV = 80,000/(1.1^2 × 1.09^3) = 80,000/1.566985 = **₦51,053.45**.
- **Loan cleared by a final payment:** Ngozi borrows ₦300,000 at 12%, repays ₦150,000 at the end of year 1. Balance after year 1: 336,000 − 150,000 = 186,000; final payment at year 3 = 186,000 × 1.12^2 = **₦233,318.40**.
- **Two equal repayments:** a ₦400,000 loan repaid by X at the end of years 1 and 2 at 8%: 400,000 = X(v + v^2) = X × 1.78326475, so X = **₦224,307.69**.
- **Equivalent single payment:** ₦40,000 due at year 1 and ₦90,000 at year 6; at 10%, the single payment at year 3 is 40,000 × 1.1^2 + 90,000 × 1.1^−3 = 48,400 + 67,618.33 = **₦116,018.33**.
- **Present value of several payments:** add each payment × v^n. At 10% half-yearly nominal, use 5% per half-year: ₦100,000 at year 1 and year 2 has PV 100,000(1.05^−2 + 1.05^−4) = 90,702.95 + 82,270.25 = **₦172,973.20**.
- **Rate, one payment:** ₦25,000 grows to ₦40,000 in 5 years. i = 1.6^(1/5) − 1 = **9.86%**.
- **Rate, quadratic:** ₦50,000 now and ₦50,000 in a year; the account holds ₦112,320 at the end of year 2. 50,000x^2 + 50,000x = 112,320, so x^2 + x − 2.2464 = 0. x = (−1 + √(1 + 8.9856))/2 = (−1 + 3.16)/2 = 1.08, so **i = 8%**.
- **Time:** ₦60,000 to ₦100,000 at 7%: n = ln(5/3)/ln 1.07 = **7.55 years**. Doubling at 8%: ln 2/ln 1.08 = 9.01 years; at δ = 8%: ln 2/0.08 = 8.66 years.
- **Trap:** never add payments at different dates at face value; move them to one date first.

### One-minute recap

- TVM: money now is worth more because it can earn interest; discounting finds present values.
- Simple interest: A = P(1 + rt), the same interest each year; simple discount: P = A(1 − dt).
- Compound: AV = P(1 + i)^n; PV = FV v^n; v = 1/(1 + i) is the discount factor.
- Under one year simple beats compound; at one year they are equal; beyond one year compound wins.
- d = i/(1 + i) = iv = 1 − v; i = d/(1 − d).
- Nominal: 1 + i = (1 + i^(m)/m)^m; compare effective rates; 1 − d = (1 − d^(m)/m)^m.
- Force: 1 + i = e^δ, δ = ln(1 + i), v = e^−δ; δ is the limit of i^(m).
- Ordering: d < d^(m) < δ < i^(m) < i.
- Equations of value: value everything at one date; any date gives the same answer.
- Time: n = ln(A/P)/ln(1 + i); rate: i = (A/P)^(1/n) − 1.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A nominal rate of interest is 8% per annum convertible quarterly. Find (a) the equivalent effective annual rate of interest, (b) the force of interest, and (c) the accumulated value of ₦100,000 after 3 years. Give rates to 4 decimal places and money to the nearest kobo.

*Solution:*
(a) Quarterly rate = 0.08/4 = 0.02. i = (1.02)^4 − 1 = 1.082432 − 1 = **0.0824 (8.24%)**.
(b) δ = ln(1 + i) = ln 1.082432 = 4 ln 1.02 = **0.0792**.
(c) 3 years = 12 quarters: 100,000 × 1.02^12 = 100,000 × 1.26824179 = **₦126,824.18**.
