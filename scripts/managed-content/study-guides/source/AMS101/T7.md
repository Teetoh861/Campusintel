## Topic 7: Individual Behaviour, Groups and Work Teams

**What you must know:** what OB studies, what shapes individual behaviour, and how groups and teams form, clash and perform.

### Lesson 7.1: Organizational behaviour

**Must know**

- **Behaviour:** actions of people. **Organizational behaviour (OB):** study of the actions of people at work. **2 areas:** individual and group behaviour. **3 goals:** explain, predict, influence (not "eliminate").
- **6 employee behaviours:**
    1. **Productivity:** performance measure of efficiency and effectiveness.
    2. **Absenteeism:** failure to show up for work.
    3. **Turnover:** voluntary and involuntary permanent withdrawal from the organization.
    4. **OCB (organizational citizenship behaviour):** discretionary behaviour, not in the formal job, that helps the organization work well.
    5. **Job satisfaction:** general attitude to the job.
    6. **Workplace misbehaviour:** intentional behaviour that could harm the organization or people in it; 4 forms: deviance, aggression, antisocial behaviour, violence.
- **Iceberg:** visible = strategies, objectives, policies, structure, technology, formal authority, chain of command. Hidden = attitudes, perceptions, group norms, informal interactions, interpersonal and intergroup conflicts. Most of what drives behaviour is below the surface.
- **7 factors influencing individual behaviour:** biography/demography, age, gender, societal responsibilities, marital status, personality, values and attitudes. **5 factors that change behaviour:** demographic/biological factors, personality, values and attitudes, motivation, learning.

**Explain more**

- **Owen:** concerned about terrible working conditions. **Munsterberg:** founded industrial psychology, the scientific study of people at work. **Follett:** studied organizations through individual and group behaviour; modern work groups trace to her. **Barnard:** organizations are social systems needing cooperation; first to call them open systems.
- **Hawthorne Studies (Elton Mayo):** began as lighting tests; biggest contribution to OB; found social norms or group standards drive individual work behaviour.
- OB is most tied to employee motivation. Current issues: generational differences, negative behaviour.

### Lesson 7.2: Attitudes

**Must know**

- **Attitudes:** evaluative statements, favourable or unfavourable, about objects, people or events; show how a person feels.
- **3 components:** **cognitive** (beliefs, opinions, knowledge); **affective** (emotions, feelings); **behavioural** (intention to behave a certain way).
- **4 job-related attitudes:** **job satisfaction** (general attitude to the job); **job involvement** (identifying with the **job**, active part in it, performance seen as important to self-worth); **organizational commitment** (identifying with the **organization** and its goals, wanting to stay); **employee engagement** (connected to, satisfied with, enthusiastic about the job).
- **Cognitive dissonance (Leon Festinger):** any incompatibility or inconsistency between attitudes, or between behaviour and attitudes.

**Explain more**

- People change the attitude or behaviour, or find a reason for the gap. Effort depends on **importance** of the elements, **influence** the person feels he has, **rewards** (high pay eases it).
- Managers measure attitudes with attitude surveys.
- Satisfied employees: lower absenteeism and turnover; satisfied, engaged ones: better customer service.

### Lesson 7.3: Personality and emotional intelligence

**Must know**

- **Personality:** unique combination of emotional, thought and behavioural patterns affecting how a person reacts and interacts with others. From **heredity and environment**. 3 influences: perception, emotions, religion.
- **Big Five (OCEAN):** **Openness** (imaginative, curious, artistically sensitive); **Conscientiousness** (responsible, dependable, persistent, achievement-oriented; best linked to job performance); **Extraversion** (sociable, talkative, assertive); **Agreeableness** (good-natured, cooperative, trusting); **Neuroticism** (tense, anxious, insecure; opposite = **emotional stability**).
- **MBTI:** 4 dimensions, **16** types: Extravert/Introvert (social interaction); Sensing/Intuitive (gathering data); Feeling/Thinking (decision making); Perceptive/Judgemental (style of making decisions).
- **Other traits:** **locus of control** (internal: "I control my fate"; external: luck or others; internals more satisfied and involved); **Machiavellianism** (pragmatic, emotionally distant, ends justify means; suits bargaining jobs such as sales, negotiating); **self-esteem** (liking or disliking oneself; low = more open to outside influence); **self-monitoring** (adjusting behaviour to the situation); **risk-taking** (willingness to take chances; high risk-takers make quick decisions on little information, low risk-takers decide slowly and want more information); **proactive personality** (spots opportunities, shows initiative, persists until change happens).
- **Emotional intelligence (EI):** ability to notice and manage emotional cues and information. **5 dimensions:** self-awareness, self-management, self-motivation, empathy, social skills. Matters most in jobs needing much social interaction.

**Explain more**

- **Holland's personality-job fit:** satisfaction highest, turnover lowest when personality matches job. 6 types: realistic, investigative, social, conventional, enterprising, artistic.
- Big Five appear across many cultures; locus of control differs between cultures.

### Lesson 7.4: Perception and learning

**Must know**

- **Perception:** giving meaning to our environment by organizing and interpreting sensory impressions. Influenced by the **perceiver** (attitudes, interests, experience, expectations), the **target**, the **situation**.
- **Attribution theory:** we judge people differently depending on whether we see the cause as internal or external. 3 factors: **distinctiveness** (acts differently elsewhere? high = external); **consensus** (others in the same situation act the same? high = external); **consistency** (acts this way over time? high = internal).
- **Fundamental attribution error:** judging **others**, we underestimate external and overestimate internal factors. **Self-serving bias:** we credit **our own** success to internal factors, failure to external ones (luck).
- **4 judging shortcuts:** **selectivity** (taking in only bits of what we see, by interests, background, attitudes); **assumed similarity** ("like me" effect); **stereotyping** (judging by the group someone belongs to); **halo effect** (one characteristic, such as looks, shapes the whole impression).
- **Learning:** relatively permanent change in behaviour from experience. **Operant conditioning (B. F. Skinner):** behaviour is a function of its consequences. **Social learning theory:** learning by observing others and direct experience; 4 processes: attentional, retention, motor reproduction, reinforcement.
- **4 ways to shape behaviour:** **positive reinforcement** (something pleasant follows; strengthens); **negative reinforcement** (something unpleasant removed, such as nagging stopping; strengthens); **punishment** (penalty follows; weakens); **extinction** (reward sustaining the behaviour removed; weakens). **Shaping behaviour:** guiding learning in graduated steps, reinforcing each step towards the desired behaviour.

**Explain more**

- Negative reinforcement is not punishment.
- Managers should reward wanted behaviour and model it; staff learn from what managers do.

### Lesson 7.5: Groups and group behaviour

**Must know**

- **Group:** two or more interacting and interdependent individuals who come together to achieve specific goals.
- **Formal:** purposeful, structured (hierarchy, rules), assigned roles, officially recognised (e.g. a bank's finance department). **Informal:** spontaneous, unstructured, based on personal connections and social needs (friendship groups, lunch or football groups, social clubs).
- **2 formal types:** **command group** (set by the organization chart: supervisor and direct subordinates); **task group** (specific task, set time; disbands after).
- **5 stages in order:** 1. **Forming:** people join; purpose, structure, leadership defined; uncertainty. 2. **Storming:** conflict over control and direction; ends when a clear leadership hierarchy emerges. 3. **Norming:** close relationships, cohesiveness, shared expectations. 4. **Performing:** structure accepted; energy on the task; last stage for permanent groups. 5. **Adjourning:** temporary groups wrap up and break up.
- **Role:** behaviour patterns expected of someone in a given position. **Role conflict:** facing different role expectations at once.
- **Norms:** standards accepted and shared by members; commonly cover work effort and performance. **Asch study:** many went along with a deliberately wrong group answer on line lengths: pressure to conform.
- **Status:** prestige grading, position or rank in a group; formal (title, office) or informal (education, age, skill). Members must see it as fair.
- **Size:** small groups finish tasks faster; large groups suit problem solving and diverse input.
- **Social loafing:** less effort when working collectively than alone. Reduce it by making each contribution identifiable and measurable.
- **Cohesiveness:** how far members are attracted to the group and share its goals. Productivity: high and aligned with organization goals = strong increase; high, not aligned = decrease; low, aligned = moderate increase; low, not aligned = no significant effect.
- **Groupthink:** heavy group pressure on an individual to align his opinion with the others.
- **Group decisions:** advantages: more complete information, more diverse alternatives, greater acceptance, greater legitimacy. Disadvantages: time-consuming, minority domination, pressure to conform, ambiguous responsibility (unclear who is accountable).

### Lesson 7.6: Teams and conflict

**Must know**

- **Work team:** members work intensely on a specific, common goal using positive synergy (whole greater than sum of parts), individual and mutual accountability, complementary skills.
- **5 differences, groups vs teams:** accountability (individual / shared); goals (individual / shared); interdependence (limited / high); focus (individual / collective performance); collaboration (less / heavy emphasis). Leadership: one leader in charge in a group; often shared in a team.
- **4 team types:** **problem-solving** (same department; suggests improvements, usually cannot carry them out); **self-managed** (no manager; whole work process; plans, schedules, assigns work, decides); **cross-functional** (different departments or specialities); **virtual** (linked by technology while apart).
- **8 characteristics of effective teams:** clear goals, relevant skills, mutual trust, unified commitment, good communication, negotiating skills, appropriate leadership, internal and external support.
- **Conflict:** perceived incompatible differences resulting in interference or opposition. **3 views:** **traditional** (bad, avoid it); **human relations** (natural, inevitable, need not be negative); **interactionist** (some conflict is necessary for good performance).
- **Functional** conflict supports group goals and improves performance; **dysfunctional** prevents reaching them.
- **3 types:** **task** (content and goals of work); **relationship** (interpersonal; almost always dysfunctional); **process** (how work gets done).
- **5 handling styles:** **avoiding** (withdraw or suppress); **accommodating** (other's needs above yours); **forcing** (yours at the other's expense); **compromising** (each gives up something); **collaborating** (win-win for all).

**Explain more**

- Too little conflict: dull, slow to change. Too much: chaos.
- Managers shape team behaviour by **selection, training, rewards** (reward cooperation and team results, not only individual effort).
- Global teams bring more ideas but face language, culture and trust problems.
- Teams cost more time and resources; use when work is complex, needs different perspectives and members depend on one another.

### One-minute recap

- OB: actions of people at work; explain, predict, influence.
- Big Five OCEAN; MBTI 16 types; EI 5 dimensions.
- Attribution: distinctiveness, consensus, consistency.
- Reinforcement strengthens; punishment and extinction weaken.
- Stages: forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning.
- Conflict: 3 views, 3 types, 5 styles.
