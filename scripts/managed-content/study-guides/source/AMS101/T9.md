## Topic 9: Leadership and Trust, and Managing Communication and Information

**What you must know:** leadership theories, power, trust, empowerment; the communication process, types, barriers, networks; information technology.

### Lesson 9.1: Leadership, leaders and traits

**Must know**

- **Leadership:** an influence process that involves motivating and inspiring people to work together to achieve a common goal.
- **Leader:** someone who can influence others and who has managerial authority.
- **9 obligations:** developing and communicating a clear vision; motivating and inspiring; setting clear expectations; making informed decisions; fostering commitment and accountability; fostering a positive work environment; delegating tasks; coaching team members; conflict resolution.
- **Manager:** appointed; influence from formal authority; focus on planning, organizing, controlling. **Leader:** appointed or emerges from a group; influence can go beyond formal authority; focus on influencing and inspiring.
- **Trait theories:** research that tried to isolate the characteristics that set leaders apart from non-leaders.
- **7 traits:** drive, desire to lead, honesty and integrity, self-confidence, intelligence, job-relevant knowledge, extraversion.

**Explain more**

- Ideally all managers should be leaders; not all leaders have a manager's abilities.
- **Supervision:** overseeing subordinates' work and giving instructions to ensure optimum use of resources and achievement of work targets.
- **Delegation:** match tasks to employees' skills and abilities; improves motivation and performance. The manager stays accountable.
- 8th trait in later lists: **proneness to guilt** (strong sense of responsibility to others). Flexibility and communication skills also mark effective leaders.
- **Weakness of trait theories:** ignored the situation and leader–group interaction. Traits only make a person more likely to be effective. Researchers then studied what leaders **do**.

### Lesson 9.2: Behavioural theories

**Must know**

- **Behavioural theories:** identify behaviours that separate effective from ineffective leaders.
- **Iowa (Kurt Lewin):** autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire; members more satisfied under democratic leaders. Lewin's early finding: democratic gave good quantity and quality; later studies mixed on performance.
- **Ohio State:** initiating structure, consideration; high–high leaders often, not always, got good results.
- **Michigan:** employee-oriented, production-oriented; employee-oriented had higher productivity and satisfaction.
- **Managerial Grid (Blake and Mouton):** concern for people, concern for production; 9,9 best.
- **Autocratic:** centralises authority, dictates work methods, decides alone, limits participation. Best in a **crisis**.
- **Democratic:** involves employees in decisions, delegates authority, uses feedback to coach. Most participatory; suits a team whose ideas improve the decision.
- **Laissez-faire:** gives group complete freedom to decide and work. Best with **highly skilled, self-motivated** employees.

**Explain more**

- Democratic leader still takes part in the decision; laissez-faire hands it over entirely.
- **Initiating structure:** how far a leader defines his role and members' roles to reach goals. **Consideration:** mutual trust and respect for members' ideas and feelings.
- **Employee-oriented:** relationships, employees' needs. **Production-oriented:** technical or task side. People side: consideration, employee-oriented.
- **Grid:** 9 × 9 = **81** positions; first number = **production**, second = **people**. 1,1 impoverished; 9,1 task management; 1,9 country club (high people, low production); 5,5 middle of the road (**medium** concern for both production and people); 9,9 team management.
- **Weakness:** ignored the situation, leading to contingency theories.

### Lesson 9.3: Contingency theories

**Must know**

- **Contingency theory:** no one best style; leadership effectiveness depends on understanding employee needs and adapting leadership style to the situation, never keeping a single style regardless of context.
- **Fiedler:** performance depends on matching leader's style to how much the situation lets him control and influence. Style **fixed**.
- **Situational Leadership Theory (Hersey and Blanchard):** depends on follower **readiness** (and task demands). Style **can change**.
- **Path-goal (Robert House):** leader helps followers reach their goals with direction and support.
- **Fiedler's 3 factors:** **leader–member relations** (confidence, trust, respect for the leader); **task structure** (how formal and structured tasks are); **position power** (over hiring, firing, discipline, promotion, pay).
- **Readiness:** followers' ability **and** willingness. R1 unable, unwilling: **telling** (high task, low relationship). R2 unable, willing: **selling** (high, high). R3 able, unwilling: **participating** (low task, high relationship). R4 able, willing: **delegating** (low, low). As readiness rises, leader cuts back both control and relationship behaviour.
- **4 path-goal behaviours:** directive, supportive, participative, achievement-oriented.

**Explain more**

- **LPC** (least-preferred co-worker): describing him kindly = high LPC = **relationship-oriented**; low LPC = **task-oriented**.
- 3 factors give **8** situations. Task-oriented do best in very favourable or very unfavourable; relationship-oriented in moderately favourable. Fix mismatch by **changing the leader or the situation**.
- **Directive:** tells what is expected, schedules work. **Supportive:** friendly, concerned for needs. **Participative:** consults, uses suggestions. **Achievement-oriented:** challenging goals, expects top performance.
- Path-goal depends on **environment** (task structure, formal authority, work group) and **follower characteristics** (locus of control, experience, perceived ability).
- **Leader–member exchange (LMX):** **in-group** and **out-group**; in-group gets more trust and attention.

### Lesson 9.4: Contemporary leadership, power, trust and empowerment

**Must know**

- **Leadership styles:** autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, transactional, transformational, charismatic, visionary. "Functional" is not a leadership style.
- **Transactional:** motivates by **exchanging rewards** for productivity, through clear goals.
- **Transformational:** **stimulates and inspires** followers to extraordinary outcomes; encourages innovation, creativity, personal growth. Built **on top of** transactional, not its opposite.
- **Charismatic:** enthusiastic, self-confident; personality and actions influence people; personal charm and vision.
- **Visionary leadership:** creating and expressing a realistic, credible, attractive vision of the future that improves on the present.
- **5 powers:** **legitimate** (from position); **coercive** (punish or control); **reward** (give positive rewards); **expert** (expertise, skills, knowledge); **referent** (desirable resources or personal traits; people admire him, want to be like him).
- **Trust:** belief in the integrity, character and ability of a leader. Makes people feel secure and confident: better collaboration, engagement, success.
- **5 Cs (how trust is built):** competence (skills, expertise), communication (clear), commitment (to organization's goals), caring (about others), character (ethical, honest).
- **Empowerment:** increasing decision-making discretion of workers.

**Explain more**

- **5 charismatic characteristics:** have a vision; express it; take risks for it; sensitive to environmental limits and followers' needs; behave out of the ordinary.
- **Team leadership:** 2 priorities: managing external boundary, helping team process. **4 roles:** liaison with outsiders, troubleshooter, conflict manager, coach.
- **5 dimensions of trust:** integrity (honesty; **most important**), competence, consistency (reliability, good judgement), loyalty (willingness to protect a person), openness.
- **Credibility:** followers see leader as honest, competent, able to inspire. **Honesty** most linked.
- Empowered workers decide alone: they know the job best and act fast. Do not mix the 5 Cs (how trust is built) with the 5 dimensions (what it is made of).

### Lesson 9.5: Communication, its process and types

**Must know**

- **Communication:** transfer and understanding of meaning; **two-way** between sender and receiver. **Lifeblood** of organizations; enables coordination.
- **Effective:** people understand your meaning and reply in terms that move the exchange forward.
- **3 rules for clarity:** be clear in your own mind about what you want to communicate; deliver succinctly; ensure it is clearly and correctly understood.
- **4 functions:** **control** (rules, policies, reporting lines direct behaviour); **motivation** (what to do, how well, how to improve); **emotional expression** (sharing feelings, frustration, satisfaction); **information** (data for decisions).
- **Process, 7 parts in order:** sender, encoding, message, channel, decoding, receiver, feedback. **Noise** (illegible print, phone static, inattention) can hit any stage; not one of the 7.
- **Encoding:** sender turns message into symbols. **Decoding:** receiver translates it. **Channel:** the medium. **Feedback:** last; checks understanding.
- **5 types:** **written words** (memos, reports, proposals, contracts); **spoken words** (interviews, phone calls, debates, announcements; effective only when heard by the right people); **symbolic gestures** (facial expressions, tone of voice, silence, posture, absence; any behaviour audience can see or hear); **visual images** (photographs, charts, logos, colour schemes); **multimedia** (combines visual-image methods: television, radio, newspapers, flyers, posters, internet).

**Explain more**

- **Interpersonal:** between two or more people. **Organizational:** all patterns, networks, systems within an organization.
- **Nonverbal:** sent without words. **Body language:** gestures, facial expressions, body movements. **Verbal intonation:** emphasis on words or phrases.
- **Face-to-face** gives most immediate feedback. The sender encodes; the receiver decodes.

### Lesson 9.6: Feedback, barriers, organizational communication and information

**Must know**

- **Feedback** checks you understood the other person's message and lets you react. Negative feedback must be given when it is the only thing to do.
- **5 rules for negative feedback:** show understanding of exactly what went wrong, and why; draw out ways to improve; use questions rather than assertions; express negative opinions honestly but positively; be objective, not emotional or personal.
- **Sandwich approach:** praise, criticism, praise.
- **6 barriers:** **filtering** (deliberately changing information to look better to receiver); **emotions** (receiver's feelings change how he reads it); **information overload** (more than a person can process); **defensiveness** (feeling threatened, reacting with attack or sarcasm); **language** (words differ by age, education, jargon); **national culture** (cultures communicate differently).
- **5 remedies:** use feedback, simplify language, listen actively, constrain emotions, watch nonverbal cues. **Active listening:** listen for full meaning without judging early: eye contact, questions, paraphrasing, no interrupting.
- **Formal:** follows chain of command or needed to do a job. **Informal:** not defined by the hierarchy. **Grapevine:** the informal network.
- **4 directions:** **downward** (manager to employees: goals, instructions, policies); **upward** (employees to manager: reports, suggestions, complaints); **lateral** (same level, e.g. cross-functional team); **diagonal** (across both work areas and levels).
- **3 networks:** **chain** (formal chain of command; moderate speed, high accuracy); **wheel** (all through strong central leader; fast, high accuracy, leader emerges strongly, **lowest** satisfaction); **all-channel** (everyone talks freely, as in self-managed teams; fast, moderate accuracy, no leader emerges, **highest** satisfaction).

**Explain more**

- Managers cannot remove the grapevine: use it as feedback source and limit harmful rumours through open, honest communication.
- **Managing information:** first find out what people most want to know (job security, working conditions, reward, location, benefits). **Honesty and feedback** essential.
- **IT:** networked systems link staff by email, instant messaging, voice mail, fax, teleconferencing, videoconferencing, web conferencing. **Wireless:** staff need not be at desks.
- **Intranet:** internet technology inside organization, staff only. **Extranet:** links it with approved outsiders (customers, suppliers).
- IT gives managers faster, more complete information. Problems: overload, data security, less face-to-face contact.

### One-minute recap

- Leadership: influence process; 9 obligations; 7 traits; managers appointed, leaders can emerge.
- Behavioural: Iowa, Ohio State, Michigan, Grid (81 positions, 9,9 best). Contingency: Fiedler (fixed), Hersey and Blanchard (readiness), House (path-goal).
- Transactional = rewards for productivity; transformational = inspires extraordinary outcomes.
- 5 powers; trust built by the 5 Cs.
- Communication: 4 functions, 7-part process, 5 types.
- 6 barriers, 5 remedies, 4 directions, 3 networks.
