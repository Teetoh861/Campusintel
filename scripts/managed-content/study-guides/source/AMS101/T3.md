## Topic 3: Foundations of Decision Making and Foundations of Planning

**What you must know:** the 8-step decision process, decision types and conditions, group decisions; planning, the 6-step strategic process, strategies, goals, MBO and planning tools.

### Lesson 3.1: The decision-making process

**Must know**

- **Decision making:** choosing among alternatives (the best course of action); closely linked with **planning**; its primary goal is to **resolve organizational issues efficiently**. It does not by itself lead to performance monitoring or industrial harmony. **Problem:** a gap between existing and desired states.
- **8 steps:** 1 identify a problem; 2 identify decision criteria; 3 allocate weights to criteria; 4 develop alternatives (**consider all alternatives**); 5 analyse alternatives; 6 select one; 7 implement it; 8 evaluate its effectiveness. **Ignoring team feedback** is not a step; corrective action follows evaluation.
- **Criteria:** factors relevant to solving the problem. **Heuristics:** rules of thumb.

**Explain more**

- **Step 1:** **pressure to act** must exist first.
- **Step 3:** score the most important criterion (10), weight the others against it; if all are equal, weighting is not needed.
- **Steps 5-6:** appraise alternatives against the **criteria**; pick the **highest score**.
- **Step 7:** letting those affected **take part** raises commitment. **Step 8:** final; reviewing outcomes and **feedback** **evaluate effectiveness** and **identify areas for improvement**; you may **restart**.
- **Confirmation bias** is the **most common** bias. **Sunk costs** are **irrelevant** to future decisions. Bias is reduced by **seeking expert advice**.
- An effective decision is **rational**; its key feature is **careful analysis of alternatives**; makers are **proactive and analytical**. The decision-making process ensures **efficient use of resources**. Under uncertainty, be **flexible and adaptive**.
- **Time pressure** hinders decisions; quick ones are **not always optimal**. **Experience, complexity and time constraints** all influence decision-making speed; risk and certainty also matter.

### Lesson 3.2: Approaches, types of decisions and conditions

**Must know**

- **Rational:** consistent choices that **maximise value** within limits; a perfectly rational maker is **objective and logical**; the model assumes **complete information** and logical evaluation (bounded rationality has limited information).
- **Bounded rationality:** rational **within limits** of information and resources; **simplified models**; **satisfice** = accept "good enough". Perfect rationality gives a **maximising** choice; bounded gives a **satisficing** one.
- **Intuition:** experience, feelings, accumulated judgement ("gut feeling"); research finds managers use information from the **subconscious** to help them decide.
- **Escalation of commitment:** more commitment to an earlier decision despite evidence it was wrong.
- **Structured problem:** familiar, easily defined (equipment breakdown). Needs a **programmed** decision: routine, repetitive, uses past solutions (daily office hours); lower-level managers. Problem, decision and level link up: **integration**. It **improves organizational efficiency** and cuts the need for costly managerial talent; a local fast-food manager makes the most.
- **Unstructured problem:** new, unusual, ambiguous or incomplete information. Needs a **nonprogrammed** decision: unique, nonrecurring, custom-made, usually under **uncertainty**; upper-level managers.
- **3 ways to make programmed decisions:** **procedure** (related, sequential steps); **rule** (explicit statement of what may or may not be done); **policy** (guideline that **sets parameters**, with an **ambiguous term**, e.g. "productive relationships with organizations in its community").
- **3 conditions:** **certainty** (every outcome known, e.g. three dealers' prices); **risk** (probabilities estimable, e.g. a Lagos shop using last rainy season's sales); **uncertainty** (neither: the outcome is **unpredictable**, with outcomes unknown and risk not measurable).
- **Under uncertainty:** **maximax** = maximise the maximum payoff (all on the long shot), optimist; **maximin** = maximise the minimum payoff, pessimist; **minimax** = minimise the maximum "regret".

**Explain more**

- **Rationality assumes** a clear problem, single goal, **constant, stable** preferences ("preferences keep changing" is not one), best **economic** interests; it is possible when problems are simple, goals clear, alternatives few. Culture, politics and power also shape decisions.
- **Intuition's aspects:** experience, feelings, cognitive skills, subconscious processing, values; never programmed decisions.
- **Styles:** directive, analytic, conceptual, behavioural.
- **Levels:** **strategic** (top; long-term); **tactical** (middle; **medium-term**, turns strategy into specific actions; if "medium-term" is absent, choose "short-term goals"); **operational** (first-line; **day-to-day operations and specific tasks**, routine).
- **Centralised:** authority at the top. **Decentralised:** delegated downward.

### Lesson 3.3: Group decisions and decision tools

**Must know**

- **Group** style = **collaboration and consensus**.
- **Benefits:** more information and alternatives, greater acceptance and legitimacy. **Drawbacks:** time-consuming, minority domination, **groupthink**, ambiguous responsibility.
- **Groupthink:** overemphasis on consensus; members hold back different views.
- **3 ways to boost creativity:** 1 **brainstorming** (many ideas, criticism withheld); 2 **nominal group technique** (members write ideas independently; discussion restricted); 3 **electronic meetings** (interaction by computer).
- **National culture** affects decisions and risk taking.

**Explain more**

- **Vroom-Yetton:** subordinates' **participation**. **Delphi:** **expert opinions**. **Decision tree:** evaluates choices systematically.
- **Cost-benefit analysis:** pros and cons. **SWOT:** **qualitative**. **Scenario analysis:** future possibilities. **PERT:** project duration.
- **Opportunity cost:** best alternative foregone. **Risk tolerance:** willingness to take risks. **Risk management:** assessing potential losses. **Ethical decisions:** morality, fairness; a step in ethical decision-making is **identifying stakeholders**. **Participative decision-making:** its key element is **employee involvement**.

### Lesson 3.4: Planning, strategic management and strategies

**Must know**

- **Planning:** making goals, policies and procedures: defining goals, setting an overall strategy, developing comprehensive plans **to integrate and coordinate** work. Deciding in advance what, how, when, by whom. Key elements: **goals**, **plans**. It includes **developing and selecting a course of action** and **decision-making**, never creating problems. Success of a plan depends on **proper planning and execution**.
- It is the **first (primary)** function, the foundation of the others, at **all levels**. Organizing is to structure as planning is to **goal setting**. Its first step is **setting objectives**; begin by working out how to satisfy customer needs.
- **Formal planning:** specific goals over years, **written**, shared. **Informal:** general, lacks continuity, unwritten, little sharing.
- **4 reasons for planning:** direction; less impact of change; less waste and redundancy; sets control standards.
- **Criticisms:** rigidity; replaces intuition; focus on today's competition; reinforces success; a **dynamic environment** cannot be planned for. Regulations, unions and other environmental forces limit options and **reduce the impact of planning** on performance.
- **Strategic management:** decisions and actions determining **long-run** performance; uses all management functions; suits all organizations.
- **6 steps:** 1 identify **mission, goals, strategies** (mission = reason for being in business; purpose and direction); 2 **external analysis** (competitors, laws, labour supply): **opportunities and threats**; it is finished when managers have an **accurate picture of the external environment** and are **aware of trends** that could affect the organization; 3 **internal analysis**: **strengths and weaknesses**; 4 **formulate** strategies: develop and evaluate alternatives, choose those that **use strengths and correct weaknesses**; 5 **implement**; 6 **evaluate** (how effective were they?).
- **SWOT:** combined internal and external analysis. **Core competencies:** major value-creating skills, capabilities, resources, exceptional or unique (e.g. meeting a target market's needs on a large scale; communicating with customers worldwide in their own languages). An organization's **strengths and weaknesses** play a **key** role in corporate planning. Strong and weak cultures affect strategy differently, and a culture's **content** affects which strategies can be pursued; a strong culture is **harder to change**.
- **Strategy levels:** **corporate** (which businesses, each unit's role); **business/competitive** (how to compete in each business); **functional** (supports **business-level**).
- **3 corporate strategies:**
    1. **Growth:** more markets or products, via concentration, integration, diversification. **Horizontal integration:** same industry (two airlines merging). **Vertical:** backward or forward. **Related diversification:** different but related industries. **Unrelated:** different, unconnected.
    2. **Stability:** continue as now; fits slow or no growth and industry upheaval.
    3. **Renewal:** addresses decline: **retrenchment** (deals with weaknesses, stabilises operations, renews resources) and **turnaround**.
- **BCG matrix:** high growth + high share = **star**; high growth + low share = **question mark**; low growth + high share = **cash cow**; low growth + low share = **dog** (neither uses nor makes much cash).
- **SBUs:** independent businesses with own strategies, each serving a clearly defined product-market segment. **Competitive advantage:** a distinct edge; steady quality makes it **sustainable**.
- **Porter's 3 strategies:** **cost leadership** (lowest cost structure); **differentiation** (unique in ways customers value, e.g. faster-response systems); **focus** (advantage in a narrow niche). Some **industries** are more profitable than others.
- **Porter's 5 forces:** **new entrants** (barriers: economies of scale, brand loyalty); **substitutes** (switching costs, buyer loyalty); **buyers**' power; **suppliers**' power (concentration, substitute inputs); **existing rivalry** (growth, demand, product differences).

**Explain more**

- Planning cannot remove change; it makes managers **anticipate** it and think about its impact. Research on planning is **positive**; **quality** and **implementation** matter more than extent. Where it failed, the **external environment** (regulations, unions) was to blame. Labour supply is **external**.
- **Strategic weapons:** customer service (staff training), employee skills, innovation (first to market = **first mover**), quality, **benchmarking**. Senior managers choose whether innovation focuses on **basic research, product innovation or process development** (process development improves existing work processes and makes the firm more efficient). **Rule of three:** three companies usually hold most of the market; only three full-line generalists.

### Lesson 3.5: Goals, plans and planning tools

**Must know**

- "To **fail to plan** is to **plan to fail**." **Who does the planning** gives the best understanding of how an organization plans. Planning tools and techniques help managers **prepare confidently for the future**.
- **Goals (objectives):** desired outcomes; same meaning; the **foundation of planning**, they give direction and are the **standard against which actual results are measured**. **Plans:** resource allocations, schedules, actions to meet goals.
- **2 goal types:** financial and strategic. **Stated goals:** what the organization wants stakeholders to believe; they seem to conflict because different groups (stockholders, customers, employees, the public) are given **different objectives**. **Real goals:** what members do.
- **Traditional goal setting:** top managers set goals, split into subgoals; they grow **less clear and unified** downward. Each level works toward the goals **assigned to its own area of responsibility**. Clear hierarchy = **means-ends chain**.
- **MBO** (**Peter Drucker**): goals **jointly** set, **progress reviewed periodically**, rewards by progress. It **increases** performance. Weaknesses: poor in changing environments, individual focus, not taken seriously.
- **Well-written goals:** outcomes not actions; measurable; timed; challenging but attainable; written; communicated. A **single goal** tempts unethical behaviour because managers **neglect other important parts of their job just to look good on that one measure**; no single measure shows success.
- **Plans by breadth:** **strategic** (whole organization, overall goals, positions it in its environment, longer, broader) vs **operational** (how to achieve goals; lower levels; concerned with **day-to-day operations and specific tasks**).
- **By time frame:** **long-term** (over 3 years) vs **short-term** (1 year or less).
- **By specificity:** **specific** (no room for interpretation, e.g. 2.45 per cent tobacco tax rise over 2 years) vs **directional** (flexible guidelines; flexibility costs the **clarity** specific plans provide).
- **By frequency of use:** **single-use** (unique situation) vs **standing** (repeated activities, e.g. skateboarding policy).
- **3 contingency factors:** organizational level, environmental uncertainty, length of future commitments (**commitment concept**: plans reach far enough to meet commitments made).
- **2 approaches:** **traditional** (top managers, helped by planning specialists) and **members take part** (plans become more than paper).
- **Uncertain environments:** keep formal planning; plans **specific but flexible**; planning **ongoing**; "long term" has changed. Pushing planning down **flattens** hierarchy. Issues are **not equally important**.

**Explain more: planning tools**

- **Budget:** numerical plan allocating resources to specific activities (even space); popular as it applies to many kinds of organizations and to **work activities within organizations**; brings financial discipline across the organization. Many managers dislike preparing budgets, seeing the process as **time-consuming, inflexible, inefficient and ineffective**.
- **Gantt chart:** **Henry Gantt** (associate of **Frederick Taylor**), for **scheduling**; **time horizontal**, activities vertical; compares plan with **actual progress**. **Load chart:** vertical axis lists departments or **specific resources**.
- **PERT:** schedules **projects**; activity order and time or costs. **Events:** end points marking completion of major activities. **Activities:** time or resources between events. **Slack time:** possible delay without delaying the project. **Critical path:** longest sequence.
- **Break-even analysis:** **profit projections** (e.g. an Ikeja store's jacket target); total revenue = **total cost**. Needs price, variable cost per unit, **total fixed costs**. Property taxes are fixed.
- **Linear programming:** needs limited resources and an outcome to optimise, so not every allocation problem; applications include choosing **transport routes** that minimise shipping costs, **sharing an advertising budget** among plans and **assigning personnel** to projects; a change in one variable gives an **exactly proportional** change in the other.
- **Project management:** completing a **project** (one-time, definite start and end) on time, within budget, to specification. **Identify** activities and resources first. Members stay in permanent work areas, so influence is **communication and persuasion**.
- **Scenario (contingency) planning:** alternative courses for unexpected events; reduces uncertainty by playing out situations; different assumptions give different outcomes; hard for **random events**. Tools never replace managers' **skills and capabilities**.

### One-minute recap

- Decision = choice among alternatives; problem = gap; 8 steps.
- Rational maximises; bounded satisfices.
- Programmed = structured; nonprogrammed = unstructured, uncertainty.
- Maximax optimist, maximin pessimist, minimax regret.
- Planning = goals and plans; 6 strategic steps.
- Growth, stability, renewal; cost leadership, differentiation, focus.
