## Topic 2: The Management Environment and Integrative Managerial Issues

**What you must know:** environment, culture, global management, social responsibility, ethics, diversity.

### Lesson 2.1: How much do managers matter? The external environment

**Must know**

- **Omnipotent view:** managers **directly responsible** for success or failure; the **dominant** view ("take-charge" executive; sacked coaches). **Symbolic view:** **external forces** responsible; managers have **limited** effect on substantive outcomes.
- Managers are limited by **culture and environment**. **Internal constraints** exist in **every** organization (staff resisting an idea). Managers **widen discretion** by changing/influencing culture and environment.
- **External environment:** forces and institutions **outside** the organization that could potentially affect performance; **uncontrollable**. **Internal:** employees, culture.
- **6 components:** **economic** (interest rates, inflation, disposable income, stock market, exchange rates); **demographic** (population characteristics); **technological** (automation, office systems; changed fastest); **sociocultural** (values, attitudes, lifestyles, expectations); **political/legal** (laws, tax, labour and trade rules, stability, government policy); **global**.
- **Business environment:** **internal and external** factors, including economic, social (**demographics** is a social factor), political and technological ones; **climate change** is an environmental factor. **Exchange-rate** changes affect **international trade**; a growing economy brings **increased business opportunities**. **Technological innovation by competitors** is an **external threat**.
- **Competitive environment:** described by **market conditions**; **market structure** = **level of competition**. **Free-market economy:** **consumer choice**, competition, prices set by supply and demand.
- **Business strategy** is influenced by **internal resources, economic conditions and technological trends**; **strategic planning** = **setting long-term goals**. **Business growth** is affected by **market demand, political stability and economic policies**. A business's **primary objective** is to **generate profits**; **business policies** **regulate internal operations**.
- **Specific (micro):** direct, immediate impact; unique, changing with conditions: **customers, suppliers, competitors, pressure groups**. Government policy is **not** micro.
- **General (macro):** the 6 components; **smaller** effect than specific. Industry conditions are **not** part of it.

**Explain more**

- **Suppliers:** materials, equipment, capital, labour (lending bank, nurses' union, local labour market); managers want steady inputs at **lowest price**. **Competitors** include substitutes (courier vs postal service; video recorder vs TV network). **Pressure group:** consumer group seeking safety laws. The **Internet** removed geographic boundaries between competitors.
- Disability law = **political/legal**; falling charity donations = **economic**. **Baby Boomers** born 1946–1964; **Gen Y** thinks, learns, shops in new ways. 2008 recession: **homeowners unable to pay mortgages**; **low** interest rates left firms leveraged.
- **SWOT:** Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. **Oligopoly** = few sellers. Inflation raises costs; **Central Bank** regulates. Firms influence environment by **lobbying**, adapt by **restructuring**.

### Lesson 2.2: How the environment affects managers

**Must know**

- 3 effects: (1) jobs and employment, (2) **environmental uncertainty**, (3) stakeholder relationships.
- **Uncertainty:** 2 dimensions, **change** (frequent = **dynamic**, minimal = **stable**) and **complexity** (**number of components**, **knowledge** of them). Managers keep it **as low as possible**.
- **Stakeholders:** groups **affected by** the organization's decisions and actions (investors, employees, customers). **External** stakeholders include **suppliers** (and customers); **internal** ones are **employees, in-house sales staff and management**.
- **4 steps:** (1) identify stakeholders; (2) find their interests or concerns; (3) decide how critical each is; (4) decide how to manage each relationship. **3 tools:** environmental scanning, forecasting, benchmarking.
- **Scanning:** screening large amounts of information to **anticipate and interpret** changes; foundation for **forecasts**; advanced scanning raised profits and growth.
- **Forecasting:** **predicting future outcomes** (e.g. changing a product because more women are expected to buy it in future); gives **information that helps decisions**; must be timely. **Quantitative:** mathematical rules on **historical data**. **Qualitative:** judgement of **knowledgeable individuals**.
- **Benchmarking:** best practices of competitors **or noncompetitors** giving **superior performance**; may study a firm with a different product.

**Explain more**

- **Competitor intelligence:** one of the fastest-growing scanning areas; **anticipates** competitors' actions; about **80%** from **your own employees, suppliers and customers**. **Reverse engineering** (studying competitor's product) is common; intelligence methods can be **legal but unethical**. **Global scanning** value depends on **own global activities**.
- **Forecasting tips:** simple methods; more than one; shorter periods; compare with **"no change"** forecast (right about **half** the time). Accuracy falls over longer periods; best when environment **not changing rapidly**.
- **Nigeria:** frequent **power outages**. Poor infrastructure: **delayed production and distribution** (government should **invest in infrastructure**). Corruption: **delays decisions, reduces efficiency** (reduce by **promoting ethical practices**). Poor education and skills: **training and skill development**. Bureaucratic delays: **technology** (social media for marketing). Ethical approach promotes **transparency and fairness**.

### Lesson 2.3: Organizational culture

**Must know**

- **Organizational culture:** shared values, principles, traditions, ways of doing things that influence how members act; **shared meaning**, like **personality**; part of corporate culture is **employee behaviour**. A **perception**; **descriptive** (not whether members **like** it); **shared**.
- **7 dimensions:** attention to detail, outcome orientation, people orientation, team orientation, aggressiveness, stability, innovation and risk taking. **Compensation is not one.**
- **Source:** **founder's** vision or mission. **4 ways passed on:** stories, rituals, material symbols, language.
- **Strong culture:** key values deeply held, widely shared; influences employees **more** than weak culture; staff **more committed**. Most are **moderate to strong**. Value-behaviour link **fairly straightforward**.

**Explain more**

- **Stories:** significant events or people. **Rituals:** repeated activities reinforcing values, key goals, who is important or expendable. **Material symbols:** office size, dress, reserved parking (**stock options are not**). **Language:** in-house jargon ("work judo"); learning it shows acceptance, keeps culture alive.

### Lesson 2.4: Globalization and managing globally

**Must know**

- **Globalization:** increasing international trade and relations; world one marketplace, borders matter less, more growth opportunities, rivals appear anywhere; managers handle economic, political, cultural differences. **Parochialism:** seeing the world from **one perspective** (sign: **monolingualism**).
- **Ethnocentric:** **home** country best; linked to parochialism and **MNCs**; least helpful. **Polycentric:** **host-country** managers know best. **Geocentric:** **world view**; best for global success. Global management needs **sensitivity to national customs**; the advice for global success is to **build an understanding of cultural differences**.
- **MNC:** operations in several countries (common from **mid-1960s**). **Multidomestic:** **decentralises**. **Global:** **centralises** at home. **Transnational/borderless:** removes structural divisions creating artificial geographic barriers (raises efficiency, effectiveness, competitiveness). Dropping country structure for industries = **borderless**.
- **3 stages:** **I** exporting, importing; **II** staff abroad as salespeople or **hiring agents**; **III** licensing, franchising, strategic alliances, **joint ventures**, subsidiaries. **Foreign subsidiary** = **greatest risk**. **Joint venture** = type of **strategic alliance** (shared plant or product costs).
- **3 environments abroad:** legal-political, economic, cultural (**religion is not** one). Economic threat: changing **inflation rates**. Culture hardest to learn: **natives least able to explain** it.
- **Hofstede's 5:** power distance; individualism vs collectivism; quantity vs quality of life; uncertainty avoidance; long-term vs short-term orientation. **GLOBE's 9:** assertiveness, future orientation, gender differentiation, uncertainty avoidance, power distance, individualism/collectivism, in-group collectivism, performance orientation, humane orientation.

**Explain more**

- **High power distance:** titles, rank, status count; meeting representatives have very different status. **Collectivism:** group looks after, protects members. **Quantity of life:** assertiveness, material things; **quality:** relationships, caring. **Uncertainty avoidance:** tolerance of risk, unusual behaviour; **high** = low tolerance, formal rules.
- **EU:** 12 countries, regaining economic strength against **United States and Japan**, removing barriers on border controls, taxes, subsidies, employment, investment, trade, nationalist policies and travel (all of the above). **EMU:** shared currency, **euro**. **NAFTA:** Mexico, Canada, United States. **ASEAN:** Southeast Asia.
- **Trap:** older "transnational" = decentralised, local nationals run operations, marketing adapted per country; "products made only locally" is **not** a feature.

### Lesson 2.5: Social responsibility, green management, ethics and diversity

**Must know**

- **Classical view:** only responsibility is to **maximise profits for stockholders**. **Milton Friedman**: spending on "social good" **adds to costs**.
- **Socioeconomic view:** business **more than an economic institution**; **protect and improve society's welfare**.
- **Social responsibility (CSR):** obligation **beyond law and economics** to pursue long-term goals good for society; ethical practice.
- **Social obligation:** does **only the minimum the law requires** (economic and legal duties; meeting pollution and anti-discrimination laws), with no extra duty to stakeholders. **Social responsiveness:** adapting to changing **societal conditions** (recycled paper). **Social responsibility:** adds **ethical** imperative. **Social screening:** social criteria in investment decisions. **Values-based management:** managers establish, promote, practise **shared values**, which guide decisions; building them **not easy**.
- **Sustainability:** goals and long-term shareholder value through **economic, environmental and social** opportunities. **Greening:** decisions tied to **natural environment**; pollution, toxic waste, global warming and resource depletion are **global environmental problems**. **Environmental sustainability** = **reducing waste and pollution**; **ethical practices** promote **long-term sustainability**.
- **4 green approaches:** **legal** (law only) → **market** (customers' preferences) → **stakeholder** (many stakeholders' demands) → **activist** (greatest sensitivity; best shows social responsibility). "Responsibility" is **not** one.
- **4 stages of expanding responsibility:** Stage 1 owners/management (stockholders); Stage 2 adds **employees (human resource concerns)**; Stage 3 adds **customers and suppliers** (safe products, fair prices); Stage 4 society.
- **Ethics:** rules or principles defining right and wrong. **4 views:** **utilitarian** (greatest good for greatest number; efficiency, profit; **most business people**); **rights** (based on **respecting and protecting individual liberties and freedoms**: privacy, free speech, conscience, life, safety, due process); **justice** (rules **fair and impartial**, within the law); **integrative social contracts** (norms already in industries and companies). "Principled" is **not** one.
- **3 ways to encourage ethics:** **code of ethics** (formal document), **ethical leadership**, **ethics training**. **Workforce diversity:** how people are **different from and similar to** one another.

**Explain more**

- **Decisions with a social responsibility dimension:** employee relations, charitable giving, pricing, conserving resources, product quality and safety, trading in countries that abuse human rights.
- **For:** **ethical obligation** (right thing); **better environment** (helping solve social problems builds a better community that attracts and keeps skilled workers); **discouraging regulation**; **balance of responsibility and power** (firms hold great power; if the responsibility they accept does not match it, the **public good suffers**); **possession of resources** (firms have the money, technical skills and management ability to support public and charitable projects); public expectations; image; long-run profits. **Against:** **violation of profit maximization** (only economic interests); dilution of purpose; **costs** (higher prices); **too much power**; lack of skills; **lack of accountability** (leaders are not directly answerable to the public for shaping social policy).
- Studies: **positive** link with economic performance; **little evidence** of harm.
- **Moral development:** preconventional; **conventional** (keep order, meet others' expectations); principled. **Ego strength:** strength of convictions. **Locus of control:** belief in controlling one's fate ("hit targets despite weather" = **internal**). **Formal rules** most encourage ethics; **strong** high-standards culture supports it. **Issue intensity:** characteristics of the issue; 6 factors (harm, consensus, probability, immediacy, closeness, concentration); "chance of being found out" is **not** one.
- Also reduce unethical behaviour: selection, codes, training; **phone listening is not** one. Global firms **clarify** guidelines.

### One-minute recap

- Omnipotent vs symbolic; 6 components; 4 specific groups.
- Scanning, forecasting, benchmarking; 7 culture dimensions; 3 global attitudes.
- Obligation, responsiveness, responsibility.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Distinguish between the classical and socioeconomic views of social responsibility, and explain social obligation, social responsiveness and social responsibility.

*Answer outline:* Classical: stockholder profit (Friedman). Socioeconomic: society's welfare. Obligation: legal and economic duties only. Responsiveness: adapting to societal conditions. Responsibility: ethical commitment beyond law.
