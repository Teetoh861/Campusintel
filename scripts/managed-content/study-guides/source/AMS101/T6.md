## Topic 6: Managing Change and Innovation

**What you must know:** forces and agents of change, Lewin, OD, resistance, stress, innovation.

### Lesson 6.1: Change, its forces and its agents

**Must know**

- **Organizational change:** any alteration of people, structure or technology in an organization.
- **Change agent:** a person who acts as a catalyst and takes responsibility for managing the change process.
- **External forces (5):** 1. marketplace (competition: rival firms and their new products or prices); 2. government laws and regulations; 3. technology; 4. labour markets; 5. economic changes (interest rates, inflation, recession).
- **Internal forces (4):** 1. changes in organizational strategy; 2. changes in the workforce (age, education, gender, nationality); 3. new equipment; 4. changes in employee attitudes.

| Change agent | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal manager** | Knows the organization; more thoughtful as he or she lives with the results | May be overcautious |
| **Outside consultant** | Objective view that insiders lack | Knows little of the organization's history, culture, procedures and people; may push more drastic change (does not live with the results) |

**Explain more**

- Sorting: laws, a skilled-labour shortage = external. A new market = strategy; new machines = equipment (internal).
- Change agents: managers, nonmanagers (e.g. HR change specialists) or outside consultants; large changes often use consultants.

### Lesson 6.2: How change happens, types of change and OD

**Must know**

**2 views of change:**

| | Calm waters | White-water rapids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture | Large ship on a calm sea, storm now and then | Small raft on constant, rough rapids |
| Change is | An occasional break in stable life; fits **Lewin's 3 steps** | Natural and constant; constant uncertainty and adjustment |
| Managing it | Done once, then things settle | A continual process |

- **Lewin's 3 steps, in order:** 1. **unfreezing** the status quo; 2. **changing** to a new state; 3. **refreezing** to make the change permanent.
- **Status quo:** an equilibrium state. **Driving forces** push behaviour away from it; **restraining forces** hold back movement away from it.
- **3 ways to unfreeze:** increase driving forces; decrease restraining forces; combine both.
- **3 types of change:** **structure** (elements: authority relationships, coordinating mechanisms, job design, span of control, formalization, departmentalization; e.g. merging departments, wider spans of control, cutting layers; attitudes and perceptions are NOT structure but people change); **technology** (equipment, tools, work processes; e.g. automation, computerization, new machines; **automation** replaces certain tasks done by people with machines); **people** (attitudes, expectations, perceptions, behaviour; e.g. training, team building, OD).
- **Organizational development (OD):** techniques or programmes to change people and the nature and quality of interpersonal work relationships. It is the main tool for people change.
- **4 OD techniques:**

| Technique | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Survey feedback** | Questionnaire assesses attitudes and perceptions about the change; results used to spot and solve problems |
| **Process consultation** | Outside consultant helps managers see how interpersonal processes affect the way work is done |
| **Team building** | Helps a work group set goals, build good relationships, clarify each member's role and responsibilities |
| **Intergroup development** | Changes the attitudes, stereotypes and perceptions work groups hold about each other |

**Explain more**

- Lewin treats change as a **break** in equilibrium. Refreezing stabilises the new situation so it is not short-lived: balance the forces again, e.g. reward the new behaviour.
- White-water fits today's uncertain environments (a course whose timetable keeps changing).
- Team building is **within** one group; intergroup development is **between** groups.

### Lesson 6.3: Resistance to change and how to reduce it

**Must know**

**4 reasons for resistance:** 1. **uncertainty** (known replaced by unknown); 2. **habit** (preferring routine); 3. **concern over personal loss** (status, money, authority, friendships); 4. **belief that the change is not in the organization's best interest**.

**6 techniques to reduce resistance:**

| Technique | Use when | Advantage | Drawback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Education and communication** (memos, meetings) | Misinformation | Clears up misunderstandings | May not work when trust and credibility are lacking |
| **Participation** (involve those affected in the decision) | Resisters have expertise | Raises involvement and acceptance | Takes time; may give a poor solution |
| **Facilitation and support** (counselling, training, paid leave) | Resisters are fearful and anxious | Helps people adjust | Can be expensive; no guarantee of success |
| **Negotiation** (exchange something of value, e.g. special pay package) | Powerful group | Can "buy" commitment | Can be costly; opens the door for others to demand deals |
| **Manipulation** (covert influence: twisting facts) and **co-optation** (manipulation plus participation: "buying off" resistance leaders with a key role) | Powerful group's support needed | Cheap and easy way to gain support | Can backfire; change agent may lose credibility |
| **Coercion** (direct threats: transfer, loss of promotion, poor reviews) | Powerful group's support needed | Cheap and easy way to gain support | May be illegal; may undermine the change agent's credibility |

**Explain more**

- Resistance can give stability and lead to useful debate, but it slows adaptation. People with a lot invested in the current system (long-serving staff) resist most.
- Change often creates **stress**.

### Lesson 6.4: Employee stress

**Must know**

- **Stress:** the adverse reaction people have to excessive pressure placed on them from extraordinary demands, constraints or opportunities.
- **Constraints:** barriers that keep us from doing what we desire. **Demands:** the loss of something desired.
- **3 groups of symptoms:** **physical** (faster heart and breathing rates, higher blood pressure, headaches); **psychological** (job dissatisfaction, tension, irritability, boredom, procrastination); **behavioural** (absenteeism, turnover, more smoking or drinking, rapid speech, fidgeting, sleep problems).
- **2 groups of causes:** 1. **organizational (job-related):** task demands, role demands, interpersonal demands, organization structure, organizational leadership; 2. **personal:** family issues, personal economic problems, personality type.

**Explain more**

- **Task demands:** the job itself (design, conditions, layout). **Interpersonal demands:** co-workers (poor support). **Organization structure:** too many rules, little say in decisions. **Organizational leadership:** a style creating tension, fear and anxiety, with unrealistic pressure to perform.
- Stress is not always bad (it can bring opportunity); excess is the problem. **Role conflict** = expectations hard to meet at once; **role overload** = more expected than time allows; **role ambiguity** = expectations unclear.
- **Type A:** chronic time urgency, excessive competitive drive, difficulty enjoying leisure. **Type B:** relaxed, no time urgency or impatience. The harmful part of Type A is **hostility and anger**.
- **Reducing stress:** employee selection (match people to jobs); realistic job preview during selection; clear goals with feedback (MBO) to cut role ambiguity; job redesign; communication during change; employee assistance programmes (EAPs: counselling for personal problems); wellness programmes (fitness, healthy eating, stopping smoking).

### Lesson 6.5: Creativity and innovation

**Must know**

- **Creativity:** the ability to combine ideas in a unique way or to make unusual associations between ideas. It produces the idea.
- **Innovation:** taking a creative idea and turning it into a useful product, service or method of operation. It improves productivity and competitiveness.
- **Systems view of innovation:** 1. **inputs:** creative people and groups; 2. **transformation:** a creative environment, process and situation; 3. **outputs:** innovative products and work methods.
- **Idea champions:** individuals who actively and enthusiastically support new ideas, build support, overcome resistance and make sure innovations are implemented. Traits: very high self-confidence, persistence, energy, risk-taking.
- **3 sets of variables that foster innovation:**
    - **Structural:** organic structure (flexible: low formalization, centralization, specialization); abundant resources; frequent communication between units; minimal time pressure; work and nonwork support.
    - **Cultural:** accept ambiguity; tolerate the impractical; keep external controls low; tolerate risk; tolerate conflict; focus on ends rather than means; open-system focus; give positive feedback.
    - **Human resource:** actively promote training and development; offer high job security; encourage people to become idea champions.

**Explain more**

- **Abundant resources:** buy innovations, afford failures, try many ideas. **Minimal time pressure:** extreme pressure lowers creativity.
- **Tolerate risk:** failures not punished. **Open-system focus:** watching the environment. **High job security:** less fear of being fired for mistakes.

### One-minute recap

- Change = people, structure or technology; 5 external, 4 internal forces.
- Calm waters = Lewin's 3 steps; white-water = constant change; OD changes people (insiders cautious, outsiders may be drastic).
- Stress: 3 symptom groups, 2 cause groups. Resistance: 4 reasons, 6 remedies. Creativity = the idea; innovation = the idea made useful.
