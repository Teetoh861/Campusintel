## Topic 4: Organization Design and Structure

**What you must know:** the six design elements; authority and power; mechanistic vs organic; the four contingency factors; the designs; today's challenges.

### Lesson 4.1: Organizing, work specialization and departmentalization

**Must know**

- **Organizing:** arranging and structuring work to accomplish organizational goals. It **follows planning**. Organizing is to **structure** as planning is to **goal setting**.
- **Structure:** the formal framework by which job tasks are divided, grouped and coordinated. **Chart:** its picture. **Design:** developing or changing it.
- **6 elements of design:** work specialization, departmentalization, chain of command, span of control, centralization/decentralization, formalization. Together they decide if a structure is **mechanistic or organic**.
- **7 purposes of organizing:** divide work into jobs and departments; assign tasks and responsibilities; coordinate tasks; cluster jobs into units; establish relationships among individuals, groups, departments; establish formal lines of authority; allocate and deploy resources. ("It creates services" is not one.)
- **Work specialization (division of labour):** the degree to which tasks are divided into separate jobs; each employee does **part** of an activity, not the whole.
- **Departmentalization:** the basis on which jobs are grouped to achieve goals. **5 types:**
    1. **Functional:** by functions or tasks.
    2. **Geographic:** by territory or location.
    3. **Product:** by product line or service.
    4. **Process:** by product or customer **flow**.
    5. **Customer:** by type of customer and needs. Now more important: tracks customer needs and responds to changes.

**Explain more**

- **Principles:** **unity of objective** (the structure must serve the organization's goals); **efficiency** (**lowest cost, least waste**); division of work; span of control; **scalar chain** (line of authority top to bottom); unity of command; **parity of authority and responsibility** (in balance); **delegation of authority** (decision power at the proper level); balance, flexibility, continuity. **Publicity** is not a principle.
- Early view: endless source of productivity. Too far: boredom, fatigue, absenteeism, turnover. More tasks per worker **reduces** it.
- **Cross-functional team:** people from different functions.

### Lesson 4.2: Chain of command, authority, power and span of control

**Must know**

- **Chain of command:** the unbroken line of authority from top to lowest levels; shows who reports to whom. The formal structure of authority is the **hierarchy**.
- **Authority:** the rights of a position to tell people what to do and expect compliance. **Responsibility:** obligation to perform a duty.
- **Unity of command:** each person reports to **only one** manager (Fayol; keeps authority continuous).
- **Delegation:** transferring authority to another person for specific duties.
- **Line authority:** directs employees' work; follows the chain of command. **Staff authority:** supports, assists, advises and reduces the load of line managers.
- **Authority** is a **right**, from the **position**. **Power** is a **capacity to influence** decisions and is the **broader** concept; authority is part of it.
- **5 sources of power:** **legitimate** (position; same as authority), **coercive** (ability to punish or control), **reward** (pay, bonuses, praise), **expert** (special knowledge), **referent** (personal traits others admire).
- **Span of control:** the number of employees a manager can efficiently and effectively supervise. It decides the number of management **levels**.

**Explain more**

- Authority stays with the post when holder leaves. Power without authority: a secretary controlling access.
- Chain of command matters less today: **information technology and empowered employees**.
- **Narrow span = tall. Wide span = flat** (few levels). Not "thin".
- 4,096 operatives: span 4 needs **1,365** managers, **7** levels (with operatives); span 8 needs **585**, **5** levels.
- Wider span is more **efficient** (lower cost, faster decisions, better communication). Too wide: effectiveness falls (too little supervision); many people to control is the disadvantage. **Trend:** wider spans (cut costs, speed decisions, empower staff).
- **Wider span when:** staff well trained; tasks similar and simple; staff close together; standardized procedures; good information system. Not a factor: organization age.

### Lesson 4.3: Centralization, formalization, mechanistic vs organic, contingency factors

**Must know**

- **Centralization:** decision making concentrated at upper levels. **Decentralization:** lower-level managers give input or make decisions. **Employee empowerment:** increasing workers' decision-making discretion.
- **Formalization:** the degree to which jobs are standardized and guided by rules and procedures.

| Mechanistic | Organic |
| --- | --- |
| Rigid, tightly controlled, efficient | Flexible; changes quickly |
| Specialized, standardized jobs | Cross-functional, cross-hierarchical teams |
| Clear chain of command | Free flow of information |
| Narrow spans | Wide spans |
| Centralized, high formalization | Decentralized, low formalization |

- **4 contingency factors:** **strategy, size, technology, environmental uncertainty.**

**Explain more**

- **Trend:** decentralization, for flexibility and responsiveness. **Centralize when:** stable environment; less capable lower managers; large firm; significant decisions; crisis. **Decentralize when:** uncertain environment; capable managers wanting a say; minor decisions; open culture; geographic spread. Every decision needing the unit head's sign-off = centralized.
- **Strategy:** **structure follows strategy.** 3 focuses: **innovation** (meaningful, unique new products) = **organic**; **cost minimization** = **mechanistic**; **imitation** (copying market leaders to cut risk) = a **mix**. Diversification is not one.
- **Size:** as an organization takes on more employees its structure becomes more **specialized** (not informal): more departmentalization, centralization, rules (not decentralization), so more **mechanistic**.
- **Technology: Joan Woodward** studied small manufacturing firms in **southern England**:
    - **Unit** (units or small batches): least complex; organic.
    - **Mass** (**large-batch**): moderate; **mechanistic**.
    - **Process** (continuous): **most** complex; organic.
- **Environment:** stable suits mechanistic; dynamic, uncertain suits **organic**. Forces towards organic: global competition, rivals' faster innovation, customers wanting better quality and faster delivery.

### Lesson 4.4: Traditional and contemporary designs

**Must know**

- **Traditional** (more mechanistic): **simple, functional, divisional.** Structures grow simple, then functional, then divisional, becoming more **specialized**.
    - **Simple:** low departmentalization, **wide spans**, authority centralized in one person, little formalization. Suits **very small firms with few employees** (even one selling worldwide). Strength: fast, flexible, cheap. Weakness: relying on one person is risky.
    - **Functional:** groups similar occupational specialties. Strength: cost savings from specialization. Weakness: functions lose sight of the whole; specialists isolated.
    - **Divisional:** **autonomous, self-contained** units linked by a **product or service**. Strength: **focus on results**. Weakness: **duplication** of activities and resources.
- **Contemporary:** team, matrix, project, boundaryless, learning organization.
    - **Team:** whole organization of work teams; **empowerment** most important.
    - **Matrix:** functional specialists assigned to projects under a **project manager**; functional plus **product** departmentalization.
    - **Project:** employees **continuously** work on projects; advanced matrix.
    - **Boundaryless:** not limited by horizontal, vertical or external boundaries.

**Explain more**

- **Team:** fewer barriers between functions; weakness: **no clear chain of command**.
- **Matrix:** **two bosses** (functional and project manager), so it **breaks unity of command**. Strength: fluid, flexible. Weaknesses: complex assignments; task and personality conflicts.
- **Project:** no formal department to return to between projects.
- **Boundaries:** horizontal = between **functions**; vertical = between **levels**; external = with customers, suppliers. Cross-level teams and outside agencies remove them; **virtual** or **network** designs minimize them.
- **3 boundaryless forms:** **virtual** (small full-time core plus outside specialists hired **temporarily**); **network** (small core **outsources** major functions such as manufacturing, accounting, payroll); **modular** (suppliers make **components**, the firm assembles).

### Lesson 4.5: Today's design challenges

**Must know**

- **4 challenges:** keeping employees connected (linking dispersed or mobile staff through **technology**); managing global structural issues (understand how **culture shapes structure**); building a learning organization; designing flexible work arrangements.
- **Learning organization:** capacity to continuously learn, adapt and change. Ability to **learn and apply** may be its only lasting advantage. It needs **information**.
- **5 flexible work arrangements:**
    1. **Telecommuting:** working from home, linked by technology.
    2. **Compressed workweek:** longer days, fewer days.
    3. **Flextime:** set weekly hours; staff vary start and finish within limits.
    4. **Job sharing:** two or more people split one full-time job.
    5. **Contingent workforce:** temporary, freelance or contract workers, as demand requires.

**Explain more**

- **Learning organization features:** design = boundaryless, teams, empowerment; information shared openly, timely, accurately; leadership = shared vision, collaboration; culture = strong relationships, community, caring, trust. All members help solve work problems.

### One-minute recap

- Organizing follows planning; 6 elements; 5 departmentalizations.
- Authority = right; power = broader; one boss.
- Narrow span = tall; wide = flat, efficient.
- Mechanistic = rigid; organic = flexible.
- Contingency: strategy, size, technology, environment.
- Traditional: simple, functional, divisional.
