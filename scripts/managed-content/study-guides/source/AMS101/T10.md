## Topic 10: Foundations of Control and Operations Management

**What you must know:** control, its process, types and tools; operations management; value chain management; quality.

### Lesson 10.1: Control and the control process

**Must know**

- **Controlling:** evaluating progress towards goals by **monitoring, comparing and taking corrective action**, so **actual performance meets planned goals**. It is the **last** management function. The real test of an effective control system is **whether the organization's goals are being achieved**. Control minimises deviations from standards because it helps managers **check errors and take corrective action**.
- **3 reasons control matters:** (1) the **planning–controlling link** (control checks planning's goals and feeds new planning); (2) **empowering employees** (managers delegate without fearing blame for mistakes); (3) **protecting the workplace** (from disasters, scandals, violence, supply disruption, security breaches, terrorism).
- **Control process, 3 steps:** (1) **measure** actual performance; (2) **compare** with the standard; (3) **take managerial action** (corrective action). Key elements: **setting (establishing) performance standards** (taken from planning's goals, and the first element of control), **measuring performance, comparing actual results with planned objectives, and taking corrective action**. Performance measurement means comparing actual results with planned objectives.
- **4 information sources for measuring:** personal observation, statistical reports, oral reports, written reports.
- **Range of variation:** acceptable limits of variance between actual performance and the standard. Act only **outside** it; watch **size and direction**.
- **3 actions:** (1) do nothing; (2) correct actual performance (e.g. change pay or training); (3) revise the standard (if unrealistic).
- **Immediate corrective action:** corrects at **once**, treats the symptom (same-day overtime). **Basic corrective action:** asks **how and why**, fixes the **source** (replace the faulty machine).

**Explain more**

- **Personal observation** (**MBWA**, management by walking around): first-hand, unfiltered, small details; but biased, slow, looks like distrust. **Statistical:** graphs show relationships; limited facts, ignore subjective factors. **Oral:** quick; filtered, no permanent record. **Written:** comprehensive, formal, easy to file; slower to prepare.
- **Twin functions:** planning and control both look at goals (planning sets, control checks), occur at **all levels**, are continuous, use quantitative techniques and standards, and use budgets and ratios.
- **What** you measure decides what employees work hard at. Any unit: satisfaction, turnover, absenteeism. Job-specific: units produced, sales. Output hard to count: **subjective** measures.
- Fair standard missed: hold to it and explain; do not lower it.
- **Trap:** if every option accurately describes control, "which does not" = none of the above.

### Lesson 10.2: Types of control and organizational performance

**Must know**

1. **Feedforward** (predictive): **before** the activity; foresee and prevent problems.
2. **Concurrent:** **during**; correct problems as they happen.
3. **Feedback:** **after** completion; uses performance data to assess whether goals were met.

- **Strategic control:** ensures the organization is **moving in the right direction** by tracking strategy **as it is implemented**, to detect problems or threats.
- **3 measures of organizational performance:** (1) **productivity** (total output ÷ inputs); (2) **effectiveness** (how appropriate goals are, how well met); (3) **industry and company rankings** (published lists that rank organizations on set measures).
- **Organizational performance:** combined result of all work activities. More productive = more output from the same or fewer inputs.

**Explain more**

- **Feedforward:** most **desirable**; needs timely, accurate information. Examples: inspecting raw materials, training before work, servicing machines.
- **Concurrent:** best known is **direct supervision**; MBWA too.
- **Feedback:** most **popular**; damage already done. Shows how effective **planning** was; can raise **motivation** (people want to know how they did). Examples: year-end financial statements, post-sale surveys, exam results.

### Lesson 10.3: Control tools and today's control issues

**Must know**

**4 financial ratio groups:**

1. **Liquidity** (meeting **current, short-term debts**): current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities; acid test = (current assets − inventories) ÷ current liabilities.
2. **Leverage** (use of **debt**, ability to pay interest): debt to assets = total debt ÷ total assets; times interest earned = profits before interest and taxes ÷ interest charges.
3. **Activity** (**efficient use** of assets): inventory turnover = sales ÷ inventory; total asset turnover = sales ÷ total assets.
4. **Profitability** (assets **generating profit**): profit margin = net profit after taxes ÷ total sales; return on investment = net profit after taxes ÷ total assets.

- **Budget:** a **planning and control** tool; gives **quantitative standards**.
- **MIS (management information system):** gives managers information **regularly**. **Data** = raw, unanalysed facts; **information** = analysed data.
- **Information controls:** a **tool** to control other activities, and an **area to be controlled** (encryption, firewalls, back-ups).
- **Balanced scorecard** (**Robert Kaplan and David Norton**): **4 areas**: financial, customer, internal processes, people/innovation/growth assets. Finance alone can hide poor service or staff development.
- **Benchmarking:** searching for **best practices** among competitors or noncompetitors; a **benchmark** is the standard of excellence compared against. **Xerox:** first major US effort. **Internal** benchmarking copies a top unit's practices elsewhere.
- **4 contemporary control issues:** (1) cross-cultural differences and global turmoil; (2) workplace concerns (privacy, theft, violence); (3) customer interactions; (4) corporate governance.

**Explain more**

- Distant global units cannot be observed directly, so controls are more **formal**.
- **Employee theft:** employees' unauthorised taking of company property for own use. Careful hiring and clear policies = feedforward; supervision = concurrent; investigation after theft = feedback.
- **Service profit chain:** satisfied employees → better service → customer loyalty → profit.
- **Corporate governance:** governing a corporation so **owners' interests are protected**. 2 reform areas: **boards of directors' role** and **financial reporting** (**Sarbanes-Oxley Act, 2002**, US).

### Lesson 10.4: Operations management and productivity

**Must know**

- **Operations function:** produces goods or services. **Operations management:** administration of business structure, practices and processes to **enhance efficiency and maximise profit**; the **design and control of the transformation process** (resources into goods and services).
- **3 goals:** controlling costs, maximising profitability, proper allocation of resources.
- It **begins and ends with the customer**; everyone has customers **at the next process**.
- **6 functions:** planning, scheduling, purchasing, controlling, quality control, inventory control.
- **4 types:** (1) **manufacturing** (goods); (2) **service** (intangible); (3) **logistics** (movement and storage of goods); (4) **project management** (a one-off project, planning to completion).
- **Transformation process:** **inputs** (people, technology, capital, equipment, materials, information) → **transformation** (work activities, operations methods, management activities) → **outputs** (goods, services).
- **Productivity = outputs ÷ inputs** (1,200 loaves ÷ 300 labour-hours = 4 per labour-hour).

**Explain more**

- Operations is vital because of **global competition**, the **rise of the service economy** and the need for efficient procurement, production and delivery.
- **Manufacturing:** physical goods, storable. **Service:** nonphysical, used as produced (banks, hospitals).
- Productivity needs **both people and operations variables**.
- **W. Edwards Deming:** **14 points** for management's productivity (plan for the **long** term, drive out fear, break down barriers, statistical control); linked with **TQM**; early influence in **Japan**.

### Lesson 10.5: Value chain management and quality

**Must know**

- **Value:** features and attributes customers will give up resources (usually money) for.
- **Value chain:** the entire series of work activities that **add value at each step**, raw materials to finished product. The **customer** decides what value is and when.
- **Value chain management:** managing activities and information along the **entire** chain. Goal: meet and exceed customer needs, with **full and seamless integration** of members. **External** focus, incoming **and** outgoing, goal **effectiveness** (most value for customers).
- **Supply chain management:** **internal** focus on efficient flow of **incoming** materials, goal **efficiency** (cut costs).
- **6 requirements:** coordination and collaboration; technology investment; organizational processes; leadership; employees; organizational culture and attitudes.
- **4 obstacles:** organizational barriers; cultural attitudes; required capabilities; people.
- **Benefits:** improved procurement, logistics, product development; enhanced customer order management.
- **Quality:** ability of a product or service to reliably do what it should and satisfy customer expectations.
- **TQM:** philosophy driven by **continuous improvement** and responsiveness to customer needs, with employee involvement.
- **Mass customisation:** a product when, where, how customers want it.
- **ISO 9000:** **international quality management standards**, uniform process guidelines; set by the International Organization for Standardization.
- **Six Sigma:** programme to reduce defects, lower costs, save time, improve satisfaction; goal **no more than 3.4 defects per million** units or procedures.

**Explain more**

- **Obstacles:** *organizational barriers* = refusing to share information, resisting change, security worries; *cultural attitudes* = lack of trust, fear of losing intellectual property; *required capabilities* = partners lack skills or systems; *people* = unwilling to change, low commitment, time and energy.

### One-minute recap

- Controlling = monitoring, comparing, correcting; the last function. Process: measure, compare, act (standards come from planning).
- Actions: do nothing, correct (immediate = symptom, basic = cause) or revise the standard.
- Feedforward before (most desirable); concurrent during; feedback after (most popular).
- 4 control issues: cross-cultural, workplace concerns, customer interactions, corporate governance.
- Tools: 4 ratio groups, budget, MIS, balanced scorecard, benchmarking.
- Operations: 3 goals, 6 functions, 4 types; productivity = outputs ÷ inputs.
- Value chain: external, effectiveness; supply chain: internal, efficiency. Six Sigma: 3.4 per million.
