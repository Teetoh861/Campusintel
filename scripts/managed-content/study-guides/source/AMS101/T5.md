## Topic 5: Managing Human Resources

**What you must know:** the 8 HRM activities.

### Lesson 5.1: Why HRM matters, the HRM process and outside influences

**Must know**

- **HRM:** getting, training, keeping and rewarding the right people. Old name: **staffing** (includes appraisal; not budgeting).
- **3 reasons HRM matters:** **competitive advantage**, **strategy**, effect on **performance**.
- **High-performance work practices:** raise individual and organizational performance, e.g. self-managed teams, decentralised decisions, flexible jobs, open communication, performance-based pay, person-job fit.
- **8 activities, 3 groups:**
    - Identify and select competent employees: HR planning (1), recruitment and decruitment (2), selection (3).
    - Give up-to-date skills and knowledge: orientation (4), training (5).
    - Retain competent, high-performing employees: performance management (6), compensation and benefits (7), career development (8).
- **External factors:** labour unions, laws and regulations, demographic trends; the whole process sits inside the external environment.

**Explain more**

- **Labour union:** represents workers via **collective bargaining**, giving a **collective bargaining agreement** (pay, hours, conditions).
- Nigeria: **NLC** (umbrella body of trade unions); **ASUU** (university academic staff union); **Labour Act** (law on hiring, pay, dismissal).
- **Affirmative action programmes:** improve protected groups' position (hiring, promotion). **Demographic trends:** diverse, ageing workforce. **Hugo Munsterberg**, father of industrial psychology: scientific selection.

### Lesson 5.2: HR planning, recruitment and decruitment

**Must know**

- **HR planning** (**employment planning**): right number and kinds of capable people, right places, right times. **2 steps:** assess current human resources, then meet future needs (from mission, goals, strategy, demand).
- **Human resource inventory:** record of current employees' education, past jobs, languages, skills.
- **Job analysis** (first): defines jobs and needed behaviours; produces:
    - **Job description:** written statement of what the job is: what the jobholder does, how, why. The **job**.
    - **Job specification:** minimum qualifications (knowledge, skills, attitudes) for the job. The **person**.
- **Recruitment:** locating, identifying, attracting capable applicants. **Decruitment:** reducing the workforce.
- **7 decruitment options:** **firing** (permanent, involuntary); **layoffs** (temporary, involuntary); **attrition** (not filling jobs left by resignations or retirements, no one forced out); **transfers** (sideways or downward, usually no cost cut, balance labour inside); **reduced workweeks** (cutting everyone's weekly hours instead of letting people go); **early retirements** (incentives for older, senior staff); **job sharing** (usually two part-timers share one full-time job).

**Explain more**

- **Recruitment sources** (advantage; disadvantage):
    - Internal search: low cost, morale, known candidates; limited supply, no diversity gain.
    - Employee referrals: staff know the job, stake their name; no diversity gain.
    - Adverts, internet: many unqualified applicants.
    - School and college: large pool; mostly entry-level.
    - Public agencies: low cost; less skilled.
    - Private agencies ("headhunters"): careful screening; costly, little commitment.
    - Temporary help: short-term needs; expensive.

### Lesson 5.3: Selection

**Must know**

- **Selection:** screening applicants to hire the most appropriate; **prediction**.
- **4 outcomes:** two correct decisions (accepting someone who succeeds; rejecting someone who would have failed), two errors. **Accept error:** hiring someone who performs poorly (costs: training, poor work, firing and rehiring). **Reject error:** rejecting someone who would have done well (costs: discrimination claims, losing talent to rivals).
- **Validity:** proven relationship between a device and a criterion such as later job performance (predicts). **Reliability:** measures the same thing **consistently**. Reliable need not be valid.
- **6 selection devices:** application forms, written tests, performance-simulation tests, interviews, background investigations, physical examinations.
- **Realistic job preview (RJP):** **positive and negative** information on job and company; raises satisfaction, lowers turnover.

**Explain more**

- **Application forms:** basic facts; screen out unqualified applicants. **Written tests:** intelligence, aptitude, personality, integrity.
- **Performance-simulation tests** use actual job behaviour. **Work sampling:** small replica of the job, central tasks performed; routine, standard jobs. **Assessment centres:** interviews, problem-solving, group exercises, panel-judged; test **managerial potential**.
- **Interviews:** more valid, reliable if **structured** (same planned questions). **Behavioural interview:** how candidates handled real past situations.
- **Background investigations:** check application facts and references; references have little value (referees say only good things). **Physical examinations:** jobs with physical demands; sometimes insurance.
- Weber: choosing by technical qualifications is **formal selection**.

### Lesson 5.4: Orientation and training

**Must know**

- **Orientation:** introducing new employees to the job and organization; moves them from **outsider to insider**, lowers anxiety, makes early quitting less likely.
    - **Work unit:** unit goals, how the job adds to them, co-workers.
    - **Organization:** company goals, history, philosophy, rules, HR policies (pay, overtime, leave, fringe benefits).
- **Training:** building job skills and knowledge to do jobs better; raises performance.
- **Training methods, 2 groups:**
    - **Traditional:** on-the-job training, job rotation, mentoring and coaching, experiential exercises (role play, simulations), workbooks, manuals, classroom lectures.
    - **Technology-based:** CDs, DVDs, video and audio recordings, videoconferencing, teleconferencing, e-learning (internet-based).

**Explain more**

- **Job rotation:** sideways moves to learn several jobs. **Mentoring and coaching:** experienced employee advises a newer one. **General** training: communication, computer skills, customer service. **Specific:** safety, leadership, diversity awareness, product knowledge, ethics.
- Signs training is needed: falling output, poorer quality, more accidents, new technology. Training fixes lack of **ability**.

### Lesson 5.5: Performance management, pay, retention and careers

**Must know**

- **Performance management system:** setting standards and appraising performance for objective HR decisions and records.
- **7 appraisal methods** (advantage; disadvantage):
    1. **Written essay:** writes strengths, weaknesses, past performance, potential; reflects writer's skill.
    2. **Critical incidents:** records behaviours separating effective from ineffective work; rich examples; slow, no numbers.
    3. **Graphic rating scales:** rates factors on a 1 to 5 scale; quick, numbers; little depth.
    4. **BARS:** rates specific job behaviours, **combining critical incidents and graphic rating scales**; slow, hard to build.
    5. **Multiperson comparisons:** compare one person with others; hard with many, legal concerns.
    6. **MBO:** judged on achieving agreed goals; slow.
    7. **360-degree:** feedback from supervisors, co-workers, subordinates, customers, self; slow.
- **3 multiperson comparisons:** **group order ranking** (top fifth etc.); **individual ranking** (best to worst); **paired comparison** (each against every other). These are **relative**; other methods are **absolute**.
- **Skill-based pay:** rewards demonstrable skills. **Variable pay:** pay depends on performance.
- **Compensation:** base pay, incentives, **benefits** (nonwage extras: health care, pensions, leave).

**Explain more**

- **Discipline:** actions to enforce standards and rules. **Employee counselling:** helps employees overcome performance problems. Cannot do it: train or coach. Will not: discipline or counsel.
- **Pay-level factors:** tenure, performance, kind of job, kind of business, unionised or not, labour- or capital-intensive, management's pay philosophy, location, profitability, size.
- **Retention:** keeping satisfied employees (fair pay, benefits, recognition, development). **Career:** sequence of positions over a lifetime. **Career development:** helps employees plan and grow careers in the organization.

### Lesson 5.6: Current HRM issues

**Must know**

- **Downsizing:** planned elimination of jobs.
- **Layoff-survivor sickness:** attitudes, perceptions, behaviours of employees who **remain** after involuntary cuts (insecurity, unfairness, guilt, stress, lower motivation).
- **Sexual harassment:** unwanted action or activity of a sexual nature that explicitly or implicitly affects employment, performance or work environment. **Quid pro quo:** job benefit tied to sexual favours. **Hostile environment:** conduct makes the workplace offensive or intimidating, no job benefit. From a manager or co-worker, against men or women.
- **Workplace spirituality:** culture whose values promote purpose through meaningful work in the context of community; not organized religion.
- **5 features of a spiritual organization:** strong sense of purpose, focus on individual development, trust and openness, employee empowerment, toleration of employee expression.
- **HR costs to control:** **health care** (wellness programmes, healthy-habit rewards) and **pensions** (reduce or redesign plans).

**Explain more**

- **Downsizing:** give leavers notice, severance pay, outplacement. Survivors: let them talk, explain reasons.
- **Preventing harassment:** written policy, training, safe reporting, prompt investigation. **Managing diversity:** wider recruiting net, bias-free selection, diversity training.

### One-minute recap

- 8 activities, 3 groups.
- Job description = job; specification = person.
- Firing permanent, layoff temporary.
- Validity predicts; reliability is consistent.
- BARS = critical incidents + graphic rating scales.
- Skill-based pay: skills; variable: performance.
