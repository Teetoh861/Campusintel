## Topic 1: Nature, Types, Roles and Objectives of Marketing Communication

**What you must know:** definitions of marketing, communication and marketing communication; the four types of communication; the Seven Ps; how marketing communication emerged and gave rise to IMC; the conditions for exchange and the stages of the marketing communications process; the roles (including DRIP) and objectives of marketing communication.

### Lesson 1.1: Marketing, communication and marketing communication

**Must know**

Marketing communication is made of two key words: **marketing** and **communication**.

- **Marketing (simple definition):** the establishment of mutually satisfying exchange relationships through the buying and selling of goods, services or ideas.
- **Baker (2000):** marketing is a way of managing profitable customer relationships.
- **Kotler and Armstrong (2006):** the process through which companies create value for customers and build strong customer relationships in order to capture value from customers in return (also worded "to generate value in return").
- **Strydom, Jooste and Cant (2000):** the process of planning and executing the conception, pricing, marketing communication and distribution of ideas, products and services to create exchanges that satisfy individual and corporate goals.
- **Stapleton and Ali (2007): 4 commonalities** shared by these definitions:
  1. a focus on **customers**;
  2. the provision of **suitable offerings** (goods, services or ideas) to meet customers' needs;
  3. **suitable benefits to the organisation**;
  4. the **relationship between the customer and the organisation**, on the basis of which value can be created.
- **Communication (1):** the exchange of thoughts or conversations through written words, verbal conversation, electronic or digital media and social media platforms, to exchange ideas and information for a pre-planned purpose.
- **Communication (2):** the process of using **words, sounds or visual cues** to supply information to one or more people.
- **Marketing communications (MarCom):** the **channels and tools** companies use to communicate the necessary message to the target group. MarCom helps customers understand a company and its products. By conveying messages of significant value, it encourages audiences to give **positive attitudinal and behavioural responses**.
- There is **no universal definition** of marketing communications, so there are many interpretations. **5 common definitions:**
  1. A **continuous interaction between buyers and sellers** in a marketplace.
  2. The means companies adopt to convey messages about their products and brands, **directly or indirectly**, to customers with the intention to **persuade them to purchase**.
  3. The process of **creating and delivering messages that promote the interests of an organisation**.
  4. The use of different marketing strategies and tools to **connect with a target audience**.
  5. At its core, the **integration of all promotional tools and sources** within a company to **maximise the impact on consumers and other end-users at minimal cost**.
- **3 questions** marketing communication answers:
  1. Who is the customer?
  2. What is being exchanged?
  3. What internal and external influences affect the exchange?

**Explain more**

- A fuller, modern definition you should recognise: marketing communication is a **strategic process of creating, delivering and exchanging value-laden messages to shape customer perceptions and behaviours**. Any option that calls it "one-way", "price announcement only", "physical movement of goods" or "manufacturing" is wrong.
- Trap: the "planning and executing the conception, pricing ... distribution" wording is **Strydom, Jooste and Cant**, **not** Baker.
- Stapleton and Ali's commonalities never include cutting staff or costs.
- **All organisations** (commercial, government, charities, educational, not-for-profit, of any size) need to communicate with a range of stakeholders: suppliers, distribution partners, other departments and consumers, who are free to choose among competing offers.
- A **customer's perception** of a company or brand is a **synthesis of bundles of messages**: media advertising, **price**, **packaging design**, direct marketing, publicity, sales promotion and websites. The price of Indomie "speaks" as much as its TV advert.
- Used well, MarCom generates **more leads, sales and loyal customers**; its ultimate aim is to influence **consumer decision-making and buying behaviour**.

### Lesson 1.2: Types of communication and the marketing mix

**Must know**

**4 types of communication** (by direction of flow between senders and receivers):

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **One-to-many** | The most original form; generated from a **single broadcast point**, then available over airwaves or in mass print runs | News distribution, general announcements, TV and radio broadcasts |
| **Many-to-one** | Generally connected to one-to-many; many receivers reply to one sender | The **reply button** in an email box |
| **One-to-one** | The **most intensive and interactive** form | A **sales presentation** or a **negotiation** |
| **Many-to-many** | Growing because of the highly developed **Internet**; everyone talks to everyone | **Online chat rooms**, blogs, websites |

**The marketing mix: the Seven Ps (formerly the Four Ps).** A brand typically builds its MarCom strategy around it:

1. **Product:** the features of the good or service the business offers.
2. **Price:** the pricing model used for the good or service.
3. **Promotion:** all the ways in which the brand, product or service is communicated to the public. It also involves another P, **Positioning**, to evaluate how people view and respond to the brand.
4. **Place:** where the good or service is bought and sold.
5. **People:** all the people who interact with a brand's customers or clients.
6. **Process:** the customer experience and communication process.
7. **Packaging:** how best to attract customers in the physical and online marketplace.

**Types of marketing communication (the tools):** the traditional **five** are advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations and direct marketing. Others include publicity, events and experiences (sponsorship), interactive marketing and word-of-mouth marketing. Each is treated fully in Topic 3.

**Explain more**

- Count senders and receivers: GTBank emailing one newsletter to all customers is **one-to-many**; hundreds of them clicking "reply" is **many-to-one**; a sales pitch to one client is **one-to-one**; UNILAG students debating phone brands in a group chat is **many-to-many**.
- "Most intensive and interactive" = one-to-one; "most original" = one-to-many. The count is **four**.
- Trap: the extra P inside Promotion is **Positioning**. In the services-marketing version of the 7 Ps you may meet **physical evidence** in place of packaging; for this course, learn the list above.
- A Lagos fashion brand training its shop attendants and delivery riders on how to treat buyers is working on **People**.
- The combination most representative of MarCom is **advertising, sales promotion and direct marketing**, not "product, price and place" or "segmentation, targeting and positioning". **Integrated advertising campaigns across multiple channels** are a core MarCom component; prototyping and warehousing are not.

### Lesson 1.3: The emergence of marketing communication and IMC

**Must know**

- Marketing communication is **not new**: communication has been part of marketing **for as long as people have exchanged goods**.
- Technology and research made it **increasingly sophisticated** in recent decades and gave rise to **integrated marketing communication (IMC)**.
- **IMC:** the use of **multiple channels to create a unified message**.
- **Consumers have changed** in 4 ways: their behaviour is **more complex**; their **media habits are different**; they are **more confident and outspoken**; and, most important, they have **more choices than ever before**.

**Timeline:**

| When | Development | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 15th century | **Johannes Gutenberg's printing press** | Before it, only handwritten manuscripts for the elite; marked the **birth of print advertising** |
| 17th century | **First printed advertisements** in newspapers | Promoted medicines to real estate |
| 1920s | **Radio commercials** begin | Reached households with audio messages |
| 1950s | **Television** becomes the **dominant platform** | Combined visuals and sound |
| 1960s | Volkswagen's **"Think Small"** campaign | Showed TV could be used for **storytelling** and **emotional connections** |
| Late 20th century | **The Internet** | Digital age: websites as **virtual storefronts**, email as a **direct line** to customers |
| 1996 | **Hotmail's viral strategy** | "Get your free email at Hotmail" on every sent email; millions of users within months; an early **viral digital marketing** success |
| Early 2000s | **MySpace, Facebook, Twitter** | Turned marketing into a **two-way conversation**; brands engage directly with customers |

**Explain more**

- The lesson of the whole journey: marketing communication is **ever-evolving**, so understanding its history helps firms **anticipate future trends** and craft strategies that resonate with audiences.
- Trap: "consumers now have **fewer** choices" is false. They have more.
- Trap: printing press **15th** century, but first newspaper adverts **17th** century.
- Hotmail's tagline is **viral** marketing: users spread the message simply by using the product.
- Social media did **not** end print and broadcast advertising; it added two-way dialogue, as when Jumia replies to customers on X and Instagram.
- IMC in practice: MTN running the same slogan, colours and offer on TV, radio, billboards and social media, so every channel tells one story.

### Lesson 1.4: Marketing communication and the process of exchange

**Must know**

- **Exchange process:** the act of **obtaining a desired object from someone by offering something of value in return**.
- **Transaction:** the result of an exchange between a person in need (offering money or other resources) and the company selling a good, service or idea.
- **5 conditions for exchange:**
  1. There must be **at least two parties**.
  2. Each must have **something that might be of value** to the other.
  3. Each can **communicate and deliver** what they are offering.
  4. Each is **free to accept or reject** what is on offer.
  5. Each party **trusts/respects** the other sufficiently to take the exchange seriously.
- **Two observable types of exchange:** **market** exchange and **relational** exchange. There is a strong shift from one-off market exchanges to long-term **relationship marketing**: "Marketing is not just about selling products; it's about building relationships and creating value."

**Process of marketing communications (steps in order):**

1. **Identify the target audience:** focus on distinct segments; presenting to everyone does not work.
2. **Determine the unique value (selling) proposition (UVP/USP):** what makes the business stand out and outperform rivals.
3. **Bring the solution to customers' queries:** reach customers' pain points with the good or service.
4. **Select channels** to deliver the brand message: depends on the **objectives** to be accomplished and **customers' preferences (choices)**.
5. **Budget allocation:** how much to spend on each channel.
6. **Message development:** crafting key messages that resonate with the audience.
7. **Execution:** implementing the chosen plan.
8. **Evaluate and measure the result:** track outcomes after the campaign runs.
9. **Develop control measures:** measure the campaign's effectiveness **against objectives** and make necessary adjustments.

**Explain more**

- Condition 3 shows most directly **why marketing communication is necessary**: a seller who cannot tell buyers what it offers cannot exchange.
- Trap: "each party must be **legally bound** to accept whatever is offered" is **not** a condition. Freedom to accept or reject is.
- Ada pays ₦4,500 to a Yaba bookshop for a book: the completed exchange is a **transaction**.
- MarCom helps exchange by **generating awareness, fostering trust and driving purchase intent**. Exchange is a **reciprocal creation of value** benefiting both organisation and customers. Partnerships between brands and consumers, and between firms in distribution channels, are vital to MarCom.
- **UVP/USP example:** Domino's Pizza positions itself as delivering the newest and hottest pizza faster than rivals, in only **30 minutes**.
- This process is sometimes introduced as "six stages"; learn each step's name and job in the order above. Step 8 tracks outcomes; step 9 compares with objectives and **adjusts**.

### Lesson 1.5: Roles and objectives of marketing communication

**Must know**

- **Primary objective:** to **increase sales volume** through **persuasive, informative and positive messages**.
- MarCom objectives are **long-term goals** that aim to **boost the brand's value over time**.
- **9 specific objectives:**
  1. **Increase brand awareness:** make people know the business and its products exist (broadcast commercials or print ads); a top priority for new and existing firms.
  2. **Change attitudes:** correct mistaken beliefs spread about the business or its products.
  3. **Influence purchase intent:** compelling ads that focus on **superior benefits to users compared with competitors**.
  4. **Stimulate trial purchase and repeat patronage:** free trials or samples take the risk off so customers experience the brand.
  5. **Drive brand switching:** get buyers of competing products to switch to your brand.
  6. **Connect companies with consumers:** convey messages so the brand is better known and trusted.
  7. **Build a brand image:** branding is not built in a short time.
  8. **Conduct research for the company:** connect company stakeholders with the market by **reading trend movements** in the sector.
  9. **Do direct selling:** market products directly into end users' hands to build trust.
- **5 important roles:**
  1. **Building brand awareness:** customers know the organisation exists and what it offers.
  2. **Maintaining customer loyalty:** customers develop a positive bond with the company and its brands.
  3. **Creating demand for products and services:** improved or innovative products are not enough; people need to know they exist to buy them.
  4. **Shortening the sales cycle:** by better understanding customer needs, buying motivations, influences and processes, and using this insight when engaging consumers.
  5. **Highlighting a competitive edge:** in a crowded 21st-century marketplace, points of differentiation must be shared with the public to enhance competitive advantage.
- **3 other specific roles:** (i) influences consumer behaviour to drive sales; (ii) engages customers at various points along the buying journey; (iii) integrates all marketing tools to achieve seamless communication.
- **DRIP (Fill):** **D**ifferentiate, **R**einforce, **I**nform, **P**ersuade.
- **Kotler's three classic aims:** **inform, persuade, remind**.

| DRIP role | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiate | Shows how the brand differs from rivals | Highlighting a unique feature |
| Reinforce | Consolidates past messages and experiences; reminds and reassures existing buyers | A well-known drink reminding loyal buyers of good times with it |
| Inform | Makes people aware of the offer | A new product launch |
| Persuade | Encourages a desired action, such as buying | "Buy now" offers |

**Explain more**

- Matching scenarios (the commonest question style):
  - A new fintech app runs radio and print ads so people know it exists: **increase brand awareness**.
  - A bottled-water brand hit by false rumours shows its NAFDAC approval and factory tests: **change attitudes**.
  - Free sample sachets of a new cream at a Lagos mall: **stimulate trial purchase**.
  - Bonus data for customers of rival networks who port their lines: **drive brand switching**.
  - Birthday messages and loyalty-point updates from an Abuja supermarket: **maintaining customer loyalty** (a role).
- Trap: a familiar brand "reminding" loyal buyers of good experiences is **Reinforce** under DRIP (Kotler would say **remind**).
- Fixing machinery, setting the minimum wage, cutting production costs or replacing a quality product are **never** MarCom objectives or roles.
- Strategically, MarCom **enhances brand equity, fosters loyalty and drives repeat purchases**, and influences purchasing patterns through **targeted messaging**. Its primary function is to **inform, persuade and remind** customers to sustain engagement with the brand.

### One-minute recap

- Marketing: exchange relationships; Baker = profitable customer relationships; Kotler and Armstrong = create value, capture value in return.
- Communication = using words, sounds or visual cues to supply information to one or more people.
- MarCom = channels and tools to communicate the necessary message to the target group; no universal definition.
- 4 types of communication: one-to-many (most original), many-to-one (email reply), one-to-one (most intensive), many-to-many (chat rooms, blogs).
- Seven Ps (formerly Four Ps): product, price, promotion (plus positioning), place, people, process, packaging.
- Printing press (15th c.), first print ads (17th c.), radio (1920s), TV dominant (1950s), Think Small (1960s), Hotmail viral (1996), social media two-way (early 2000s); IMC = multiple channels, one unified message.
- 5 conditions for exchange; exchange process yields a transaction; nine-step MarCom process from target audience to control measures.
- Primary objective: increase sales volume; 9 specific objectives; 5 main roles plus 3 others; DRIP = Differentiate, Reinforce, Inform, Persuade.

### Theory practice

**Question:** State the five conditions for an exchange to take place and explain which of them shows why marketing communication is needed.

*Answer outline:* (1) at least two parties; (2) each has something that might be of value to the other; (3) each can communicate and deliver what it offers; (4) each is free to accept or reject the offer; (5) each trusts/respects the other enough to take the exchange seriously. Condition 3 shows the need for marketing communication: without a way to tell buyers what is on offer, no exchange (and so no transaction) can happen. MarCom fills this gap by generating awareness, fostering trust and driving purchase intent.
