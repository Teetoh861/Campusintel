## Topic 6: Marketing Communication in the Internet Era, Social Media and Word-of-Mouth

**What you must know:** how the internet changed marketing communication (Hotmail 1996, MySpace, Facebook and Twitter, monologue to dialogue, many-to-many, consumer power, pull); interactivity; social media marketing and the types of platforms; roles, advantages and risks of social media; reach, impressions, organic reach and engagement; paid, owned and earned media; user-generated content, influencers and brand advocates; word-of-mouth, eWOM, viral, buzz, seeding and referral marketing; managing WOM.

### Lesson 6.1: How the internet changed marketing communication

**Must know**

- **The road to the internet era:** printing press (Johannes Gutenberg, 15th century) gave print advertising; first printed adverts in **17th-century newspapers**; radio commercials from the **1920s**; television dominant by the **1950s**; the **Internet in the late 20th century** ushered in the digital age, with **websites as virtual storefronts** and **email as a direct line to customers**.
- **Hotmail, 1996:** one of the earliest successful digital marketing cases. It added **"Get your free email at Hotmail"** to every email its users sent and gained **millions of users within months**. This is **viral marketing**.
- **Early 2000s:** **MySpace, Facebook and Twitter** rose and changed how businesses interact with audiences. **Social media turned marketing into a two-way conversation**, letting brands engage directly with customers.
- **Many-to-many communication:** grown on the back of the highly developed Internet, e.g. **online chat rooms, blogging websites**, discussion forums. (The other three types: one-to-many, many-to-one, one-to-one.)
- **Interactivity:** firms and consumers can communicate **in both directions** and respond to each other's messages, often in real time.

**The key changes** (what marketing communication in the internet era is distinguished by):

1. From a **one-way monologue** (firm to customer) to a **two-way conversation** (dialogue). This is the most important change.
2. **Greater interactivity, real-time engagement and integration** across many channels.
3. **Speed and reach:** messages spread faster and reach **global** audiences.
4. **Greater consumer power and control:** consumers compare prices, read reviews and share opinions publicly.
5. A move towards **pull communication:** consumers search, follow pages and choose what content to watch.
6. **Measurability:** clicks, views and shares can be measured quickly and accurately.
7. **Precise targeting** using data about the audience.
8. **Word-of-mouth amplified** through online reviews, shares and influencer endorsements.

**Explain more**

- Traps: the internet era did **not** reduce customer interaction, did **not** limit firms to traditional media, did **not** reduce feedback and did **not** reduce advertising options. Every option of that kind is wrong.
- Push versus pull: a TV advert interrupts you (push); you searching "best blender in Lagos" and reading reviews on Jumia is pull. The internet did not remove push communication; it shifted the balance towards pull.
- Audiences now have more choices than ever, are more confident and outspoken, and their media habits have changed. This is also why integrated marketing communication (IMC) matters more: many channels must carry one unified message.
- Marketing communication is ever-evolving: understanding its history from the printing press to social media helps firms anticipate future trends.

### Lesson 6.2: Social media marketing and types of platforms

**Must know**

- **Social media marketing:** using social media platforms (such as Facebook, Instagram, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube) to **engage audiences, build the brand, and drive traffic and sales**, by delivering **targeted, interactive content**.
- Its effectiveness comes mainly from **two-way communication and real-time interaction** with customers.
- It lets firms deliver **closely targeted messages based on data about their audience** (age, location, interests).

**Types of social media platforms**

| Type | Main use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social networks | Connecting with friends, family and brands | Facebook |
| Media-sharing networks | Sharing photos and videos | YouTube, Instagram, TikTok |
| Microblogging | Short, fast public posts and conversations | X (Twitter) |
| Professional networks | Careers and business contacts (strong for B2B) | LinkedIn |
| Messaging apps | Private one-to-one and group chats | WhatsApp, Telegram |
| Blogs and forums | Longer posts and many-to-many discussion | Blogs, Nairaland, Reddit |
| Review sites | Ratings and reviews of products and places | Google reviews, store review pages |

- **Content marketing:** creating and sharing **valuable, relevant content** (articles, videos, tips) to **attract and keep** a target audience.

**Explain more**

- Search engines (Google) and online banking platforms are **not** social media. YouTube, Instagram and TikTok are media-sharing networks; LinkedIn is the professional network.
- A recipe brand posting short cooking videos using its seasoning on TikTok is content marketing on a media-sharing network: the audience gets something useful, and the brand stays in mind.
- Social media marketing does **not** replace all other forms of marketing communication, does not work without content, and does not mean sending one message to everyone with no targeting.

### Lesson 6.3: Roles, advantages, risks and measures of social media

**Must know**

**Roles of social media in marketing communication:**

1. **Foster dialogue, build communities and strengthen customer relationships** (the strategic role).
2. **Allow direct engagement** with customers.
3. **Provide customer insights** (what people like, complain about, want).
4. **Increase brand visibility, affinity and long-term loyalty.**
5. Give **real-time feedback**.
6. Support customer service and drive traffic and sales.

**Advantages:** precise targeting at relatively low cost; direct two-way interaction; quick, real-time feedback; measurable responses (clicks, shares, views); reach to large audiences.

**Risks:**

1. **Loss of control over the message:** customers can comment, share and change its meaning. Social media never gives full control over what customers say.
2. **Negative eWOM spreads quickly** and damages reputation.
3. **Hashtag hijacking:** people use a brand's hashtag for complaints or mockery instead of the intended purpose.
4. **Fake reviews and astroturfing** mislead customers.
5. **Influencer scandals** damage the brand's image.

**Key terms:**

- **Community management:** the ongoing work of building and managing relationships with online followers (replying to comments, answering questions).
- **Social listening:** monitoring online conversations to learn what people say about a brand, its competitors and its industry.
- **Engagement:** likes, comments, shares (and saves, clicks) on a post.
- **Reach:** the number of **different people** who saw the content.
- **Impressions:** the **total number of times** the content was displayed (one person can produce several).
- **Organic reach:** people who see content **without the brand paying** to promote it. Because platforms decide what appears in feeds, organic reach is often low, so brands **pay to boost posts**.

**Explain more**

- Swap trap: reach counts **people**, impressions count **displays**. Impressions are always equal to or greater than reach. Frequency = impressions ÷ reach.
- Worked example: a Lagos fashion store boosts a post for ₦30,000. It reaches 12,000 people, records 30,000 impressions and gets 900 likes, comments and shares.
    - Frequency = 30,000 ÷ 12,000 = 2.5 views per person.
    - Cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM) = (₦30,000 ÷ 30,000) × 1,000 = ₦1,000.
    - Engagement rate (one common method, by reach) = 900 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = 7.5%.
    - Cost per engagement = ₦30,000 ÷ 900 = ₦33.33 (rounded to the nearest kobo).
- Real-time feedback: a Lagos bakery posts two new cake flavours in the morning; by evening the comments show which one customers prefer, so it bakes more of that one.
- "Reaching large audiences at relatively low cost" is an **advantage**, never a risk. A firm's electricity bill or staff numbers are not social media measures.
- Hashtag hijacking example: McDonald's #McDStories (2012) invited happy stories but attracted complaints.

### Lesson 6.4: UGC, influencers, advocates and paid, owned, earned media

**Must know**

- **User-generated content (UGC):** content such as reviews, photos and videos about a brand **created by customers rather than by the brand**.
- **Social media influencer:** a person who has **built a following online** and can **affect the opinions and purchases** of that audience. Influencers are the online version of **opinion leaders** (two-step flow).
- **Micro-influencers** (compared with celebrity/mega influencers): **smaller, more specialised audiences**, often **higher engagement and trust**, and lower fees. Mega influencers have much larger audiences and much higher fees.
- **Disclosure:** an influencer must clearly label a paid partnership **so that followers know it is advertising and are not misled**.
- **Brand advocates:** loyal customers who **voluntarily recommend and defend** a brand, **without being paid**.

**Paid, owned and earned media**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid | Media space the firm **buys** | Boosted or sponsored Instagram post, Facebook ad, TV ad |
| Owned | Channels the firm **controls** | Official website, Instagram page, company blog, app |
| Earned | Exposure gained **free** through customers, fans and the press | Shares, reviews, UGC, a free newspaper feature |

**Explain more**

- Customers of a Lekki restaurant post their own photos of its jollof rice with its hashtag, unpaid: **UGC** (and earned media), not paid advertising or sales promotion.
- Choosing influencers: a small Abuja skincare brand with ₦200,000 wanting believable recommendations to women who follow skincare closely should use **several micro-influencers who focus on skincare**, not one celebrity musician or a TV commercial. Fit and trust beat raw follower numbers.
- In Nigeria advertising, including on digital platforms, is regulated by **ARCON** (Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria, formerly APCON). Honest labelling protects followers and the brand's credibility.
- A free newspaper story about a Lagos fintech app after customers praised it online is **earned** media. A firm's own blog is **owned**. A brand advocate differs from a paid influencer: the advocate is unpaid.

### Lesson 6.5: Word-of-mouth, eWOM, viral and buzz marketing

**Must know**

- **Word-of-mouth (WOM):** informal person-to-person communication among consumers about products, services and brands; customers share their experiences with peers and friends about goods and services they bought recently. It is one of the most widely practised communication tools.
- **Why WOM is credible:** it comes from people seen as **independent of the firm and not paid** to promote the product. It **builds trust, credibility and purchase intent through peer influence**.
- **Electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM):** positive or negative statements made by **potential, actual or former customers** about a product or company, made available to **many people through the internet** (tweets, online reviews, forum posts).
- **eWOM vs WOM:** eWOM reaches **many more people, faster**, and **stays available online for a long time**; it can be positive or negative.
- **Online WOM is dynamic:** its impact can be **positive or negative** depending on customers' feelings and experiences.
- **Viral marketing:** creating content so interesting that people **pass it on**, spreading it rapidly online. Content goes viral when it is **emotional, entertaining or useful** enough to share. **Limitation:** firms **cannot guarantee** it will spread or **control how people interpret** it.
- **Buzz marketing:** getting **opinion leaders and early users talking** about a product so excitement spreads to others.
- **Seeding:** giving a new product or content to selected **well-connected people first** so they share it with their networks.
- **Referral marketing:** rewarding existing customers for bringing in new ones (e.g. ₦500 per friend who signs up with a personal code). It encourages WOM.
- **Astroturfing:** creating **fake reviews or fake "ordinary customer" accounts** to fake genuine public support. It is unethical.

**Managing WOM:**

1. Deliver good products and service (the source of positive WOM).
2. Seed and use opinion leaders, influencers and advocates; run referral schemes.
3. Make sharing easy (hashtags, share buttons, review requests).
4. Listen (social listening) and engage (community management).
5. When negative posts spread, **respond quickly, honestly and politely, and address the problem openly**. Do not argue, ignore, or delete and block critics.

**Explain more**

- WOM is **not** fully controlled by the company, is **not** free of strategy, does **not** spread only face to face and is **not** less relevant online: the internet **amplified** it.
- Amaka reading 40 buyer reviews of a blender on an online store before buying: **eWOM**. A company's television commercial is **not** eWOM (it is paid advertising from the firm).
- A customer's video complaint about a bank branch in Ikeja, shared thousands of times in a day, shows the risk of **negative eWOM spreading fast**. The bank should reply publicly, apologise where due, and fix the issue.
- Buzz and seeding often work together: a phone brand gives early units to Nigerian tech reviewers (seeding) to get people talking (buzz). Hotmail's footer is the classic case of viral spread.

### One-minute recap

- Internet era = monologue to dialogue, interactivity, speed, global reach, consumer power, pull, measurability, targeting.
- Hotmail 1996 "Get your free email at Hotmail" = viral; MySpace, Facebook, Twitter (early 2000s) made marketing two-way.
- Many-to-many = chat rooms, blogs, forums.
- Social media marketing = engage audiences, build the brand, drive traffic and sales with targeted, interactive content.
- Roles: dialogue, communities, relationships, insights, visibility and loyalty. Biggest risk: loss of control.
- Reach = different people; impressions = total displays; organic = unpaid.
- Paid = bought; owned = controlled; earned = free from customers, fans, press.
- Influencers = online opinion leaders; micro = smaller, specialised, higher trust; disclose paid posts.
- WOM credible because independent and unpaid; eWOM = online, faster, wider, lasting.
- Viral = passed on; buzz = opinion leaders talking; seeding = give to connectors first; astroturfing = fake support; answer negative posts quickly, honestly, politely.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A brand pays ₦40,000 to boost a TikTok video. It reaches 8,000 people, each seeing it 3 times on average, and gets 360 likes, comments and shares. Find (a) impressions, (b) engagement rate by reach, (c) cost per 1,000 impressions. State whether the boost is paid, owned or earned media.

*Solution:*

- (a) Impressions = reach × frequency = 8,000 × 3 = 24,000.
- (b) Engagement rate = 360 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = 4.5%.
- (c) CPM = (₦40,000 ÷ 24,000) × 1,000 = ₦1,666.67 (rounded to the nearest kobo).
- The boost is **paid** media; any free shares it triggers are **earned** media.
