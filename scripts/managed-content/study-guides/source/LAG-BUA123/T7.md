## Topic 7: Communication in Market Relationships

**What you must know:** market (transactional) versus relational exchange; relationship marketing, trust and commitment, retention, loyalty, churn and customer lifetime value; the role of communication in the relationship with the market; the interactional, relational (contextual) and network approaches to communication and how each differs from the linear model.

### Lesson 7.1: Market exchange and relational exchange

**Must know**

- **Exchange process:** obtaining a desired object from someone by offering something of value in return. A completed exchange is a **transaction**.
- **5 conditions for exchange:** (1) at least two parties; (2) each has something that might be of value to the other; (3) each can communicate and deliver what it offers; (4) each is free to accept or reject the offer; (5) each party **trusts/respects** the other sufficiently to take the exchange seriously.
- **Two types of exchange most readily observed in marketing:** **market** exchange and **relational** exchange. Marketing communication plays roles in **both**.
- **Market (transactional) exchange:** a **one-off, short-term** exchange focused mainly on the **product and its price**. The relationship **ends once the goods and payment have changed hands**.
- **Relational exchange:** an **ongoing** exchange in which buyer and seller **build trust and interact repeatedly over time**.

| Feature | Market (transactional) exchange | Relational exchange |
|---|---|---|
| Time scale | Short, one-off | Long-term, ongoing |
| Main focus | Product and price | Relationship, customer value |
| Contact after sale | None expected | Regular, two-way |
| Basis | Bargain on the deal | Trust and commitment |
| Parties | Independent | Interdependent |
| Role of MarCom | Inform and persuade so a sale is completed | Build and maintain trust, commitment and dialogue |

**Characteristics of relational exchange:**

1. Long-term orientation towards keeping the customer.
2. Trust and commitment between buyer and seller.
3. Regular two-way communication and feedback over time.
4. **Interdependence:** each party relies on the other and gains from continuing the relationship.
5. Cooperation, such as joint planning and shared problem-solving.

**Explain more**

- Chidi bargains with a hawker at a Lagos bus stop, pays ₦2,500 for a used phone charger, and neither expects to meet again: a **market (transactional) exchange**.
- A cement manufacturer and an Abuja construction firm have dealt with each other for eight years, share delivery schedules, plan supply together and meet regularly to solve problems: a **relational exchange**. "Barter" and "spot purchase" are wrong here.
- Even a single transaction needs a **basic level of trust** (condition 5). It does not need a long-term written contract, the lowest price or television advertising.
- Trap: "focus on completing a single sale with no further contact" is the one statement that is **not** a characteristic of relational exchange.
- The shift: there is a strong **paradigm shift** away from reliance on one-off market exchanges towards a longer-term perspective that creates and maintains enduring partnerships. This approach is **relationship marketing**. Partnerships between brands and consumers, and between organisations within distribution channels or networks, are a vital perspective of marketing communication.
- Options that exams swap in for the two types: barter and monetary, direct and indirect, internal and external. None of these is the correct pair; the answer is **market and relational**.

### Lesson 7.2: Relationship marketing, trust and commitment

**Must know**

- **Relationship marketing:** attracting, maintaining and enhancing **long-term relationships** with customers (Berry). The idea behind it: "Marketing is not just about selling products; it is about building relationships and creating value."
- **Trust:** a customer's **confidence in a supplier's reliability and integrity** (Morgan and Hunt).
- **Commitment:** an **enduring desire to maintain a valued relationship** (Moorman, Zaltman and Deshpande).
- **Trust and commitment** are the **two variables central to the success of relationship marketing** (Morgan and Hunt's commitment-trust theory).
- **Dialogue:** communication in which both parties listen, respond and build shared understanding together.

**Transactional marketing versus relationship marketing:**

| Transactional marketing | Relationship marketing |
|---|---|
| Focus on the single sale | Focus on customer retention |
| Orientation on product features | Orientation on customer value and benefits |
| Short time scale | Long time scale |
| Little emphasis on customer service | High emphasis on customer service |
| Limited customer commitment | High customer commitment |
| Moderate customer contact | High customer contact |
| Quality is mainly a concern of production | Quality is a concern of all staff |

**Explain more**

- Trust in action: for five years a Kano wholesaler has delivered rice on the agreed date, in the agreed quantity and at the agreed price, so the retailer now orders without checking every bag. That quality is **trust** (not impulse buying, cognitive dissonance or brand switching).
- Commitment in action: Aisha has banked with the same bank for ten years. A rival offers a slightly better savings rate, but she stays because she values her account officer and the bank. That is **commitment to the relationship**, the opposite of price sensitivity, a transactional orientation or brand switching.
- Trap: trust is about **confidence** in the partner; commitment is about the **desire to stay**. Satisfaction and awareness are not the names for either.
- Trap: "high emphasis on customer service and long-term contact" is **not** typical of a transactional approach. Compared with transactional marketing, relationship marketing focuses more on **customer value and benefits over a long time scale**.
- Nigerian example: a telecom operator such as MTN or Airtel that sends each subscriber a personalised birthday message with bonus airtime, while its care team remembers past complaints, is practising **relationship marketing** (not price skimming, mass marketing or transactional marketing).

### Lesson 7.3: Retention, loyalty and customer lifetime value

**Must know**

- **Customer retention:** a firm's ability to **keep its existing customers buying from it over time**. Attracting first-time buyers is **acquisition**, not retention.
- **Why retention matters:** keeping an existing customer **usually costs less than attracting a new one** (often quoted as up to five times less).
- **Customer loyalty:** a customer's **continued preference for and repeat purchase** of a firm's brand over time.
- **Customer churn (defection):** the **rate at which customers stop doing business** with a firm over a period.
- **Customer lifetime value (CLV):** the **total value** a customer is expected to bring to a firm **over the whole relationship**.
- **Ladder of loyalty (in order):** prospect, customer, client, supporter, **advocate** (and, at the top in some versions, partner). An **advocate actively recommends** the firm to other people.

**Formulas (plain text):**

1. Churn rate (%) = customers lost during the period / customers at the start of the period x 100
2. Retention rate (%) = 100 - churn rate (when no new customers are counted)
3. CLV (revenue) = average spend per purchase x purchases per year x expected years of relationship
4. CLV (profit) = CLV (revenue) x profit margin

**Benefits of strong customer relationships:**

1. More repeat purchases.
2. Customers less likely to switch to rivals.
3. Positive word-of-mouth recommendations.
4. Lower cost of serving and keeping customers than of winning new ones.
5. Higher customer lifetime value.

**Explain more**

- Trap: "customers who buy once and never return" is **not** a benefit of strong relationships.
- A loyalty card at an Ikeja supermarket that earns points redeemable for gifts exists to **encourage repeat purchases and retain customers**.
- Trap: CLV is not the value of the first purchase, nor the price of the most expensive product.
- **Worked example (churn):** a Lagos gym starts the year with 2,000 members and 150 leave.
  - Churn rate = 150 / 2,000 x 100 = **7.5%**.
  - Retention rate = 100 - 7.5 = **92.5%**.
- **Worked example (CLV):** a customer spends ₦6,000 per visit at a restaurant, visits 4 times a month and is expected to stay 5 years; profit margin is 20%.
  - Visits per year = 4 x 12 = 48.
  - Spend per year = ₦6,000 x 48 = ₦288,000.
  - CLV (revenue) = ₦288,000 x 5 = **₦1,440,000**.
  - CLV (profit) = ₦1,440,000 x 0.20 = **₦288,000**.
- The lesson of CLV: a ₦6,000 customer is really worth ₦1,440,000 in sales over the relationship, which is why firms spend on communication that keeps people.

### Lesson 7.4: The role of communication in the relationship with the market

**Must know**

- **Role of communication in market relationships:** to **build trust, loyalty and lasting customer commitment**.
- **Fundamental aim:** to **increase customer trust, satisfaction and retention**.
- **Strategic aim:** to **strengthen customer loyalty through consistent delivery of value**.

**Roles of communication in a market relationship:**

1. Builds and maintains **trust** by keeping promises visible and consistent.
2. Strengthens **commitment** and loyalty.
3. Opens **dialogue**: listens to customers and responds to feedback and complaints.
4. Keeps customers **informed** after the sale (updates, service, new offers).
5. **Coordinates** with partners in the network (suppliers, distributors, agencies).
6. Turns satisfied customers into **advocates** who spread positive word-of-mouth.

**Explain more**

- Every wrong option in this area describes the opposite of relationship building: completing a single sale with no further contact, minimising contact to save resources, avoiding customer engagement, restricting product availability to create scarcity, or chasing new customers while ignoring existing ones.
- In a **transactional** exchange the main job of communication is to **inform and persuade** buyers about the offer so a sale is completed. In a **relational** exchange its job is to **build and maintain trust, commitment and dialogue**.
- Example: GTBank and other Nigerian banks send transaction alerts, account statements and service updates. These messages keep the customer informed and confident, so they support retention rather than one sale.

### Lesson 7.5: Interactional and relational (contextual) approaches

**Must know**

- **Linear model (for contrast):** a one-way flow of messages from a sender to a **passive** receiver who does not reply (Shannon and Weaver). The **hypodermic needle** view is a similar one-way idea.
- **Interactional approach:** views communication as a **two-way process** in which participants send messages, respond and give **feedback** to each other. It focuses on **two-way dialogue** between businesses and customers.
  - Most central element: **feedback and response** between the parties.
  - Differs from the linear model because it **includes feedback and response, so communication flows both ways**.
  - It ensures **active engagement with customers and responsiveness to their needs**.
- **Relational (contextual) approach:** holds that the **meaning of a message depends on the relationship between the participants and the context** of the message.
  - It is mainly concerned with communication that **develops and sustains the relationship** between the parties.
  - It is centred on **building lasting, value-based relationships** with customers, enabling firms to **build trust and develop long-term partnerships**.
  - It treats **sustained, long-term customer relationships** as the basis of competitive advantage.

| Approach | Main focus |
|---|---|
| Linear | One-way flow from sender to passive receiver |
| Interactional | Two-way message exchange with feedback |
| Relational (contextual) | Meaning shaped by the relationship and context |
| Network | Spread of messages across many connected people and organisations |

**Explain more**

- Interactional example: a customer complains on social media that her data was wrongly deducted. The telecom firm replies in minutes, asks for details, she responds, the firm fixes the problem and checks back. Send, respond, feedback, follow-up: the **interactional approach**. It is not the network approach because only two parties are exchanging messages.
- Relational example: two suppliers send an Ibadan food processor the same message, "Your order will arrive tomorrow." The processor believes the supplier it has used reliably for years but doubts the new one. Same words, different meaning, because of the **relationship and context**: the **relational (contextual) approach**.
- Traps: the interactional approach does not suppress feedback, keep messages one-way or stop customers replying. The relational approach does not say meaning is fixed and identical for every receiver, that only channel quality decides meaning, or that communication works best with no previous relationship.
- Matching trap: interactional = **two-way exchange**; relational = **meaning shaped by the relationship**. Options that swap these are wrong.
- **Dialogue** is the opposite of monologue, broadcasting and propaganda, where one party simply talks at the other.

### Lesson 7.6: The network approach to communication

**Must know**

- **Network approach:** views communication as **flows of information through a web of interconnected people and organisations**. It emphasises **connected exchanges among many stakeholders**, including partners and suppliers.
- **Valuable for:** managing relationships across a **web of interdependent stakeholders**.
- **Supports firms by:** enabling **effective collaboration across different stakeholder groups**.
- **Well-connected members** (hubs, opinion leaders) matter because they **speed up and widen the spread** of messages through the network.
- The network approach best explains how **word-of-mouth and viral messages** spread among consumers.
- **Shared feature of the three approaches:** interactional, relational and network approaches all treat communication as **more than a one-way transmission** from sender to receiver.

**Stakeholders in a firm's communication network:**

1. Customers and consumers.
2. Suppliers.
3. Distributors and retailers.
4. Advertising and media agencies.
5. Regulators (for example ARCON for advertising, NCC for telecoms).
6. Employees, the media and the wider community.

**Explain more**

- Viral example: a few UNILAG students share a new fintech app in class WhatsApp groups, members forward it to other groups, and within days thousands have heard. That pattern is the **network approach**, not personal selling or the linear model.
- Stakeholder example: a Lagos beverage company coordinates communication with suppliers, distributors, retailers, agencies and regulators, recognising that information passes among all of them: the **network approach**.
- Traps: the network approach is not communication between one seller and one buyer only, not one-way mass messages, not limited to one department, and it does not remove the need to talk to suppliers.
- Well-connected people do not block messages or only receive them; they pass them on to many others.
- Link to Topic 6: word-of-mouth, eWOM and buzz all travel through networks, which is why firms court influencers who sit at the busy centre of a network.

### One-minute recap

- Two observable types of exchange: **market** (one-off, product and price, ends at payment) and **relational** (ongoing, trust, repeated contact, interdependence).
- **Relationship marketing** = attracting, maintaining and enhancing long-term customer relationships; it is about building relationships and creating value.
- **Trust** = confidence in reliability and integrity; **commitment** = enduring desire to maintain a valued relationship; both are central.
- Retention keeps existing customers and costs less than acquisition; churn is the rate of defection; loyalty is continued preference and repeat buying; an **advocate** recommends the firm.
- CLV = spend per purchase x purchases per year x years (x margin for profit).
- Communication's role: build trust, loyalty and commitment; increase satisfaction and retention.
- **Interactional** = two-way dialogue with feedback; **relational (contextual)** = meaning depends on relationship and context; **network** = flows through a web of many stakeholders; all go beyond one-way transmission.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Bukola buys bread worth ₦3,500 from a neighbourhood bakery twice a week, every week of the year, and is expected to stay a customer for 4 years. The bakery's profit margin is 25%. Its customer base fell from 16,000 to 15,200 over the year with no new customers counted. Calculate (a) Bukola's CLV in revenue and profit terms and (b) the bakery's churn and retention rates.

*Solution:*

- (a) Purchases per year = 2 x 52 = 104. Spend per year = ₦3,500 x 104 = ₦364,000. CLV (revenue) = ₦364,000 x 4 = **₦1,456,000**. CLV (profit) = ₦1,456,000 x 0.25 = **₦364,000**.
- (b) Customers lost = 16,000 - 15,200 = 800. Churn rate = 800 / 16,000 x 100 = **5%**. Retention rate = 100 - 5 = **95%**.
- Interpretation: losing Bukola would cost the bakery ₦364,000 of profit, so communication that keeps her (friendly dialogue, a loyalty card, remembering her order) is worth far more than one sale.
