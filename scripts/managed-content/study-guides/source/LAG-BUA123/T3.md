## Topic 3: MarCom Context, Mix and Selection Criteria

**What you must know:** the four contexts of marketing communication; the tools and media of the marketing communications mix and what each does; strengths and weaknesses of each tool and the four comparison criteria; the four groups of factors for selecting the mix, plus push, pull and profile strategies, product life cycle and buyer readiness; market segmentation as it applies to communication.

### Lesson 3.1: The marketing communication context

**Must know**

- **Marketing communication context:** the circumstances, inside and outside the organisation, in which communication takes place and is interpreted. It is not just the words and pictures in an advert, the size of the budget or the price.
- Context is analysed **before** a campaign is planned, so that tools, messages and media fit the situation.
- **The 4 contexts:**

1. **Customer context:** the needs, perceptions, attitudes and decision-making process of the target segment; who the customers are, how they buy and how they use media.
2. **Business context:** the organisation's corporate and marketing strategy, its brand and market position, and its competitors and market trends in the industry.
3. **Internal context:** what is inside the organisation: budget and other resources, staff skills, culture and values, internal policies and procedures.
4. **External context:** forces outside the organisation's control: political, economic, social and technological (**PEST**) factors and the wider stakeholders (government, media, community, investors).

**Explain more**

- **Internal vs external trap:** "company policies and procedures", "organisation culture and values" and "staff appraisals" are **internal**. "Competitive positioning and market trends" are **outside** the organisation: in a two-way internal/external question, they are the **external** factor. In the four-context framework they sit under the business context, while PEST and wider stakeholders are the external context.
- Example: a Kano food company finds it has a small promotion budget, few staff skilled in social media and a cautious culture. All three findings concern the firm itself, so they are the **internal context**.
- Example: GTBank studying how young customers choose banking apps is analysing the **customer context**; studying new regulations or rising data costs is the **external context**.

### Lesson 3.2: The marketing communications mix: tools and media

**Must know**

- **Marketing communications mix:** a set of tools (disciplines) that can be used in various combinations and different degrees of intensity in order to communicate with a target audience. Its purpose is to reach and persuade the intended audience effectively across channels. The whole process of marketing is executed through it.
- **The 5 traditional (main) tools:**

1. Advertising
2. Sales promotion
3. Personal selling
4. Public relations (with publicity)
5. Direct marketing

- **Media:** the other part of the mix: the channels in which **time and space can be bought or used** to deliver messages to target audiences (TV, radio, press, outdoor, internet).
- Price, product design, distribution and inventory are **not** communication tools. Do not confuse the communications mix (tools) with the marketing mix (4 Ps: product, price, place, promotion). Promotion is the one P that the communications mix fills out.

**Definitions of each tool**

- **Advertising:** an **indirect, paid** method used by firms to inform customers about goods and services via television, radio, print media and online websites. One of the most widely used tools. Main job: build brand recognition and reinforce market presence through paid media.
- **Sales promotion:** aims to stimulate sales (business revenue) through **incentives, short-term discounts and other promotional offers**, boosting sales and strengthening customer satisfaction. Forms: **coupons, rebates, paybacks, buy-one-get-one-free**, price-offs, free samples, contests. Role: trigger **immediate** purchase.
- **Public relations (PR):** focuses on the firm's **reputation** through **unpaid or earned** communication (traditional media, social media, in-person engagement). Activities: **press releases, communication campaigns, crisis management**, building positive relationships with media figures and other stakeholders. Role: enhance reputation and credibility through earned media.
- **Publicity:** promotes to the mass market like advertising **except that it is free**. It appears in the news media's **editorial section** and is about **newsworthy** events. Most common forms: **news (press) releases, photos and feature stories**.
- **Personal selling:** the traditional method in which salespeople approach prospects directly and inform them about goods and services; **face-to-face** selling in which a salesperson uses skills and techniques to build personal relationships. Regarded as one of the **most reliable** modes because it is done **directly**, orally face to face or in writing (emails, text messages).
- **Direct marketing:** a **more personalised** form of communication through direct communication or distribution to **individuals**, rather than generalised messages on mass media. Examples: emails, brochures, interactive consumer websites, telemarketing, catalogues, direct mail.

**Other tools in the wider mix**

- **Events and experiences (sponsorship):** companies sponsor sports, entertainment, non-profit or community events to **reinforce their brand** in customers' minds and create a **long-term association** for brand exposure.
- **Interactive marketing:** customers **interact with the firm online** and get queries resolved online; the firm can show each customer what they chose or ordered in the recent past (Amazon is the classic example). This is the digital/interactive tool.
- **Word-of-mouth marketing:** customers **share their experiences** of goods and services they recently bought with peers and friends. One of the most widely practised methods.
- **Packaging:** the pack itself communicates: brand name, colours, information, claims; often the last message seen before purchase.
- **Point of purchase (POP):** displays, shelf strips, posters and stands in the shop where the buying decision is made.

**Explain more**

- **Advertising vs publicity:** both reach the mass market, but advertising is **paid** and the firm controls the message; publicity is **not paid for** and appears as **news** chosen by editors. Exams swap these constantly.
- **PR vs publicity:** PR is the broader management of reputation and relationships with all stakeholders; publicity is one output of PR (the free news coverage).
- **Direct marketing vs advertising:** direct marketing speaks to **selected individuals** in a personalised way; advertising speaks to the general public.
- **Scenario practice:**
    - A Lagos water company, after an online rumour of contamination, holds a press conference, issues a press release and invites journalists on a factory tour: **public relations** (crisis management).
    - A Surulere supermarket offers "buy two packs of noodles, get a third free, this weekend only": **sales promotion** (short-term incentive).
    - An Ikeja furniture company mails brochures, each addressed by name with a reply form, to 2,000 customers on its database: **direct marketing**.
    - A personalised email offer to customers on a mailing list: **direct marketing**.
    - A telecom company such as MTN or Glo pays to put its name, logo, jerseys and banners on a university inter-faculty football competition for the season: **events and experiences (sponsorship)**.
    - Jumia answering customer questions online and showing each buyer their recent orders: **interactive marketing**.
- A press release is **not** sales promotion; a 30-second TV commercial is **advertising**, not PR.

### Lesson 3.3: Strengths, weaknesses and the comparison criteria

**Must know**

- Tools are compared on **4 criteria:** **communication effectiveness, credibility, cost and control**. (Not SWOT, not the 4 Ps, not AIDA.)
- **Absolute cost:** the total bill paid. **Relative cost (cost per contact):** total cost divided by the number of people reached.
- Formulas:
    - Cost per contact = total cost ÷ number of people reached
    - Cost per thousand (CPM) = (total cost ÷ audience reached) × 1,000

| Tool | Main strengths | Main weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising | Reaches a large, geographically dispersed audience at a **low cost per contact**; firm controls message, timing and media; repeats message; builds image | **High absolute cost** (TV); largely **one-way and impersonal**, cannot adapt to each person's questions; low credibility (seen as biased); clutter |
| Sales promotion | Quick, measurable sales boost; encourages trial and brand switching; attracts attention | Effects mainly **short-term**; buyers may **wait for deals**; can cheapen the brand; little help in building loyalty |
| Public relations and publicity | **Highest credibility** (third party, such as the news media, delivers it); low cost; reaches people who avoid adverts | **Least control**: editors decide whether, when and how the story appears; no guarantee of coverage or immediate sales |
| Personal selling | **Two-way**, immediate feedback; tailored to each customer's needs; can answer objections and close the sale; builds relationships | **Highest cost per contact**; reaches relatively **few** people; depends on salesperson quality |
| Direct marketing | Personalised and targeted; responses easily **measured**; private from competitors | Seen as intrusive **"junk"** mail, calls or emails; needs a good database; can be costly per contact |
| Sponsorship | Long-term brand association; reaches fans emotionally | Hard to measure; little control over the event's image |

**Explain more**

- Quick-fire rules exams use:
    - Most **credible**: public relations (publicity).
    - **Least control**: public relations (publicity).
    - **Highest cost per contact**: personal selling.
    - Greatest **two-way interaction** and immediate feedback: personal selling.
    - **Low cost per contact, wide reach**: advertising.
    - **Short-term sales boost**: sales promotion.
- **Worked example: high absolute cost, low cost per contact.** A prime-time TV spot package costs ₦3,000,000 and reaches 6,000,000 viewers.
    - Cost per contact = ₦3,000,000 ÷ 6,000,000 = ₦0.50 per viewer.
    - CPM = ₦0.50 × 1,000 = ₦500 per thousand viewers.
    - The total bill (₦3m) is large, but spread across a huge audience the cost per person is tiny. That is what "high absolute cost, low cost per contact" means.
- **Worked example: personal selling.** A sales representative costs the firm ₦600,000 a month (salary, transport, allowances) and makes 120 calls.
    - Cost per contact = ₦600,000 ÷ 120 = ₦5,000 per call.
    - Each sales call costs ₦5,000 ÷ ₦0.50 = 10,000 times as much as one TV contact. In return, each call is two-way, tailored and can close a sale.
- **Worked example: direct marketing cost per response.** A firm mails 2,000 brochures at ₦750 each and 3% reply.
    - Total cost = 2,000 × ₦750 = ₦1,500,000.
    - Replies = 3% × 2,000 = 60.
    - Cost per response = ₦1,500,000 ÷ 60 = ₦25,000. Being able to count replies like this is why direct marketing is called measurable.
- **Small budget, wide coverage:** a new Yaba fashion start-up with little money gains most cheaply by sending a **news release about an interesting launch event** to local media (publicity). National TV, league sponsorship or a large sales force are far too costly.
- Trap: advertising does **not** give the firm "no control" (it gives high control) and does **not** have a high cost per person reached.

### Lesson 3.4: Selecting the mix: factors, strategies, PLC and buyer readiness

**Must know**

- **Selection criteria** are used to **identify and prioritise the tools that will be most effective** for the audience and the objectives. They do **not** mean equal budget shares, copying competitors, or dropping objectives.
- Factors influencing the choice of mix (and how often each tool is used) fall into **4 groups:**

1. **Product related factors:**
    - the amount and complexity of product information to be communicated
    - the stage of the product in the product life cycle
    - product type and unit price
2. **Customer related factors:**
    - characteristics of the target market
    - type of buying decision
3. **Organisation related factors:**
    - marketing channel adopted
    - branding strategies
    - budget
4. **Competition and situation related factors:**
    - visibility of the firm and its political, legal and social environment
    - impact of competition

- **Push strategy:** communication (personal selling, trade promotions, extra margins, display allowances) is directed at **channel members** so that they stock and promote the product to end users.
- **Pull strategy:** communication (advertising, consumer promotions) is directed at **final consumers** so that they ask retailers for the product, and retailers then order from the manufacturer.
- **Profile strategy:** communication aimed at a **wide range of stakeholders** (investors, government, local community, media) to build **corporate reputation**, not to move products through the channel.

| PLC stage | Communication emphasis |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Advertising and publicity to build **awareness**; sales promotion (samples, coupons) to encourage **trial**; personal selling to win distributors |
| Growth | Advertising and publicity continue to build preference; word of mouth spreads; less need for promotion incentives |
| Maturity | **Sales promotion** becomes relatively more important; reminder and persuasive advertising against intense competition |
| Decline | Promotion **reduced to a low level**; some **reminder advertising** kept; publicity and selling cut back |

**Buyer readiness stages:**

| Stage | Most cost-effective tools |
|---|---|
| Awareness and knowledge | Advertising and public relations (publicity) |
| Liking, preference and conviction | Personal selling (advertising helps less) |
| Purchase (ordering, closing) | Personal selling and sales promotion |

**Explain more**

- **Classification traps:** budget is an **organisation** factor, not product. Branding strategy is an **organisation** factor, so it is **not** a product-related factor. PLC stage is a **product** factor. Type of buying decision is a **customer** factor. Impact of competition is a **competition and situation** factor, not product.
- **Product type and price:**
    - Expensive, technically complex products sold to a few buyers (medical scanners for hospitals, industrial machines, Dangote Cement supply contracts with builders): rely most on **personal selling**, because the information is complex and the buyer needs explanation and negotiation.
    - Low-priced, frequently bought consumer goods sold to millions (soft drinks, Indomie noodles, detergents): lean most on **advertising and sales promotion**, because reach and low cost per contact matter.
- **Push example:** a new seasoning brand gives Idumota wholesalers extra margins, display allowances and regular visits from sales reps so they stock it and recommend it to retailers: **push**.
- **Pull example:** a manufacturer runs heavy TV adverts and consumer coupons so consumers ask retailers for the brand, and retailers then order it: **pull**.
- **Profile example:** an oil company's annual report, community-development announcements and investor briefings: **profile**.
- Many firms combine push and pull: national adverts (pull) plus distributor incentives (push).
- Other selection criteria to keep in mind: cost, control, credibility, reach, target audience, budget and objectives. A firm with an awareness objective and a mass audience picks advertising; one with a closing objective and a few big buyers picks personal selling.

### Lesson 3.5: Market segmentation and communication

**Must know**

- **Market segmentation:** the grouping of consumers who are homogeneous in some way and then differentiating the products or brands to meet the groups' needs. Also: identifying specific market segments in a marketplace, then developing different marketing offerings that will be attractive to each segment. Equally: dividing the market of potential customers into groups by shared characteristics so a custom marketing mix can be built for each.
- Why group at all: every consumer is unique, and tailoring products to each individual is very expensive.
- The concept was introduced by **Wendell R. Smith** (1956).
- **5 reasons for segmentation:**

1. It is easier to meet the needs of a group than the needs of every individual in a whole market.
2. Meeting a group's needs better brings greater **loyalty, increased trial and higher perceived quality**.
3. It gives the marketer a **target advertising group**, which makes advertising more **cost-effective**.
4. Identifying and covering the main segment can help **keep competition out** of the market.
5. An identified unmet need can give **entry into the market** if its segment is large and profitable.

- **3 bases (consumer profile):**

1. **Demographics:** age, income, marital status, and geography or location. This gives a focus for producing the product and communicating with consumers.
2. **Physical needs:** e.g. oily or sensitive skin needing a particular cream; taste needs such as salty or sweet. Focus falls on product performance and delivery.
3. **Lifestyle:** found through consumer immersion, e.g. busy career woman, diet consciousness, caring parenting. Focus falls on product performance and delivery.

- **3 effects of segmentation:**

1. **On consumers:** an easy, clear choice of product; consumers identify their class and buy in line with it.
2. **On brands:** brands are produced for different classes, with variants such as "mild", "strong", "smooth" or "extra".
3. **On the marketer:** a focus for planning marketing activities and communication or advertising; the marketer knows the target and directs all strategies at it.

- **4 common types:** geographic, demographic (most common), behavioural, psychographic.
- **A good segment** is internally **homogeneous** and externally **heterogeneous**: members respond alike, and differ from other segments.

**Explain more**

- **Wider bases:** gender, age, income (high, middle, low), place, occupation, usage (heavy, medium, light), lifestyle.
- **Geographic:** by region; bottled water sells strongly where clean water is scarce. **Demographic:** age, gender, marital status, family size, income, religion, occupation, nationality. **Behavioural:** knowledge and use of the product (non-users, ex-users, potential, first-time, current users), loyalty (brand loyal, brand-neutral, competitor loyal), usage rate. **Psychographic:** personality, lifestyle and attitude.
- **Why segmentation matters for communication:**
    - It defines the **target audience**, the first step in any communication plan.
    - It shapes the **message**: a youth data plan from MTN speaks in campus slang; a premium banking product speaks of security and status.
    - It guides **media and tool choice**: students are reached on Instagram, TikTok and campus events; older rural farmers through radio in local languages and market-day activations.
    - It **cuts waste**: money is spent only on people likely to respond, raising conversions. That is the "cost-effective advertising" reason.

### One-minute recap

- Context = circumstances inside and outside the firm; 4 contexts: customer, business, internal, external (PEST and stakeholders).
- The mix = tools used in combinations and intensities; 5 traditional tools: advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, PR, direct marketing; plus media (bought or used time and space).
- Advertising = indirect, paid. Publicity = free, editorial, newsworthy. PR = reputation, earned, crisis management. Sales promotion = short-term incentives. Personal selling = face-to-face, most reliable. Direct marketing = personalised to individuals.
- Extra tools: events and experiences (sponsorship), interactive marketing, word of mouth, packaging, point of purchase.
- Compare tools on effectiveness, credibility, cost and control. PR is most credible but least controlled; personal selling costs most per contact and gives most feedback.
- 4 factor groups: product, customer, organisation, competition and situation. Budget, channel and branding = organisation.
- Push = at channel members; pull = at consumers; profile = at stakeholders for reputation.
- Introduction = awareness plus trial; maturity = sales promotion; decline = reminder only.
- Segmentation = grouping homogeneous consumers; 5 reasons, 3 bases (demographics, physical needs, lifestyle), 3 effects (consumers, brands, marketer).

### Theory practice

**Question:** A drinks firm compares two options. A radio campaign costs ₦2,400,000 and reaches 1,600,000 listeners. A sales team costs ₦1,800,000 and makes 300 calls on supermarket buyers. Calculate the cost per contact of each, the radio CPM, and state which tool suits building awareness and which suits closing orders.

*Solution:*

- Radio cost per contact = ₦2,400,000 ÷ 1,600,000 = ₦1.50 per listener.
- Radio CPM = ₦1.50 × 1,000 = ₦1,500 per thousand listeners.
- Sales team cost per contact = ₦1,800,000 ÷ 300 = ₦6,000 per call.
- Each sales call costs ₦6,000 ÷ ₦1.50 = 4,000 times a radio contact.
- Radio (advertising) suits **awareness**: wide reach at low cost per contact. The sales team (personal selling) suits **closing orders** with supermarket buyers: two-way, tailored, able to negotiate. Selling to supermarkets so they stock the drink is also a **push** approach.
