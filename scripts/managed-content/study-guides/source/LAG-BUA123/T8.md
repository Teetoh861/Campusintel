## Topic 8: Online and Offline Media and Digital Marketing

**What you must know:** what offline and online media are, with examples and the merits and demerits of each; paid, owned and earned media; what digital marketing and digital marketing communication mean; how to craft a digital marketing strategy; the types of digital marketing (SEO, SEM/PPC, content, social media, email, affiliate, influencer, mobile, video, display, viral); the key digital metrics; the importance and challenges of digital marketing.

### Lesson 8.1: Offline and online communication media

**Must know**

- **Communication medium:** the channel through which a marketing message is carried to the target audience.
- **Offline (traditional) media:** established channels that do not need the internet. They are best exemplified by **television, radio and print advertising**.
- **Online (digital) media:** channels delivered through the internet and digital devices. They are best exemplified by **digital platforms such as social media, websites and apps**.

| Offline (traditional) media | Online (digital) media |
|---|---|
| Television | Search engines (organic results and paid search ads) |
| Radio | Display advertising (banners on websites and apps) |
| Newspapers | Social media (Instagram, Facebook, X, TikTok, YouTube) |
| Magazines | Email marketing and newsletters |
| Outdoor (billboards, posters, transit ads on buses) | Mobile (SMS, apps, mobile sites) |
| Cinema | Company websites and blogs |
| Direct mail, flyers, brochures | Online video and podcasts |

- **How the media evolved:** printing press (Gutenberg, 15th century) gave birth to print advertising; the first printed adverts appeared in **17th-century newspapers**; **radio commercials began in the 1920s**; by the **1950s television** became the dominant platform, combining visuals and sound; the **internet (late 20th century)** brought websites as virtual storefronts and email as a direct line to customers; the **early 2000s** brought social media (MySpace, Facebook, Twitter), turning marketing into a two-way conversation.

**Explain more**

- **Group test trap:** exam options mix the two lists. "Television, radio, newspapers and billboards" is entirely offline. "Websites, search engines, social media and email" is entirely online. Any option with even one item from the other side (for example "radio, social media, magazines and websites") is wrong.
- **Odd-one-out:** a sponsored search engine advert is **not** offline; a painted advert on the side of a **danfo bus** is **not** online (it is outdoor, transit advertising). A banner on a news website and an email newsletter are online.
- Streaming services and podcasts are online, so they never describe offline media.
- One of the earliest successful pieces of digital marketing was **Hotmail's viral strategy in 1996**: it added "Get your free email at Hotmail" to every email sent and gained millions of users within months.

### Lesson 8.2: Merits and demerits of offline and online media

**Must know**

| Medium | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| Television | Combines **sight, sound and motion**; reaches a **large mass audience**; strong for storytelling and emotion | High absolute cost; short exposure; clutter; viewers can switch channel |
| Radio | **Low cost**, **locally targeted**, reaches people in local languages, rural and mobile listeners | **Audio only**; fleeting message; low attention |
| Newspapers | **Timeliness**, **local coverage**, broad acceptance, flexible | **Short life**; poor reproduction quality; many ads compete on a page |
| Magazines | **Long life**, **high-quality colour reproduction**, **selective readership** with specific interests, pass-along readers | Long lead time; higher cost; less timely |
| Outdoor (billboards, posters, transit) | **Repeated exposure** to passers-by; low cost per exposure; good location selection | Carries only **brief messages**; cannot target individuals; creative limits |
| Cinema | Captive audience; big screen, sound and motion | Limited reach; expensive production |
| Online media (overall) | **Interactive (two-way)**; **precise targeting**; **measurable** (clicks, views tracked quickly, real-time); data-driven; relatively low cost; global reach; messages can be changed fast | Does not reach people **without internet**; audiences can **block or skip** ads; heavy **clutter**; privacy concerns; negative posts spread fast |

- **Offline media remain relevant** because they reach audiences, including local ones, through established traditional channels and strengthen brand presence through **tangible, traditional touchpoints**.
- **Online media stand out** for **precise, interactive and data-driven** interaction with audiences.
- **Integration:** online and offline media can be **combined so that each supports the other** in an integrated campaign.

**Explain more**

- **Choosing for the audience:** an agro-chemical firm reaching farmers in remote Benue villages, with poor internet but wide listening to local-language programmes, should use **local radio**. Email, Instagram influencers and search ads all need the internet.
- **Swap traps:** "Long life, colour, selective readership" is **magazines**, not newspapers. "Timeliness and local coverage but short life" is **newspapers**. "Repeated exposure but brief messages" is **outdoor**. "Low cost, local and audio only" is **radio**. "Sight, sound and motion to a mass audience" is **television**.
- **NOT a limitation of online media:** "results can be tracked in detail". That is a strength.
- Online media do not stop people blocking or skipping ads, do not always cost more than national TV, and cannot reach people with no internet.
- **Integration example:** a Lagos fashion brand puts a **QR code** on billboards on Third Mainland Bridge which takes viewers straight to its online store. The billboard (offline) drives traffic to the store (online): this is **integrating offline and online media**. Online media have not fully replaced offline media in every market, and both channels may carry the same core message (IMC).

### Lesson 8.3: Digital marketing, digital marketing communication and paid, owned, earned media

**Must know**

- **Digital marketing:** the use of the **internet and digital channels** (websites, search engines, social media, email and mobile devices) to **promote products and engage customers** (build customer relationships).
- **Digital marketing communication:** marketing messages delivered through digital channels, for example **targeted email campaigns and social media advertising**. It is distinguished by **interactivity, measurability and adaptability** to audience needs, and it lets businesses **monitor and analyse campaign performance with real-time metrics**.
- **The 3 media types (POE):**

1. **Paid media:** advertising space the firm **buys** on other people's websites and platforms (search ads, display banners, sponsored social posts, influencer fees).
2. **Owned media:** channels the firm **controls itself**: its website, blog, app, email list, official social media pages.
3. **Earned media:** exposure the brand receives because of **what others say** about it: customer reviews, social shares, mentions and free press coverage.

- **Key metrics (formulas):**
    - Click-through rate (CTR) = clicks / impressions x 100
    - Conversion rate = conversions / clicks (or visitors) x 100
    - Cost per click (CPC) = total spend / clicks
    - Cost per conversion = total spend / conversions
    - Cost per thousand impressions (CPM) = total spend / impressions x 1,000
- **Impression:** one showing of an ad. **CTR:** the proportion of people who saw an ad and then clicked it. **Conversion:** a visitor completes a **desired action**, such as making a purchase or signing up.

**Explain more**

- Traditional TV, radio, print and billboards are not digital marketing communication. Digital communication is not one-way, fixed or hard to measure.
- **POE examples:** Jumia's own website and its official Instagram page are **owned**; Jumia buying banner space on a news site is **paid**; a shopper's glowing review shared widely is **earned**. Billboards rented from an outdoor firm are bought space, but they are offline, so they never answer "owned media".
- Earned media links to PR and word of mouth: it is unpaid and the firm does not control it.
- **Worked example:** a Lagos bakery's ad is shown 40,000 times, gets 800 clicks at ₦40 per click, and 24 people order.
    - CTR = 800 / 40,000 x 100 = **2%**
    - Spend = 800 x ₦40 = **₦32,000**
    - Conversion rate = 24 / 800 x 100 = **3%**
    - Cost per conversion = ₦32,000 / 24 = ₦1,333.33 (rounded to 2 decimal places)
- A visitor who leaves without doing anything is not a conversion; changing a logo is not a conversion.

### Lesson 8.4: Crafting digital marketing

**Must know**

- **Crafting digital marketing:** developing **strategic content suited to how audiences behave online**. It requires **deep insight into online consumer behaviour and how each platform works**.
- **Steps in crafting a digital marketing strategy:**

1. **Set clear objectives** (SMART: awareness, traffic, leads, sales).
2. **Define the target audience** and build **buyer personas**.
3. **Choose the right digital channels and content** for that audience.
4. **Create and publish** the content and campaigns (with a budget and schedule).
5. **Measure with analytics and improve** the campaign based on the results.

- **Buyer persona:** a **detailed profile representing a typical member of the target audience** (age, location, job, interests, online habits, problems).
- **Analytics after launch:** used to **measure performance and improve** the campaign.
- **Mobile-friendly (responsive) website:** essential because **many users access the internet mainly through their phones**.
- **Inbound vs outbound:**

| Inbound marketing | Outbound marketing |
|---|---|
| **Attracts** customers to the firm through useful content, search and social media | **Pushes** messages at people through cold calls and interruptive adverts |
| Customer finds the firm | Firm interrupts the customer |

**Explain more**

- **NOT a step:** "skipping measurement because digital results cannot be tracked". Digital results can be tracked, so measurement is always a step.
- Bad crafting: copying a print advert unchanged onto every website, using exactly the same message on every platform, ignoring which platforms the audience uses, focusing only on the cheapest channel, suppressing feedback, or avoiding measurement. Each platform has its own format: short videos on TikTok, professional posts on LinkedIn, pictures on Instagram.
- **Persona example:** "Tola, 20, UNILAG student, lives in Akoka, spends most of her data on TikTok and Instagram, buys affordable skincare, pays by transfer." A brand plans content for Tola. A persona is not a product list, website owner or password.
- In Nigeria, most people go online by phone, so a site that does not work well on a mobile phone loses customers.

### Lesson 8.5: Types of digital marketing

**Must know**

1. **Search engine optimisation (SEO):** improving a website so it **ranks higher in unpaid (organic) search results**.
2. **Search engine marketing (SEM) / pay-per-click (PPC):** **paid search ads**; in PPC the advertiser **pays a fee each time someone clicks** its advert. Gives **immediate website traffic**.
3. **Content marketing:** creating and sharing **free, useful content** (blogs, videos, guides) to attract an audience without pushing a direct sale each time.
4. **Social media marketing:** using social platforms to **share content, engage users and build brand communities**.
5. **Email marketing:** sending commercial messages such as **offers and newsletters** to people on an email list.
6. **Affiliate marketing:** paying partners **commission for each sale or lead** they bring through **referral links**.
7. **Influencer marketing:** partnering with individuals who have a **large, engaged online following** to promote a brand.
8. **Mobile marketing:** reaching consumers on their **phones** through **SMS, apps, mobile sites and location-based offers**.
9. **Video marketing:** using online video (YouTube, TikTok, Reels) to promote and explain products.
10. **Display advertising:** **visual adverts such as banners** shown on websites and apps; includes **retargeting (remarketing)**, which shows ads to people who visited a site but did not buy.
11. **Viral marketing:** designing a message so that **people pass it on to others**, spreading it rapidly like a virus.

- **Keywords:** the words and phrases people **type into a search engine** to find something.
- **Permission-based (opt-in) email:** send only to people who have **agreed (opted in)** to receive messages. **Spam:** unsolicited bulk commercial email sent to people who did not ask for it.

**Explain more**

- **SEO vs paid search:** SEO targets **unpaid organic listings**; paid search **buys advertising positions**. A Port Harcourt hotel paying to appear at the top for "hotels in Port Harcourt" and charged only per click is **PPC search advertising**, not SEO.
- **Affiliate vs influencer:** affiliate marketing pays partners **mainly according to sales or leads actually generated**. A Nigerian e-commerce site giving bloggers a unique link and paying 5% commission only on purchases through that link is **affiliate**. Influencer marketing often pays for exposure (a flat fee) whether or not sales follow: a drinks company paying a popular Nigerian comedy skit-maker with millions of Instagram followers to feature its drink is **influencer marketing**.
- **Content:** a Lagos skincare brand posting free weekly videos and blogs on caring for skin in hot, humid weather, without a hard sell, is **content marketing**.
- **Email:** an online bookshop's monthly email of new arrivals and discount codes to customers who signed up is **email marketing**. Good practice: never hide the unsubscribe option, never buy lists of strangers' addresses, never flood inboxes.
- **Mobile:** an operator such as MTN or Airtel texting subscribers discounted data bundles bought by dialling a short code is **mobile (SMS) marketing**.
- **Retargeting:** a shopper views sneakers on a Nigerian online store, does not buy, and ads for the same sneakers follow her on other websites.
- **Viral:** the Hotmail signature line is the classic case. Text messages to phones are mobile, not display; supermarket shelf arrangement is merchandising, not display advertising.

### Lesson 8.6: Importance and challenges of digital marketing

**Must know**

- **Importance (advantages) of digital marketing:**

1. Reaches **large and even global audiences efficiently and at relatively low cost**.
2. **Precise targeting** of specific audiences, so even **small businesses** can compete on a small budget.
3. **Measurable in real time** (CTR, conversions, cost per sale).
4. **Instant customer engagement** and quick responses (two-way).
5. **Personalisation**, which improves customer experiences and engagement.
6. **Flexibility:** campaigns can be changed quickly while running.
7. Builds brand awareness and relationships 24 hours a day.

- **Challenges (limitations):**

1. **Data privacy and security** concerns.
2. **Negative messages spread very quickly and widely** online.
3. **Rapid change** in technology, platforms and search or social **algorithms**, so marketers must keep learning.
4. Does **not reach people without internet or mobile access**.
5. **Clutter**, ad blocking and skipping.
6. Fraud, fake reviews and high competition for attention.

**Explain more**

- **NOT an advantage:** "messages reach people who have no internet or mobile access". That is false; it is a limitation.
- Digital marketing does not guarantee every customer will buy, does not remove competition, does not remove the need for a marketing plan or for understanding customers, and does not work only for big firms.
- **Negative spread:** an Abuja restaurant finds one angry customer's post about poor service shared thousands of times in a day. Firms must monitor and respond quickly and politely.
- Data privacy matters in Nigeria too: firms holding customers' phone numbers and emails must protect them and use them only with consent.

### One-minute recap

- Offline = TV, radio, newspapers, magazines, outdoor, cinema; online = search, display, social, email, mobile, websites.
- TV: sight, sound, motion, mass. Radio: low cost, local, audio only. Newspapers: timely, local, short life. Magazines: long life, colour, selective. Outdoor: repeated exposure, brief message.
- Online media: interactive, targeted, measurable; cannot reach people without internet.
- Combine both (QR code on a billboard) in an integrated campaign.
- Paid = bought; owned = firm controls; earned = reviews, shares, free press.
- CTR = clicks / impressions x 100; conversion = desired action completed.
- Crafting: objectives, audience and personas, channels and content, publish, measure with analytics.
- SEO is organic; PPC pays per click; affiliate pays per sale; influencer uses followers; opt-in email, not spam.
- Importance: low-cost global reach, targeting, real-time measurement, engagement, personalisation. Challenges: privacy, fast negative spread, changing algorithms.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A Yaba phone-accessories shop runs a pay-per-click campaign. Its ad gets 60,000 impressions and 900 clicks at ₦60 per click, and 27 shoppers buy, each spending ₦6,000. Find the CTR, total spend, conversion rate, cost per sale, CPM and the revenue earned.

*Solution:*

- CTR = 900 / 60,000 x 100 = **1.5%**
- Total spend = 900 x ₦60 = **₦54,000**
- Conversion rate = 27 / 900 x 100 = **3%**
- Cost per sale = ₦54,000 / 27 = **₦2,000**
- CPM = ₦54,000 / 60,000 x 1,000 = **₦900**
- Revenue = 27 x ₦6,000 = **₦162,000**, which is 3 times the ad spend (₦108,000 more than the spend).
