## Topic 6: Case Studies of Successful Managers

**What you must know:** the career path, key dates, leadership style, big decisions and lessons of six Nigerian managers (Dangote, Awosika, Otedola, Maduka, Elumelu, Wigwe) and five global managers (Welch, Nooyi, Nadella, Musk, Polman); the Igbo apprenticeship system; the core management lessons they share.

### Lesson 6.1: Aliko Dangote and Femi Otedola: industry and energy

**Must know**

**Aliko Dangote**

- Born **1957** in **Kano State**. Studied business at **Al-Azhar University, Cairo**.
- **1977:** started a **small commodity-trading business with a loan from his uncle** (a relative). This grew into the **Dangote Group**.
- **Strategy: import substitution.** He moved from **importing** cement and sugar to **producing them in Nigeria**.
- Built **Dangote Cement** into **Africa's largest cement producer**.
- **Dangote Refinery:** commissioned in **2023** at Lekki, **Lagos**, with a designed capacity of 650,000 barrels a day, Africa's largest refinery. Purpose: to **reduce Nigeria's dependence on imported fuel**.
- **Impact:** major contributor to GDP, job creation and industrialisation.

**Femi Otedola**

- Born **1962**.
- **2003:** founded **Zenon Petroleum and Gas**, mainly in **fuel marketing and distribution, especially diesel**.
- **2007:** became chairman of **African Petroleum**; in **2010** renamed it **Forte Oil Plc** and built it into a major energy company.
- **2019:** sold his stake in Forte Oil and concentrated on **electricity generation** through **Geregu Power Plc**: a **strategic divestment into power generation**.
- Since **January 2024**: chairman of **First HoldCo** (formerly FBN Holdings), the parent of First Bank.

**Explain more**

- **Import substitution** means making at home what a country used to import. It saves foreign exchange, creates jobs and builds local value. Dangote reinforced it with **backward integration** (owning the supply of his own inputs, e.g. limestone quarries for cement).
- **Decision pattern (Dangote):** big, long-term bets on basic goods Nigeria imports (cement, sugar, salt, fertiliser, refined fuel), with heavy capital and scale.
- **Divestment** means selling a business or stake to free capital for a better opportunity. Otedola sold Forte Oil to move into power; he is known for capital-market efficiency and restructuring. In **December 2025** he sold his controlling interest in Geregu Power and stepped down as its chairman, and he has since kept increasing his stake in First HoldCo. Geregu Power still operates in **electricity generation**.
- **Lesson:** spot where a market is moving and reposition capital before others do.

### Lesson 6.2: Cosmas Maduka and the Igbo apprenticeship system

**Must know**

**Cosmas Maduka**

- Born **1958**; from Nnewi, Anambra State. Founder and chairman of the **Coscharis Group**.
- Lost his father as a small child, left primary school and hawked akara to support his mother.
- Learned business through the **Igbo apprenticeship system**, as an apprentice in his uncle's motor-parts business.
- **1977:** started Coscharis as a dealer in **automobile spare parts**; expanded into vehicle sales, assembly and other sectors.
- **Name:** **Cos**mas + **Charis** from his wife **Charity**: the founder's first name and his wife's name.
- **2015:** Coscharis Motors became the **sole distributor of BMW** in Nigeria.
- **Style:** **faith-driven and discipline-oriented**. Impact: youth mentorship and automotive-sector development.

**Igbo apprenticeship system (Igba Boi)**

- A **traditional, mentorship-based business incubation system**.

1. The apprentice trains **5 to 7 years** under a **master trader**.
2. **Post-training settlement capital** is provided to start the apprentice's own business.
3. Agreements are **trust-based and community-enforced** (not written contracts filed in court).
4. A major contributor to **SME dominance in South-East Nigeria**.
5. Recognised as one of **Africa's largest informal venture systems**.

**Explain more**

- Igba Boi works like a **business school plus venture fund**: the apprentice learns by doing (stock, pricing, customers, suppliers) and is then "settled", so the master creates a future business partner, not a rival with nothing.
- Trap: settlement capital comes **after** training; the period is years (5 to 7), not months, and it is not a graduate trainee scheme or Six Sigma.
- **Lesson:** mentorship and succession build lasting enterprise; credibility and discipline are capital.

### Lesson 6.3: Ibukun Awosika, Tony Elumelu and Herbert Wigwe: governance, finance and Africapitalism

**Must know**

**Ibukun Awosika**

- Born **1962**; Chemistry graduate of the University of Ife (now Obafemi Awolowo University).
- Founder of **The Chair Centre Group**, a **furniture manufacturing** business: she built a manufacturing brand in a **male-dominated industry**.
- **2015:** became the **first woman to chair First Bank of Nigeria** (served until 2021).
- Strong advocate of **corporate governance and ethical leadership**; promotes **women entrepreneurship and SME development**. Impact: inclusive leadership and institutional reform.

**Tony Elumelu**

- Born **1963**. Led investors who took over Crystal Bank in **1997** and renamed it **Standard Trust Bank**, which **merged with United Bank for Africa (UBA) in 2005**.
- **2010:** founded both **Heirs Holdings** (investment company) and **The Tony Elumelu Foundation**.
- **2015:** committed **100 million US dollars** to the Tony Elumelu Foundation Entrepreneurship Programme to **train, fund and mentor 10,000 African entrepreneurs over 10 years**.
- **Africapitalism:** the philosophy that **Africa's private sector can transform the continent through long-term investment that creates both economic prosperity and social wealth**.
- Invested heavily in banking, power and hospitality (Transcorp). Chairman of Heirs Holdings and Transcorp; chaired UBA from 2014 until **August 2026**, when he retired at the maximum tenure.

**Herbert Wigwe**

- With his partner **Aigboje Aig-Imoukhuede**, acquired **Access Bank in 2002** and grew it into a **multinational institution**.
- Group Managing Director/CEO of Access Bank from 2014, then Group CEO of Access Holdings.
- Led the **merger with Diamond Bank** (completed 2019), making Access **Nigeria's largest bank**: a contribution to **banking consolidation and corporate expansion**.
- Strong focus on **risk management**; invested in **education and leadership development**, including founding **Wigwe University in Isiokpo, Rivers State**.
- **Died in February 2024** in a helicopter crash in California, USA.

**Explain more**

- **Awosika's lesson:** gender is no barrier to building a manufacturing firm or chairing a top bank; governance and ethics are her signature. A young woman building a manufacturing brand in a male-dominated industry while championing governance would model her career, not Welch's or Otedola's.
- **Elumelu's decision pattern:** buy a small or struggling bank, turn it round, then grow by merger (STB with UBA); later, use wealth to fund entrepreneurs, which is Africapitalism in practice. Do not confuse Africapitalism with Polman's "responsible capitalism" or Nooyi's "Performance with Purpose".
- **Wigwe's decision pattern:** growth by acquisition and merger, backed by strict risk management; his legacy includes a university to develop future leaders.

### Lesson 6.4: Jack Welch, Indra Nooyi, Satya Nadella, Elon Musk and Paul Polman

**Must know**

**Jack Welch (General Electric)**

- **Chairman and CEO of GE, 1981 to 2001.**
- Introduced **Six Sigma** (quality improvement) in the **mid-1990s**.
- **Performance-based ranking ("rank and yank", vitality curve):** each year reward the **top 20%** of managers (bonuses, stock options), develop the **middle 70%**, dismiss the **bottom 10%**.
- **"Number one or number two":** every GE business should be No. 1 or No. 2 in its industry, or be fixed, sold or closed.
- Focus: **efficiency and restructuring**. Impact: modern performance-management systems.

**Indra Nooyi (PepsiCo)**

- **CEO of PepsiCo, 2006 to 2018.**
- **"Performance with Purpose":** long-term growth while having a positive impact on society and the environment.
- Grouped products into **"fun for you", "better for you" and "good for you"** and grew the healthier ones: a **shift toward healthier product diversification**.
- Focus: sustainability and environmental responsibility.

**Satya Nadella (Microsoft)**

- **CEO of Microsoft since February 2014**, succeeding **Steve Ballmer**; also chairman since 2021.
- Transformed Microsoft into a **cloud-first** company; focus on AI and sustainable technology.
- **Growth-mindset, empathetic leadership:** changed a "know-it-all" culture into a "learn-it-all" culture. Wrote *Hit Refresh* (2017).

**Elon Musk (Tesla and SpaceX)**

- Founded **SpaceX in 2002**; became **CEO of Tesla in 2008**.
- **High-risk, innovation-driven** leadership; revolutionised electric vehicles and private space travel; major driver of the renewable-energy transition. Impact: accelerated EV adoption and technology disruption.

**Paul Polman (Unilever)**

- **CEO of Unilever, 2009 to 2019.**
- **2010:** launched the **Unilever Sustainable Living Plan**, which aimed among other things to **halve the environmental footprint** of Unilever's products.
- **Stopped giving quarterly earnings guidance** to investors, arguing that quarterly targets push managers into short-term decisions.
- Integrated sustainability into core strategy; promoted **responsible capitalism**. Impact: a global benchmark for corporate sustainability.

**Explain more**

- **Matching traps:** Six Sigma, rank and yank, No. 1 or No. 2 = **Welch**. Performance with Purpose, fun/better/good for you = **Nooyi**. Cloud-first, growth mindset, *Hit Refresh*, succeeded Ballmer = **Nadella**. High-risk innovation, SpaceX and Tesla = **Musk**. Sustainable Living Plan, no quarterly guidance, responsible capitalism = **Polman**.
- **Date traps:** 1981 to 2001 = Welch; 2006 to 2018 = Nooyi; 2009 to 2019 = Polman; 2014 = Nadella became CEO.
- **Welch versus Nadella** shows two change styles: Welch drove change through hard targets, ranking and restructuring; Nadella drove it through culture, empathy and learning. A CEO who wants employees to keep learning and experimenting should follow Nadella.
- **Nooyi and Polman** both put sustainability at the centre of strategy; Polman also attacked short-termism.

### Lesson 6.5: Styles, decisions and core lessons

**Must know**

| Manager | Signature style or decision | Main impact |
|---|---|---|
| Dangote | Import substitution; large-scale manufacturing | Industrialisation, jobs, less import dependence |
| Awosika | Ethical, governance-focused, inclusive | Institutional reform, women and SMEs |
| Otedola | Strategic divestment; restructuring | Power generation, investor confidence |
| Maduka | Faith-driven, discipline-oriented; apprenticeship-trained | Automotive sector, youth mentorship |
| Elumelu | Africapitalism; growth by merger | Entrepreneurship-driven empowerment |
| Wigwe | Acquisition and merger with risk management | Banking consolidation, financial-sector growth |
| Welch | Efficiency, ranking, restructuring, Six Sigma | Modern performance management |
| Nooyi | Performance with Purpose | Corporate sustainability model |
| Musk | High-risk, innovation-driven | EV adoption, technology disruption |
| Nadella | Empathy, growth mindset, cloud-first | Revitalised culture, tech leadership |
| Polman | Responsible capitalism, long-termism | Sustainability benchmark |

**Core lessons (6):**

1. Vision and strategic alignment.
2. Import substitution and local value creation.
3. Sustainability integrated into core strategy.
4. Risk management and corporate governance.
5. Mentorship and succession planning.
6. Innovation as competitive advantage.

**Explain more**

- Link each lesson to a person: import substitution (Dangote); sustainability (Nooyi, Polman); risk management and governance (Wigwe, Awosika); mentorship and succession (Maduka and Igba Boi, Elumelu's foundation); innovation (Musk, Nadella); vision (all of them).
- Common career pattern: start small (Dangote's loan, Maduka's spare parts, Elumelu's small bank), reinvest, then grow by scale, merger or new sectors.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A Lagos firm with 200 managers copies Jack Welch's vitality curve. How many managers fall in each group, and what happens to each?

*Solution:*

1. Top 20%: 200 × 0.20 = **40** managers, rewarded with bonuses and stock options.
2. Middle 70%: 200 × 0.70 = **140** managers, kept and developed.
3. Bottom 10%: 200 × 0.10 = **20** managers, dismissed.
4. Check: 40 + 140 + 20 = 200.
