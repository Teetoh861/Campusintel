## Topic 7: The Business Environment

**What you must know:** meaning of the business environment; internal vs external; micro (task) vs macro (general); the six PESTLE factors with Nigerian examples; environmental scanning; SWOT; competitive advantage and flexibility; how Nigeria's environment affects performance.

### Lesson 7.1: Meaning of the business environment; internal vs external

**Must know**

- **Business environment:** all the internal and external factors and forces that affect an organisation's operations and performance. It is not only the building, only the customers or only the laws.
- **Internal environment:** the factors **within** the organisation. These are **more within the control** of the firm's management.
- **External environment:** the factors **outside** the organisation that affect it but over which it has **little or no direct control**. Managers cannot stop them, so they must **anticipate and adapt** to them.

**Components of the internal environment**

1. **Owners** (shareholders)
2. **Board of directors**
3. **Employees** (their number, skills and morale)
4. **Organisational (corporate) culture:** the shared values, beliefs and norms of members
5. **Physical work environment and resources:** buildings, machines, equipment, technology in use
6. **Financial resources** (capital, cash)
7. **Management and structure** (management style, systems, procedures)

**Components of the external environment:** the **micro (task)** environment and the **macro (general)** environment (Lesson 7.2).

| Point | Internal environment | External environment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Inside the firm | Outside the firm |
| Control | Largely controllable by management | Largely uncontrollable |
| Examples | Culture, employees' skills, board, owners, equipment, finance | Customers, suppliers, competitors, inflation, exchange rate, government policy, insecurity |
| SWOT link | Strengths and weaknesses | Opportunities and threats |
| Same for all firms? | No, unique to each firm | Macro part is shared by all firms |

**Explain more**

- **Culture shapes ethics.** A firm's corporate culture directly influences its **ethical climate**: whether staff feel free to cut corners or to report wrongdoing. It does **not** influence national interest rates, the exchange rate or inflation; those are external.
- **Trap:** "The skills of the firm's own employees" is internal, so it is **not** part of the external environment. The country's exchange rate, the firm's suppliers and its competitors are all external.
- **Trap:** The board and the shareholders are **internal**, even though shareholders may live far away. They are part of the organisation itself.
- **Uncertainty.** The greatest source of uncertainty is what lies **outside** management's control, e.g. **political instability**. Internal resources, culture and employee morale can be managed.
- **Stable vs dynamic.** A **stable (static)** environment changes slowly and predictably. A **dynamic** environment changes **rapidly and unpredictably**. Nigeria's environment is highly dynamic: in 2023 alone, petrol subsidy was removed (May) and the naira exchange-rate windows were unified (June), sharply changing costs for almost every firm. Environments also differ in **complexity** (how many factors a firm must watch). Dynamic and complex environments create the most uncertainty.

### Lesson 7.2: Micro (task) and macro (general) environments

**Must know**

- **Micro (task) environment:** the forces **close to** a company that **directly** affect its ability to serve its customers. They affect a **particular** firm.
- **Macro (general) environment:** the **broad** external forces (political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental) that affect **all** businesses in an economy, usually indirectly.
- **Key distinction:** the micro environment affects a particular firm directly; the macro environment consists of broad forces affecting all firms. Both are **external**.

**Components of the micro (task) environment**

1. **Customers**
2. **Suppliers** (of raw materials, foodstuffs, equipment, labour, money)
3. **Competitors**
4. **Intermediaries** (distributors, wholesalers, retailers, transporters, banks that help the firm sell and deliver)
5. **Publics** (host communities, the media, pressure groups)
6. **Specific regulators** of the industry, **labour unions** and **strategic partners**

**Components of the macro (general) environment:** the six PESTLE factors (Lesson 7.3), e.g. elections and ideology, inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, unemployment, population growth, culture and religion, internet penetration, new laws, climate and pollution.

| Factor | Micro or macro? |
| --- | --- |
| Farmers and traders who supply a Lagos restaurant | Micro |
| A new competitor opening next door | Micro |
| A dispute with the main raw-material supplier | Micro |
| A complaint from a distributor | Micro |
| National inflation or unemployment rate | Macro |
| Nigeria's population growth rate | Macro |
| Level of internet penetration in Nigeria | Macro |
| Government's national fiscal policy | Macro |

**Explain more**

- **Trap:** "The micro environment is inside the firm, the macro is outside" is **wrong**. The inside of the firm is the **internal** environment. Micro and macro are both outside; they differ in **closeness and directness**.
- When a Nigerian bottling company loses sales because its biggest South-West **distributor** collapses, the distributor is part of its **micro** environment.
- A foreign company with lower prices opening a large plant in Nigeria and taking customers from a local biscuit maker is a **threat in the local firm's micro (task) environment** (it is a competitor), not anything internal.
- Two components of the task environment, if asked for a pair: **suppliers and competitors** (or customers and suppliers). Elections, inflation, unemployment, climate and pollution are macro.
- The **government's fiscal policy** (national taxes and spending) is macro; a specific regulator acting on one industry (such as the Nigerian Communications Commission for a telecom firm) is close to the firm and is often treated as part of its task environment.

### Lesson 7.3: PESTLE analysis and environmental scanning

**Must know**

- **PESTLE:** **P**olitical, **E**conomic, **S**ocial, **T**echnological, **L**egal, **E**nvironmental. It is a tool for assessing **macro-environmental (external) factors**.
- **Purpose:** to identify external macro-environmental factors that may create **opportunities or threats**.
- PESTLE does **not** examine the firm's internal strengths and weaknesses, competitors' strategies or employee performance.
- **Environmental scanning:** the process of monitoring and analysing the external environment to detect changes that may affect a business. PESTLE is most useful **during environmental scanning**.
- Shorter and longer versions: **PEST** (four factors), **STEEPLE** (adds Ethical).

| Factor | What it covers | Nigerian examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Political** | Government stability, ideology, policy direction, elections, insecurity, government attitude to business | Investors delaying projects before a general election; a state policy favouring locally made goods in public procurement |
| **Economic** | Inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, income levels, unemployment, recession, economic growth | Naira depreciation raising import costs; inflation eroding a family's budget; CBN raising the monetary policy rate |
| **Social** | Population size and age structure, urbanisation, education, culture, religion, lifestyles, tastes | A young, urbanising population; respecting religious values in Kano; preference for locally made fashion |
| **Technological** | Inventions, internet and mobile access, mobile payments, automation, e-commerce | Smartphones and payment apps letting an Aba shoe-maker sell online; ATMs and online banking replacing tellers |
| **Legal** | The actual laws and regulations firms must obey: company, labour, tax, consumer, data laws | Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023; a new minimum wage law; NIN-SIM linkage rules; CAMA 2020 |
| **Environmental (ecological)** | Climate, weather, pollution, waste, natural resources, sustainability | Flooding in Kogi State; oil pollution complaints in the Niger Delta |

**Explain more**

- **Political vs legal.** Political factors concern government **stability, ideology and policy direction**; legal factors concern the **actual laws and regulations** firms must obey. A policy direction announced by a government is political; once it becomes a binding law or regulation with penalties, compliance with it is legal. A new tax **law** passed by the National Assembly is legal (and economic in effect), never social.
- **Economic factors in action:**
    - A **recession** reduces demand mainly because of **reduced disposable income**.
    - **Inflation** reduces purchasing power. **Worked example:** a family in Ibadan spends ₦50,000 a month on food. If food prices rise by 25%, the budget buys only ₦50,000 ÷ 1.25 = ₦40,000 worth of food at the old prices, a loss of ₦10,000 in real terms. The supermarket's sales **volume** falls: an economic factor.
    - **Inflation may benefit debtors** because the **real value of the money they owe declines**. **Worked example:** a trader owes ₦1,000,000, repayable in a year. With 25% inflation over that year, the ₦1,000,000 repaid is worth only ₦1,000,000 ÷ 1.25 = ₦800,000 in today's purchasing power.
    - **Exchange rate.** An importer's container rises from ₦18 million to ₦27 million because the naira weakened. Increase = ₦27m − ₦18m = ₦9m; percentage increase = 9 ÷ 18 × 100 = **50%**. This is an **economic** factor.
    - **Interest rates.** When the Central Bank of Nigeria raises its **monetary policy rate (MPR)**, loans become dearer: an **economic** factor, even though the CBN is a government body.
- **Mixed cases:** a regulation requiring SIM cards to be linked to National Identification Numbers is **legal**; rapid growth of mobile internet use is **technological**. A fall in household incomes is economic, not technological. Changes in religious beliefs are social, not economic. Climate change and extreme weather are **environmental**; a new competitor is micro, and a change of government is political.
- **Economic liberalisation** (opening markets to private and foreign firms) tends to **increase competition**. Nigeria's telecom liberalisation, with GSM licences issued in 2001, ended NITEL's near-monopoly and brought in operators such as MTN, which lowered prices and expanded access.
- **External change cuts both ways.** If the Federal Government cuts import duty on industrial machinery and a packaging firm saves ₦3 million on a new line, the environment has created an **opportunity**. External changes can create opportunities as well as threats.

### Lesson 7.4: SWOT analysis, competitive advantage and flexibility

**Must know**

- **SWOT:** **S**trengths, **W**eaknesses, **O**pportunities, **T**hreats.
- **Strengths and weaknesses** come from the **internal** environment. Evaluating the firm's strengths in a SWOT is part of **internal environment analysis**.
- **Opportunities and threats** come from the **external** environment.

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal** | Strengths (e.g. strong brand, skilled staff, modern plant) | Weaknesses (e.g. old machines, staff lacking modern skills, poor cash flow) |
| **External** | Opportunities (e.g. lower import duty, growing young urban market, new technology) | Threats (e.g. new low-price competitor, inflation, insecurity, unfavourable regulation) |

- **Sustainable competitive advantage** arises from resources that are **valuable, rare and hard to imitate** (and that the organisation is set up to exploit). This is the **resource-based view**, associated with **Jay Barney** (the VRIN/VRIO test).
- **Strategic flexibility:** the ability to change plans and resources quickly; it mainly enhances a firm's **ability to adapt to changes in its environment**.

**Explain more**

- **PESTLE feeds SWOT.** PESTLE scans the macro environment; its findings become the **O** and **T** of SWOT. A look inward at resources gives the **S** and **W**.
- **Paint manufacturer:** old machines and staff lacking modern skills are **weaknesses arising from the internal environment**.
- **Fashion brand:** more young customers preferring locally made clothing and promoting it on social media is a **social factor that creates an opportunity**.
- **State procurement policy favouring local goods:** for local manufacturers, a **political factor that creates an opportunity**.
- **Trap:** opportunities do **not** come from the firm's weaknesses, its culture or its financial ratios; those are internal.
- Resources that are common, easily accessible, easily transferred to rivals or short-term cannot give a **sustainable** advantage, because rivals can copy them.
- Strategic flexibility does not raise the likelihood of exiting markets, governance failure or exposure to inflation; it reduces the damage from change.

### Lesson 7.5: Impact of Nigeria's business environment on performance

**Must know**

**Why managers must study the environment:** to **anticipate changes, seize opportunities and reduce the impact of threats**. The environment keeps changing, so it is a reason for planning, not a substitute for it.

**Main Nigerian pressures and their effects**

1. **Inflation:** higher input costs, falling real incomes, lower sales volumes.
2. **Exchange-rate depreciation and forex scarcity:** dearer imported raw materials and machines; difficulty paying foreign suppliers.
3. **High interest rates:** costlier borrowing, less expansion.
4. **Insecurity:** higher insurance and security costs, disrupted distribution, lost markets.
5. **Infrastructure deficits** (power, roads, ports): high energy and logistics costs.
6. **Policy and regulatory change:** subsidy removal, tariffs, new laws that raise compliance costs or open new markets.
7. **Social and technological change:** a large, young, urbanising population and fast mobile adoption, which create opportunities.

**Explain more**

- **Macro factors affect every business, including small ones.** An Enugu shop with ₦2 million monthly sales still feels inflation (its stock costs more), interest rates (its loans cost more) and government policy (taxes, levies) through **costs, prices and demand**. The owner who says "only my shop matters" is wrong.
- **Insecurity:** a Kaduna food distributor facing risky highways pays more for insurance and security escorts; the external environment **increases operating costs and reduces performance**. Well-managed firms cannot ignore it or change it alone.
- **Uncontrollable but manageable:** a Nigerian firm cannot stop a rise in world crude oil prices, but it can adjust its pricing and sourcing. External factors are **largely uncontrollable, so managers must anticipate and adapt**. This affects all firms, not only oil sellers, because fuel, transport and the naira respond to oil prices.
- **Exits as evidence:** in 2023 several multinationals scaled down manufacturing in Nigeria, citing the difficult operating environment and forex problems: GSK Consumer Nigeria announced it would stop operating its own business there, and Procter & Gamble moved to an import-only model.
- **Opportunities exist too:** Nigeria's young population and mobile adoption have fuelled fintech growth and online selling; telecom liberalisation in 2001 transformed connectivity and created a large new industry.
- **How firms respond:** environmental scanning (PESTLE), SWOT-based strategy, local sourcing of raw materials (backward integration), hedging and cost control, investment in alternative power, strategic flexibility and building valuable, rare, hard-to-imitate resources.

### One-minute recap

- Business environment = all internal and external factors affecting a firm's operations and performance.
- Internal (owners, board, employees, culture, resources) is largely controllable; external is largely uncontrollable, so anticipate and adapt.
- Micro (task) = customers, suppliers, competitors, intermediaries, publics: close and direct. Macro (general) = broad PESTLE forces affecting all firms. Both are external.
- PESTLE = Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental; used in environmental scanning for macro factors; never for internal strengths.
- Political = stability, ideology, policy direction; Legal = actual laws to obey.
- SWOT: S and W internal; O and T external.
- Inflation erodes purchasing power and benefits debtors; recession cuts disposable income; liberalisation increases competition.
- Sustainable advantage = valuable, rare, hard-to-imitate resources; strategic flexibility = ability to adapt.
- Nigeria: inflation, forex, interest rates, insecurity, infrastructure, policy change; they raise costs but also create opportunities.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A Lagos bakery uses 400 bags of imported-wheat flour a month. Because the naira weakened, the price per bag rose from ₦60,000 to ₦78,000. (a) Compute the percentage rise in the price per bag and the extra monthly cost. (b) Classify the cause under PESTLE and SWOT, and state whether it is micro or macro.

*Solution:*

(a) Rise per bag = ₦78,000 − ₦60,000 = ₦18,000. Percentage rise = 18,000 ÷ 60,000 × 100 = **30%**.
Old monthly cost = 400 × ₦60,000 = ₦24,000,000. New monthly cost = 400 × ₦78,000 = ₦31,200,000. Extra monthly cost = ₦31,200,000 − ₦24,000,000 = **₦7,200,000** (check: 400 × ₦18,000 = ₦7,200,000).

(b) Naira depreciation is an **economic** factor in the **macro (general)** environment. In SWOT it is a **threat**, because it is external and harmful. The bakery cannot control it but can adapt, e.g. by blending in local cassava flour, adjusting prices or buying ahead.
