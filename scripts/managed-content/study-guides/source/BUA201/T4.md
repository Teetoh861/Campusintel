## Topic 4: Forms of Business Ownership

**What you must know:** sole proprietorship, partnership (ordinary, limited, LLP), companies (limited by shares, by guarantee, unlimited; private and public), cooperatives, franchises, family businesses and public enterprises: features, advantages and limitations; how to choose a form; the Nigerian rules under CAMA 2020 and the role of the CAC.

### Lesson 4.1: Sole proprietorship

**Must know**

- **Sole proprietorship (sole trader):** a business owned and controlled by **one person**, who takes all the profits and bears all the risks. It may employ others and use family help, but there is one owner.
- **Unlimited liability:** the owner's **personal assets can be taken to pay the debts of the business**. The business and the owner are the same person in law.
- **Registration:** a sole trader using a name other than his or her own true name registers a **business name** with the **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)**.

| Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Easy, cheap and quick to start | **Limited access to capital** (the major weakness) |
| **Quick decision-making**: no one to consult | **Unlimited liability** |
| Owner keeps all profits | **Lack of continuity**: may end on the owner's death |
| Close contact with customers; privacy of affairs | Owner's limited skills; heavy workload |
| Little regulation | Hard to grow large |

**Explain more**

- **Worked example (unlimited liability):** Ngozi's fashion shop owes suppliers ₦3,000,000 but has assets of only ₦1,000,000. Shortfall = ₦3,000,000 − ₦1,000,000 = ₦2,000,000. Ngozi can be made to pay the **₦2,000,000 from her personal assets** (car, savings, land). Suppliers are not forced to write it off.
- **Lack of continuity:** Mr Adewale's well-run printing shop in Ibadan closes after his sudden death because nobody else is legally entitled to continue it.
- **Traps:** continuity after death, easy access to large capital, and limited liability are company features, not sole-trader advantages. Separation of ownership and control and double taxation are company issues, not sole-trader weaknesses.
- Most Nigerian businesses (market traders, barbers, tailors, provision shops) are sole proprietorships.

### Lesson 4.2: Partnerships

**Must know**

- **Partnership:** the relationship between persons **carrying on a business in common with a view of profit**.
- **Size limit (section 19, CAMA 2020):** a partnership formed to carry on business for profit may not have **more than 20 persons** unless it is registered as a company or formed under another enactment. The limit does **not** apply to partnerships of **legal practitioners or accountants**.
- **Partnership deed (agreement):** capital contributed by each partner; profit- and loss-sharing ratio; partners' duties and salaries; interest on capital and drawings; procedure for **admitting or retiring a partner**; dissolution. (A **memorandum of association** belongs to a company, not a partnership deed.)
- **No agreement on profits:** profits and losses are shared **equally**.
- **Joint and several liability:** partners are liable together, **and each partner can also be held personally liable for all the firm's debts**.
- **Mutual agency:** each partner can **bind the firm and the other partners** by acts done in the ordinary course of business.

**Types of partners**

1. **Active (general) partner:** contributes capital and manages; unlimited liability.
2. **Sleeping (dormant) partner:** contributes capital and shares profits but **takes no part in management**.
3. **Nominal partner:** lends only his or her **name** for reputation; contributes no capital and no management.
4. **Limited partner:** liability limited to capital contributed; must not manage.

**Types of partnership under CAMA 2020**

| | Ordinary partnership | Limited partnership (LP) | Limited liability partnership (LLP) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members | 2 to 20 (except lawyers, accountants) | At least **one general partner and one limited partner** | At least **two designated partners** who are individuals, **at least one resident in Nigeria** |
| Liability | Unlimited, joint and several | General: unlimited; limited partner: limited to contribution | Partners' liability limited |
| Legal person? | No | No | **Yes**: can sue, be sued and hold property in its own name |
| Registration | Business name with CAC | Registered with CAC | Registered with CAC |

**Explain more**

- **Limited partner who manages:** Funke, a limited partner, starts managing the firm and signing contracts. She **becomes liable for the firm's debts incurred while she takes part in management**. She does not become a director or lose her profits permanently.
- **Profit-sharing worked example:** Ade, Bisi and Chuka contribute ₦6,000,000, ₦3,000,000 and ₦1,000,000; profit is ₦4,500,000.
  - No agreement: ₦4,500,000 ÷ 3 = **₦1,500,000 each**.
  - If the deed says "in capital ratio" (6:3:1, total 10): Ade 6/10 × ₦4,500,000 = ₦2,700,000; Bisi 3/10 × ₦4,500,000 = ₦1,350,000; Chuka 1/10 × ₦4,500,000 = ₦450,000. Check: 2,700,000 + 1,350,000 + 450,000 = 4,500,000.
- **Advantages:** more capital and skills than a sole trader; shared workload and risk; easy to form; privacy. **Limitations:** unlimited liability; mutual agency means one partner's mistake binds all; disagreements; lack of continuity (death, retirement or bankruptcy of a partner can dissolve the firm); still limited capital.
- Law and accounting firms often run as partnerships, which is why they are exempt from the 20-person limit.

### Lesson 4.3: Limited liability companies

**Must know**

- **Corporate personality (separate legal entity):** a registered company is a legal person **separate from its owners**; it can own property, sue and be sued in its own name. Established in **Salomon v Salomon and Co Ltd (1897)**. It mainly protects shareholders from **personal liability for the company's debts**.
- **Perpetual succession:** the company continues to exist even when shareholders die or sell their shares.
- **Limited liability (company limited by shares):** a member's liability is limited to the **amount, if any, unpaid on the shares** he or she holds.

**Types of company (section 21, CAMA 2020)**

1. **Company limited by shares:** private ("Ltd") or public ("Plc").
2. **Company limited by guarantee:** no share capital; liability limited to the **amount each member undertook to contribute to the company's assets if it is wound up**. Used for non-profit objects (education, charity, religion, sport); no profits are distributed to members.
3. **Unlimited company:** if liabilities exceed assets on winding up, members **can be required to contribute from their personal assets** to settle the shortfall.

**Private vs public company (CAMA 2020)**

| | Private company (Ltd) | Public company (Plc) |
|---|---|---|
| Members | Minimum **1** (single-member company, new in CAMA 2020); maximum **50** (joint holders count as one) | Minimum 2; no maximum |
| Shares | **Restricts transfer**; may **not** invite the public to subscribe | **May invite the public to subscribe**; may list on the Nigerian Exchange |
| Minimum issued share capital | **₦100,000** | **₦2,000,000** |
| Directors | At least 2 (a small company may have 1) | At least **three independent directors** among its directors |
| Company secretary | **Not mandatory** for a small private company | Required |

**Other CAMA 2020 rules**

- **Small company:** need not **appoint auditors**. (It still registers with the CAC, keeps accounting records and has at least one member.)
- **Section 305:** a director stands in a fiduciary relationship towards **the company itself** (not primarily the shareholders, customers or employees).
- **Section 307:** no person may be a director of **more than five public companies** at the same time.
- **Memorandum of association:** the company's basic constitution: name, registered office, objects or business, liability of members, share capital. **Articles of association:** detailed internal rules (meetings, appointment of directors, transfer of shares).

**Explain more**

- **Worked example (limited liability):** Tunde holds 20,000 shares of ₦5 each and has paid ₦3 per share. If the company fails, his maximum further liability = 20,000 × (₦5 − ₦3) = **₦40,000**. Once shares are fully paid, he owes nothing more. A guarantee member who undertook ₦50,000 can be called on for at most ₦50,000, and only on winding up.
- **Joint-stock companies** emerged mainly to **finance large-scale ventures** (overseas trade, railways) that no single owner could fund; investors pooled capital and later gained limited liability. Modern companies differ from sole traders mainly by **separate legal personality and limited liability**.
- **Best mix of limited liability and operational flexibility:** a **private limited company** (fewer disclosure rules than a Plc, yet limited liability).
- **PLC vs private company trap:** both give limited liability and both have legal personality; what makes a Plc different is that it **can invite the public to subscribe for its shares**. Sharing profits by patronage describes a cooperative.
- **Plc advantages:** raises large capital from the public; perpetual succession; professional managers; shares are easy to transfer. **Plc disadvantages:** must **disclose a great deal of information and comply with heavy regulation** (CAC, SEC, the Nigerian Exchange); costly to form; separation of ownership and control; double taxation (company tax on profits, then tax on dividends).
- CAMA 2020 was signed in **August 2020**, replacing CAMA 1990. It introduced the single-member private company and replaced "authorised share capital" with **minimum issued share capital**.
- Nigerian examples: Dangote Cement Plc, MTN Nigeria Communications Plc and Nestlé Nigeria Plc are public companies listed on the Nigerian Exchange.

### Lesson 4.4: Cooperatives, franchises, family businesses and public enterprises

**Must know**

- **Cooperative society:** a voluntary association of people who join together on equal terms to meet their common economic needs through a **jointly owned and democratically controlled** enterprise. Its aim is to **provide benefits to its members**, not returns for outside investors.
  - Voting: **one member, one vote**, regardless of shares held.
  - Surplus: shared in proportion to the **business each member did with the society (patronage)**.

**Types of cooperative**

1. **Thrift and credit (savings and loans):** members save monthly and borrow at low interest.
2. **Marketing:** producers collect, grade and sell their output together for better prices.
3. **Consumers':** members buy goods in bulk for their own use.
4. **Producers':** members pool resources to produce goods.
5. **Multipurpose:** combines several of these.

- **Franchise:** an arrangement in which a business owner (**franchisor**) grants another party (**franchisee**) the right to sell its products under its **brand name and business system**, in return for fees or royalties. The franchisor earns mainly through **royalties and franchise fees**.
- **Family business:** owned and controlled by members of one family, often across generations; can take any legal form.
- **Public enterprise:** a business owned and controlled wholly or mainly by **government** to provide goods or services to the public. A **statutory corporation** is a public enterprise set up by a **specific Act of the legislature** that defines its powers, functions and management.
- **Privatisation** transfers ownership to private investors. **Commercialisation** keeps government ownership but requires the enterprise to run on commercial, profit-making lines.

**Explain more**

- **Cooperative cases:** hospital staff in Lagos contributing monthly from salaries for low-interest loans = **thrift and credit**. Ondo State cocoa farmers collecting, grading and selling cocoa together = **marketing**.
- **Cooperative strengths:** democratic control, cheaper goods or credit for members, limited liability once registered. **Weaknesses:** limited capital, weak management, member disputes.
- **Franchise:** Domino's Pizza and Cold Stone Creamery in Nigeria are run by Eat'N'Go as franchisee. **For the franchisee:** proven brand, training, support; **but** fees, royalties and strict rules. **For the franchisor:** fast growth with others' capital; **but** less control over quality.
- **Family business:** strengths are trust, commitment and long-term view; weaknesses are **succession** disputes, mixing family and business money, and appointing relatives over qualified outsiders.
- **Why governments set up public enterprises:** to control strategic sectors, provide essential services private firms may neglect, prevent private monopolies exploiting consumers, create jobs. **Maximising profits for private shareholders is not a reason.**
- **Common problems in Nigeria:** **political interference** in appointments and decisions, inefficiency, corruption, dependence on subsidies.
- The Bureau of Public Enterprises (BPE) carries out privatisation. Examples: the power sector (PHCN successor companies handed to private owners in 2013); NNPC moved the other way, from statutory corporation to NNPC Limited under the Petroleum Industry Act 2021.

### Lesson 4.5: Choosing a form of ownership and the Nigerian regulatory context

**Must know**

**Considerations when choosing a form**

1. **Financial:** amount of capital needed and how it can be raised; cost of formation; tax.
2. **Managerial:** skills available; how quickly decisions must be made; desired control.
3. **Legal:** **extent of owners' liability** for business debts; registration and disclosure requirements; continuity; ease of transfer of ownership.

**Regulator:** the **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)** registers companies, business names, limited partnerships, limited liability partnerships and incorporated trustees under the **Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020**. (Not the CBN, SEC or NIM.)

| Form | Owners | Liability | Legal person | Continuity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | 1 | Unlimited | No | Weak |
| Ordinary partnership | 2 to 20 | Unlimited, joint and several | No | Weak |
| LLP | 2 or more | Limited | Yes | Strong |
| Private company (Ltd) | 1 to 50 | Limited | Yes | Perpetual |
| Public company (Plc) | 2 or more | Limited | Yes | Perpetual |
| Cooperative | Members, equal votes | Limited (when registered) | Yes (when registered) | Strong |

**Explain more**

- An entrepreneur who needs **substantial capital** and wants to **protect owners' personal assets** should choose a **limited liability company**, not a sole proprietorship, ordinary partnership or business name.
- A Lagos alumni body promoting education and awarding scholarships, with no profits distributed, should register a **company limited by guarantee**.
- **Trap:** "liability" is the **legal** consideration; capital is financial; skills and decision speed are managerial.
- The **SEC** regulates public offers of shares; the **CBN** regulates banks; the CAC handles registration. Cooperatives register with the government's cooperative department under cooperative law, not as CAMA companies.

### One-minute recap

- Sole trader: one owner, all profits, all risk, unlimited liability; major weakness is limited capital; may end on death.
- Partnership: business in common with a view of profit; maximum 20 (lawyers and accountants exempt); equal sharing if no agreement; joint and several liability; mutual agency.
- Partners: active, sleeping (capital, no management), nominal (name only), limited.
- LP: at least one general and one limited partner; LLP: separate legal person, two designated partners, one resident in Nigeria.
- Company: separate legal person (Salomon, 1897), perpetual succession; limited by shares (unpaid amount), by guarantee (amount undertaken), or unlimited.
- CAMA 2020: single-member private company; private maximum 50 members; ₦100,000 and ₦2,000,000 minimum issued capital; Plc at least three independent directors; maximum five public directorships; small companies need no auditor or mandatory secretary; directors owe duties to the company.
- Cooperative: one member, one vote; surplus by patronage; thrift and credit, marketing, consumers', producers'.
- Franchise: royalties and fees. Public enterprise: government-owned; statutory corporation = created by Act; privatisation sells, commercialisation keeps ownership.
- Choose by financial, managerial and legal factors; CAC registers under CAMA 2020.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Kemi and Dayo start a business with ₦5,000,000 of capital. It later owes ₦8,000,000 and its assets are worth ₦2,000,000. How much of the debt can creditors recover from the owners' personal assets if the business is (a) an ordinary partnership, (b) a private company limited by shares whose shares are fully paid?

**Solution:**

1. Shortfall = ₦8,000,000 − ₦2,000,000 = ₦6,000,000.
2. (a) Ordinary partnership: liability is unlimited, joint and several, so creditors can recover the full **₦6,000,000** from the partners' personal assets, from either partner alone if necessary.
3. (b) Private company with fully paid shares: the company is a separate legal person and members owe only unpaid amounts on their shares, which is ₦0. Creditors recover **nothing** from the owners personally; they bear the ₦6,000,000 loss.
