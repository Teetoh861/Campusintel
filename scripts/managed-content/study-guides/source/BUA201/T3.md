## Topic 3: Managers as Change Agents

**What you must know:** what a change agent is and does; internal vs external change agents; types and drivers of change; change leadership vs change management; Lewin's three steps and force field analysis; Kotter's eight steps; causes of resistance and the six ways to reduce it; Nigerian cases: banking consolidation (2004-2005) and recapitalisation (2024), GSM (2001) and the Petroleum Industry Act (2021).

### Lesson 3.1: The manager as change agent

**Must know**

- **Organisational change:** any alteration of people, structure or technology in an organisation.
- **Change agent:** a person or group who initiates, guides and supports change in an organisation; acts as a **catalyst** for change and takes responsibility for initiating and managing the change process. A manager can be one; so can a staff specialist or an outside consultant.
- **Manager's responsibility for transformation:** managers must **recognise the need for change, plan and lead it, and help staff adjust** to new ways of working. They do not avoid change, hand it all to consultants, or simply announce it and expect staff to adapt.

**Roles of a change agent**

1. Diagnosing problems.
2. Communicating the need for change and a clear vision.
3. Facilitating communication and feedback.
4. Planning and supporting implementation.
5. Helping people cope (training, coaching, listening).
6. Evaluating results and helping make the change stick.

**Maintaining existing structures unchanged** is the opposite of a change agent's role.

| | Internal change agent (a manager or staff member) | External change agent (e.g. management consultant) |
|---|---|---|
| Advantage | Knows the organisation's history, culture and people; has existing relationships | More **objective, fresh view**; new ideas; not tied to internal politics |
| Disadvantage | Colleagues may see him or her as **too close to existing relationships and politics to be objective**, so may resist | **May not fully understand the culture, history and people**; seen as an outsider; may push drastic change because he or she does not live with the results |

**Explain more**

- **Qualities of an effective managerial change agent:** builds trust, communicates a clear vision, listens to concerns, involves people, persists. **Keeping plans secret from those affected until the last minute** is a bad practice: it breeds rumour and resistance.
- **Organisational development (OD):** a planned, organisation-wide effort to improve effectiveness and health using **behavioural science** knowledge (team building, survey feedback, process consultation). It is not costing, company registration or a one-off staff cut.
- **Organisational learning** (a learning organisation keeps learning, adapting and changing) supports **innovation**, not bureaucracy, rigidity or strategic drift.
- Two metaphors: the **"calm waters"** view sees change as an occasional break in a stable voyage (Lewin's model fits it); the **"white-water rapids"** view sees change as constant and unpredictable, which is closer to Nigerian business today.

### Lesson 3.2: Types and drivers of change

**Must know**

**Types of change**

1. **Planned change:** deliberately designed and implemented in advance by management to achieve specific goals. **Unplanned change** just happens (e.g. a sudden fire or a key manager's death).
2. **Incremental change:** small, continuous improvements. **Transformational (radical) change:** fundamentally alters strategy, structure or culture.
3. **Proactive change** anticipates problems or opportunities; **reactive change** responds after they arrive.

**Three areas managers change:** **structure** (design, authority, jobs), **technology** (equipment, processes, systems), **people** (attitudes, skills, behaviour).

**Corporate restructuring** may involve **downsizing**, mergers, delayering or selling off divisions.

**Drivers (forces) of change**

| External drivers | Internal drivers |
|---|---|
| New **government regulation** or law (e.g. CBN capital rules, PIA) | Persistently **high staff turnover** |
| **Technology** (GSM, mobile apps, fintech) | **Low morale** among employees |
| **Competition** (new local or foreign rivals) | A new managing director or new strategy |
| Economic conditions (inflation, exchange rate, tax law) | A decision to restructure departments |
| Changing customer needs and demographics | Poor performance, conflict between departments |

**Explain more**

- **Trap:** a new tax law, rising inflation or a new foreign competitor is **external**; the appointment of a new MD or a decision to restructure departments is **internal** (it comes from inside, even if it responds to outside pressure).
- **Example:** a traditional bank sees young customers move to mobile-only fintech apps, so it launches its own app and closes some branches. The driver is **technological change and competition in the external environment**.
- For Nigerian firms in 2001, GSM was a **technological driver in the external environment**: they changed how they sold goods, talked to customers and managed staff in the field.
- The 2004-2005 banking consolidation was **radical (transformational)** change: the whole industry was remade, not adjusted.

### Lesson 3.3: Leading and managing change: Lewin and Kotter

**Must know**

- **Change management:** a structured approach to moving individuals and the organisation **from the current state to a desired future state**, using processes and tools to implement and control the change (plans, timetables, training, milestones).
- **Change leadership:** creating the **vision** and driving people to **embrace** change (inspiring, energising, urgency).

| Change leadership | Change management |
|---|---|
| Vision, inspiration, urgency | Structure, plans, tools, control |
| Answers "why" and "where to" | Answers "how" and "when" |
| CEO tours regions painting a picture of a digital bank | Project office schedules branch software migration and tracks milestones |

**Kurt Lewin's three-step model** (in order):

1. **Unfreezing:** making people aware that the current situation must change and preparing them for it.
2. **Changing (moving):** installing the new system, structure or behaviour.
3. **Refreezing:** **stabilising** the change so new behaviours become the norm (through rewards, policies, structures).

**Lewin's force field analysis:** the current state is held in balance by **driving forces** (pushing for change) and **restraining forces** (resisting change). To change, increase driving forces, reduce restraining forces, or both.

**John Kotter's eight steps** (Leading Change, 1996):

1. Establish a **sense of urgency** (first).
2. Create a **guiding coalition**: a group with enough power, credibility and skill to lead the change.
3. Develop a **vision and strategy**.
4. Communicate the change vision.
5. Empower employees for broad-based action (remove obstacles).
6. Generate **short-term wins**.
7. Consolidate gains and produce more change.
8. **Anchor** new approaches in the organisation's **culture** (last).

**Explain more**

- **Force field worked example:** a manager rates forces for a new shift system from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). Driving: competition from cheaper rivals 4, customer demand for longer hours 3, idle machines 5. Total driving = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12. Restraining: workers' fear of losing overtime pay 3, cost of retraining 4, union opposition 2. Total restraining = 3 + 4 + 2 = 9. Net = 12 − 9 = 3 in favour of change, but the manager should still reduce restraining forces (for instance, explain how pay will be protected). "Workers' fear of losing overtime pay" is a **restraining** force; "competition from cheaper rivals" is a **driving** force.
- **Kotter's short-term wins:** three months into a customer-service programme, publicly celebrating branches that halved queue times keeps momentum. A "guiding coalition" is **not** a union opposing change, a merger or government approval.
- **Why change efforts fail (Kotter):** too much complacency (no urgency), no powerful guiding coalition, underestimating the vision, under-communicating it, letting obstacles block it, no short-term wins, **declaring victory too soon**, and **not anchoring the change in the culture**. **Clear communication of the reasons for change** is a cure, not a cause of failure.
- Lewin's refreezing matches Kotter's step 8.

### Lesson 3.4: Resistance to change and how to manage it

**Must know**

**Causes of resistance**

1. **Fear of the unknown** / fear of uncertainty (the most common cause).
2. Fear of losing one's job, pay or skills (self-interest).
3. Threats to existing **status and power**.
4. Habit and comfort with the familiar.
5. Belief that the change is wrong for the organisation; lack of trust.
6. Poor communication and no involvement.

**Clear communication and involvement of employees** is **not** a cause; it reduces resistance.

**Six methods of reducing resistance** (Kotter and Schlesinger):

1. **Education and communication:** workshops, data, answering questions so staff see why change is needed.
2. **Participation and involvement:** letting affected people help design the change so they feel ownership. The **most effective** where it is feasible.
3. **Facilitation and support:** training, counselling, time off to adjust.
4. **Negotiation and agreement:** exchanging something of value (retraining, bonus) for acceptance.
5. **Manipulation and co-optation:** covert influence, or giving resisters a token role.
6. **Explicit and implicit coercion:** threats of job loss, transfer or no promotion.

**Explain more**

- **Match each case:** workshops before a new appraisal system = education and communication. Workers' representatives design a new schedule = participation and involvement. Union accepts an automated line in return for retraining and a one-off bonus = negotiation and agreement. "Use the new attendance system by Friday or lose your job" = explicit and implicit coercion.
- Coercion, manipulation and threats are fast but damage trust and can backfire; managers use them only when speed is vital and other methods have failed.
- An internal change agent may face more resistance because colleagues see him or her as part of existing politics; an outsider may face resistance for not understanding the culture.

### Lesson 3.5: Nigerian cases of change

**Must know**

**Banking consolidation (2004-2005)**

- CBN Governor **Charles Soludo** (announced July 2004) raised banks' minimum capital base from **₦2 billion to ₦25 billion**, deadline 31 December 2005.
- Banks merged, were acquired or failed; the number fell from about **89 to 25**.
- It was **radical (transformational)** change and, for bank managers, an **external, regulatory driver** of change.

**Bank recapitalisation (announced March 2024, Governor Olayemi Cardoso)**

| Licence | New minimum capital |
|---|---|
| Commercial, international | **₦500 billion** |
| Commercial, national | ₦200 billion |
| Commercial, regional | ₦50 billion |
| Merchant bank | ₦50 billion |
| Non-interest, national / regional | ₦20 billion / ₦10 billion |

- Deadline: **31 March 2026**. Banks had to raise fresh capital, merge, or change licence category.

**Telecommunications liberalisation (GSM, 2001)**

- The **Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC)** auctioned GSM licences in **January 2001**; each successful bidder paid **285 million US dollars**.
- **MTN Nigeria** and **Econet Wireless** launched commercial GSM services in **August 2001**.

**Petroleum Industry Act (PIA)**

- Signed into law in **August 2021** (by President Muhammadu Buhari).
- **NNPC** incorporated as a **limited liability company** (NNPC Limited) with the Corporate Affairs Commission.
- **Two regulators:** the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (**NUPRC**, upstream) and the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (**NMDPRA**, midstream and downstream).
- Upstream companies contribute **3%** of their actual annual operating expenditure of the preceding year to **host community development trusts**.

**Explain more**

- 89 to 25 means 89 − 25 = 64 fewer banks, a fall of about 72% (64 ÷ 89 = 71.9%, rounded). The capital base rose 12.5 times (25 ÷ 2).
- Econet's Nigerian business later became Vmobile, then Celtel, then Zain, and today **Airtel**. Globacom entered later (licensed 2002, launched 2003), so it was not one of the August 2001 launchers. NITEL was the old state operator, not a 2001 auction winner that launched in August.
- The NCC is the telecoms regulator; do not confuse it with the CBN (banks), the CAC (company registration) or the BPE (privatisation).
- **Host community example:** an upstream firm whose operating expenditure last year was ₦300 million must contribute 3% × ₦300 million = ₦9 million.
- Lesson for managers in all four cases: the driver was external (regulation or technology), and firms that unfroze early, built a clear vision and communicated it handled the change better than those that resisted.

### One-minute recap

- Change agent = person or group who initiates, guides and supports change; a catalyst.
- Internal agent knows the culture but may seem too close to politics; external agent is objective but may not understand the culture.
- Types: planned/unplanned, incremental/transformational, proactive/reactive. Areas: structure, technology, people.
- External drivers: regulation, technology, competition, economy. Internal: turnover, morale, new MD, restructuring.
- Change leadership = vision and inspiration; change management = structured process and tools.
- Lewin: unfreezing, changing, refreezing; force field = driving vs restraining forces.
- Kotter: 8 steps, from sense of urgency (first) to anchoring in culture (last).
- Main cause of resistance: fear of the unknown. Most effective cure: participation and involvement.
- Nigeria: ₦2bn to ₦25bn, 89 to 25 banks (Soludo, 2004-2005); ₦500bn international licence by 31 March 2026; NCC GSM auction, 285 million US dollars each, MTN and Econet launched August 2001; PIA August 2021, NNPC Limited, NUPRC and NMDPRA, 3% to host communities.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Under the 2024 recapitalisation, a commercial bank with ₦120 billion of qualifying capital wants to keep an international licence. How much new capital must it raise, and what would the shortfall be if it chose a national licence instead? Name two ways it could close the gap.

**Solution:**

1. International licence: ₦500 billion − ₦120 billion = **₦380 billion** to raise.
2. National licence: ₦200 billion − ₦120 billion = **₦80 billion**.
3. Ways to close the gap: raise fresh equity (rights issue or public offer), or merge with or acquire another bank; alternatively, downgrade to a licence category whose threshold it can meet.
