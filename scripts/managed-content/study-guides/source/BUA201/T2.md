## Topic 2: Universality of Management and Managerial Roles

**What you must know:** the four management functions (POLC) and how they link; the process and systems views; universality and management across functional areas; top, middle and first-line managers; Mintzberg's ten roles in three groups; Katz's three skills and how they change by level; managers as organisational stabilisers.

### Lesson 2.1: The four management functions and how they link

**Must know**

- **Manager:** a person who plans, organises, leads and controls the work of others so that organisational goals are achieved.
- **The four functions (POLC):**

1. **Planning:** setting goals and deciding in advance what to do, how and when to do it. It comes **first** and is the **foundation** of the other functions because it gives them direction.
2. **Organising:** allocating tasks, resources and authority: creating departments, assigning jobs, setting reporting lines. Its result is the **organisation structure** (the formal framework of jobs, departments, reporting relationships and lines of authority).
3. **Leading (directing):** motivating, communicating with and influencing employees to work towards goals.
4. **Controlling:** monitoring performance and ensuring that **deviations from plans are corrected**.

- "Bargaining" is **not** one of the POLC functions.
- **Control process (4 steps in order):**

1. Set standards.
2. Measure actual performance.
3. Compare performance with standards.
4. Take corrective action.

- **Interrelationship:** the functions are **interrelated and continuous**, not separate one-off steps. **Control provides feedback for future plans**: deviations found during controlling lead managers to revise their plans.

| Function | Example activity |
| --- | --- |
| Planning | Writing a five-year strategy; setting a target of three new depots |
| Organising | Creating units, assigning tasks, allocating equipment |
| Leading | Praising a worker; settling a quarrel; morning pep talk |
| Controlling | Checking whether production costs are within budget |

**Explain more**

- **Mission statement:** an organisation's fundamental, long-term purpose (why it exists). A **vision** states what it wants to become. Goals and budgets are narrower.
- **Levels of strategy:**
  - **Corporate-level:** portfolio decisions about **which businesses or industries** the firm should be in.
  - **Business-level:** how to compete in one business.
  - **Functional-level:** how each department (marketing, production) supports the business strategy.
- **Levels of control:** **strategic control** focuses on the organisation's **long-term direction**; **operational control** focuses on daily tasks and routine supervision.
- **Centralisation:** authority concentrated at the top; it **limits employee empowerment**. **Decentralisation:** authority pushed down; it promotes **faster decision-making at lower levels**.
- Fayol listed five functions (planning, organising, commanding, coordinating, controlling); modern texts merge them into four, with leading replacing commanding.
- **Worked example (controlling):** an Ibadan distributor budgets sales of ₦10,000,000 for the month; actual sales are ₦8,200,000.
  - Shortfall = ₦10,000,000 − ₦8,200,000 = ₦1,800,000.
  - Shortfall as % of budget = ₦1,800,000 ÷ ₦10,000,000 × 100 = **18%** (so 82% of target was achieved).
  - Comparing and investigating this is **controlling**; the findings feed into next month's **plan**.
- A bottling-plant manager who sees bottles leaving the line under-filled and adjusts the filling machine is at step 4, **taking corrective action**.

### Lesson 2.2: Process and systems perspectives; universality

**Must know**

- **Process perspective:** management is a **continuous series of interrelated functions** (planning, organising, leading, controlling).
- **Systems perspective:** an organisation is a **set of interrelated parts** that takes **inputs** from the environment, **transforms** them and returns **outputs**, with **feedback**.

| Element | Meaning | Bakery | Commercial bank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Resources taken in | Flour, sugar, bakers' labour | Deposits, staff skills |
| Transformation | Processing | Mixing and baking | Assessing loans, processing transactions |
| Outputs | What goes out | Loaves sold | Satisfied customers, profit |
| Feedback | Information about outputs used to adjust inputs or the process | Complaints about stale bread | Complaints to the call centre |

- **Open system:** constantly interacts with and depends on its environment for resources and customers. A **closed system** does not. **Subsystems** (departments) are **interdependent**.
- A manager using systems theory **emphasises the interdependence of subsystems** and does not ignore the environment.
- **Universality of management:** management principles and functions apply to **all kinds of organisations** (business, government, church, club, school) and at **all levels**.

**Explain more**

- When production breaks down at a Lagos beverage company and marketing misses delivery promises, that shows **interdependent subsystems**.
- A church youth fellowship leader in Surulere sets goals, forms committees, motivates members and reviews each event: POLC outside business, so **universality**.
- Universality does not mean principles are fixed laws or that every manager uses the same style; how they are applied varies with level, size, culture and country.
- **Management across functional areas:** production, marketing, finance, HR and operations managers each **plan, organise, lead and control** within their own areas. Marketing needs control systems too; planning is not only the finance manager's job.

### Lesson 2.3: Types and levels of managers

**Must know**

| Level | Main job | Typical titles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Top** | Organisation-wide decisions, long-term strategy and policies | Managing director, CEO, chairman, executive director |
| **Middle** | Translate top management's goals into specific plans for their units; coordinate first-line managers | Regional sales manager, plant manager, head of department, branch manager |
| **First-line** (supervisory) | Direct day-to-day work of operatives | Foreman, shift supervisor, team leader |

- Operative (non-managerial) employees do the work themselves and manage no one.
- **Functional manager:** responsible for a **single specialised area** (finance, marketing, HR).
- **General manager:** responsible for **several areas** (production, marketing, finance, personnel) of a whole unit or division.
- Top managers make decisions with a **longer time horizon that affect the whole organisation**.
- All levels perform all four functions, but **top managers spend relatively more time planning**; **first-line managers spend relatively more time leading and directing** day-to-day work.

**Explain more**

- A foreman in a Kaduna factory who assigns daily tasks to machine operators, checks their work and reports to the production manager is a **first-line** manager.
- The head of a regional division of a food company running production, marketing, finance and personnel is a **general** manager (middle level in a large group).
- A **project manager** coordinates people from different departments for a temporary project.
- Traps: representing shareholders at the AGM is the board's role; personally doing routine production is operative work.

### Lesson 2.4: Mintzberg's ten managerial roles

**Must know**

**Henry Mintzberg** studied five chief executives at work and identified **10 roles** in **3 groups**:

| Group | Role | What the manager does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Interpersonal (3)** | Figurehead | Ceremonial and symbolic duties: cutting ribbons, signing certificates |
| | Leader | Hiring, training, motivating and praising staff |
| | Liaison | Building a network of contacts **outside** the unit (regulators, other firms) for information and favours |
| **Informational (3)** | Monitor | Seeking and receiving information: reading trade journals and reports |
| | Disseminator | Passing information **inside** the unit to subordinates |
| | Spokesperson | Passing information **outside** to the public, media, regulators |
| **Decisional (4)** | Entrepreneur | Starting new projects and improvements |
| | Disturbance handler | Taking charge in crises (fire, strikes) |
| | Resource allocator | Deciding who gets what (budget, people, time) |
| | Negotiator | Bargaining with unions, suppliers, partners |

Memory aid: **3 + 3 + 4 = 10**.

**Explain more**

- MD of a Nigerian bank opens a branch and signs long-service certificates = **figurehead**.
- Plant manager reads trade journals and daily reports = **monitor**.
- Branch manager passes a new customer-service policy to staff = **disseminator**.
- Head of corporate affairs of a telecom company explains a six-hour network outage on television = **spokesperson**.
- Lagos fintech manager launches USSD account opening = **entrepreneur**.
- Warehouse fire and threatened walk-off = **disturbance handler**.
- Divisional head shares a ₦50,000,000 budget among sales, production and training = **resource allocator**. For example 40%, 35% and 25% give ₦20,000,000, ₦17,500,000 and ₦12,500,000.
- HR director agrees wages with union officials = **negotiator**.
- Common traps: **spokesperson** is informational (not interpersonal or decisional); **liaison** is interpersonal (not informational); **monitor** is informational (not decisional); **negotiator** is decisional. Strategic/tactical/operational are levels, not Mintzberg groups; technical/human/conceptual are Katz's skills.

### Lesson 2.5: Management skills

**Must know**

**Robert Katz** identified **three** basic skills:

1. **Technical skill:** using the tools, techniques and specialised knowledge of a field (accounting, engineering). **Most important for first-line managers.**
2. **Human (interpersonal) skill:** working well with others, communicating, motivating, building cooperation. **Roughly equally important at all levels.**
3. **Conceptual skill:** seeing the organisation as a whole, how its parts relate to each other and the environment; abstract thinking. **Most critical at the top**, e.g. when formulating corporate strategy.

- Moving from first-line to top: **technical skill becomes less important, conceptual skill becomes more important**; human skill stays important throughout.

| Level | Technical | Human | Conceptual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Top | Low | High | Highest |
| Middle | Medium | High | Medium |
| First-line | Highest | High | Low |

- **Other skills** often added: political (building a power base and networks), diagnostic and analytical, decision-making, communication, time management, design (solving problems in ways that benefit the organisation).

**Explain more**

- A conglomerate CEO studying how an import ban, rising interest rates and a rival's expansion will together affect each business is using **conceptual** skill.
- A new supervisor calmly settling a quarrel between two operators uses **human** skill.
- Do not mix Katz's skills with Mintzberg's role groups (interpersonal, informational, decisional).

### Lesson 2.6: Managers as organisational stabilisers

**Must know**

- **Organisational stabilisers:** managers maintain **order, continuity and predictable operations** so the organisation keeps running smoothly, especially when disruptions hit.
- They do this through plans, policies, procedures, coordination, control and quick problem-solving.

**Explain more**

- During fuel scarcity, a transport company's operations manager reorganises schedules, secures alternative suppliers and keeps buses running. That is the manager as **stabiliser**.
- Stabilising is **not** resisting every change or avoiding decisions. Managers balance stability with change: they keep today's operations steady while leading the changes the organisation needs (managers as change agents).

### One-minute recap

- Manager = plans, organises, leads and controls others' work towards goals.
- POLC: planning first (foundation); organising gives the structure; leading motivates; controlling corrects deviations.
- Control steps: set standards, measure, compare, correct. Control feeds back into planning.
- Process view = continuous interrelated functions; systems view = inputs, transformation, outputs, feedback; open system; interdependent subsystems.
- Universality: management applies to all organisations and levels.
- Levels: top (strategy, more planning), middle (translate and coordinate), first-line (daily supervision, more leading).
- Mintzberg: 10 roles; interpersonal 3, informational 3, decisional 4.
- Katz: technical (first-line), human (all levels), conceptual (top).
- Managers stabilise: order, continuity, predictability.

### Theory practice

**Question:** An Abuja water factory sets a standard of 5,000 cartons a week. Actual output this week is 4,600 cartons. Find the shortfall and the percentage of the standard achieved, and name the control step at which each piece of work happens.

**Solution:**

- Step 1, set standards: 5,000 cartons a week.
- Step 2, measure actual performance: 4,600 cartons.
- Step 3, compare: shortfall = 5,000 − 4,600 = **400 cartons**. Percentage achieved = 4,600 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = **92%** (an 8% shortfall).
- Step 4, take corrective action: find the cause (for example machine downtime or power cuts) and fix it; if the standard is unrealistic, revise the plan, which shows control feeding back into planning.
