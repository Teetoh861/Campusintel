## Topic 5: Corporate Governance, Leadership and Management as a Profession

**What you must know:** what a profession is and its characteristics; why management is a profession in Nigeria (NIM, Act 14 of 2003) and why some call it a semi-profession; ethics, codes and the NIM values; corporate governance meaning, principles and theories; the NCCG 2018 and the FRC; the leadership theories; leadership ethics and responsible leadership.

### Lesson 5.1: Profession and management as a profession

**Must know**

- **Profession:** a regular work or occupation. Profession and practitioner: teaching/teacher, medicine/doctor, engineering/engineer, law/lawyer, architecture/architect, carpentry/carpenter.
- **Characteristics of a profession (6):**

1. **Training:** extensive training to acquire specialised knowledge. Most professions require both education and training.
2. **Skills/expertise:** these result from experience and practice in applying knowledge.
3. **Licence or certification:** a permission granted by a professional body or government authority to individuals who meet specific qualifications, which allows them to practise a profession.
4. **Ethics:** the principles an organisation sets for itself in its internal and external dealings. A **code of ethics** provides the overarching moral principles and values.
5. **Code of conduct:** translates the ethical principles into practical guidelines for behaviour, e.g. compliance with the law and avoiding conflicts of interest.
6. **Standards:** criteria or measures for the quality of practice or performance, e.g. units of measurement, building regulations, quality management systems such as **ISO 9000**.

- **Management (Weihrich, Cannice and Koontz, 2008):** the process of designing and maintaining an environment in which individuals, working together in groups, efficiently (and effectively) accomplish selected aims. It is the art of getting things done through and with others.
- **Management is a profession in Nigeria** because it involves **education, training (specialised knowledge), skills, ethical codes of conduct and a statutory or regulatory body**.
- **Nigerian Institute of Management (Chartered), NIM:** the statutory regulatory body established by an Act of the National Assembly, the **NIM (Establishment) Act No. 14 of 2003**, to regulate and control the management profession in Nigeria. NIM **determines standards** for the management profession and **controls the practice** of management in Nigeria.

**Explain more**

- **What separates a profession from an ordinary occupation:** it **enforces discipline through a recognised professional body** (which licenses members, sets standards and can sanction misconduct). A profession does **not** guarantee a high income, and long experience or working with people alone does not make an occupation a profession.
- **Not characteristics of a profession** (exam distractors): a guaranteed high income for every member; membership passed down automatically from parent to child; paying a fee; holding a government post.
- **Match the term to its description:** "permission to practise" = licence/certification; "practical guidelines such as obeying the law and avoiding conflicts of interest" = code of conduct; "overarching moral principles" = code of ethics; "quality measures such as ISO 9000" = standards; "extensive preparation combining education and practical instruction" = training.
- **Code of ethics vs code of conduct:** the code of ethics states the broad moral principles (the "why"); the code of conduct turns them into specific rules of behaviour (the "what to do").
- **Which body regulates what (do not swap them):**

| Body | What it does |
|---|---|
| Nigerian Institute of Management (Chartered), NIM | Regulates and controls the management profession; sets standards |
| Financial Reporting Council of Nigeria (FRC) | Issued the Nigerian Code of Corporate Governance 2018; regulates financial reporting and governance |
| Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) | Registers companies and business names (under CAMA 2020) |
| Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria (ICAN) | Regulates the accountancy profession |

- NIM's statutory role came from the **2003 Act**, not from CAMA 2020, not from the NCCG 2018 and not from a members' resolution.
- **Management as a semi-profession:** in many countries management is called a semi-profession rather than a full profession because of **free (unrestricted) entry into practice, no compulsory licence and no statutory regulation**: anyone can be appointed a manager without a specific required qualification. This is **not** because management lacks a body of knowledge or formal training. Management **does** have a systematic body of principles that can be taught, has ethical codes, and many managers receive formal training. So "management has a systematic body of principles" is an argument **for** professional status, not for calling it a semi-profession. In Nigeria the NIM Act gives management a statutory regulator, which is why it is treated as a profession here.

### Lesson 5.2: Ethics, the NIM values and efficiency versus effectiveness

**Must know**

**NIM Code of Ethics / Values (7):**

1. **Integrity and honesty:** demonstrate integrity in professional activities.
2. **Transparency:** openness in dealings.
3. **Fairness:** be impartial; no favouritism or discrimination.
4. **Accountability:** the obligation to give account for a job done or how a responsibility was undertaken.
5. **Responsibility:** the obligation or duty (moral and legal) to perform a task.
6. **Effectiveness:** achieving objectives or desired results.
7. **Efficiency:** accomplishing a task or responsibility with the least resources.

- **Responsibility vs accountability:** responsibility is the duty to **perform** a task; accountability is the obligation to **give account** for how it was done.
- **Ethics and responsibility in managerial practice:** managers set standards through **integrity, fairness and transparency**, and make decisions that benefit not just themselves and shareholders but also **other stakeholders**, including employees, customers and their operating environment.
- **Ethical theories (decision rules):**

| Theory | Rule |
|---|---|
| Utilitarianism | The greatest good for the greatest number |
| Deontology (duty/rights) | Follow moral duties and rules whatever the outcome |
| Ethical egoism | Act in your own self-interest |
| Virtue ethics | Act as a person of good character would |

- **Ethical compliance:** obeying laws, regulations and rules; it is **driven by laws and rules**. Ethics in general is wider and includes voluntary moral choices beyond what the law requires.

**Explain more**

- **Not NIM values:** favouritism, secrecy, profit maximisation at all costs.
- **Applying the values:**
    - A procurement officer awards a ₦12 million contract to his cousin's company although a qualified bidder quoted less: **fairness** is violated (favouritism).
    - An agency publishes every bidder, bid price and winning bid online: **transparency**.
    - Manipulating reports to protect management's image is a breach of **integrity and honesty**. Refusing work beyond your competence, declining gifts from clients and updating your skills are all **ethical** behaviour.
- **Worked example (efficiency vs effectiveness).** Two Lagos bakeries each plan and produce 2,000 loaves a day of the same quality. Bakery A spends ₦180,000 on inputs daily; Bakery B spends ₦240,000.
    - Step 1, effectiveness: both reach the planned 2,000 loaves, so they are **equally effective**.
    - Step 2, cost per loaf: A = ₦180,000 ÷ 2,000 = **₦90**; B = ₦240,000 ÷ 2,000 = **₦120**.
    - Step 3, daily saving: ₦240,000 − ₦180,000 = **₦60,000**, which is ₦60,000 ÷ ₦240,000 × 100 = **25%** less than B.
    - Conclusion: A is **equally effective but more efficient**. Efficiency is about resources used (inputs per output); effectiveness is about hitting the objective.
- **Trap:** "treating staff impartially" is fairness, and "reporting results to the board" is accountability; neither is effectiveness.

### Lesson 5.3: Corporate governance: meaning, principles and theories

**Must know**

- **Corporate governance (CG):** a system or framework of policies/guidelines by which organisations are **directed and controlled** (**Cadbury Report, 1992**).
- **Key principles of CG (4):** **responsibility, accountability, transparency, fairness** (meanings as in Lesson 5.2). Secrecy is **not** a principle.
- **Why CG matters:** it protects the interests of shareholders and other stakeholders and builds investor trust. It does **not** guarantee profit, give managers unlimited power or remove the need for external audit.
- **Agency theory (Jensen and Meckling, 1976):** shareholders (**principals**) hire managers (**agents**). **Agency costs** arise from the **divergence of interests between owners and managers** (e.g. managers chasing perks or short-term bonuses), plus the cost of monitoring them. A major objective of CG is to **resolve conflicts between owners and managers**.
- **Stakeholder theory (R. Edward Freeman, 1984):** a firm should serve all groups affected by it (employees, customers, suppliers, community, government), not only shareholders. **CSR aligns most closely with stakeholder theory.**
- **Shareholder primacy / classical profit doctrine (Milton Friedman):** the firm's main social responsibility is to maximise profit for shareholders within the law.

**Explain more**

- **Board of directors:** executive directors run the business day to day; **non-executive directors** do not. An **independent non-executive director (INED)** has no material ties to management or major shareholders, so INEDs mainly enhance **monitoring effectiveness**: they strengthen accountability and oversight of management. They do not raise executive power or production output.
- **Internal audit** checks that controls work and records are reliable, so it strengthens **operational control**.
- **Social responsibility (sustainability) reporting** discloses a firm's social and environmental impact, so it improves **transparency**.
- **Governance failure** often results in **financial misreporting**, fraud and loss of stakeholder confidence; **corporate failure is often linked to weak corporate governance**. High CSR, stakeholder engagement and ethical leadership reduce, not cause, failure.
- **Stakeholder capitalism:** when managers sacrifice some short-term profit to build long-term trust with employees, customers and the community. It is the opposite of shareholder primacy.
- **Stakeholder mapping** (e.g. **Mendelow's power–interest matrix**) classifies stakeholders by their **power or influence** and their **interest** in the organisation:

| | Low interest | High interest |
|---|---|---|
| High power | Keep satisfied | Manage closely (key players) |
| Low power | Monitor (minimum effort) | Keep informed |

### Lesson 5.4: Governance in Nigeria: the NCCG 2018

**Must know**

- **Nigerian Code of Corporate Governance (NCCG) 2018:** states the standard of principles and practices for corporate governance in Nigerian **public and private organisations**.
- **Issued by:** the **Financial Reporting Council of Nigeria (FRC)** (unveiled in January 2019).
- **Aim:** to promote **transparency, integrity and public trust**.
- **Content:** requirements for **board structure, board and committee meetings, and disclosure reports**.
- **Number of principles:** **28**.
- **Compliance approach:** **"Apply and explain"**: companies apply the principles in a way that suits their size and business, and explain how they have done so. (The older "comply or explain" approach is **not** the NCCG 2018 approach.)

**Key NCCG 2018 requirements:**

1. The **Chairman** and the **MD/CEO** roles are **separate** and held by **different persons**; the Chairman should be a non-executive director.
2. The board includes **independent non-executive directors**, to strengthen accountability and oversight of management.
3. The board meets **at least once every quarter** (at least four times a year).
4. The board evaluates itself every year, and an **independent external consultant** evaluates it **at least once every three years**.
5. A **whistleblowing framework** lets staff and others report unethical conduct confidentially and without suffering harm.
6. Board committees (e.g. audit, risk, remuneration, nomination and governance), sustainability policies and disclosure in the annual report.

**Explain more**

- The 28 principles are grouped under themes: the board of directors and officers of the board, assurance (audit and risk), relationship with shareholders, business conduct and ethics, sustainability, and transparency.
- **Trap on counts:** 4 = the key principles of CG in general; **28** = the principles in the NCCG 2018.
- **Trap on bodies:** the NCCG was issued by the **FRC**, not the CAC (company registration), NIM (management profession) or CBN (banking regulator).
- A proposal for one person to be both Chairman and MD/CEO of a Nigerian plc is **inconsistent with the Code**; separation stops one person from dominating the board and supervising himself.
- The NCCG does not fix prices, register companies, set exchange rates, the minimum wage or tax rates.

### Lesson 5.5: Leadership theories

**Must know**

- **Leadership:** the process in which an individual **influences** other group members towards the attainment of group or organisational goals. Leadership theories explain **how and why people lead**.

**Leadership theories (7):**

1. **Great Man / Trait theories:** people are **born** with innate abilities such as intelligence, charisma and courage that predispose them to leadership; the fate of organisations and societies depends on them. "Leaders are born, not made."
2. **Behavioural theories:** focus on **what leaders do** instead of what they are (e.g. **task-oriented** or **people-oriented**), which determines leadership success.
3. **Contingency/Situational theories:** the **situation** should determine the leadership approach. People become leaders not only because of personality but also because of situational factors and the interaction between leaders and group members. **No single style is effective in every situation.**
4. **Transformational/Inspirational theories:** leaders **inspire** and motivate followers to achieve **more than the expected result**, e.g. **charismatic** leadership.
5. **Participative/Democratic theories:** **involving followers in decision making** promotes consensus and commitment.
6. **Transactional theories:** using **rewards and punishments** to motivate followers.
7. **Servant leadership:** focus on **empowering and supporting** group members rather than exercising authority, by showing **empathy, listening** and commitment to their personal and professional development.

**Explain more**

- **Scholars:** Great Man idea is linked with Thomas Carlyle; behavioural research includes the **Ohio State** studies (initiating structure vs consideration), the **Michigan** studies (job-centred vs employee-centred), **Blake and Mouton's Managerial Grid**, and **Kurt Lewin's** styles (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire); contingency models include **Fiedler's** contingency model and **Hersey and Blanchard's situational leadership**; transformational leadership comes from **James MacGregor Burns** (1978), developed by **Bernard Bass**; servant leadership from **Robert Greenleaf** (1970).
- **Hersey and Blanchard:** a leader who changes style according to the **maturity (readiness)** of followers is applying **situational leadership theory** (telling, selling, participating, delegating).
- **Styles:** an **autocratic** style (leader decides alone) can work best when **urgent decisions are needed in a crisis**; it suits creativity, high-morale or decentralised settings poorly. **Laissez-faire** leaves decisions to the group.
- **Transformational vs transactional:** transformational inspires with a vision to achieve **more than expected**; transactional exchanges rewards and punishments for performance and relies on routine supervision.
- **Apply the theories:**
    - A Kano sales manager pays a ₦50,000 bonus to every officer who meets the monthly target and issues a query to those who fall short: **transactional**.
    - A Port Harcourt plant manager lets workers discuss and vote on three shift options before changing the schedule: **participative (democratic)**.
    - An Ibadan branch manager is directive during a cash-system failure but consults staff freely at normal times, saying "the right style depends on the circumstances": **contingency/situational**.
    - A board member who says "great leaders are born, not made": **Great Man/Trait**.
- **Not a leadership theory:** comparative advantage (an international trade theory).

### Lesson 5.6: Leadership ethics and responsible leadership

**Must know**

- **Leadership ethics:** leaders guide people with the principles of **honesty, fairness, respect and accountability** in pursuing organisational or group goals. The interest of **all stakeholders** is considered instead of profit alone.
- **Responsible leadership:** pays attention to **ethics, sustainability and accountability**, and considers the interests of **employees, customers, shareholders and the wider society**. Responsible leaders **promote organisational effectiveness**.

**Explain more**

- Leadership ethics most directly influence **organisational trust**; ethical leadership mainly enhances an organisation's **reputation**. They do not directly move interest rates, inflation or the exchange rate.
- Unethical leadership (secret decisions, rewarding friends and relatives, profit at any cost) breaks trust and is a common route to governance failure.
- Responsible leadership links all of this topic: professional ethics (NIM values), good governance (NCCG 2018) and a stakeholder view of the firm.

### One-minute recap

- Profession = regular work or occupation; 6 characteristics: training, skills, licence/certification, ethics, code of conduct, standards (e.g. ISO 9000).
- Management is a profession in Nigeria: education, training, skills, ethical codes and a statutory regulator, **NIM (Act No. 14 of 2003)**; elsewhere it is often called a semi-profession because entry is free and no licence is compulsory.
- NIM values (7): integrity and honesty, transparency, fairness, accountability, responsibility, effectiveness, efficiency.
- Effectiveness = achieving objectives; efficiency = least resources.
- CG = system by which organisations are directed and controlled (Cadbury, 1992); 4 principles: responsibility, accountability, transparency, fairness.
- Agency costs come from owner–manager divergence; CSR fits stakeholder theory.
- NCCG 2018: issued by FRC; 28 principles; "apply and explain"; separate Chairman and MD/CEO; quarterly board meetings; external evaluation every 3 years; whistleblowing.
- 7 leadership theories: trait, behavioural, contingency/situational, transformational, participative, transactional, servant.
- Leadership ethics: honesty, fairness, respect, accountability for all stakeholders; responsible leadership promotes organisational effectiveness.

### Theory practice

**Question:** Two Abuja water factories each plan and produce 500 cartons of the same quality a day. Factory X spends ₦400,000 on inputs and Factory Y spends ₦500,000. Compare their effectiveness and efficiency.

*Solution:*

1. Effectiveness: both meet the 500-carton objective, so they are **equally effective**.
2. Cost per carton: X = ₦400,000 ÷ 500 = **₦800**; Y = ₦500,000 ÷ 500 = **₦1,000**.
3. X saves ₦500,000 − ₦400,000 = **₦100,000** a day, which is ₦100,000 ÷ ₦500,000 × 100 = **20%** of Y's cost.
4. Conclusion: X is **equally effective but more efficient** than Y.
