## Topic 8: Functional Areas I: Marketing and Human Resources

**What you must know:** market and marketing; the six marketing functions and customer value; STP and the four bases of segmentation; the 4Ps; rural vs urban consumers in Nigeria; HRM meaning, functions and importance; how marketing and HR depend on each other and coordinate.

### Lesson 8.1: Market, marketing and the marketing functions

**Must know**

- **Market (old view):** a place where buying and selling takes place.
- **Market (modern view):** all **individuals and organisations that are actual or potential buyers** of a product. "The car market" means all actual or potential car buyers, not a place where cars are sold. The meaning grew beyond a place because of **innovation and globalisation**.
- **2 broad categories of market:**
    1. **Consumer market:** all individuals who buy goods and services for **direct consumption or household use**.
    2. **Industrial market:** organisations in agriculture, forestry, fisheries, transport, construction, mining, utilities and services that buy goods and services **for further production**.
- **Marketing (limited definition):** the performance of business activities that direct the flow of goods and services to the consumer. It is too limited because marketing **starts before production** and **continues after the purchase**.
- **Starting point of marketing:** identifying and analysing the **needs, wants and demands** of consumers.
- **Marketing concept:** find out what customers need first, then produce it to satisfy them (e.g. starting production only **after market research**).

**The 6 marketing functions**

1. **Pricing** the product
2. **Distribution** of the product to places where it is needed
3. **Promotion** of the product and the company (making products known; making the company a household name)
4. **Rendering after-sale services** (maintenance, repairs, returning defective items)
5. **Training and motivating salespersons**
6. **Planning and controlling** the marketing effort of the company

- **Customer value:** customer value = total benefits the customer receives − total cost to the customer (money, time, effort).
- **Market share:** market share = firm's sales ÷ total market sales × 100.

**Explain more**

- **Consumer vs industrial purchases:** a family buying rice for its meals is in the **consumer** market. A construction firm in Abuja buying 2,000 bags of cement for an estate, a mining firm buying explosives, a transport company buying tyres and a fishery buying nets are all in the **industrial** market.
- **After-sale service:** an Ikeja phone retailer offering six months of free repairs and screen checks. A beverage firm's workshop that improves its sales team's selling skills and rewards top performers is **training and motivating salespersons**.
- **Trap:** preparing the statement of financial position is accounting, **not** a marketing function. Testing in a pilot market, researching needs and designing packaging happen **before** purchase.
- **Concepts compared:** the **production concept** stresses producing at the lowest cost; the **selling concept** stresses aggressive selling; the **marketing concept** starts from customer needs.
- **Customer value worked example:** a buyer values a blender's benefits at ₦90,000 and pays ₦70,000 in total. Customer value = ₦90,000 − ₦70,000 = ₦20,000. The higher the value, the likelier the purchase and loyalty.
- **Market share worked example:** total annual juice sales in a city are ₦48,000,000; a brand sells ₦7,200,000. Market share = 7,200,000 ÷ 48,000,000 × 100 = **15%**. (Same method: ₦3,000,000 of ₦12,000,000 = 25%.)

### Lesson 8.2: Segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP)

**Must know**

- **STP:** a customer-centred framework in which a broad market is divided into **Segments**, the most attractive are chosen as **Targets**, and a unique image is created to **Position** the product in target consumers' minds. **Order: segmentation, targeting, positioning.**
- **Market segmentation:** dividing the market into smaller groups of consumers who share similar **needs, characteristics or behaviours**. It improves strategy mainly by **enhancing targeting precision**.

**4 bases of segmentation**

| Basis | Variables |
| --- | --- |
| **Demographic** | Age, gender, income, education, family size |
| **Geographic** | Location: country, region, city |
| **Psychographic** | Lifestyle, values, personality, interests |
| **Behavioural** | Purchase habits, usage rate, loyalty, benefits sought |

- **Targeting:** **evaluating** segments and **selecting** one or more to focus on. Evaluate **segment size, growth potential, profitability and competition**; select segments that fit the company's **goals and resources**.
- **Positioning:** crafting a distinct, desirable image of the product in the target customers' minds **relative to competitors**. It involves **unique value**, adapting the **marketing mix (4Ps)** to the target, and **perception** (being seen as the best solution).
- **Why STP matters (4):** **focus** (resources on the most promising customers), **relevance** (personalised messages), **loyalty** (repeat business), **competitive edge** (standing out in crowded markets).
- **Segment size:** customers reached = total population × % in segment × % of segment targeted.

**Explain more**

- **Classify the basis:** savings accounts for those earning below ₦100,000 and above ₦1,000,000 a month = **demographic** (income). Different paint ranges for the South-South and the North-West because of climate and building style = **geographic**. A fitness brand for all ages sharing an active lifestyle = **psychographic**. Rewards for weekly shoppers vs occasional shoppers = **behavioural** (purchase habits, loyalty).
- **Trap:** lifestyle is **psychographic**, not demographic. Positioning is not shelf placement or logistics.
- A juice advertised as "the 100% natural choice for health-conscious mothers" against sugary rivals is **positioning**.
- Directors' personal hobbies are not a targeting criterion. STP does **not** remove the need for promotion.
- **Segment-size worked example:** Ibadan has 2,500,000 adults; 40% use smartphones; a fintech aims to reach 15% of that segment in year one. Segment = 2,500,000 × 0.40 = 1,000,000; customers = 1,000,000 × 0.15 = **150,000**. (Same method: 1,800,000 Lagos women aged 18-35 × 35% × 40% = 252,000.)
- **Growth strategies (Ansoff):** **market penetration** = growing sales of **existing products in existing markets**; market development = existing products in new markets; product development = new products for existing markets; diversification = new products in new markets.

### Lesson 8.3: The marketing mix (4Ps)

**Must know**

- **Marketing mix:** the set of factors (**product, price, place, promotion**) that help to execute a marketing programme.

| P | Meaning | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price** | The value or worth placed on an item | How much are customers willing to pay? |
| **Product** | Anything offered to the public for consumption or use | What is being offered for sale? |
| **Promotion** | Making the product known and communicating persuasively: **advertising, public relations, sales promotion** | How do we make it known; are customers aware? |
| **Place** | Making the product accessible and available | Where can it be got; how do we get the right product to the right place? |

- **Consumer products:** used directly by final users. **Industrial products:** used to produce other products or in the course of production.
- **Place** is linked to the view that **without customers accessing the products, production is incomplete**.

**Explain more**

- Flour sold to bakeries that make bread is an **industrial product** to the bakeries; bread bought by a household is a consumer product.
- Radio jingles in Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo persuading people to try a soap = **promotion**. Signing 300 new kiosks and supermarkets in Ibadan so chips are easy to find = **place**.
- **Trap:** choosing warehouses and transport routes is **place**, not promotion. **People** is **not** one of the 4Ps; it belongs to the extended **7Ps** for services (adding people, process, physical evidence).

### Lesson 8.4: Rural vs urban consumer markets in Nigeria

**Must know**

| Feature | Urban market | Rural market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income and spending | Higher disposable income; more on non-necessities | Often lower incomes, yet highly knowledgeable about quality and value |
| Preferences | International trends, digital marketing; quality and aesthetics | Functionality, durability, bulk purchases; strong cultural norms |
| Retail | More modern retail, though informal markets still dominate; high digital adoption | Informal open-air markets and kiosks; less modern retail |
| Other | Competitive; price-sensitive in some categories; affected by global factors | Fast-growing mobile access, rising literacy and connectivity |
| Value proposition | Brand and experience | Durability and value for money |

**Blurring lines:** infrastructure is better in towns but improving in villages; rural consumers are adopting digital and e-commerce tools **through mobile phones**; foodstuff markets are **integrated, with urban prices often driving rural prices**; urban buyers increasingly value traditional goods while rural buyers research online.

**Marketing implications:** adapt segmentation to the differences, using digital to reach both; build **robust distribution channels to overcome infrastructure gaps, especially in rural zones**; stress durability and value in rural areas and brand and experience in urban areas.

**Explain more**

- A detergent firm entering Benue villages should stress **durability and value for money** (affordable sachets, bulk packs), not celebrity endorsements, luxury or supermarket-only sales.
- Urban consumers switch brands more easily mainly because of **high exposure to alternatives**.
- **Trap:** "Rural consumers have little knowledge of product quality and value" is **false**; they are highly knowledgeable.

### Lesson 8.5: Human Resource Management (HRM)

**Must know**

- **HRM:** the practice of **recruiting, hiring, deploying and managing** an organisation's employees; the function that focuses on recruiting, managing and providing guidelines to the company's manpower.
- **Evolution:** from **planned exploitation of staff** to **HR metrics and measurement and strategic direction** to display value.

**6 functions of HRM**

1. **Recruitment** (selection and hiring)
2. **Training and development**
3. **Compensation and benefits** (pay, welfare packages)
4. **Performance appraisal**
5. **Ensuring legal compliance** (employment laws, tax, minimum wage)
6. **Advising** top management on policies and department heads on job design, recruitment, selection, job descriptions

**5 areas of HRM's importance**

1. **Recruitment and retention**
2. **Employee development and engagement**
3. **Compliance and risk management**
4. **Performance management**
5. **Organisational strategy and planning** (worker needs, restructuring, **succession plans**)

- **Labour turnover rate:** turnover rate = number of leavers ÷ average number of employees × 100.

**Explain more**

- **Classify:** checking that Ota factory workers get at least the national minimum wage and tax rules are followed = **legal compliance**. Yearly evaluation of each officer to find skill gaps = **performance appraisal**. Reviewing salaries, housing allowances and medical cover by grade = **compensation and benefits**. Setting selling prices is marketing, **not** HR.
- **Workforce (HR) planning** ensures the organisation has **the right number of people with the right skills when needed**.
- **Succession planning** reduces the risk of a **leadership vacuum**.
- **Training effectiveness** is best measured by **changes in trainees' behaviour and job performance** (Kirkpatrick's levels: reaction, learning, behaviour, results). Training and development is the HR activity that **most directly supports change management**, because it equips staff for new ways of working.
- **High engagement** mainly improves **productivity**. **More team autonomy** mainly improves **motivation**. **High turnover** raises **recruitment and training costs**.
- **Turnover worked example:** a firm starts the year with 110 staff and ends with 130; 18 people left. Average staff = (110 + 130) ÷ 2 = 120. Turnover rate = 18 ÷ 120 × 100 = **15%**.

### Lesson 8.6: Interdependence of functional areas and cross-functional coordination

**Must know**

- **Core idea:** **marketing sells the brand externally; HR builds the brand internally.** Employees are the first customers who become brand advocates.

**4 key areas of interdependence**

1. **Employer branding and recruitment:** marketing helps HR with messaging, **candidate personas** and channels such as social media; HR helps marketing by **hiring skilled, culturally aligned people** who can deliver what marketing promises.
2. **Internal communication and culture:** consistent internal messages turn employees into brand ambassadors.
3. **Onboarding and retention:** a joint onboarding process; **employee retention = customer retention**.
4. **Data and strategy:** marketing's analytics help HR read talent markets; HR gives insight into skills and engagement. Marketing drives revenue; HR drives workforce development.

**Key areas of collaboration (5):** employer branding; recruitment and onboarding; training and development; internal and external communication (one cohesive story); sustainability initiatives.

**Benefits of coordination (5):** stronger brand identity; improved talent acquisition; enhanced employee engagement; greater innovation; increased efficiency.

**Strategies for effective coordination (4):**

1. **Shared goals and metrics** (aligned KPIs)
2. **Regular meetings and workshops**
3. **Integrated technology** (shared platforms for data and communication)
4. **Cross-functional teams** for key initiatives, e.g. a new product launch

**Explain more**

- Coordination **breaks down silos**. Keeping each department's data on separate, unconnected systems is the opposite of integrated technology.
- A bank whose branches with highly engaged staff also have the most loyal customers shows **employee retention supports customer retention**.
- Putting marketing and HR staff into one team to plan a launch = **cross-functional team**.
- Wider view: marketing, HR, finance and production all depend on one another. Marketing's sales forecast tells production what to make, finance what money is needed, and HR how many staff to hire and train.

### One-minute recap

- Market = actual or potential buyers; consumer (own use) vs industrial (further production).
- Marketing starts with needs, wants and demands and continues after sale.
- 6 marketing functions: pricing, distribution, promotion, after-sale service, training and motivating salespersons, planning and controlling.
- STP in order: segmentation (demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural), targeting (size, growth, profitability, competition), positioning (image relative to competitors).
- 4Ps: product, price, place, promotion (advertising, PR, sales promotion). People is not one.
- Rural: value, durability, bulk, kiosks and open markets; urban: income, trends, digital, brand switching.
- HRM: 6 functions (recruitment, training, compensation, appraisal, legal compliance, advising); 5 areas of importance.
- Marketing sells the brand externally; HR builds it internally. Coordinate through shared KPIs, meetings, integrated technology, cross-functional teams.
- Formulas: market share = firm sales ÷ market sales × 100; segment reach = population × % × %; turnover = leavers ÷ average staff × 100.

### Theory practice

**Question:** A beverage firm estimates 3,000,000 adults in Kano. Research shows 30% prefer low-sugar drinks, and the firm aims to reach 20% of that segment in year one. Total low-sugar drink sales in Kano are ₦2,000,000,000, and the firm expects sales of ₦450,000,000. (a) How many customers will it reach? (b) What market share does it expect? (c) Which segmentation basis is "prefer low-sugar drinks"?

*Solution:*

(a) Segment = 3,000,000 × 0.30 = 900,000. Customers = 900,000 × 0.20 = **180,000**.

(b) Market share = 450,000,000 ÷ 2,000,000,000 × 100 = **22.5%**.

(c) Preference for low sugar is a **benefit sought**, so the basis is **behavioural** (if framed as a health-conscious lifestyle, psychographic).
